# Disciplined Opportunity-Search Method

## Purpose

This document defines a **disciplined method to search cyberspace for new product opportunities**.

The goal is not random browsing.

The goal is to create a repeatable process for identifying opportunities with:

- real pain,
- real buyers,
- real spend,
- and realistic entry wedges.

This method should be used as a recurring Discovery activity.

---

## Core Principle

Do not search the internet for ideas.

Search the internet for:

- repeated pain,
- expensive inefficiency,
- urgent frustration,
- weak current solutions,
- and visible buying behavior.

The opportunity usually sits at the intersection of:

**Pain × Spend × Frequency × Weak competition × Founder fit**

---

## The Main Rule

An opportunity is not validated because:

- it sounds exciting,
- people discuss it online,
- AI can be applied to it,
- or startups are being launched in the area.

It becomes interesting only when there is evidence that:

- the pain is real,
- the pain is repeated,
- buyers exist,
- current solutions are weak or incomplete,
- and we have some strategic right to enter.

---

## The Six-Step Method

## 1. Start from problems, not solutions

### Wrong starting point
- “What SaaS can I build?”
- “What AI product is trendy?”
- “What category is hot?”

### Right starting point
- What painful work is still being done badly?
- Which professionals complain repeatedly?
- Where are people spending time on low-value tasks?
- What categories exist but still leave users unhappy?

The starting point must be the problem, not the product fantasy.

---

## 2. Scan five signal surfaces

The internet should be treated as a **signal radar**.

Each surface reveals a different part of opportunity reality.

### A. Search-intent surface
Look for:
- growing search terms,
- related searches,
- rising category vocabulary,
- geographic concentration,
- evidence of active curiosity or demand.

Purpose:
to detect where attention is forming.

### B. New-product surface
Look for:
- new launches,
- recurring themes,
- crowded categories,
- overhyped categories,
- underexplored niches.

Purpose:
to detect where builders are moving.

### C. Buyer-spend surface
Look for:
- software categories with many products,
- buyer comparisons,
- category reviews,
- pricing pages,
- review density,
- category maturity.

Purpose:
to detect where budget already exists.

### D. Builder-pain surface
Look for:
- infrastructure trends,
- open-source momentum,
- emerging technical capabilities,
- tooling shifts,
- workflow-enabling technologies.

Purpose:
to detect enabling technology before it fully commercializes.

### E. Complaint / workflow-friction surface
Look for:
- repeated complaints,
- workaround discussions,
- “how do I…” threads,
- frustration with incumbent products,
- role-specific pain,
- operational bottlenecks.

Purpose:
to detect real-world workflow pain.

---

## 3. Turn raw signals into candidate opportunities

Every signal observed should be converted into a structured candidate.

### Candidate format
- Who is in pain?
- What job are they trying to do?
- What current workaround do they use?
- Why is the workaround bad?
- Is there visible software spend already?
- Why might this matter now?
- Why might we be able to enter?

The objective is to force discipline.

A trend without a buyer is weak.
A complaint without budget is weak.
A market without reach is weak.

---

## 4. Score each candidate

Each candidate should be scored against a standard opportunity rubric.

### Suggested dimensions
- pain intensity
- frequency
- spend evidence
- competitive crowding
- compliance burden
- speed to MVP
- founder fit
- distribution access
- strategic reuse across future products

### Suggested formula
**Opportunity Score = (Pain + Spend + Distribution + Founder Fit + Reuse) - (Crowding + Regulation + Time-to-MVP)**

This formula is not meant to produce perfect truth.

Its function is to force comparison and kill weak opportunities earlier.

---

## 5. Apply the three filters

A candidate should survive only if it passes all three filters.

## Filter 1 — Signal filter
There must be visible evidence from cyberspace that the opportunity is real.

Examples:
- repeated search behavior,
- active community discussion,
- repeated workflow complaints,
- launch activity,
- category formation.

## Filter 2 — Monetization filter
There must be evidence that money can exist in this space.

Examples:
- existing paid products,
- active software categories,
- buyer comparison behavior,
- visible pricing,
- recurring services or consulting around the problem.

## Filter 3 — Strategic-fit filter
There must be a plausible reason why we can enter.

Examples:
- customer access,
- domain credibility,
- existing relationships,
- strategic architecture reuse,
- language/geography wedge,
- underserved niche,
- workflow insight.

If a candidate fails one of these filters, it should not advance.

---

## 6. Run it on a fixed cadence

Opportunity search must be an operating system, not an occasional brainstorming session.

### Weekly cadence
- scan signal surfaces,
- capture candidate opportunities,
- score candidates,
- keep only the strongest,
- select one or two for deeper Discovery.

### Why cadence matters
Without cadence, opportunity search becomes mood-driven.
With cadence, it becomes strategic.

---

## What to search for

Avoid broad, vague search patterns.

### Weak searches
- AI ideas
- startup ideas
- legal tech
- healthcare AI

### Better searches
- workflow problems in a profession
- alternatives to a disliked tool
- software pain in a specific role
- manual processes still done by hand
- category pricing and buyer comparisons
- complaints about specific tools
- “how do I do X faster”
- “best software for Y”
- “problems with [category/tool]”
- “workflow automation for [role/industry]”

The goal is not general curiosity.  
The goal is high-signal discovery.

---

## The Three Best Opportunity Patterns

## 1. Expensive work still done manually
This is one of the strongest SaaS signals.

## 2. Specialized workflows forced into generic tools
This often reveals a vertical SaaS gap.

## 3. Existing categories with persistent user complaints
This often reveals a market where demand exists but incumbents are weak.

---

## Recommended Weekly Output

At the end of each weekly scan, produce:

- 5 candidate opportunities
- 2 opportunities worth deeper research
- 1 opportunity worth discussing with real potential buyers

This creates an opportunity funnel rather than random idea accumulation.

---

## Recommended Candidate Record

Each candidate opportunity should be documented with:

- Candidate title
- Target role or profession
- Problem statement
- Current workaround
- Pain evidence
- Search / signal evidence
- Spend evidence
- Competitor density
- Entry wedge
- Founder fit
- MVP path
- Strategic reuse potential
- Opportunity score
- Recommendation

---

## Common Errors to Avoid

## 1. Confusing attention with opportunity
A topic can be trendy without being commercially useful.

## 2. Confusing complaints with budget
People complain about many things they will never pay to solve.

## 3. Confusing AI applicability with market need
A workflow is not attractive just because AI can be inserted into it.

## 4. Ignoring distribution
A strong product with no route to customers is a weak opportunity.

## 5. Ignoring founder fit
A great market may still be the wrong market for us.

---

## How this fits Discovery

This method should feed the Discovery Phase.

The recommended Discovery structure is:

- opportunity source
- problem evidence
- buyer evidence
- spend evidence
- competitor map
- wedge hypothesis
- founder-fit assessment
- MVP difficulty
- portfolio reuse value
- viability score
- recommendation

This turns cyberspace exploration into a disciplined opportunity funnel.

Possible names for this process:

- Opportunity Radar
- Cyberspace Opportunity Scan
- Discovery Signal Scan
- Market Signal Funnel
- Opportunity Sensing Method

The best working name is probably:

**Opportunity Radar**

because it suggests:
- continuous scanning,
- signal interpretation,
- prioritization,
- and directional judgment.

---

## Final Principle

The purpose of disciplined opportunity search is not to collect ideas.

It is to find **high-probability opportunities** and eliminate low-probability distractions before they consume strategic energy.

A good opportunity-search method should produce fewer ideas, but better ones.

That is the point.
