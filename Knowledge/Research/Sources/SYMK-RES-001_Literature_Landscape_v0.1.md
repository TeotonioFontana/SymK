
# SYMK-RES-001
# Literature Landscape
## A Research Compass for SymK

**Status:** Research Draft  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Classification:** Living Document

---

# 1. Purpose

This document is **not** a traditional literature review.

Its purpose is to map the bodies of knowledge that are relevant to SymK, identify
what each discipline already solves, establish common terminology where possible,
and highlight the engineering problems that remain open.

The objective is to help SymK **stand on existing knowledge rather than unknowingly
reinvent it.**

---

# 2. Guiding Principles

1. Reuse established concepts whenever they satisfy SymK's needs.
2. Reuse established terminology whenever it is sufficiently precise.
3. Introduce new concepts only when repeated engineering evidence demonstrates a gap.
4. Evaluate ideas based on engineering value rather than novelty.

---

# 3. The Landscape

| Discipline | Primary Question | Expected Contribution |
|------------|------------------|----------------------|
| Ontology Engineering | What exists? | Identity, entities, relationships |
| Knowledge Engineering | How is knowledge acquired and organized? | Knowledge lifecycle |
| Terminology Science (ISO 704, Wüster) | What is a concept? | Concept systems, definitions |
| Domain-Driven Design | How do domains express meaning? | Ubiquitous language, domain boundaries |
| Knowledge Management | How do organizations preserve knowledge? | Governance and evolution |
| Knowledge Graphs | How are concepts connected? | Semantic networks |
| Systems Engineering | How should complex systems be structured? | Architectural separation |
| Human–AI Cooperation | How do humans and AI collaborate? | Cooperative workflows |

---

# 4. Evaluation Template

Every discipline will be analyzed using the same template.

- Primary engineering question
- Mature contributions
- Standards and influential works
- Terminology worth adopting
- Concepts SymK should reuse
- Concepts SymK should avoid reinventing
- Open problems from the SymK perspective
- Relevance score (1–10)

---

# 5. Preliminary Observations

## Ontology Engineering

Strengths:
- Entity identity
- Formal relationships
- Upper ontologies

Potential gap:
- Long-term cooperative evolution of knowledge.

---

## Terminology Science

Strengths:
- Concept orientation
- Controlled vocabularies
- Definition methodology

Potential gap:
- Multi-agent knowledge evolution.

---

## Domain-Driven Design

Strengths:
- Ubiquitous language
- Bounded contexts
- Domain modeling

Potential gap:
- Cross-domain conceptual integration.

---

## Knowledge Management

Strengths:
- Organizational learning
- Governance
- Curation

Potential gap:
- Engineering semantics independent of organizational practice.

---

## Human–AI Cooperation

Rapidly evolving field.

Potential contribution:
- Operational cooperation models.

Potential gap:
- Stable knowledge engineering independent of specific AI technologies.

# 7. Research Questions

1. Which SymK concepts already exist under different names?
2. Which concepts require new engineering definitions?
3. Which standards should SymK explicitly adopt?
4. Which unresolved problems justify new SymK contributions?

---

# 9. Success Criterion

SYMK-RES-001 succeeds when future architectural decisions can answer:

- Which discipline already addresses this problem?
- What should SymK reuse?
- What should SymK contribute?
- Why is a new concept justified?

---

# Closing

SymK does not seek to replace Ontology Engineering, Knowledge Engineering,
Terminology Science, Domain-Driven Design, or Knowledge Management.

Its ambition is to integrate their strongest engineering ideas into a coherent
framework for long-term cooperation between human and artificial intelligence,
while remaining disciplined enough to introduce new concepts only when justified
by repeated evidence.
