# PLC_META — 1.6 Discovery Outcomes (Overview)

## Mission of PLC_1.6

The mission of PLC_1.6 is **not** to prove that an idea is good or to authorize a market launch.

Its job is to answer a simpler, harsher question:

> **Does this idea deserve to enter the architecture & engineering lab at all?**

In other words:

- PLC_1 (Discovery) exists to:
  - Take a raw idea,
  - Refine the problem definition,
  - Build a deep understanding of the context, stakeholders, and market,
  - And crystallize a **clear, coherent product hypothesis**.

- **PLC_1.6 is the gate into the design / architecture phases (PLC_2+).**  
  Its primary value is in **negative decisions** such as:
  - “This is not worth taking into architecture and engineering.”
  - “This is interesting but should stay as a concept or note, not a project right now.”
  - “At most, this might later become a small internal tool, but not a structured product effort.”

We start from a simple assumption:

> The set of problems that do **not** deserve a solution is much larger  
> than the set that can justify real architecture, engineering, and go-to-market effort.

So PLC_1.6 is optimized to:

- **Avoid bad paths** that would waste time and money in design and build.
- Make “No-Go” and “Internal-only / Park” outcomes **legitimate successes** of Discovery, not failures.
- Ensure that ideas which **do** advance to architecture (PLC_2) already have:
  - A clear product hypothesis,
  - A grounded view of their market and stakeholders,
  - And early signals that the engineering effort *might* be worth it.

The **final product/market decision** does **not** happen at PLC_1.6.  
It only becomes solid after the architecture and sizing phases (PLC_2/PLC_3), when we understand the **real complexity, effort, and risk** involved.

## Sub-items in this folder

This folder defines **how any product instance** (IaaC, LexBrain, etc.) must close **PLC_1 – Discovery**, split into smaller building blocks:

- **1.6.1 Decision Framework** — allowed decisions at PLC_1.6 and required preconditions.
- **1.6.2 Business Size Fit Template** — how to classify which business sizes the product suits.
- **1.6.3 Consulting Leverage Template** — how to assess the product as a consulting / services weapon.
- **1.6.4 Career Leverage Template** — how to assess the product as a career asset.
- **1.6.5 Success Metrics & Risks Template** — how to define PLC_2/3 success metrics and key risks.

Each product-specific PLC (e.g., `PLC_IaaC`) must instantiate these templates with its own **1.6.x** answers.
