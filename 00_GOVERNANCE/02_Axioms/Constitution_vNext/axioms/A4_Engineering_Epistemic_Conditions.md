# A4 — Engineering of Epistemic Conditions

**Status:** Proposed  
**Version:** 0.2  
**Scope:** Universal SymK engineering  
**Ratified:** No

## Normative statement

> **SymK does not engineer Knowledge or truth directly. It engineers accountable and evolvable conditions through which Intelligences can pursue, achieve, express, preserve, share, challenge, apply, and revise Knowledge responsibly within stated scopes.**

## Constitutional purpose

A4 defines the honest object of Knowledge Engineering. It prevents builders from claiming to manufacture or store Knowledge while still recognizing that representations, processes, institutions, and technologies materially affect whether Knowledge can be pursued and responsibly used.

## Scope and jurisdiction

A4 governs SymK conceptual design, architecture, implementation, operation, and evolution. It applies to technical and institutional conditions. It does not deny that engineering produces concrete artifacts or that such artifacts participate causally in knowing.

## Governed definitions

- **Epistemic conditions:** arrangements that enable or constrain inquiry, warrant, expression, preservation, challenge, application, and revision.
- **Knowledge Engineering:** disciplined construction and governance of such conditions and their computable artifacts.
- **Accountable:** attributable, inspectable, challengeable, and answerable to consequences.
- **Lifecycle:** the continuing movement from acquisition and representation through validation, application, consequence, and revision.
- **Scope:** declared boundary within which a claim, model, capacity, or validation supports reliance.

## Derivation from the foundation papers

The Knowledge paper makes Knowledge an epistemic achievement rather than a storable object. Knowledge Engineering reframes acquisition as situated construction, representation as projection, validation as plural, and consequence as part of the lifecycle. Properties and Projections explains the selectivity of formalization. Intelligence adds governed capacity attribution; Education reveals that architectures also form habits. A4 identifies the common engineering object: epistemic conditions.

## Foundational commitments absorbed

- Reality precedes models.
- Engineering precedes implementation.
- Acquisition is constructive and situated.
- Representation is a governed projection.
- Validation is plural and continuing.
- Application and consequence belong to the epistemic lifecycle.
- Generative capacity accelerates proposition, not warrant.

## Relationship with other axioms

A4 realizes the purpose of A0 without violating A1. It operationalizes A2 role governance and A3 formative responsibility. J1 limits its philosophical and engineering claims, while A6 carries its constraints into project Engineering Charters.

## Authoritative interpretation

“Does not engineer directly” is a limit on ontological and epistemic claims, not a retreat from engineering responsibility. Conditions may strongly affect what becomes knowable, trusted, forgotten, or acted upon. “Evolvable” requires lineage and governed migration; it does not authorize silent mutation.

## Derived principles

- Preserve claim, evidence, representation, context, status, and history as distinct governable objects.
- Make projection purpose and excluded dimensions visible where material.
- Continue validation after deployment through consequence feedback.
- Treat education, authority, access, and memory as engineering concerns.

## Engineering consequences

Architectures require provenance, versioning, scoped validation, uncertainty, challenge, rollback or correction paths, and consequence feedback. Generated ontologies or claims cannot enter canonical use solely through production. Implementation choices that change constitutional meaning require governed review.

## Derived-project inheritance

Every derived project must define its epistemic objects, lifecycle, validators, application boundaries, feedback paths, and migration rules in an Engineering Charter. Domain systems may engineer authoritative processes without claiming that authority creates truth.

## Prohibited interpretations

- Knowledge Engineering is merely data engineering with semantic labels.
- SymK can manufacture truth through formalization or approval.
- Because Knowledge is not directly engineered, technical design is epistemically neutral.
- Deployment ends validation.
- Formal correctness guarantees responsible application.

## Counterexamples and compatibility tests

- A knowledge base is treated as a manufactured stock of Knowledge: incompatible.
- A generated ontology enters canonical use without governed review: incompatible.
- A formally valid inference is applied beyond its stated scope: incompatible.
- A system traces sources, limitations, decisions, outcomes, and revisions: compatible in principle.

## Open questions and preserved dissent

- The boundary between an epistemic condition and an epistemic achievement requires further clarification.
- The minimum lifecycle obligations for low-consequence systems remain open.
- Dissent is preserved over whether “Knowledge Engineering” should be retained as the discipline's name given the risk of objectifying Knowledge.
