# A6 — Constitutional Inheritance

**Status:** Proposed  
**Version:** 0.2  
**Scope:** Every SymK-derived project  
**Ratified:** No

## Normative statement

> **Every SymK-derived project inherits the SymK Constitution. It may introduce project axioms that specialize inherited commitments for its domain, but it shall not silently contradict, weaken, redefine, or bypass them. Every specialization, exception, and incompatibility must be explicit, justified, scoped, and governed.**

## Constitutional purpose

A6 preserves coherence across SymK, LexBrain, sshConnectivity, and future projects without freezing either parent or child. It prevents silent divergence while giving domain evidence a governed route to specialize, challenge, and improve the Constitution.

## Scope and jurisdiction

A6 applies to every project claiming to derive from or be constitutionally compatible with SymK. It governs conceptual, design, and engineering inheritance. It does not require identical implementations or automatic adoption of every nonconstitutional SymK artifact.

## Governed definitions

- **Derived project:** a project that claims SymK lineage or constitutional compatibility.
- **Inheritance:** acceptance of a declared SymK constitutional version as a higher constraint.
- **Specialization:** a scoped refinement preserving the inherited commitment.
- **Strengthening:** a stricter local obligation that remains compatible with its parent.
- **Exception:** a disclosed, justified, bounded departure under governance.
- **Constitutional challenge:** evidenced claim that an inherited commitment is incoherent, harmful, or inapplicable.

## Derivation from the foundation papers

The SymK Charter distinguishes discipline, Body of Knowledge, and implementations. Progressive specialization supplies scoped inheritance; epistemic humility denies parent infallibility. Philosophical jurisdiction separates universal Epistemology from domain Ontology and Logic. Project analysis demonstrates the missing middle layer: project axioms plus Engineering Charters. A6 makes lineage and conflict explicit rather than relying on naming or directory placement.

## Foundational commitments absorbed

- Core commitments are not silently rewritten by specialization.
- Domain knowledge does not contaminate or redefine the universal Core by accident.
- Standards evolve with lineage and compatibility analysis.
- Stable products do not justify preserving known constitutional harm.
- Projects may return evidence that challenges SymK.

## Relationship with other axioms

A6 is the proposed transmission rule for A0–A4 under J1. It cannot strengthen project authority above A1 humility or use inheritance to defeat A2 non-privilege. J1 determines which commitments belong at the parent, project, organization, deployment, or external-authority level. Conflicts among inherited commitments must use the constitutional challenge protocol.

## Authoritative interpretation

Inheritance is neither copying nor automatic synchronization. A project inherits a declared version and performs compatibility review before migration. Explicit exceptions remain incompatibilities unless validly governed; disclosure alone does not make them compatible. Parent precedence is provisional when credible domain evidence challenges the parent.

## Derived principles

- Declare the inherited constitutional version.
- Map every project axiom to its parent, or identify it as a new domain commitment.
- Classify relationships as instantiation, specialization, strengthening, extension, exception, or challenge.
- Preserve conflict evidence, decisions, dissent, and migration history.

## Engineering consequences

Projects require a constitution-to-design traceability matrix and an Engineering Charter. Releases must state compatibility and known exceptions. Parent amendments trigger impact review rather than silent adoption. Automated checks may detect missing mappings but cannot settle philosophical compatibility alone.

## Derived-project inheritance

This axiom is self-applying: every derived project must reproduce its inheritance declaration, conflict procedure, and compatibility status in its own governance. Subprojects inherit through the same chain, with each intermediate specialization visible.

## Prohibited interpretations

- SymK is infallible because it is the parent.
- A local need silently overrides a universal axiom.
- All parent documents, including drafts and implementation choices, are inherited norms.
- Disclosure alone legitimizes an exception.
- Compatibility requires identical architecture or vocabulary.

## Counterexamples and compatibility tests

- A project silently changes the meaning of `Knowledge`: incompatible.
- A legal constraint is presented as a universal Human/AI truth: incompatible.
- A project rejects an inherited axiom, discloses the conflict, supplies evidence, and suspends its compatibility claim pending review: procedurally compatible challenge.
- A stronger audit obligation preserves all parent commitments: compatible specialization.

## Open questions and preserved dissent

- The authority competent to approve exceptions remains to be constituted.
- The conditions under which a project may continue using SymK artifacts while declaring incompatibility remain open.
- Dissent is preserved over whether any exception can retain “SymK-compatible” status or must use a distinct status.
