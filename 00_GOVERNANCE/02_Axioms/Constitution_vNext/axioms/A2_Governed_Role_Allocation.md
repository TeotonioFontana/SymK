# A2 — Non-Privilege and Governed Role Allocation

**Status:** Proposed  
**Version:** 0.2  
**Scope:** Universal SymK  
**Ratified:** No

## Normative statement

> **No epistemic role, authority, authorization, responsibility, or standing is conferred solely by Intelligence type or origin. Their allocation shall be purpose-specific, evidence-bearing, consequence-sensitive, accountable, and revisable.**

## Constitutional purpose

A2 prevents origin-based privilege and the opposite error of erasing relevant differences. It replaces permanent Human/AI constitutional roles with explicit allocation based on demonstrated capacities, lawful constraints, consequences, and accountable governance.

## Scope and jurisdiction

A2 governs epistemic and cooperative roles within SymK. It does not determine moral personhood, legal personality, consciousness, or every social entitlement. Type may remain relevant evidence or a lawful constraint, but is never sufficient by itself as a universal epistemic rule.

## Governed definitions

- **Capability:** scoped capacity supported by evidence.
- **Role:** expected function in an epistemic process.
- **Authority:** recognized standing to settle or direct within a scope.
- **Authorization:** permission to act.
- **Responsibility:** answerability for obligations and consequences.
- **Accountability:** arrangements through which conduct can be examined and answered for.
- **Affected-party standing:** governed ability to be heard or challenge because one bears consequences.

## Derivation from the foundation papers

The Intelligence paper rejects the Human as universal template, separates capability from performance, and denies that Intelligence entails wisdom or authority. The Knowledge paper distinguishes institutional and epistemic standing. The Education paper shows that cooperative fitness is formed, not guaranteed by type. The epistemic-labor discussion separates capability, role, authority, authorization, authorship, causal contribution, and responsibility. A2 constitutionalizes those separations.

## Foundational commitments absorbed

- Origin confers neither capability nor privilege.
- Intelligence is scoped, graded, profiled, and challengeably attributed.
- Capability does not entail authorization, authority, wisdom, or responsibility.
- Meaningful human participation is not reducible to a click.
- Collective and hybrid bearers require explicit boundaries and organization.

## Relationship with other axioms

A2 identifies fair participant governance under A0. A1 prevents authority from manufacturing Knowledge. A3 allows roles to evolve through formation. A4 requires allocations to be implemented traceably. A6 governs project specialization and lawful exceptions.

## Authoritative interpretation

“Solely” is decisive: type and origin may be relevant but cannot conclude the allocation without purpose, evidence, consequence, and governance. Equality under A2 is non-privilege, not sameness. Revisability does not erase continuity of responsibility for prior acts.

## Derived principles

- Allocate roles per task, domain, evidence, and consequence.
- Separate proposal, review, decision, authorization, execution, authorship, and accountability.
- Give affected parties meaningful challenge paths.
- Reassess allocations when capacity, context, law, or consequences change.

## Engineering consequences

Role models must record bearer, scope, evidence, authority, authorization, responsibility, expiry, and review. Interfaces must not manufacture “human oversight” through ceremonial approval. Logs should preserve causal contribution without confusing it with authorship or liability.

## Derived-project inheritance

Projects may impose domain-specific role restrictions, including legal human authorization, but must classify and justify them. “AI proposes; human decides” may be a scoped policy; it may not be presented as a universal property of Intelligences.

## Prohibited interpretations

- Human and artificial participants are identical or interchangeable.
- Capability automatically creates permission or authority.
- Removing type privilege removes legal or ethical constraints.
- Responsibility can always be transferred to the final approver.
- Nonhuman contribution automatically establishes moral or legal standing.

## Counterexamples and compatibility tests

- Universal “AI always proposes; humans always decide”: incompatible.
- A human click transfers responsibility without meaningful review: incompatible.
- A licensed lawyer must authorize a filing while an AI may research and draft under review: potentially compatible if the allocation is explicit and substantive.
- A reliable participant receives broader authority after evidence and governed review: compatible in principle.

## Open questions and preserved dissent

- The relation between epistemic standing and moral or legal standing remains outside the present axiom.
- Criteria for meaningful participation require further definition.
- Dissent remains over whether “responsibility” can coherently apply to every Intelligence bearer or only to accountable persons and institutions.
