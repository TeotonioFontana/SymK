# A1 — Epistemic Humility

**Status:** Proposed  
**Version:** 0.2  
**Scope:** Universal SymK  
**Ratified:** No

## Normative statement

> **Knowledge is distinct from every claim, representation, artifact, consensus, computation, and institutional status through which it is expressed or governed. No SymK system may create truth or epistemic warrant merely by storing, generating, inferring, approving, or naming an object.**

## Constitutional purpose

A1 prevents SymK from confusing its manipulable objects with the epistemic achievements they may support. It protects inquiry from manufactured certainty, institutional fiat, representational reification, and unjustified authority.

## Scope and jurisdiction

A1 applies to all claims, representations, validations, classifications, inferences, approvals, and canonical artifacts governed by SymK. It does not settle a complete theory of truth or Knowledge and does not prevent institutions from making operational decisions under uncertainty.

## Governed definitions

- **Claim:** a truth-apt or action-guiding proposition attributable to a source.
- **Representation:** a purpose-dependent projection used to express selected aspects of an object or claim.
- **Epistemic warrant:** grounds sufficient for responsible reliance within a stated scope.
- **Institutional status:** an authorized designation whose force is organizational or legal, not automatically epistemic.
- **Knowledge:** governed by `FC-006 Knowledge`.

## Derivation from the foundation papers

The Knowledge paper distinguishes Knowledge from claims, assets, representations, and statuses. The Intelligence paper makes performance evidence of capacity rather than its identity, reinforcing the same restraint toward outputs. Properties and Projections shows that every representation selects and suppresses dimensions for a purpose. Knowledge Engineering limits engineering to conditions rather than truth itself. A1 compresses these conclusions into a prohibition on manufactured warrant.

## Foundational commitments absorbed

- Knowledge is an epistemic achievement.
- Claims, grounds, assets, representations, and statuses remain distinguishable.
- Formal validity does not establish premise truth or application fitness.
- Canonization and consensus do not manufacture Knowledge.
- All epistemic reliance is scoped and challengeable.

## Relationship with other axioms

A1 constrains the medium named by A0 and the engineering object of A4. It prevents A2 authority from becoming truth and A6 inheritance from becoming infallibility. J1 keeps its epistemological reach bounded.

## Authoritative interpretation

The axiom asserts distinction, not separation: representations can be indispensable to knowing without becoming Knowledge themselves. Institutional approval may authorize action but does not erase the need to distinguish authority from warrant. Humility requires proportionate claims, not paralysis or universal skepticism.

## Derived principles

- Preserve provenance, grounds, scope, context, status, and uncertainty separately.
- Keep observations, hypotheses, candidates, accepted claims, and superseded claims distinguishable.
- Treat confidence and consensus as evidence requiring interpretation.
- Preserve challenge and revision even for canonical artifacts.

## Engineering consequences

Schemas must not collapse claim, source, evidence, representation, decision, and epistemic status into one object. Interfaces must avoid presenting generated confidence as truth. Validation must be plural, scoped, and traceable. Canonicalization must record authority and reasoning without claiming infallibility.

## Derived-project inheritance

Projects must identify what their stored objects represent, what status they have, who conferred it, and what reliance it supports. Domain terms such as “valid,” “approved,” or “authoritative” must not silently mean “true.”

## Prohibited interpretations

- Representations cannot participate in Knowledge.
- Truth is inaccessible or action is impossible under uncertainty.
- Institutional or legal authority is illegitimate.
- Every claim deserves equal standing.
- Human approval or AI confidence is sufficient warrant by itself.

## Counterexamples and compatibility tests

- A field named `truth=true` settles epistemic status: incompatible.
- A knowledge-graph triple becomes Knowledge by insertion: incompatible.
- A court judgment is treated as legally authoritative while its factual and doctrinal grounds remain distinguishable: compatible.
- A generated answer is relied upon within a tested scope with provenance and review: potentially compatible.

## Open questions and preserved dissent

- The necessary and sufficient conditions for Knowledge remain governed outside this axiom.
- The threshold for “responsible reliance” varies by consequence and remains open to domain specialization.
- Dissent is preserved over whether “truth” should remain in the statement or whether “warrant” is the only governable object.
