# Constitutional Conflict, Challenge, and Feedback Protocol

**Status:** Proposed directional baseline
**Version:** 0.2
**Ratified:** No
**Authority:** `SYMK-2X-DR-005`, Stage-Accepted for SymK 2.0 migration

## 1. Purpose and limits

This protocol governs the direction in which conflicts, challenges, constraints, evidence, and change travel among SymK, derived projects, engineering, implementation, organizations, deployments, and external authorities.

It establishes:

- when a difference becomes a conflict;
- minimum conflict classes;
- the nearest-adequate-owner rule;
- ownership limits during resolution;
- upward evidence and lateral external authority;
- immediate containment duties;
- and visibility of material unresolved incompatibility.

It does not finalize adjudication bodies, voting, deadlines, appeals, exception approval, compatibility-status vocabulary, or project migration mechanics. Those mechanisms belong to SymK 2.3 and 2.6.

## 2. Trigger

Use this protocol when any of the following appears materially incompatible with another applicable commitment, authority, meaning, realization, or observed consequence:

- a SymK constitutional or conceptual commitment;
- a derived-project Constitution, domain concept, Ontology, or Logic;
- an Engineering Charter, standard, specification, architecture, or projection;
- an implementation or operational behavior;
- an organizational authorization or policy;
- applicable law, professional duty, institutional authority, or external standard;
- evidence supplied by a participant or affected party;
- or a consequence observed during inquiry, testing, deployment, or use.

The protocol is not limited to a child project appearing incompatible with SymK. Any layer may contain the defect, exceed its jurisdiction, or receive evidence challenging its commitments.

## 3. A difference is not automatically a conflict

The following are normally compatible when explicit, traceable, and correctly placed:

- instantiation;
- specialization that preserves applicable parent meaning;
- stronger local constraints;
- domain extension outside the parent's jurisdiction;
- alternative projection preserving applicable obligations;
- alternative implementation satisfying the same governing specification;
- and stricter organizational or deployment policy within legitimate authority.

A difference becomes a conflict when applicable commitments, meanings, authorities, or realizations cannot be honored together within overlapping scope, when a realization fails a governing obligation, or when one layer assumes authority belonging to another.

Before asserting contradiction, normalize terminology, scope, version, time, population, task, stakes, and jurisdiction.

## 4. Immediate response

When credible material conflict or harm is identified:

- do not conceal it through ambiguous wording, terminology, or unqualified compatibility claims;
- contain immediate risk at the nearest operational layer capable of acting;
- freeze promotion or expansion that materially depends on the disputed conclusion;
- preserve evidence, affected artifacts, and relevant system state;
- notify the known owners of contested artifacts and applicable external authority;
- and distinguish safe, unaffected activity from activity dependent on resolution.

Containment does not decide which proposition is true or which governing artifact must ultimately change. Severe harm with a credible causal path may justify immediate escalation without waiting for recurrence.

## 5. Minimum conflict and feedback record

Record at least:

- the originating inquiry, project, system, organization, deployment, or affected party;
- the exact statements, behaviors, decisions, or authorities in tension;
- affected artifacts and versions;
- definitions, mappings, assumptions, and projection purposes;
- applicable jurisdictions and authority kinds;
- domain, time, population, task, stakes, and environment;
- provenance and method of observation;
- expected and actual behavior;
- concrete cases, affected participants, and consequences;
- plausible alternative explanations and dissent;
- recurrence or reproducibility evidence where available;
- immediate containment already taken;
- the owners of the contested artifacts;
- the nearest layer believed capable of correcting the demonstrated cause;
- compatibility claims affected while resolution is pending;
- and future review or reopening conditions.

This is the minimum information envelope, not a final record schema.

## 6. Conflict classes and initial routing

| Class | Meaning | Initial route |
|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary conflict | The same term carries materially different meanings, or different terms conceal the same meaning | Semantic owners at S2 or P2; clarify mappings and scopes |
| Scope conflict | Statements appear incompatible because domain, time, population, task, stakes, or jurisdiction differ | Owners of both statements; normalize scope first |
| Version conflict | Artifacts depend on different parent or dependency versions | Adopting and source-owning layers for impact and compatibility review |
| Projection conflict | A representation omits, strengthens, distorts, or contradicts its source | Projection owner first; source owner only if the loss reveals a source defect |
| Conformance conflict | A realization fails its governing specification, standard, concept, or commitment | Nearest realization or specification owner capable of correction |
| Jurisdiction conflict | A layer governs outside its legitimate reach or authority kind | Owners of both boundaries; remove or explicitly reclassify the overreach |
| Contradiction | Applicable propositions cannot both hold in overlapping scope | Each proposition's owner; compatibility remains unresolved until scope or commitment changes |
| Weakening or bypass | A narrower layer evades an applicable inherited commitment while claiming compatibility | Narrower owner for correction or source owner through explicit challenge; no silent exception |
| External-authority collision | Internal commitment conflicts with applicable law, professional duty, institutional authority, or external standard | O1 containment plus the internal and external owners within their jurisdictions |
| Operational invalidation | Observed behavior, failure, or consequence defeats an assumption, requirement, or capability claim | Local containment, then nearest adequate causal owner with evidence |
| Responsibility conflict | Capability, causation, authority, authorization, accountability, or consequence-bearing are allocated incompatibly | Project, engineering, organizational, and external owners according to authority |

Classification may change as evidence develops. One case may involve several classes.

## 7. Nearest-adequate-owner rule

> **A conflict is first examined at the nearest layer that both owns the contested decision and can correct the demonstrated cause. It moves upward when evidence challenges a parent commitment, reveals a systemic cause, recurs beyond the narrower layer's jurisdiction, or shows severity requiring broader review.**

Examples:

- an isolated parser defect begins at I1;
- repeated parser failures caused by an ambiguous interface contract challenge E2;
- cross-implementation failures caused by a binding engineering obligation challenge E1;
- failures rooted in a domain concept challenge P2 or P1;
- cross-domain evidence against a universal semantic distinction challenges S2;
- evidence that a constitutional commitment causes unavoidable incompatibility or harm challenges S1.

This rule prevents both constitutional inflation of every local defect and suppression of systemic counterevidence inside implementation or operation.

## 8. Ownership limits during conflict

Each layer changes only the artifacts it owns:

- I1 may patch implementation but not rewrite E2 requirements silently;
- E2 may revise architecture but not redefine a P2 domain concept silently;
- P2 may revise a domain concept but not amend S2 universal meaning silently;
- P1 may qualify or suspend its compatibility claim but not amend S1;
- O1 may prohibit or condition local use but not rewrite the project Constitution;
- S1 or S2 may revise their own artifacts through governance but may not erase domain or operational evidence to preserve authority.

When several owners must change, each change occurs under its own authority. The shared conflict record identifies dependencies and sequence.

## 9. Direction rules

### 9.1 Downward constraint

Accepted broader or more abstract commitments constrain narrower and more concrete realizations within their jurisdiction. A narrower layer must comply, narrow its claim, raise an evidenced challenge, or declare incompatibility.

Downward precedence does not imply infallibility. Credible challenge may require caution, containment, or suspension while review proceeds.

### 9.2 Downward impact after change

When an owner changes an artifact, dependent layers receive an impact-review obligation rather than an automatic rewrite. A dependent owner may adopt, migrate, remain temporarily on the prior version, challenge, or declare an exception or incompatibility according to later governance.

### 9.3 Upward evidence

Evidence may originate in research, projects, engineering, implementation, operation, or affected external parties. It may challenge any layer whose commitment plausibly caused or materially contributed to the observed issue.

Evidence has epistemic force, not automatic amendment authority.

### 9.4 Lateral external authority

Law, professional standards, scientific findings, technical limits, institutional decisions, and other external constraints enter at their applicable scope. They may decisively constrain action without becoming concepts or axioms owned by SymK.

### 9.5 Lateral peer coordination

Peer projects and sibling components may exchange evidence, mappings, and artifacts. Reuse does not grant authority over the source or the peer. Implications for a shared parent return to the parent's owner.

## 10. Evidence strength and escalation

| Evidence pattern | Default implication |
|---|---|
| Isolated implementation error | Review I1; no automatic architectural or conceptual consequence |
| Recurrent error across one implementation family | Review E2 assumptions and specifications |
| Recurrent failure across independent implementations of one project | Review E1, P2, or P1 according to demonstrated cause |
| Recurrent failure across different SymK-derived domains | Review S2 and possibly S1 |
| Single severe harm with credible causal connection | Immediate containment and direct notice to every plausibly causal owner; recurrence not required |
| Successful operation | Supporting evidence within observed scope, never universal proof |
| No observed failure | Weak evidence unless observation coverage and detection capacity are demonstrated |

Escalation depends on severity, causal credibility, scope, recurrence, affected rights and responsibilities, and the capacity of the current owner to correct the cause.

## 11. External authority and operational precedence

An external authority may control action within its jurisdiction without possessing semantic precedence over SymK.

For example, a court may prohibit an AI-assisted procedure, privacy law may forbid a data flow, a professional regulator may require human authorization, or a safety standard may require stronger controls than a project baseline.

The operational response is to comply, restrict, redesign, suspend, or decline deployment within the applicable jurisdiction. The conceptual response is separate: determine whether the external constraint is a local condition, a domain commitment, or evidence challenging SymK.

Neither SymK compatibility nor epistemic warrant creates legal or organizational authorization. Conversely, legal or organizational authorization does not establish truth or epistemic warrant.

## 12. Compatibility during unresolved conflict

> **A material unresolved conflict remains visible in every affected compatibility, conformance, authorization, and reliance claim. Disclosure is necessary but does not itself make the conflict compatible or the affected use responsible.**

An affected artifact, project, system, or deployment must not present unqualified compatibility when it:

- knowingly contradicts an applicable inherited commitment;
- cannot demonstrate preservation of material parent meaning;
- operates under an unresolved exception;
- depends on a challenged commitment whose possible failure makes the claim misleading;
- or remains subject to unresolved external prohibition or authorization uncertainty.

Safe inquiry and unaffected operations may continue only when their independence from the conflict is stated and justified.

The final compatibility-status vocabulary and exception mechanics remain deferred to SymK 2.3 and 2.6.

## 13. Recurring lineage patterns

### Child conflicts with parent

The child corrects its artifact, narrows its compatibility claim, or raises an evidenced challenge. It does not silently reinterpret the parent.

### Parent changes after child adoption

The child receives an impact review. It does not inherit automatically and is not retroactively rewritten or silently made incompatible.

### Sibling projects disagree

Domain-specific difference is permitted. A parent conflict exists only when siblings claim incompatible interpretations of the same inherited commitment within overlapping scope.

### Organization conflicts with project design

The organization may decline or constrain adoption. A changed design becomes a governed local variant with an explicit compatibility impact.

### Implementation contradicts documentation

The discrepancy is a conformance conflict. Running code does not automatically become the source of truth; the relevant owners decide whether implementation, specification, or a higher artifact changes.

## 14. Resolution directions

Depending on ownership, jurisdiction, and evidence, resolution may:

- clarify terminology or mappings;
- correct or narrow scope;
- restore conformance in a realization;
- revise an owned statement or design;
- reclassify a misplaced commitment;
- preserve a prior version pending migration;
- introduce a governed local strengthening;
- suspend or restrict use;
- propose an explicit exception through later governance;
- qualify or withdraw a compatibility claim;
- amend a governing artifact through its own process;
- or preserve a visible unresolved incompatibility.

No resolution may silently amend another owner's artifact or erase material evidence, dissent, consequences, migration duties, or reopening conditions.

## 15. Anti-suppression, anti-inflation, and anti-overreach

### Anti-suppression

No layer may conceal, downgrade, or trap evidence merely to protect the authority, reputation, or stability of a governing artifact, project, organization, or system.

### Anti-inflation

No participant may present an isolated defect or disagreement as universal refutation without evidence connecting it to the broader commitment.

### Anti-overreach

Frequent conflict may mean that a narrower layer violates inherited commitments, that a broader layer has exceeded its jurisdiction, or both. Neither interpretation is presumed without evidence.

## 16. Direction test

When a conflict appears, ask:

1. What exact statements, behaviors, decisions, or authorities are in tension?
2. Do terminology, scope, versions, time, population, task, stakes, and jurisdictions overlap?
3. Is this a permitted specialization, strengthening, extension, alternative projection, or local restriction rather than conflict?
4. Which layer owns each contested artifact?
5. What is the nearest owner capable of correcting the demonstrated cause?
6. Does evidence implicate a parent commitment or only a realization?
7. Is immediate containment required?
8. Which external authorities apply and what do they control?
9. Which compatibility, conformance, authorization, or reliance claims must be qualified?
10. Which dependent owners need impact notice if a governing artifact changes?

## 17. Decision record requirements

Every governed disposition preserves:

- evidence and provenance;
- classification and routing rationale;
- dissent and alternatives;
- owners and authorities involved;
- immediate and long-term consequences;
- affected artifacts and dependent-document impact;
- compatibility, conformance, and authorization effects;
- migration or containment duties;
- unresolved questions;
- and review or reopening conditions.
