# J1 — Philosophical and Engineering Jurisdiction

**Artifact class:** Constitutional Jurisdiction Article
**Status:** Proposed
**Version:** 0.3
**Scope:** SymK, derived projects, organizations, deployments, and external-authority boundaries
**Ratified:** No
**Predecessor:** A5 — Philosophical Jurisdiction v0.2, formerly located at `axioms/A5_Philosophical_Jurisdiction.md` and preserved through version-control and evolution lineage

## Normative statement

> **SymK has constitutive jurisdiction over the universal epistemic and Knowledge Engineering requirements necessary to create Domain Intelligence Amplifiers. It adopts only the minimal commitments concerning Intelligence, Amplified Intelligence, cooperation, representation, responsibility, Education, Training, and evolution required for that discipline. Derived projects govern their domain purpose, Ontology, Logic, substantive reasoning, professional standards, and answer thresholds. Organizations and deployments govern local authorization and operation. Legitimate external authorities retain authority within their jurisdictions. No layer may silently convert its local model, authority, or implementation into universal SymK truth.**

## Constitutional purpose

J1 prevents conceptual imperialism, jurisdictional leakage, accidental metaphysics, and the disappearance of responsibility between layers. It identifies the questions SymK must answer for disciplinary coherence, the questions derived projects must answer for their domains, and the boundaries beyond which no SymK layer may pretend comprehensive authority.

J1 is not an axiom because its function is to delimit and route authority rather than state an independent substantive truth about every possible SymK system. It governs the interpretation and application of every axiom and downstream artifact.

## Scope and jurisdiction

### SymK jurisdiction

SymK governs universal commitments necessary for Domain Intelligence Amplifiers concerning:

- Knowledge and epistemic status;
- Intelligence and capability attribution;
- Amplified Intelligence and cooperative organization;
- cooperation, challenge, memory, and feedback;
- representation, projection, provenance, context, and semantic lineage;
- responsibility distinctions necessary to prevent accountability from disappearing;
- Education, Training, habit formation, and continuing challenge capacity where persistent systems shape participants;
- and governance of evolution, inheritance, and consequence return.

SymK does not claim complete Epistemology, philosophy of mind, ethics, pedagogy, Ontology, Logic, law, economics, or social policy.

### Derived-project jurisdiction

Derived projects govern:

- domain purpose and boundaries;
- domain Ontology and Logic;
- substantive domain reasoning;
- professional standards and answer thresholds;
- domain concepts and methods;
- project axioms and durable obligations;
- product and subproject specialization;
- and project-specific consequence and compatibility decisions.

### Organization and deployment jurisdiction

Organizations and deployments govern local authorization, policy, staffing, investment, risk acceptance, configuration, access, adoption, suspension, and operation under applicable external authority.

### External authority

Law, regulation, professional institutions, ethics, science, technical standards, and other legitimate fields retain authority within their jurisdictions. SymK traces and respects their constraints without claiming to own them or treating authority as epistemic truth.

## Governed definitions

- **Jurisdiction:** responsibility and authority to govern a class of questions within a declared scope; not exhaustive ownership of a field.
- **Constitutive jurisdiction:** the minimum authority SymK must exercise for its identity and discipline to remain coherent.
- **Derived project:** a governed domain specialization that inherits applicable SymK commitments and adds domain authority.
- **External authority:** a legitimate source of constraints whose authority arises outside SymK.
- **Ontology:** an explicit account of entities, relations, categories, and commitments recognized within a domain.
- **Logic:** governed forms and rules of inference, constraint, and consequence under stated assumptions.
- **Projection:** a purpose-dependent representation that preserves selected meaning while necessarily excluding other dimensions.

## Derivation from the foundation papers

The Knowledge paper places Epistemology at SymK's center without making SymK a complete epistemology. The Intelligence paper supplies the minimum distinctions necessary to identify participants and attribute capabilities without using the Human as universal template. Properties and Projections prevents models and schemas from becoming reality or Ontology by accident. Knowledge Engineering restricts engineering claims to governed conditions rather than truth itself. Derived-project analysis shows that domain entities, inference rules, professional standards, and domain ends belong to narrower authorities under inherited epistemic constraints.

## Foundational commitments absorbed

- SymK does not attempt a complete philosophy of Knowledge or Intelligence.
- Intelligence must be non-anthropocentric but not difference-blind.
- Models and schemas are purpose-dependent projections.
- Derived projects declare their ontological, logical, professional, and purpose commitments.
- Authority, authorization, epistemic warrant, and truth remain distinct.
- Constraint travels downward within jurisdiction; evidence and challenge may travel upward.
- Scope is a discipline of explicit constraint, not intellectual isolation.

## Relationship with candidate axioms

J1 bounds every candidate axiom without being one of them. It locates epistemic commitments within SymK, constrains claims made about Intelligence and cooperation, routes domain commitments to derived projects, and prevents inheritance or implementation from expanding an artifact beyond its jurisdiction. No axiom may override J1 by asserting universality outside SymK's constitutive scope.

## Authoritative interpretation

“Constitutive” is stronger than mere relevance and weaker than exhaustive coverage. “Minimal” means only what SymK needs for coherent identity, governance, and engineering. Ontology and Logic are required project disciplines, not merely documents or technologies. Jurisdiction follows the level at which a commitment is universal, domain-specific, organizational, operational, or externally governed.

J1 constrains the scope and authority of propositions; it does not decide their epistemic truth. A narrower authority may specialize or add within its jurisdiction, but cannot silently redefine a parent commitment. Evidence from any layer may challenge the adequacy of the governing boundary.

## Derived principles

- Place universal epistemic and Knowledge Engineering constraints in SymK.
- Place domain entities, reasoning commitments, standards, and ends in derived projects.
- Declare assumptions, exclusions, imported authority, and projection purposes.
- Give every governed artifact one primary owner accountable for status, scope, lineage, and challenge.
- Route conflicts to the nearest adequate owner and escalate evidence when a parent commitment is challenged.
- Keep material unresolved incompatibility visible.

## Engineering consequences

Architectures must trace concepts, schemas, rules, policies, and implementations to their governing jurisdiction. Reviews must detect domain leakage into SymK, universal assumptions hidden in product code, and external constraints misrepresented as epistemic truth. Material changes of Ontology, Logic, authority, projection, or scope require governed versioning and lineage.

## Derived-project inheritance

LexBrain governs legal-domain Ontology, Logic, professional standards, and answer thresholds without redefining SymK Knowledge or Intelligence. IaaC and sshConnectivity govern their own domain entities, trust relations, operational reasoning, and standards under the same boundary. Every future project must declare its domain, conceptual imports, external authorities, local additions, inherited constraints, and known incompatibilities.

## Prohibited interpretations

- SymK claims the whole of Epistemology or a complete philosophy of Intelligence.
- Derived projects are conceptually unconstrained because they are engineering products.
- A database schema, ontology language, formal logic, model, or implementation automatically determines domain truth.
- Constitutional, legal, professional, or organizational authority creates epistemic warrant by itself.
- Scope boundaries prohibit interdisciplinary evidence or correction.
- A local success justifies silent promotion into universal SymK.
- J1 can be used to evade a problem necessary to apply an inherited commitment coherently.

## Counterexamples and compatibility tests

- A LexBrain database field silently defines what a legal norm is: incompatible.
- A project documents its domain entities, inference assumptions, professional standards, and inherited epistemic limits: compatible in principle.
- A product-specific category is promoted into SymK because it is locally useful: incompatible absent universal justification.
- Evidence from psychology revises a minimal Intelligence commitment through governance: compatible.
- A law prohibits an otherwise SymK-compatible deployment: use remains prohibited within that jurisdiction; neither legal authority nor SymK compatibility establishes truth.
- Implementation evidence exposes a defect in a SymK concept and reaches its owner through governed challenge: compatible and required.

## Open questions and preserved dissent

- The threshold at which a neighboring philosophical or engineering question becomes constitutionally necessary requires continued testing.
- Dissent remains over whether Ontology and Logic must be named constitutionally or only in the derived-project framework.
- The exact procedural force of jurisdiction articles relative to Ratified axioms belongs to SymK 2.3.
- Operational evidence may require later adjustment of the boundaries stated here.
