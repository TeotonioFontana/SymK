# Constitutional Sources and Foundation Papers

**Status:** Proposed source map  
**Normative force:** None by source status alone

## 1. Purpose

SymK axioms should not appear as unsupported declarations. Their documents must preserve the governed reasoning through which particular commitments were selected.

Foundation papers serve as **constitutional sources**. They contain:

- literature and conceptual history;
- alternative positions;
- working definitions;
- evidence and argument;
- candidate commitments;
- engineering consequences;
- open questions;
- and limitations.

Publication as a foundation paper does not ratify its conclusions. Ratification occurs only when a specific commitment is selected through constitutional governance.

## 2. Source-to-norm relationship

```text
Foundation Paper
    full inquiry, alternatives, evidence, open questions
        ↓ governed constitutional selection
Axiom Exposition
    adopted reasoning and authoritative interpretation
        ↓ normative compression
Axiom Statement
    concise binding commitment
        ↓ derivation
Principles, project constitutions, charters, and standards
```

## 4. Required source metadata

Before an axiom becomes Candidate, each cited source should have:

- stable title and version;
- governed location;
- status and owner;
- relevant section identifiers;
- exact commitments selected;
- alternatives rejected or deferred;
- open questions preserved;
- and supersession history.

Conversation-derived conclusions should be converted into governed notes or papers before ratification depends upon them.

## 5. Intelligence as a constitutional source

The Intelligence paper is not peripheral. A0 cannot identify participants, and A2 cannot reject type privilege, without a governed Intelligence concept.

The paper contributes:

- Intelligence is a substrate-realized capacity of an organized system;
- Human Intelligence is one manifestation, not the universal template;
- origin confers neither capability nor privilege;
- performance is evidence of Intelligence, not its identity;
- Intelligence is scoped, graded, profiled, and challengeably attributed;
- individual, collective, social, and hybrid bearers require explicit organization and boundaries;
- Intelligence does not entail authority, wisdom, consciousness, responsibility, or moral status;
- and Knowledge and Intelligence are distinct but reciprocal.

Its working definition should mature through `FC-005 Intelligence`. Its selected constitutional conclusions should be incorporated into A0, A1, A2, A3, A4, and A5 expositions.

## 6. Knowledge as a constitutional source

The Knowledge paper supplies the central distinctions for A1 and A4:

- Knowledge is an epistemic achievement;
- Knowledge, claims, assets, and representations are distinct;
- knowing-that, knowing-how, and acquaintance are plural modes;
- truth is not created by governance;
- grounds, competence, context, and scope matter;
- consensus is not Knowledge;
- and challenge and revision belong to responsible reliance.

Its working definition should mature through `FC-006 Knowledge` rather than being copied into every axiom.

## 7. Source-governance questions

- Which authority approves a document as a constitutional source?
- Can a rejected theory remain cited as an interpretive warning?
- When a source paper changes, does every dependent axiom require review?
- Which source passages are interpretively authoritative after ratification?
- How should minority or dissenting reasoning be preserved?
