# Constitutional Sources and Decision Lineage

**Status:** Proposed governed source map
**Version:** 0.3
**Date:** 18 August 2026
**Normative force:** None by source designation alone

## 1. Purpose

SymK constitutional commitments must not appear as unsupported declarations. Their artifacts must preserve the governed path through which evidence, argument, alternatives, dissent, and prior decisions support a selected commitment.

This map distinguishes:

- epistemic sources;
- canonical orientation;
- evolution decisions;
- constitutional selection;
- constitutional norms;
- derived artifacts;
- and external authority.

Calling a document “foundational,” “canonical,” “accepted,” or “authoritative” does not make every proposition in it constitutionally binding.

## 2. Source and authority classes

| Class | Function | Authority supplied | Does not acquire automatically |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation Paper or research source | Preserves inquiry, evidence, alternatives, candidate conclusions, and dissent | Epistemic and interpretive support according to quality and scope | Constitutional or semantic normative force |
| Product Vision | States canonical identity, purpose, outcome, boundaries, and desired direction | Program orientation within its declared status | Ratification of axioms, concepts, standards, or implementations |
| Evolution decision record | Records a reviewed choice, its scope, status, dissent, and migration consequences | Program and migration authority explicitly granted by its status | Constitutional Ratification or truth |
| Constitutional-source map | Traces sources and selections | Governed provenance | Authority to select or Ratify its own targets |
| Proposed axiom or jurisdiction article | Presents a candidate constitutional commitment and exposition | Deliberative authority only | Binding compatibility force |
| Ratified axiom or constitutional article | States an adopted universal commitment within jurisdiction | Constitutional normative force | Epistemic infallibility or authority outside jurisdiction |
| Governed concept, principle, or standard | Develops meaning or obligations under declared status | Semantic, methodological, or engineering authority within scope | Constitutional force unless expressly selected and Ratified |
| External source or authority | Supplies evidence or legitimate constraints from outside SymK | Epistemic, legal, professional, scientific, ethical, or technical authority within its jurisdiction | Ownership by SymK or universal semantic precedence |

## 3. Governed selection path

```text
Epistemic sources and inquiry (S0)
    evidence, argument, alternatives, dissent
        ↓ governed review and explicit selection
Product Vision and evolution decisions
    orientation and recorded program choices
        ↓ constitutional proposal with exact lineage
Proposed axiom or jurisdiction article (S1)
    normative statement + exposition + scope + dissent
        ↓ explicit constitutional Ratification
Ratified constitutional commitment (S1)
        ↓ governed derivation and specialization
Concepts, principles, projects, charters, standards, and realizations
        ↑ operational evidence and challenge
```

Every arrow represents a governed relationship. No document promotes itself merely by publication, directory placement, citation, repetition, prestige, or technical implementation.

## 8. Required source metadata

Before a constitutional artifact becomes Candidate, every source necessary to its reasoning must identify:

- stable title and version;
- governed path or external citation;
- artifact class, status, and owner;
- relevant section identifiers;
- the exact proposition or distinction used;
- scope and known limitations;
- alternatives rejected or deferred;
- dissent and open questions preserved;
- the selection decision and selecting authority;
- dependent artifacts;
- and supersession or migration history.

Conversation-derived conclusions must be transformed into a governed note, paper, evidence entry, or decision record before Ratification depends upon them.

## 9. Source-selection rules

1. **Citation is not selection.** A cited paper may support, oppose, qualify, or contextualize a constitutional proposal.
2. **Selection is proposition-specific.** Constitutional governance selects an exact commitment, not an entire paper by association.
3. **Status remains visible.** A canonical working paper remains non-normative unless a separate artifact receives normative status.
4. **Dissent travels with selection.** Material rejected alternatives and uncertainty must remain traceable.
5. **Source change triggers impact review.** A new paper version does not silently rewrite a dependent constitutional artifact.
6. **External authority remains external.** SymK may import its constraint without claiming ownership or confusing authority with truth.
7. **Operational evidence can reopen selection.** Ratification does not make a commitment immune to observed consequence.

## 10. Foundation-specific contributions

### 10.1 Intelligence

The Intelligence paper contributes the minimum distinctions needed to identify participants and allocate roles without type privilege:

- Intelligence is a substrate-realized capacity of an organized system;
- Human Intelligence is one manifestation, not the universal template;
- origin confers neither capability nor privilege;
- performance is evidence of Intelligence, not its identity;
- Intelligence attribution is scoped, graded, profiled, evidence-bearing, and revisable;
- individual, collective, social, and hybrid bearers require explicit organization and boundaries;
- Intelligence does not entail authority, wisdom, consciousness, responsibility, or moral status;
- and Knowledge and Intelligence are distinct but reciprocal.

These conclusions require maturation through the future `FC-005 Intelligence`; citation here does not finalize that concept.

### 10.2 Knowledge

The Knowledge paper contributes the distinctions needed to prevent representation or governance from manufacturing warrant:

- Knowledge is an epistemic achievement;
- Knowledge, claims, assets, and representations are distinct;
- knowing-that, knowing-how, and acquaintance are plural modes;
- truth is not created by governance;
- grounds, competence, context, and scope matter;
- consensus is not Knowledge;
- and responsible reliance remains open to challenge and revision.

These conclusions require maturation through the future `FC-006 Knowledge`; citation here does not finalize that concept.

### 10.3 Education and Training

The Education paper contributes the distinction between developmental formation and repeated capability or habit modification, while keeping them interdependent. It supports constitutional attention to persistent systems that shape participants, but does not settle a complete philosophy of Education, social policy, or pedagogy.

### 10.4 Knowledge Engineering

The Knowledge Engineering paper limits SymK's direct engineering object to accountable epistemic conditions, representations, roles, processes, validation arrangements, and environments. It denies that an engineered artifact becomes Knowledge or truth by construction, formalization, storage, inference, approval, or naming.

## 11. Source-governance questions preserved

- Which authority and procedure approve a new artifact as a constitutional source?
- Can a rejected theory remain cited as an interpretive warning?
- Which source changes require review of every dependent constitutional artifact?
- Which selected source passages become interpretively authoritative after Ratification?
- How should minority or dissenting reasoning be preserved and weighted?
- How will external sources receive stable citations and jurisdiction metadata?
- Should the missing *Properties and Projections* inquiry be recovered, reconstructed, or retired as a separate source claim?

These questions belong primarily to SymK 2.3 and later source-governance maturation.
