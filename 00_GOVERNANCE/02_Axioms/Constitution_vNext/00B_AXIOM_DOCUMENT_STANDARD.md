# SymK Axiom Document Standard

**Status:** Proposed scaffold standard  
**Applies to:** Candidate and ratified SymK constitutional axiom documents

## 1. Purpose

An axiom is not only a sentence. Its statement must remain concise, but its meaning, derivation, boundaries, consequences, and tests must be governed so that future implementations cannot preserve the wording while silently changing the commitment.

## 2. Required anatomy

Every axiom document shall have an identifier, title, and governance metadata followed by:

1. **Normative statement** — the compressed binding commitment
2. **Constitutional purpose** — the harm or incoherence the axiom prevents
3. **Scope and jurisdiction** — where it applies and where it does not
4. **Governed definitions** — authoritative references for key terms
5. **Constitutional sources** — governed provenance and exact source versions
6. **Derivation from the foundation papers** — the reasoning by which conclusions were selected
7. **Foundational commitments absorbed** — prior principles compressed into the axiom
8. **Relationship with other axioms** — dependencies, tensions, and non-duplication
9. **Authoritative interpretation** — how the statement shall be read
10. **Derived principles**
11. **Engineering consequences**
12. **Derived-project inheritance**
13. **Prohibited interpretations**
14. **Counterexamples and compatibility tests**
15. **Open questions and preserved dissent** — including unresolved exception and challenge conditions
16. **Version and ratification history** — including amendment and supersession lineage when applicable

## 3. Normative force by section

| Section | Force after ratification |
|---|---|
| Normative statement | Constitutionally binding |
| Scope and governed definitions | Constitutionally binding for application |
| Authoritative interpretation | Binding interpretive guidance |
| Prohibited interpretations | Binding exclusion |
| Derived principles | Binding only after ratified at their proper level; otherwise proposed consequences |
| Engineering consequences | Constraints requiring later specification; not self-executing implementation rules |
| Sources and derivation | Governed provenance and interpretive evidence; source paper is not incorporated wholesale |
| Counterexamples and tests | Compatibility evidence; may evolve without changing the statement if meaning is preserved |
| Open questions and dissent | Non-binding but constitutionally preserved |

## 4. Source-selection rule

The derivation section must identify:

- source title and stable version;
- exact sections or commitments selected;
- how each selected conclusion contributes;
- alternatives rejected or deferred;
- known limitations;
- and whether a later source revision requires constitutional review.

“Based on the Intelligence paper” is insufficient for a Candidate or Ratified axiom.

## 5. Compression rule

The axiom statement should express one irreducible constitutional commitment. Explanatory completeness belongs in the exposition. If the statement attempts to contain every consequence, it is probably combining several axioms or confusing an axiom with a standard.

## 6. Semantic-change rule

A change to examples, tests, or exposition does not necessarily amend the axiom. It becomes a constitutional amendment when it changes:

- the normative statement;
- scope or jurisdiction;
- governed meaning of a key term;
- authoritative interpretation;
- prohibited interpretations;
- or compatibility outcomes for a material class of systems.

## 7. Citation and lineage rule

Every dependent principle, project axiom, Engineering Charter obligation, and standard should cite the axiom identifier and version. Axiom documents should link back to the constitutional sources and forward to the governed dependency registry.

## 8. Current baseline status

The A0–A6 version 0.2 files implement this anatomy as a questioning baseline. Their completeness of structure does not imply philosophical completeness or ratification. Stable section-level source citations, final governed concept references, ratifying authority, effective dates, and decisions on preserved open questions remain maturation requirements.

They remain **Proposed**, not Candidate or Ratified.
