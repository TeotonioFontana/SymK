## 1. Correction result

Package A v0.2 corrected uncontrolled start, but its three-gate description could
still be applied too linearly. It required comparative rationale at Method Selection
while leaving insufficiently explicit how competing methods obtain empirical
evidence *before* one becomes the primary delivery method. A documentary comparison
could therefore rationalize a familiar or already-developed method without testing
a credible AI–Human, deterministic, standards-led or hybrid alternative.

Version 0.3 preserves three independent decision types and adds two execution lanes:

1. **Objective Acceptance** — accepts the outcome-facing objective as a valid basis
   for method inquiry;
2. **Exploration lane** — a bounded **Trial Release** authorizes reversible,
   decision-informing comparison without selecting a primary delivery method;
3. **Method Selection** — selects, combines, limits or rejects candidate methods
   using proportionate comparative evidence; and
4. **Delivery lane** — a separate **Delivery Release** authorizes implementation or
   operation of the selected method inside an explicit execution envelope.

Objective Acceptance, Method Selection and Execution Release remain distinct
decision types. A Trial Release is an Execution Release with exploratory purpose;
a Delivery Release is an Execution Release for selected-method delivery. A Trial
Release does not imply Method Selection, and neither Method Selection nor trial
success implies a Delivery Release.

No approval of a plan, roadmap, proposal, objective, candidate, experiment or method
authorizes later execution. No amount of prior implementation effort establishes
comparative method fitness.

## 2. Decision requested

Accept, correct or reject a project-method governance profile that:

- requires an externally meaningful and assessable outcome-facing objective;
- governs every material candidate and selected method as a separate, challengeable
  method hypothesis;
- requires a credible alternative set and proportionate comparative validation
  before primary-method commitment, subject to explicit exception control;
- permits bounded pre-selection trials only after Objective Acceptance and a Trial
  Release;
- prohibits delivery execution before independent Objective Acceptance, Method
  Selection and Delivery Release decisions pass;
- scales the execution-release envelope and evidence burden to consequence,
  sensitivity, reversibility and external effect;
- permits useful autonomy inside an accepted envelope rather than requiring
  approval for every step; and
- stops or escalates only when authority is absent, expired, materially ambiguous or
  exceeded, or when a declared material stop condition occurs.

Acceptance would make this a provisional SymK project-method governance profile and
authorize later operational template work and competent 2.4 classification. It
would not select or trial a real method, open a closed evolution stage, create
constitutional force or accept GMC-003–014.

## 3. Problem addressed

A technically controlled project may still be strategically uncontrolled when it:

- starts from an objective expressed mainly as its own stages or deliverables;
- assumes a method without comparing relevant alternatives;
- treats technical feasibility, repeatability or already-incurred effort as proof
  that the method is the best route to the external outcome;
- evaluates alternatives only on paper when a bounded empirical comparison is
  feasible and decision-relevant;
- treats plan approval or a conversational continuation instruction as authority for
  every downstream action;
- accumulates implementation and assurance evidence while outcome-relevant learning
  remains weak;
- advances from proposal to construction, external access or operation without a
  separately bounded release; or
- continues after the objective, method, risk or permitted-effect boundary changes
  materially.

A project may therefore be operationally controlled yet spend substantial time on
an inadequate method. Correct execution of the wrong method is not method fitness.

Conversely, preventing every action until a new micro-approval is issued creates
delay, ceremonial review and false safety. Package A must therefore prevent
unauthorized start while preserving bounded autonomy for reversible, low-consequence
work.

## 4. Compatibility boundary

This corrected proposal operationalizes, without reopening, accepted distinctions
among:

- target, objective, expectation, intended outcome and actual outcome;
- recommendation, proposal, plan, instruction, decision and Authorization;
- assessment, method selection, execution, action and consequence;
- Authority capacity, an actual authorization occurrence and implementation fact;
- output, quality, cost, Value, feedback and Learning; and
- project methods versus universal SymK concepts.

It proposes no new Foundational concept. Its objective, method-fitness and
execution-release structures are project-governance profiles subject to later
classification.

## 6. Outcome-facing objective rule

A material plan must answer:

> **What externally meaningful condition, capability or decision quality should be
> different for identified beneficiaries or affected subjects if this work
> succeeds?**

“External” means outside the internal anatomy of the plan. It may refer to a Domain
practice, user capability, sponsor decision, governed knowledge state,
organizational resilience or another declared beneficiary-facing boundary. It does
not require a commercial customer, immediate public effect or a single numerical
measure.

Legitimate objectives may concern long-horizon outcomes, research, infrastructure,
governance or harm prevention when their horizon, contribution claim, evidence,
uncertainty and affected boundary are explicit.

An objective fails when it can only be explained by restating internal stages,
document names, implementation components or compliance activities and cannot say
why their completion matters outside that structure.

## 7. Minimum objective envelope

A consequence-proportional objective record identifies:

1. objective Identity and version;
2. proposing subject and acceptance authority;
3. beneficiaries and materially affected subjects;
4. Domain, Scope and Context;
5. intended external condition, capability or decision-quality change;
6. baseline, reference condition or acknowledged absence;
7. intended and intermediate horizons;
8. observable outcome criteria, counterevidence and important unknowns;
9. indicators or proxies and their validity limits;
10. purpose and Value claim, separately governed;
11. constraints and non-compensable conditions;
12. assumptions, dependencies, uncertainty and alternative interpretations;
13. internal deliverables and their claimed contribution without substitution;
14. challenge, revision, expiry and reopening paths; and
15. the explicit statement that Objective Acceptance creates no Method Selection or
    Execution Release.

## 8. Black-box objective test

Before Objective Acceptance, a reviewer evaluates the plan while treating its
implementation as a black box:

| Question | Passing evidence | Failure exposed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is different outside the plan? | named condition, capability or decision-quality change | internal completion used as objective |
| For whom or for what affected boundary? | beneficiaries and affected subjects | provider- or artifact-only framing |
| Why does the difference matter? | scoped purpose and Value claim | deliverable assumed valuable |
| When could it be assessed? | horizon and observation conditions | timeless aspiration |
| What counts against success? | failure, uncertainty and counterevidence conditions | unfalsifiable objective |
| Can it be explained without internal component names? | sponsor-readable statement | recursive technical justification |

Passing this test does not establish that the objective is true, valuable,
authorized for all affected subjects or achievable. Detailed Engineering criteria
remain necessary at their own boundary.

## 9. Objective Acceptance gate

Objective Acceptance passes only when competent authority records:

- the exact objective version accepted;
- the declared beneficiaries and affected horizon;
- the evidence and uncertainty supporting its adequacy;
- material dissent, exclusions and unresolved questions;
- the applicable observation and challenge basis;
- acceptance conditions, expiry and reopening triggers; and
- explicit zero authority for method selection and execution.

Failure or deferral returns the plan for reframing or records a qualified no-objective
result. It does not authorize candidate generation, method trials or delivery beyond
separately applicable authority. After Objective Acceptance, bounded candidate
inquiry may proceed only under an applicable standing authority or Trial Release.

## 10. Method-hypothesis rule

Every material method selection states:

> **For accepted objective version O, in Context C, method version M is expected to
> contribute through rationale R, relative to alternatives A, under assumptions S,
> with evidence E, costs and risks K, and review conditions T.**

The hypothesis may be uncertain. Sunk work, method availability, familiarity,
technical elegance, compliance, repeatability or prior success may be evidence but
cannot substitute for objective-relative or comparative fitness. Engineering
correctness demonstrates that a method works as specified; it does not demonstrate
that the method is the best available route to the objective.

A material primary-method hypothesis must be compared with credible alternatives
before selection unless a documented exception passes Section 13C. A method already
under construction receives no evidential preference merely because abandoning or
changing it would expose prior cost.

A mandated method record distinguishes the mandate, issuing authority,
non-discretionary elements, purpose of the mandate, remaining choices and escalation
route when fitness evidence is adverse. Package A does not authorize deviation from
a competent mandate.

## 11. Minimum method-selection record

Method Selection preserves:

1. method Identity, version and owner;
2. exact accepted objective Identity and version;
3. proposing, assessing and selecting authorities;
4. intended contribution and causal or practical rationale;
5. Domain, Scope, Context, horizon and affected subjects;
6. prerequisites, assumptions and dependencies;
7. the credible candidate set, including continuation, combination, controlled
   experiment and qualified no-method where material;
8. candidate-admission and exclusion reasons, including why each retained candidate
   is materially plausible rather than ceremonial;
9. a common comparison frame and proportionate validation design;
10. evidence and counterevidence for each candidate's comparative fitness;
11. applicable fitness dimensions and non-compensable conditions;
12. direct, indirect, transition, verification, opportunity and residual costs;
13. sensitivity, reversibility, external effects and failure consequences;
14. uncertainty, dissent and missing evidence;
15. observation, stop-loss, reassessment and pivot triggers;
16. decision conditions, expiry and reopening paths;
17. any comparative-validation exception and its authority; and
18. explicit zero Delivery Release.

## 12. Method-fitness profile

Method fitness is plural and objective-relative. Candidate dimensions include:

- relevance to the intended outcome;
- Domain and stakeholder adequacy;
- epistemic yield and uncertainty reduction;
- feasibility and available capability;
- time to useful or reliance-ready result;
- direct and total cost;
- safety, privacy, legality and ethical compatibility;
- reversibility, recoverability and correction cost;
- interoperability, dependency and lock-in;
- evidence quality and observability;
- adaptability and Learning capacity;
- burden distribution and affected-subject consequences; and
- compatibility with mandatory constraints.

For semantic-discovery or knowledge-structure work, comparison should consider, when
material, Domain usefulness, coverage, novelty, redundancy, expert acceptance,
explainability, discovery latency, correction burden, adaptability and total cost.
These are candidate dimensions, not universal measures or fixed thresholds.

No dimension is universally mandatory or sufficient. No aggregate score may erase a
material failed, disputed, unknown or non-compensable condition. Measures,
thresholds and weights remain within competent jurisdiction.

## 13. Method Selection gate

Method Selection passes only when competent authority records:

- the exact accepted objective and selected method versions;
- the credible candidates admitted and alternatives actually considered;
- the comparative-validation design, results, limitations and any justified
  exception;
- the fitness profile, evidence, counterevidence, uncertainty and dissent;
- mandated and discretionary components;
- review triggers and expiry;
- the intended form of later Execution Release; and
- explicit zero execution authority at selection time.

Selection may authorize preparation of a separate execution-release proposal only
when that non-executing preparation is itself within existing authority.

### 13A. Credible-candidate rule

Before material primary-method selection, the decision record must admit at least
two credible candidates or document why only one is genuinely available. Candidate
families may include deterministic, AI-led, Human-led, standards-led, hybrid,
continuation, replacement or qualified no-method approaches when relevant to the
objective and Domain.

This rule does not require false symmetry. A candidate may be excluded when it is
legally unavailable, violates a non-compensable condition, lacks a necessary
capability, is clearly disproportionate or cannot plausibly contribute. Each
material exclusion requires an attributable reason and enough evidence to challenge
it. Naming a deliberately weak alternative does not satisfy the rule.

Prior success in a materially similar Context may reduce the comparison burden, but
does not erase changed Domain, data, affected-subject, cost or capability conditions.

### 13B. Comparative-validation rule

When a bounded empirical comparison is feasible, proportionate and capable of
changing the decision, Method Selection requires it. The comparison preserves:

1. accepted objective and decision question;
2. candidate identities, versions, owners and material configurations;
3. a common or explicitly normalized task, sample, corpus or operating Context;
4. Domain representativeness and known sampling limits;
5. outcome-facing comparison dimensions and non-compensable conditions;
6. baseline, expected differences and decision-relevant thresholds where competent;
7. evidence collection, provenance and reproducibility conditions;
8. Human or affected-subject assessment where semantic or experiential adequacy is
   material;
9. direct, transition, verification, opportunity and projected scaling costs;
10. time to learning and time to useful delivery;
11. uncertainty, counterevidence, failure modes and confounders;
12. permitted adaptations and rules against selectively optimizing one candidate;
13. result, dissent and unresolved questions; and
14. the decision consequence the comparison may support, without automatic
    selection.

The cheapest valid test is preferred over premature full construction. A trial
should be large enough to discriminate the material hypotheses and no larger than
needed for that decision. Trial success is evidence, not proof of general fitness,
production readiness, authority or Value.

### 13C. Comparative-validation exception rule

Method Selection may proceed without a new empirical comparison only when competent
authority records one or more of the following and the resulting uncertainty:

- an external mandate leaves no lawful method choice;
- existing evidence is sufficiently applicable, current and discriminating;
- a trial would create disproportionate cost, delay, harm or exposure;
- no second credible candidate survives the admission test; or
- urgent conditions require a provisional, expiring selection before comparison.

The exception records the rejected test, reason, evidence, dissent, compensating
observation, expiry and earliest reassessment condition. “We already started,”
“we invested heavily,” “the implementation is nearly complete,” or “the method is
technically sophisticated” are not valid exceptions.

An urgent or mandated selection does not convert unknown comparative fitness into a
favorable assessment. It preserves the mandate or urgency as the selection basis.

### 13D. Trial Release and exploration lane

A comparative trial is execution and therefore requires an applicable Trial Release.
The release identifies the candidates, common test boundary, permitted data and
tools, evaluation roles, budget, duration, external effects, evidence requirements,
stop conditions and prohibited production use.

The exploration lane may begin after Objective Acceptance without Method Selection
because its purpose is to inform that selection. It may not:

- designate a trial candidate as the primary delivery method;
- expand a prototype into production, sustained migration or operational rollout;
- consume the delivery budget by default;
- use trial completion as implied selection; or
- bypass a new Delivery Release after selection.

Candidate-specific configuration work may occur only to the comparable level needed
for the decision. Material asymmetry in preparation, access, tuning, reviewer effort
or evidence quality must be disclosed.

### 13E. Continuing fitness, stop-loss and pivot rule

Method Selection remains a revisable decision, not a permanent property of the
method. It must define proportionate triggers for reassessment, such as:

- outcome-relevant learning remains below the declared expectation while cost,
  implementation or control work grows;
- a rejected candidate gains materially stronger evidence or capability;
- Domain reviewers reject the usefulness or adequacy of produced results;
- observed total cost, time, correction burden or scaling behavior exceeds the
  accepted range;
- assumptions, data, constraints, affected subjects or operating Context change;
- a non-compensable condition fails; or
- evidence shows that a hybrid, replacement or no-method option may dominate.

At a trigger, competent authority records continue, constrain, retrial, combine,
pivot, pause or terminate. The trigger does not automatically dictate which response
is correct. Prior spending, artifact volume, test coverage, implementation maturity
and schedule pressure may describe transition consequences but cannot establish
continued fitness.

## 14. Why independent execution release remains mandatory

Objective Acceptance answers **what result is worth pursuing**. Comparative
validation asks **which candidates perform better under decision-relevant
conditions**. Method Selection answers **which route is presently preferred and
why**. None answers:

- who may act;
- which tool may operate;
- which data or system may be accessed;
- which state changes are permitted;
- when work may begin or must end;
- how much time, money, volume or external interaction may be consumed; or
- what event must stop or escalate execution.

Those questions belong to an independent Execution Release. Before selection, a
Trial Release answers them for exploration only. After selection, a Delivery Release
answers them for implementation or operation. Without this distinction, a trial may
silently become the delivery architecture or a valid selection may still begin
uncontrolled execution.

## 15. Execution-release rule

> **No material execution may begin solely from proposal preparation, plan approval,
> Objective Acceptance, candidate admission, trial design, Method Selection,
> conversational continuation, artifact placement, implementation readiness or
> prior related authority. Execution begins only under a current, attributable and
> sufficiently bounded Execution Release whose purpose covers that execution.**

Exploration execution requires Objective Acceptance plus a Trial Release. Delivery
execution requires Objective Acceptance, Method Selection and a Delivery Release.
Authority for one lane does not transfer to the other.

A continuation phrase such as “go ahead” is interpreted against the exact current
checkpoint and immediately proposed next action. It does not silently authorize
later gates, external effects, sensitive access, irreversible change or indefinite
autonomy.

When the intended scope is materially ambiguous, the executor must narrow to the
clearly authorized reversible boundary or request clarification. Ambiguity does not
justify either unlimited action or unnecessary paralysis.

## 16. Minimum execution envelope

A material Execution Release identifies, proportionately:

1. release Identity, version, issuer and authority source;
2. exact objective and applicable candidate or selected-method versions;
3. release purpose and type: trial or delivery;
4. authorized actors, agents, tools and delegation limits;
5. permitted work and explicitly excluded work;
6. environments, systems and resources in scope;
7. permitted data classes and prohibited data/content;
8. allowed operations, state changes and external effects;
9. forbidden operations and effects;
10. sensitivity, reversibility, affected horizon and consequence classification;
11. time, cost, volume, request, access or other material budgets;
12. start condition, effective time, expiry and completion condition;
13. required validation, monitoring and evidence;
14. material stop, reassessment and escalation conditions;
15. rollback, recovery, containment or safe-stop capability where applicable;
16. expected outputs and their status limitations;
17. reporting, accountability and evidence owner; and
18. change-control rule for non-material adjustments and material envelope changes.

The envelope may refer to governed standing policies or profiles rather than repeat
them verbatim. Referenced authority must be current and applicable.

## 17. Proportional execution-release forms

Trial and Delivery Releases state purpose. Independently, Package A proposes three
consequence-proportional forms without fixing a universal risk scale:

### 17.1 Standing or sandbox release

Suitable for low-consequence, reversible work within a controlled environment, such
as read-only inspection, synthetic analysis or isolated drafting. It may authorize a
broad class of actions for a bounded period without per-step approval.

### 17.2 Task release

Suitable for reversible or moderately consequential work with a defined deliverable,
workspace, data boundary, budget and completion condition. It authorizes normal
implementation choices inside the envelope.

### 17.3 Exact or high-consequence release

Suitable when work involves sensitive data, external systems, publication,
production, irreversible effects, significant affected-subject risk or expensive
recovery. It uses narrower identities, budgets, monitoring and stop conditions.

The purpose and form compose: for example, a sandbox Trial Release or an exact
Delivery Release. The form follows competent assurance assessment. Labels do not
establish actual risk. Package C remains responsible for fuller
proportional-assurance development.

## 18. Bounded autonomy rule

An accepted Execution Release should authorize enough discretion to complete the
declared task efficiently. The executor need not request approval for every ordinary
choice when the choice:

- remains inside the accepted objective, applicable candidates or selected method,
  and execution envelope;
- creates no materially different effect, exposure or affected horizon;
- stays within budgets and stop conditions;
- preserves required evidence and reversibility; and
- does not cross a separately governed decision or authority boundary.

Repeated micro-approval is not the default assurance mechanism. Material expansion,
not routine execution detail, requires renewed authority.

## 19. Material change and stop rule

Execution must stop safely or escalate when:

- no applicable Execution Release can be identified;
- the release is expired, consumed, revoked or superseded;
- objective or method identity no longer matches the authorized versions;
- trial activity begins to create delivery dependence or operational reliance;
- an actor, tool, data class, environment, operation or effect exceeds the envelope;
- a material budget or stop condition is reached;
- required monitoring or safe-stop capability fails;
- new evidence materially changes the risk, legality, fitness or affected horizon;
- a review trigger is reached and the release requires suspension; or
- authority becomes materially ambiguous.

Non-material implementation adjustments may proceed when the envelope's
change-control rule permits them. Every anomaly does not require shutdown.

## 20. Definition of uncontrolled execution

For Package A, execution is uncontrolled when material action:

1. begins without an applicable Execution Release;
2. relies on plan or method approval as if it were execution authority;
3. cannot be attributed to a current authority, actor/tool and envelope;
4. exceeds permitted operations, data, systems, effects, budgets or time;
5. continues after expiry, consumption, revocation or a mandatory stop condition;
6. converts trial work into delivery, or changes objective or method materially,
   without renewed decisions; or
7. cannot produce the evidence required to distinguish authorized from unauthorized
   effects.

An undesired outcome does not prove uncontrolled execution, and a desired outcome
does not prove controlled execution.

## 21. Role and authority separation

| Role | Contribution | Does not establish automatically |
| --- | --- | --- |
| objective sponsor or claimant | proposes outcome and justification | Domain adequacy, Value or authority for all affected subjects |
| Objective Acceptance authority | accepts objective version for method consideration | Method Selection or Execution Release |
| Domain authority or reviewer | assesses meaning and applicability | funding, project selection or universal truth |
| candidate proposer or assessor | supplies hypotheses, admission/exclusion grounds and fitness evidence | fair comparison, Method Selection or execution |
| trial designer or executor | performs the bounded comparison under a Trial Release | candidate neutrality, Method Selection or Delivery Release |
| Method Selection authority | selects method and conditions | Execution Release, implementation or success |
| Execution Release authority | authorizes bounded actors/actions/effects | actual execution, semantic authorship or favorable outcome |
| executor, agent or tool | performs permitted work | authority beyond the envelope or ownership of prior decisions |
| affected subject or representative | supplies experience, standing, challenge or consent where applicable | sole control of every decision absent governing authority |

One subject may occupy several roles only when each authority and occurrence remains
separately evidenced.

## 22. SPServices-like start-control stress test

The motivating scenario has two distinct control questions:

1. **Operational control:** Were live access, protected data and technical effects
   bounded? Extensive technical gates may make the answer yes.
2. **Strategic control:** Was the plan prevented from advancing a poorly justified
   primary method merely because earlier steps were approved? The v0.1 answer was no.

Version 0.2 prevented uncontrolled delivery start, but it did not make the minimum
pre-selection empirical comparison sufficiently explicit. Under v0.3, a plan framed
mainly as a sequence from deterministic scanning to property drafting would be
evaluated as follows:

| Decision point | Test result under v0.3 | Required disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Objective Acceptance | fail if success is stated mainly through internal stages rather than a defensible Domain outcome | reframe objective before material method selection |
| Candidate admission | fail if deterministic scanning is treated as the default architecture while the already-demonstrated AI–Human approach or a hybrid is excluded without evidence | admit credible deterministic, AI–Human and hybrid candidates, or record a valid exclusion |
| Trial Release | pass only for a bounded, representative comparison with prohibited production use and common evidence conditions | run the cheapest discriminating trial; do not build the full deterministic system |
| Comparative validation | fail if technical feasibility or repeatability substitutes for property usefulness, coverage, expert acceptance, adaptability, discovery speed and total cost | compare candidates on outcome-facing dimensions with competent Domain review |
| Method Selection | fail if the deterministic route is selected because work has already begun or its internal controls are mature | select, combine or reject candidates from comparative evidence and preserved uncertainty |
| Delivery Release | fail if broad plan approval, trial authority or repeated continuation instructions are the only authority for downstream construction | issue a bounded release for the selected method and exact next delivery class |
| Continuing fitness | trigger reassessment when engineering and control progress grows while useful-property learning does not | record continue, constrain, retrial, combine, pivot, pause or terminate; do not infer automatic pivot |

Low-risk read-only inspection or synthetic experimentation could still proceed under
a standing authority or sandbox Trial Release. Sensitive live access or external
effects would require an exact Trial Release. Only the evidence needed to select a
method—not full delivery infrastructure—would be authorized at that stage.

**Stress-test result:** v0.3 would have prevented premature deterministic-method
commitment while allowing a bounded comparison with the previously successful
AI–Human approach and credible hybrids. It would also prevent the winning trial from
becoming delivery by implication. It cannot guarantee a good choice if candidate
generation is weak, Domain review is ceremonial, trials are manipulated, tools
ignore releases or no enforcement mechanism exists; those remain governance,
implementation and Package B/C responsibilities.

## 23. Additional counterexamples

The v0.1 and v0.2 cases remain applicable. Version 0.3 adds:

| Case | Required result |
| --- | --- |
| plan and method are accepted but no execution actor is named | no material start |
| “go ahead” follows a proposal checkpoint | authorize only the exact immediately presented next action when sufficiently clear; no downstream inheritance |
| sandbox analysis discovers need for live data | stop at sandbox boundary and propose a new release |
| executor makes an ordinary reversible design choice inside a task release | proceed without micro-approval |
| monitoring fails during sensitive execution | safe stop or containment under the envelope |
| objective wording changes only typographically | proceed if change-control rule classifies it non-material |
| selected method changes materially during implementation | require renewed Method Selection and Execution Release |
| successful unauthorized operation produces desired outcome | record desired outcome and authorization failure independently |
| authorized operation produces adverse outcome | record adverse outcome without inventing authorization failure |
| standing authority is too broad to identify data or effects | fail the release-sufficiency test |
| deterministic method is preferred because its implementation is advanced | treat maturity as transition evidence, not comparative fitness |
| AI–Human alternative previously worked in a similar task | admit it or document why the present Context makes it non-credible |
| two alternatives are named but one is deliberately underconfigured | fail comparable-treatment review |
| a small representative trial can distinguish candidates | require the trial before primary-method selection |
| a trial would expose protected data disproportionately | narrow, synthesize or document an exception and residual uncertainty |
| one candidate wins on speed but fails a non-compensable Domain condition | do not erase the failure through aggregate scoring |
| trial prototype is complete and stakeholders ask to continue | stop at the trial boundary pending Method Selection and Delivery Release |
| selected method accumulates code and tests while Domain usefulness remains flat | trigger reassessment; sunk effort cannot decide continuation |
| evidence supports complementary strengths | permit a hybrid selection with separately governed roles and interfaces |
| urgent provisional selection is necessary | record expiry, compensating observation and earliest comparison point |

## 26. Prohibited inferences — what v0.3 does not mean

1. Objective Acceptance does not establish objective truth, Value or attainability.
2. Candidate admission does not establish equal plausibility, desirability or
   entitlement to equal resources.
3. Comparative validation does not establish universal superiority, causal proof or
   freedom from sampling and configuration effects.
4. Trial success does not establish Method Selection, delivery readiness, production
   authority or Value.
5. Method Selection does not establish fitness, causal contribution or execution
   authority.
6. Execution Release does not establish that execution occurred, complied, succeeded
   or produced Value.
7. Three decision types and two execution lanes do not require separate people,
   documents, meetings or manual clicks for every occurrence.
8. Separate decisions do not prohibit one competent authority from issuing more than one
   decision when each effect remains explicit.
9. A bounded release is not a guarantee against misuse, error or harm.
10. “Go ahead” is not always invalid authority; its scope depends on the exact current
   checkpoint and presented action.
11. Ambiguity does not authorize unlimited execution or require paralysis beyond the
   materially uncertain boundary.
12. Low-consequence standing authority does not make low-consequence work risk-free.
13. Exact release does not make maximal governance universally appropriate.
14. Requiring alternatives does not require retaining a non-credible or prohibited
    candidate.
15. An AI–Human method is not presumed superior merely because it succeeded before;
    its applicability remains Context-dependent.
16. A deterministic method is not presumed inadequate; repeatability may be valuable
    without establishing semantic or outcome adequacy.
17. A pivot trigger does not establish project failure or dictate abandonment.
18. Documentation compliance does not prove substantive control, fair comparison or non-ceremonial
   review.
19. Acceptance of v0.3 would not accept Packages B–D or GMC-003–014.
20. Package A is not the only or necessarily highest-priority SymK improvement.

## 27. Dissent and open questions

1. Which minimum artifact identity is needed when all three decisions occur in one
   compact low-risk record?
2. When is a conversational instruction sufficiently exact to constitute an
   Execution Release?
3. Which subjects may issue standing or sandbox authority, and for how long?
4. How should distributed or institutional authority be represented without forcing
   one approver?
5. Which materiality test distinguishes ordinary implementation choice from method
   change?
6. How should emergency authority shorten the gates while preserving retrospective
   evidence and challenge?
7. When should a review trigger require automatic stop rather than reassessment while
   continuing?
8. How should tools enforce envelope limits without mistaking machine checks for
   complete governance?
9. How are overlapping releases composed, prioritized, revoked or consumed?
10. How should a release handle probabilistic or emergent effects that cannot be
    enumerated exactly?
11. What evidence demonstrates that review was substantive rather than ceremonial?
12. Which execution-envelope fields belong in universal policy, project profiles or
    Domain standards?
13. What evidence is sufficient to call a candidate credible without creating a
    costly pre-trial?
14. When are historic results applicable enough to replace a new comparison?
15. How should preparation and tuning budgets be normalized across structurally
    different candidates?
16. Which Domain-review composition prevents both ceremonial adjudication and
    impractical review burden?
17. When should a continuing-fitness trigger suspend delivery automatically rather
    than permit bounded continuation during review?
18. How should delivery dependencies created by a trial be detected before they
    become path dependence?

## 28. Reopening conditions

Reassess v0.3 if:

- the three decision types or two execution lanes become ceremonial duplicates;
- the execution envelope forces maximal documentation on low-consequence work;
- standing releases become broad substitutes for accountable authority;
- candidate requirements produce token alternatives or prohibit legitimate
  single-method cases;
- comparative trials become full parallel implementations or impose costs greater
  than the decision they inform;
- candidate preparation, tuning or review is systematically asymmetric;
- Trial Releases routinely become Delivery Releases without a new decision;
- continuing-fitness triggers are ignored, gamed or cause premature churn;
- shorthand-instruction interpretation repeatedly over- or under-authorizes work;
- executors evade material-change review by decomposing changes;
- stop requirements create greater material harm than controlled continuation;
- enforcement mechanisms cannot represent the envelope faithfully;
- Package C or D establishes incompatible assurance or lifecycle semantics;
- multi-Domain tests expose authority or affected-subject failures; or
- accepted SymK conceptual or constitutional changes alter the dependencies.
