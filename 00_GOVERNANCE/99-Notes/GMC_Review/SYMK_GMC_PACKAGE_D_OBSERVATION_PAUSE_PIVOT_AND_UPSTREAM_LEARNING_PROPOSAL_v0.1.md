## 1. Proposal summary

Package D v0.1 converts GMC-011–014 into a candidate lifecycle-observation and
upstream-learning profile. Its core rule is:

> **Outcome/learning progress and control/readiness progress must remain separately
> observable. Material divergence is a review signal, not an automatic verdict.
> Pause is a governed lifecycle state, not a synonym for failure or abandonment.
> Derived-project evidence may challenge SymK only through a qualified upstream
> proposal and competent review; transmission does not amend the parent.**

The profile closes the feedback loop among accepted Packages A–C without turning
measurement into authority, progress into one score, a signal into a pivot, a pause
into indefinite limbo or local experience into universal SymK doctrine.

## 2. Decision requested

Accept, correct or reject a provisional observation, pause, pivot and upstream-
learning profile that:

- keeps outcome/learning progress distinct from control/readiness progress;
- preserves plural indicators, Evidence, uncertainty, disagreement and affected
  distribution rather than collapsing them into one dashboard score;
- treats material divergence as a trigger for competent method/lifecycle review;
- separates observation, signal, trigger, assessment, decision, Authorization and
  action;
- defines pause as a bounded, attributable and reversible lifecycle state with
  preservation, expiry and resumption obligations;
- prevents pause from suspending legal, ethical, safety, security, retention,
  maintenance or affected-subject duties by implication;
- packages derived-project evidence for upstream challenge without silent
  universalization or authority transfer;
- composes with accepted Packages A–C without changing their authority; and
- remains compatible with later 2.6 and observability work without opening either.

Acceptance would make this a provisional SymK lifecycle-observation and
upstream-learning profile and incorporate GMC-011–014 only through its exact bounded
formulations. It would make later operational-template work eligible for a separate
Execution Release and later classification or specialization eligible when the
owning stages are competent. It would not select indicators or thresholds for a
real project, trigger a real pause or pivot, open 2.6, approve an upstream proposal,
amend SymK or create a universal lifecycle workflow.

## 3. Problem addressed

Projects commonly confuse visible internal motion with progress toward the result
that justified the work. Code, documents, tests, approvals, integrations and control
coverage can grow while useful learning or external outcome does not. The reverse
also occurs: valuable learning or favorable outcomes can appear before a system is
controlled, reproducible, authorized or ready for reliance.

Once divergence appears, governance can fail again:

- a metric is treated as a verdict;
- a trigger is treated as an automatic pivot or stop;
- sunk cost or implementation maturity is used to justify continuation;
- early exploratory lag is misclassified as method failure;
- pause is confused with rejection, rollback, abandonment or completion;
- paused work loses evidence, security, maintenance or decision lineage;
- resumption relies on expired evidence or authority;
- a local project conclusion is generalized to SymK without qualification; or
- upstream review erases conflicting Domain experience and dissent.

Package D makes these transitions explicit and challengeable.

## 4. Compatibility and jurisdiction boundary

This proposal operationalizes without reopening accepted distinctions among:

- objective, method, implementation, output, outcome, consequence and Value;
- observation, datum, indicator, signal, Ground, Evidence, assessment and decision;
- progress, completion, readiness, compliance, success and improvement;
- expected, target and actual state;
- correlation, divergence, causal Contribution and method inadequacy;
- review trigger, pause condition, stop condition, decision and authorized action;
- pause, waiting, blocked, suspended, failed, rejected, rolled back, terminated,
  abandoned, completed and archived;
- Feedback, Uptake, Correction, Learning and improvement;
- local project evidence, generalization claim, upstream proposal and accepted SymK
  change;
- source project, derived project, SymK parent and external authority; and
- project-process profile, Domain policy, organizational procedure and
  constitutional rule.

It proposes no new Foundational concept, universal progress ontology, fixed metric
set, mandatory dashboard, automatic lifecycle engine, parent-precedence rule or
constitutional inheritance mechanism. Actual indicators, thresholds, causal
methods, pause/pivot authority and upstream decision procedures remain with their
competent owners.

The current 2.6 and 2.8 directories are closed scaffolds. This profile neither opens
them nor treats their scaffold text as effective authority. Current 2.8 corpus work
also must not be assumed to own every observability question merely because earlier
2.1 material used “2.8” as a broad downstream route.

| progress observation | attributed record of an identified state, change, event, absence or uncertainty at a declared time and boundary | Evidence, assessment, progress status or causal conclusion |
| progress profile | plural, versioned set of observations and assessments against declared expectations for a bounded purpose | one score, objective, outcome or project truth |
| outcome/learning progress | observations about movement toward the outcome-facing objective or reduction/revision of decision-relevant uncertainty | output volume, implementation maturity, readiness or favorable Value automatically |
| control/readiness progress | observations about capability to execute, constrain, verify, monitor, correct or support bounded reliance | outcome achievement, semantic adequacy or authorization automatically |
| divergence signal | declared material mismatch within or between progress profiles relative to expectations, uncertainty and time | proof of method failure, causal diagnosis or lifecycle decision |
| review trigger | governed condition requiring competent reassessment | automatic pause, pivot, rejection, rollback or termination |
| lifecycle decision | attributable choice to continue, constrain, retrial, combine, pivot, pause, resume, terminate or take another bounded response | the resulting action or successful effect |
| pause | explicitly authorized temporary lifecycle state that limits specified work while preserving declared assets, duties, evidence and decision options | failure, rejection, rollback, abandonment, cancellation, completion or absence of work |
| resumption | attributable decision that specified paused work may continue under a current envelope after required reassessment | proof of unchanged method fitness or restoration of expired authority |
| upstream evidence bundle | provenance-bearing project observations, claims, methods, limitations, dissent and applicability information prepared for parent review | accepted Evidence, universal rule or amendment |
| upstream proposal | attributable request that a competent parent process assess a declared change, exception, challenge or learning claim | automatic acceptance, inheritance or synchronization |
| generalization claim | evidence-bearing assertion about transfer from observed project conditions to a wider Scope | repetition, popularity or universal validity |

## 6. Lifecycle observation contract

For material monitoring, a progress claim has the bounded form:

> **For project object P, objective and method version M, observation interval T,
> affected horizon H and progress family F, observation O is assessed against
> expectation E under method V, Evidence G, uncertainty U, assessor A, authority R
> and lineage L.**

The contract keeps the observed object, expected comparator, observation method,
assessment and decision distinct. It must support the answer “unknown” when the
available observation cannot justify a progress conclusion.

Observation burden follows accepted Package C proportionality. Continuous
measurement, detailed surveillance or maximum retention is not required merely
because a progress family exists.

## 7. GMC-014 — two independently inspectable progress families

Material projects must keep at least these two questions independently answerable:

1. **Outcome/learning:** Are we obtaining outcome-relevant evidence, reducing or
   correctly revising important uncertainty, and moving toward the accepted
   externally meaningful objective?
2. **Control/readiness:** Are we gaining the bounded capability, verification,
   governance, reliability, security, correction and operational readiness needed
   for the intended use?

The questions may be answered with plural qualitative, quantitative or mixed
profiles. Neither family is intrinsically primary in every phase, and neither may be
used as a proxy for the other.

## 8. Outcome/learning progress profile

Where material, the profile may include:

1. evidence about the outcome-facing objective and affected horizon;
2. decision-relevant uncertainty reduced, increased or reframed;
3. candidate alternatives discovered, defeated, retained or newly admitted;
4. Domain coverage, usefulness, validity and expert/affected-subject challenge;
5. assumptions confirmed, weakened, contradicted or left unknown;
6. quality of Grounds, counterevidence and causal understanding;
7. failures that improve the question or method choice;
8. time to responsible Answer or decision-relevant learning;
9. actual benefits, harms, side effects and distributed consequences when
   observable;
10. unresolved dissent, blind spots and unrepresented subjects;
11. transfer, replication and boundary conditions; and
12. changes to the objective, method hypothesis or stopping rationale.

Learning may consist of justified rejection, qualification, reframing or discovery
that the original objective is infeasible or undesirable. More activity or more
data does not establish Learning.

Long-horizon outcomes may require leading observations, hypotheses or proxy
indicators. Their predictive relation and uncertainty must remain explicit; a
leading indicator is not the later outcome.

## 9. Control/readiness progress profile

Where material, the profile may include:

1. implementation capability and verified behavior;
2. data, access, security, privacy and dependency controls;
3. test, validation, reproducibility and failure-handling coverage;
4. role, authority, handoff and conflict-control readiness;
5. provenance, version, traceability and change control;
6. monitoring, alert, stop, rollback, correction and recovery capability;
7. operational capacity, reliability, scalability and support;
8. documentation, training and affected-user readiness;
9. applicable policy, standard, legal and professional checks;
10. residual uncertainty and unverified conditions;
11. Trial or Delivery Release conditions satisfied, failed or expired; and
12. governance burden and governance-caused risk from Package C.

Control/readiness progress can be real and valuable even before outcome evidence is
available. It does not by itself establish semantic adequacy, successful outcome,
Value, compliance or permission to rely.

## 10. Profile independence and relation

The two progress families must be separately inspectable but may interact:

- outcome learning may justify new controls, relaxed controls or a changed method;
- control limitations may constrain what outcome evidence can be gathered safely;
- control investment may be a justified precursor whose outcome contribution is
  expected only later;
- favorable outcomes may expose missing readiness or unauthorized reliance;
- control readiness may enable learning without guaranteeing it; and
- governance burden may itself change outcome and readiness progress.

No weighted average, maturity score or traffic light may erase a material failed,
unknown, disputed, distributed or non-compensable condition. A combined display is
permitted only if the underlying families and losses remain available.

## 11. Material observation record

A material progress observation identifies proportionately:

1. observed object, Identity, version and state/event;
2. progress family and specific question;
3. objective, method, release and assurance references;
4. Scope, Context, Domain, population and affected horizon;
5. observation interval, event time, recording time and latency;
6. baseline, comparator, expectation and source of expectation;
7. measure, instrument, method or interpretive procedure;
8. source, contributor, assessor and applicable authority;
9. Evidence role, provenance, quality and protected-access conditions;
10. uncertainty, missingness, disagreement and alternative explanations;
11. aggregation, transformation, exclusions and declared loss;
12. affected distribution and non-compensable conditions;
13. current status and whether a declared trigger is met;
14. challenge, correction and supersession route; and
15. lineage to any assessment or lifecycle decision.

Record size is proportional. A concise evidence-bearing observation may be better
than a large dashboard detached from its decision.

## 12. Indicator, metric and proxy rule

An indicator is selected for a declared question and use. It does not become the
objective, outcome, control or truth through repetition, convenience or dashboard
placement.

For a material indicator, declare:

- construct and intended interpretation;
- observed object and unit;
- relation to the progress question;
- baseline, comparator and expected direction or range;
- data source, coverage, latency and missingness;
- validity, reliability and known gaming pathways;
- affected distribution and aggregation loss;
- uncertainty and conflicting indicators;
- decision use and prohibited use;
- owner, challenge route and review horizon; and
- retirement or replacement conditions.

Targets may change behavior and corrupt the indicator's relation to the underlying
question. Metric gaming is evidence about the observation arrangement, not proof
that the underlying objective improved or failed.

## 13. Temporal, lag and expectation rule

Progress is time- and phase-sensitive. A valid profile distinguishes:

- immediate response, inquiry, implementation, verification, decision, action,
  correction, recovery and lifecycle intervals;
- expected investment or learning lag from unexpected stagnation;
- event time from observation and reporting time;
- leading, coincident and lagging observations;
- reversible exploration from reliance-bearing operation; and
- one-time variation from persistent or cumulative patterns.

An expectation requires a source, basis, uncertainty and review point. An unrealistic
plan cannot manufacture divergence by declaring impossible progress, and a vague
plan cannot hide divergence by omitting expectations.

## 14. GMC-011 — divergence as a governed review signal

Divergence is material when the relationship within or between progress families
differs enough from declared expectations that continuing without reassessment could
materially affect the objective, method fitness, assurance, authority, affected
subjects, cost or consequences.

Examples include:

- control/readiness activity and cost grow while outcome-relevant learning remains
  below the declared expectation;
- favorable outcome indicators grow while control, authorization or correction
  capability remains inadequate;
- one affected group benefits while another bears worsening unexamined consequence;
- local performance improves while transfer, robustness or external effect worsens;
- a project reports completion while key outcome or readiness dimensions remain
  unknown; or
- governance progress expands while its own delay and burden defeat the objective.

Divergence may occur within one profile as well as between the two families. It is a
signal to review the relevant claim, method or configuration—not proof of cause,
failure or the correct response.

## 15. Divergence-detection pattern

A material divergence signal records:

1. the two or more compared observations or profile states;
2. their versions, periods, baselines and expected relationship;
3. the magnitude or qualitative nature of the mismatch;
4. Evidence, uncertainty, lag and missingness;
5. affected subjects and possible consequences;
6. possible measurement, method, execution, Context and external explanations;
7. whether the declared review condition is met;
8. urgency and permitted interim containment;
9. competent review owner and decision deadline; and
10. dissent, challenge and correction lineage.

The pattern may use thresholds when locally authorized and validated. Package D does
not supply a universal threshold or formula.

## 16. False-positive and false-negative divergence controls

Review must test whether apparent divergence is caused by:

- expected discovery or infrastructure lag;
- a long-horizon or indirectly observable objective;
- stale, delayed, selectively reported or incomparable data;
- an invalid proxy or changed measurement method;
- seasonality, exogenous shock or Context change;
- a changed objective, population, Scope or baseline;
- non-linear progress, threshold effects or deliberate exploration;
- distribution hidden by aggregation;
- unrecorded work outside the observed boundary;
- metric gaming or reporting incentives; or
- a legitimate mandated method that cannot pivot freely.

Review must also test whether apparent alignment hides common-mode error, correlated
indicators, shared data, target gaming, suppressed dissent or an omitted affected
group. Agreement is not independent confirmation when the observations share a
material dependency.

## 17. Lifecycle-response decision rule

When a review trigger is met, competent authority chooses and records a bounded
response. Candidate responses include:

- continue under the existing envelope with rationale;
- correct the observation or expectation;
- increase, narrow or redesign monitoring;
- constrain Scope, access, effects or reliance;
- gather additional Evidence or run a comparative retrial;
- combine or replace methods;
- revise the objective under Package A procedure;
- adjust assurance under Package C procedure;
- pause specified work;
- resume previously paused work;
- terminate or abandon specified work; or
- route an upstream challenge or external-authority question.

The decision identifies authority, object, status, permitted effect, residual
uncertainty, affected parties, obligations and review conditions. The signal does not
choose the response. A decision does not establish that the authorized action
occurred or succeeded.

## 18. GMC-012 — pause as a distinct lifecycle state

Pause is an explicit temporary decision to limit identified work while preserving
declared assets, duties, evidence and future decision options.

Pause is distinct from:

- **waiting:** no active decision necessarily limits work;
- **blocked:** a condition prevents progress, with or without a pause decision;
- **stop condition:** an event may require action under an execution envelope;
- **suspension:** an external or organizational status may carry different legal or
  procedural effects;
- **failure:** an assessment that a declared condition was not satisfied;
- **rejection:** a decision not to accept an object or claim;
- **rollback:** an action restoring or compensating for a prior state;
- **termination/cancellation:** a decision to end specified work;
- **abandonment:** work ceases without necessarily preserving a governed return;
- **completion:** declared obligations are satisfied; and
- **archive:** a retention/access status for records or artifacts.

These states may coexist in a real case only when each predicate and effect is
separately established.

## 19. Minimum pause envelope

A material pause decision records proportionately:

1. paused object, version, Scope and effective time;
2. decision authority and source of authority;
3. triggering observations, assessment and rationale;
4. activities, accesses, changes and effects prohibited during pause;
5. activities and expenditures permitted during pause;
6. preservation, security, safety, privacy, retention and maintenance obligations;
7. commitments, dependencies, affected subjects and notification duties;
8. evidence, data, environment and reproducibility state to preserve;
9. credential, service, contract and resource disposition;
10. owner and capacity for monitoring, correction, incident response and remedy;
11. expiry, review cadence and decision deadline;
12. resumption, modification, termination and archival conditions;
13. Evidence validity and revalidation requirements;
14. dissent, challenge and appeal route; and
15. lineage to subsequent decisions and actions.

Pause must not become a zero-cost or duty-free state by omission.

## 20. Partial, nested and emergency pauses

A pause may apply to one method, data source, deployment, workstream, effect or
reliance condition while other authorized work continues. The boundary must prevent
nominal continuation from bypassing the pause.

Nested projects or systems identify which parent and child activities are paused and
which obligations remain. A parent pause does not automatically pause every child;
a child pause does not automatically invalidate the parent.

An emergency stop or containment action may precede a complete pause record when
competent authority and material harm require immediate response. The shortened
record must preserve the exact action and create a bounded completion obligation.
Urgency does not manufacture authority or retroactively validate the action.

## 21. Resumption and changed-condition rule

Resumption requires a current decision, not mere passage of time or renewed activity.
Before resumption, assess proportionately:

1. whether the objective and method remain current and fit;
2. whether Evidence, data, assumptions and affected horizon changed;
3. whether authority, consent, credentials, contracts and releases remain valid;
4. whether dependencies, tools, standards and environments changed;
5. whether preserved artifacts remain interpretable and reproducible;
6. whether unresolved incidents, harms, duties or remedies remain;
7. whether Package B role/handoff arrangements remain competent;
8. whether Package C assurance calibration remains adequate;
9. whether new alternatives or counterevidence require comparison; and
10. which monitoring and review conditions apply after resumption.

Resumption may continue, narrow, retrial, combine or replace earlier work. It does
not prove that the pause was successful, the old method remains fit or prior
authority has revived.

## 22. Pause expiry, staleness and decision debt

Every material pause has a review horizon or explicit reason why no date can yet be
set. Indefinite pause is not neutral when evidence decays, security obligations
continue, affected parties wait, costs accumulate or options disappear.

At expiry or material change, competent authority must renew, modify, resume,
terminate, transfer or otherwise dispose of the paused object. Failure to decide is
recorded as decision debt or governance failure where applicable; it does not
silently convert pause into abandonment or authorization to resume.

Evidence staleness is assessed by object and use. Time alone does not invalidate all
evidence, and archival preservation does not make evidence currently applicable.

## 23. GMC-013 — qualified upstream learning rule

A derived project's observation, success, failure, repeated practice or accepted
local decision does not amend SymK or establish universal validity. It may support an
upstream proposal when the evidence bundle makes the transfer claim inspectable.

The governed route is:

```text
project occurrence or observation
  -> scoped project claim
  -> Ground / Evidence / assessment
  -> local decision or preserved dissent
  -> upstream evidence bundle
  -> upstream proposal or challenge
  -> competent SymK review
  -> accepted, qualified, rejected, deferred or returned decision
  -> separately governed amendment, if any
```

Every arrow is contingent. Transmission, registration, repetition, compatibility or
favorable outcome does not skip a step.

## 24. Minimum upstream evidence bundle

A material upstream proposal identifies:

1. source project, version, lineage and claimed SymK relationship;
2. exact observed object, event, outcome or counterevidence;
3. source Scope, Context, Domain, population and affected horizon;
4. relevant inherited SymK version and challenged or supported object;
5. question, claim and requested upstream disposition;
6. observation, measurement, validation and analysis methods;
7. data provenance, access constraints and reproducibility conditions;
8. Grounds, Evidence role, counterevidence and defeaters;
9. alternative explanations, method candidates and counterfactuals;
10. uncertainty, missingness, selection effects and reporting bias;
11. affected subjects, benefits, harms, burdens and distribution;
12. positive, negative, null and anomalous results;
13. Domain expertise, participant roles, authority and conflicts;
14. dissent, minority positions and unresolved questions;
15. transfer conditions, boundary conditions and generalization claim;
16. conflicting evidence from other projects or Domains;
17. proposed target, change type and non-effects;
18. compatibility, specialization, exception and migration implications;
19. challenge, correction and withdrawal route; and
20. accountable submitter, date and current status.

The bundle is proportional to the claim. It need not expose protected material when
a competent protected-review route or limitation statement is appropriate.

## 25. Cross-project comparison and generalization

Before a project conclusion can support a wider SymK change, review asks:

- Are the source objects, questions and outcomes comparable?
- Which Context, Domain, population, institutional and temporal conditions differ?
- Do methods, role configurations and assurance arrangements differ materially?
- Are positive, negative and null cases all visible?
- Could selection, survivorship, publication or availability bias explain the
  pattern?
- Is repeated evidence independent or copied from one shared source?
- Which proposition is invariant, which is a Domain specialization and which is a
  project practice?
- What counterexample would defeat the proposed generalization?
- Who bears consequences if the generalization is wrong?
- Which authority is competent to decide the target artifact?

Conflicting project evidence is preserved and may justify qualification, multiple
profiles, further inquiry or rejection. Forced consensus is not an upstream gate.

## 26. A6 and closed-2.6 boundary

Proposed A6 describes an intended inheritance and constitutional-challenge direction,
but remains unratified. Package D does not borrow constitutional force from it.

The 2.6 scaffold reserves future work on exceptions, experiments, upstream
proposals, sunset and derived-project governance. It is closed and inert. Package D
may prepare candidate inputs and tests, but cannot:

- open or start 2.6;
- constitute its decision authorities;
- decide project classes or compatibility;
- approve an exception, specialization or upstream proposal;
- establish parent precedence or project continuation rights;
- reserve evidence, change or decision identifiers; or
- satisfy a 2.6 gate.

If Package D is later accepted, its project-process profile remains provisional
input to competent 2.6 work, not a substitute for it.

## 27. Composition with accepted Package A

Package A supplies the outcome-facing objective, method hypothesis, candidate
comparison, selected method, release envelope and continuing-fitness triggers.
Package D supplies versioned observation and lifecycle-decision structures.

Material divergence may return Package A for objective clarification, new candidate
admission, retrial, method reassessment or a new release. Package D does not select
the new objective or method, issue a release or decide the response automatically.

Package A's prior spending, artifact volume, test coverage and implementation
maturity remain transition considerations; they do not establish continued method
fitness.

## 28. Composition with accepted Package B

Package B supplies the participant/function, overlap, handoff, authority, provenance
and declared-loss configuration. Package D identifies who observes, assesses,
challenges, decides, acts and preserves evidence without collapsing those functions.

The same participant may perform several functions where the overlap is explicit and
proportionate. Reviewer or monitor count does not establish independence. An
automated alert, AI interpretation, standard threshold or Human judgment may
contribute to review, but none becomes the lifecycle decision or Authority by type.

## 29. Composition with accepted Package C

Package C supplies the consequence profile, assurance configuration, governance-
cost/risk expectations, escalation/de-escalation conditions and non-compensable
boundaries. Package D observes their operation and returns material divergence for
reassessment.

Monitoring burden, surveillance, retention, alert fatigue, review delay and metric
gaming are themselves possible Package C costs and Package D observations. More
observability does not automatically establish more assurance. Package D neither
sets a risk tolerance nor accepts residual risk.

## 30. SPServices medical-property stress test

The SPServices plan accumulated substantial deterministic, migration and control
work while the more fundamental outcome—identifying a useful medical-document
property set—remained insufficiently validated against a credible AI–Human
alternative already supported by earlier vocabulary experience.

Package D would have required two visible progress profiles:

| Progress family | Relevant observations | Prohibited substitution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| outcome/learning | useful candidate-property coverage, medical-expert judgment, unresolved concepts, rejected candidates, comparative learning, adaptability and evidence about intended use | files scanned, endpoints migrated or tests passing do not establish a good property set |
| control/readiness | authentication, API/libscan migration, deterministic validation, migrator behavior, test coverage, provenance, access control and deployment readiness | technical readiness does not establish medical semantic adequacy or business outcome |

When control/readiness progress and cost grew faster than outcome-relevant learning,
the mismatch would have triggered a method-fitness review under Package A and an
assurance-cost review under Package C. It would not have proved that deterministic
work was worthless or dictated an AI–Human pivot.

The actual governance response—pause the plan, preserve the technical work, record
the method conclusion and extract bounded GMCs for SymK—illustrates the candidate
Package D loop. The local experience supports the proposal; it does not validate the
universal profile by itself.

## 31. Multi-Domain stress-test suite

Package D v0.1 must remain usable in at least these cases:

1. **Long-horizon research:** no near-term outcome is expected; hypothesis quality,
   defeaters and learning milestones remain distinct from later outcome.
2. **Regulated fixed method:** divergence triggers review and escalation but cannot
   authorize deviation from a mandated method.
3. **Infrastructure migration:** readiness may legitimately lead visible user
   outcome; dependency, outage and recovery signals preserve the expected lag.
4. **Clinical terminology:** semantic usefulness, affected-subject consequence and
   deployment control progress remain separate.
5. **Emergency response:** observation and containment may be abbreviated without
   erasing authority, evidence or retrospective completion.
6. **Community knowledge:** plural and contested outcomes resist one aggregate
   progress score and require standing and dissent preservation.
7. **Small reversible experiment:** compact observation and pause records may be
   proportionate; absence of ceremony is not absence of governance.
8. **High-volume automation:** small per-event effects can accumulate while outcome
   and control indicators share hidden dependencies.
9. **Favorable unauthorized result:** outcome success does not create authorization,
   compliance or adequate control.
10. **Well-controlled failed result:** disciplined execution may still produce
    useful negative learning without achieving the intended outcome.

The suite passes only if the profile changes the questions and record requirements
without determining the locally competent conclusion.

## 34. Additional counterexamples

1. A dashboard is green because unmeasured dimensions were omitted.
2. A team produces more reports after progress becomes a target, while useful
   learning declines.
3. A favorable outcome occurs through an unauthorized process.
4. A technically controlled release causes delayed harm outside the monitoring
   horizon.
5. An experiment disproves its hypothesis and is reported as “no progress.”
6. A project improves its metric by narrowing the population it serves.
7. A pause preserves files but lets credentials, dependencies and evidence expire.
8. A partial pause is bypassed through a nominally different workstream.
9. Resumption occurs because a meeting was missed rather than through a decision.
10. A successful rollback is reported as proof that the original deployment was
    harmless.
11. Multiple projects repeat one inherited assumption and are counted as independent
    validation.
12. A local medical-domain conclusion is presented as a universal SymK rule.
13. Parent rejection is treated as proof that project evidence is false.
14. A popular upstream proposal bypasses competent authority.
15. Observability costs more and causes more exposure than the consequence it is
    intended to detect.

## 37. Prohibited inferences — what v0.1 does not mean

1. More implementation, documentation, tests or approvals do not by themselves
   establish more outcome/learning progress.
2. More outcome/learning progress does not by itself establish readiness,
   authorization or responsible reliance.
3. A favorable outcome does not retrospectively establish adequate process,
   authorization, Knowledge or Responsibility.
4. An unfavorable outcome does not by itself establish method failure, negligence or
   absence of Learning.
5. A progress observation does not by itself establish Evidence, cause or status.
6. An indicator does not become the objective or outcome through use as a target.
7. A dashboard, score, average or traffic light does not erase a material underlying
   dimension.
8. Agreement among dependent indicators does not establish independent confirmation.
9. Absence of a signal does not establish absence of divergence or consequence.
10. A divergence signal does not establish cause, method inadequacy, failure or the
    correct response.
11. A review trigger does not automatically require pause, pivot, rollback,
    termination or abandonment.
12. A decision to pivot does not establish that the prior method was irrational or
    useless.
13. Prior spending, artifact volume or implementation maturity does not establish
    continued fitness.
14. Pause does not by itself establish failure, rejection, rollback, abandonment,
    cancellation, completion or inactivity.
15. Pause does not suspend legal, ethical, safety, security, privacy, retention,
    maintenance, contractual or affected-subject obligations automatically.
16. Passage of time or renewed activity does not establish resumption.
17. Resumption does not establish that the old method, evidence, authority or
    assurance remains current.
18. Preserving an artifact does not establish preservation of its environment,
    meaning, applicability or reproducibility.
19. Registration of project evidence does not make it accepted Evidence or a SymK
    rule.
20. Local success, failure or repetition does not by itself establish universal
    validity or invalidity.
21. An upstream proposal does not amend, supersede or Ratify its target.
22. Parent acceptance does not erase the source project's limits, dissent or
    provenance.
23. Parent rejection does not erase the project occurrence or prove its claim false.
24. A derived project's authority does not transfer to SymK through evidence
    submission.
25. Proposed A6 does not supply Ratified constitutional authority.
26. A 2.6 or 2.8 scaffold does not open its stage or govern current work.
27. Observability does not by itself establish assurance, safety, compliance or
    control.
28. Package D does not own Domain semantics, causal truth, objective selection,
    method selection, role assignment, risk tolerance or external law.
29. Package D preparation does not accept GMC-011–014 or create a lifecycle policy,
    upstream procedure or observability standard.
30. Package D is not the only or necessarily highest-priority remaining SymK
    improvement.

## 39. Reopening conditions

Reassess Package D if:

- the two progress families erase important independent kinds of progress;
- dashboards or metrics become the objective or a universal project score;
- false divergence repeatedly causes premature pivot or suppresses exploration;
- real divergence remains hidden by lag, aggregation or common dependencies;
- review triggers become automatic lifecycle decisions;
- pause becomes indefinite limbo, silent abandonment or a duty-avoidance mechanism;
- resumption occurs without current evidence, authority or assurance;
- partial pauses are routinely bypassed;
- upstream bundles become ceremonial, excessively burdensome or biased toward
  well-resourced projects;
- local project experience is generalized without adequate transfer tests;
- parent review suppresses credible conflicting Domain evidence or dissent;
- Package A, B or C interfaces create circular authority;
- later 2.6, observability, constitutional, conflict or correction governance is
  incompatible with this profile;
- current stage ownership changes materially; or
- SPServices or another project produces material counterevidence.
