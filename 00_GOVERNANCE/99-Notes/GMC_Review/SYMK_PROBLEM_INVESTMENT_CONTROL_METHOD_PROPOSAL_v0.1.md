## 1. Purpose

Provide a governed way for a project to:

1. represent a particular problem situation;
2. assess the form of complexity currently evidenced;
3. determine whether further attention is worthwhile;
4. identify the smallest bounded next investment;
5. reject methods that introduce disproportionate complexity or depend on unavailable
   capabilities; and
6. state the evidence and conditions required before a larger commitment is considered.

The method does not presume that every identified problem should be solved or that a
more complex problem deserves greater investment.

## 2. Present-sufficiency principle

> A bounded, evidence-supported method executable with presently viable capabilities
> and explicit limitations is preferable to a more complete-looking method that
> depends on unavailable technology, unaffordable coordination or unverified future
> promises.

This is not an eighty-percent score or a license to ignore material gaps. Present
sufficiency requires that:

- the intended use and boundary are explicit;
- no known non-compensable condition is violated;
- limitations and unknowns remain visible;
- the method can produce a useful result or decision-relevant evidence now; and
- correction, rejection and later extension remain possible.

Completeness beyond those conditions is optional until evidence justifies its cost.

## 3. SymK relationship and status

This is a project-governance method candidate, not a foundational SymK concept,
axiom, universal standard or automatic policy. It coordinates, without superseding:

- Package A — Objective and Method Fitness;
- Package B — Epistemic Role Cooperation;
- Package C — Proportional Assurance and Governance Cost; and
- Package D — Observation, Pause, Pivot and Upstream Learning.

Its distinctive contribution is the bridge from a represented problem situation to
a bounded investment recommendation under present capability constraints.

## 4. Governing question

> Given this problem situation, in this context and at this time, what is the
> smallest worthwhile next investment, which presently viable method can perform it,
> and what evidence or change would justify reconsidering the commitment?

## 5. Unit of assessment

The assessed object is not an abstract or permanent Problem. It is a versioned
problem situation:

> Problem situation P, under objective O, within scope S and context C, during time
> horizon T, according to evidence E.

A changed objective, scope, context, time horizon or evidence state may justify a new
profile without contradicting the previous one.

## 6. Required distinctions

The method keeps distinct:

- problem complexity and solution complexity;
- inherent problem conditions and method-induced complexity;
- complexity and uncertainty;
- complexity and importance;
- complexity and consequence or risk;
- expected value and required cost;
- worthwhile investment and authorized investment;
- next-step investment and full-solution commitment;
- market existence of a capability and its availability to the project; and
- observation, assessment, recommendation, decision and authorization.

No aggregate score may erase one of these distinctions.

## 7. Multidimensional problem-complexity profile

| Dimension | Question | Illustrative conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| structural | What elements, dependencies and decomposition limits materially shape the situation? | bounded; decomposable; tightly coupled; unresolved |
| epistemic | What is known, observable, evidenced, disputed or opaque? | established; analysable; uncertain or contested; opaque |
| dynamic | How do change, feedback, delay, nonlinearity, path dependence or instability matter? | stable; variable; adaptive or nonlinear; unstable |
| agency and coordination | Which autonomous actors, objectives, knowledge and authorities affect the situation? | aligned; coordinated; plural or negotiated; adversarial or fragmented |
| contextual and boundary | How dependent is the situation on external systems, institutions and local conditions? | contained; context-dependent; open and coupled; boundary-uncertain |

The conditions are descriptive claims, not numerical levels. Unknown and disputed
conditions remain visible. One favorable dimension cannot automatically compensate
for a material condition in another.

### 7.1 Response orientations

The profile may identify one or more non-authorizing response orientations:

- **execution-oriented:** use an established procedure under known conditions;
- **analysis-oriented:** decompose and study a tractable but complicated situation;
- **exploration-oriented:** use bounded probes where uncertainty or emergence is
  material;
- **stabilization-oriented:** contain instability before expecting reliable inquiry;
- **hybrid:** apply different orientations to separately represented aspects; or
- **indeterminate:** improve framing or evidence before a stronger claim.

The response orientation informs the form of inquiry. It does not determine value,
investment size, method selection or authorization.

## 8. Investment-worth assessment

Investment means money, elapsed time, engineering effort, expert attention, evidence
collection, assurance work, coordination, opportunity cost, exposure, transition,
maintenance and remedy.

Investment worth is comparative and incremental. The assessment compares the
proposed next commitment with inaction, delay and credible alternatives.

### 8.1 Value sources

Value claims may concern:

- useful outcome;
- avoided harm, loss, failure or deterioration;
- uncertainty reduction and learning;
- preservation or creation of future options;
- enablement of another objective;
- satisfaction of a legitimate obligation; or
- strategic capability and resilience.

Each material value claim identifies its beneficiary, basis, time horizon and
assessor.

### 8.2 Total burden

The assessment includes:

- direct financial and infrastructure cost;
- time, delay and opportunity cost;
- engineering, operational, expert and stakeholder effort;
- evidence, assurance and governance burden;
- privacy, safety, security and affected-party exposure;
- handoff and context-transfer loss;
- dependency, lock-in and maintenance; and
- transition, rollback, correction and remediation.

Sunk cost does not justify continuation. It matters only when stopping creates a new
material consequence.

### 8.3 Worth gates

1. **Legitimacy:** Is the objective clear enough, within authority and attentive to
   affected parties and mandatory constraints?
2. **Additionality:** Could the proposed commitment improve the outcome or reduce
   material uncertainty beyond inaction, reuse or a simpler alternative?
3. **Comparative proportionality:** Are its expected value and learning proportionate
   to total burden and downside exposure?
4. **Boundedness and reversibility:** Can scope, time, cost and exposure be capped,
   observed and corrected before a larger commitment?
5. **Authorization readiness:** Are the required authority, resources, controls,
   review conditions and stop conditions explicit?

Passing worth assessment does not authorize execution.

## 9. Provisional investment envelopes

These are ceilings, not targets, importance ratings or automatic permissions.

| Envelope | Purpose | Maximum kind of commitment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I0 — decline or monitor | preserve awareness without active intervention | record, observe and define reconsideration triggers |
| I1 — frame | improve the represented problem and decision basis | clarify objective, evidence, actors, constraints and alternatives |
| I2 — probe | obtain bounded outcome or learning evidence | limited investigation, prototype, experiment, consultation or comparison |
| I3 — bounded intervention | attempt a limited operational improvement | controlled change with explicit scope, exposure, rollback and review |
| I4 — staged program | coordinate sustained investment | multiple separately authorized stages with reassessment between them |

No envelope automatically opens its successor. The appropriate investment is the
smallest commitment capable of producing the intended outcome or decision-relevant
evidence.

## 10. Method-induced complexity control

Before an I2 or higher recommendation, assess complexity introduced by the proposed
method or control configuration, including:

- procedural steps and dependencies;
- active role instances rather than headcount alone;
- handoffs and repeated review encounters;
- context-transfer and reconstruction effort;
- artifacts, manifests, approvals and evidence custody;
- coordination latency and availability dependencies; and
- continuing maintenance and correction burden.

> A more complex configuration may be preferred only when its additional expected
> outcome or learning contribution justifies the induced complexity relative to a
> credible simpler alternative.

Role identification does not require immediate role activation. Only the competence
or authority needed for the current bounded decision becomes an active dependency.
Other roles may remain deferred, conditional or escalation-only.

## 11. Enabling-capability viability boundary

A method is feasible only when each essential capability is evidenced as available,
or attainable within the proposed investment envelope, time horizon, access
conditions, competence boundary and acceptable total cost.

Capability assessment distinguishes:

1. **existence:** the capability exists in a technical or commercial form;
2. **project accessibility:** this project can obtain it under its account,
   permissions, region, infrastructure and security conditions;
3. **operational adequacy:** it performs the required function reliably enough in the
   intended workflow; and
4. **economic viability:** acquisition, operation, support and maintenance fit the
   bounded investment.

### 11.1 Capability states

- **verified available:** demonstrated in the intended project environment;
- **conditionally available:** available under explicit currently satisfiable
  conditions;
- **attainable within envelope:** acquisition or construction is proportionate and
  separately authorizable;
- **unavailable:** cannot currently be obtained or used within the boundary;
- **speculative:** depends on future technology, a vendor roadmap, research or
  unapproved development; or
- **unknown or disputed:** evidence is insufficient or conflicting.

An essential capability in an unavailable, speculative, unknown or disputed state
prevents the method from being represented as execution-ready. The project must
remove the dependency, substitute a viable capability, separately evaluate
capability acquisition, or defer or reject the method.

Future capability may be recorded as an alternative. It cannot be assumed by the
present executable baseline.

## 12. Context portability

Every required participant must be able to receive the minimum sufficient, current,
comprehensible and appropriately protected context, and return an attributable
contribution, using capabilities evidenced as viable for the project.

When coherent cooperation depends on unavailable shared context, the method must:

1. externalize context into a maintained and versioned system of record;
2. reduce the number of handoffs, simultaneous roles or required review encounters;
3. substitute another evidenced collaboration arrangement; or
4. be classified as presently infeasible.

A summary, static snapshot or review packet is not equivalent to a shared evolving
conversation. It may be used only when its expected information loss is acceptable
for the bounded decision and its correction route is explicit.

## 13. Outcome-to-control divergence

Outcome or learning progress and control or readiness progress remain separately
observable.

> When control artifacts, approvals, coordination dependencies or readiness claims
> accumulate while outcome-relevant evidence does not materially change, the next
> investment must be reconsidered before more control work proceeds.

Reconsideration may recommend simplification, narrowing, substitution, further
framing, pause or termination. This proposal does not yet define the complete
lifecycle semantics for those decisions.

## 14. Minimum usable procedure

Until a fuller lifecycle is accepted, the method can be used through this bounded
sequence:

1. state and version the problem situation, objective, scope, context, time and
   evidence boundary;
2. record the five-dimensional complexity profile and response orientation;
3. state credible next-step alternatives, including inaction and a simpler option;
4. assess value, total burden, consequences, uncertainty and reversibility;
5. identify essential capabilities and test their present viability;
6. assess method-induced complexity and participant-context feasibility;
7. apply the five worth gates and recommend at most one bounded investment envelope;
8. record why a smaller envelope is insufficient and why a larger one is unnecessary;
9. define the evidence expected from the commitment and conditions requiring
   reconsideration; and
10. obtain any required authorization separately from this assessment.

## 15. Minimum decision record

Every material use records:

- profile identity and version;
- problem situation, objective, scope, context, time and evidence;
- complexity claims, uncertainty, counterevidence and response orientation;
- alternatives, including inaction and the simplest credible option;
- value claims, beneficiaries, total burden and distributed consequences;
- required capabilities, evidence and viability state;
- induced complexity, role activation and context-transfer design;
- proposed envelope, ceilings and expected evidence;
- non-compensable conditions and known limitations;
- assessor, competence, authority, dissent and decision status; and
- reconsideration conditions.

## 16. Non-goals and prohibited inferences

This proposal does not:

- define a universal ontology or scalar of Problem, Complexity, Value or Risk;
- make a complexity condition permanent;
- infer importance or investment from complexity;
- allow a favorable aggregate to erase a material failure or unknown;
- assume action is preferable to monitoring or inaction;
- convert role recognition into mandatory staffing at every stage;
- convert technology market availability into project capability;
- treat future technology or lower future prices as present evidence;
- bypass Objective Acceptance, Method Selection, Trial Release or Delivery Release;
- override Domain, legal, privacy, safety or professional authority; or
- authorize any SymK or derived-project execution.

## 17. Known incompleteness deliberately retained

The following remain later work rather than prerequisites for bounded present use:

- full authorization, observation, escalation, de-escalation, pause, pivot, resume,
  termination and reopening lifecycle semantics;
- cross-domain stress tests and counterexamples;
- canonical identifier and final repository placement;
- machine-readable schema or automated assessment tooling;
- project-specific thresholds and evidence instruments; and
- any decision to generalize this proposal beyond its declared governance scope.
