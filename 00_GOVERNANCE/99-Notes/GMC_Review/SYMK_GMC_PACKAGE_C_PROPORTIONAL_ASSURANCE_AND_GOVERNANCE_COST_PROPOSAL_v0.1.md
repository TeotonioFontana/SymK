## 1. Profile summary

Package C v0.1 converts GMC-009 and GMC-010 into a provisional assurance profile
that asks two questions together:

> **What assurance is warranted by the material consequences of this work, and what
> consequences are created by the assurance arrangement itself?**

Assurance burden is neither maximized by default nor minimized for convenience. It
is calibrated to a declared configuration, affected horizon and consequence profile.
The profile makes under-governance and over-governance separately visible and
challengeable without reducing sensitivity, uncertainty, harm, reversibility,
external effect, governance cost or risk tolerance to one universal scalar.

## 2. Acceptance decision and effect

The project steward accepts this exact v0.1 as a provisional SymK
proportional-assurance and governance-cost profile. The accepted profile:

- relates assurance measures to material consequence rather than document or gate
  count;
- preserves distinct consequence dimensions, unknowns, disagreements and
  non-compensable conditions;
- requires escalation and permits evidence-bearing de-escalation;
- treats governance delay, opportunity cost, fatigue, displacement, exposure and
  learning suppression as material possible costs and risks;
- compares credible assurance configurations rather than assuming maximum ceremony;
- prevents urgency, pilot labels, compliance labels or low direct price from
  understating material consequence;
- composes with accepted Packages A and B without changing their authority; and
- supplies Package D with observable assurance assumptions, burdens and outcomes
  without accepting Package D.

Acceptance incorporates GMC-009–010 only through this profile's exact bounded
formulations, controls and non-entailments. It makes later operational-template work
eligible only under a separate Execution Release and makes later 2.4 classification
or 2.6 specialization eligible when those stages are competent. It does not set a
universal risk tolerance, assign an assurance level to a real project, issue an
Execution Release, open a stage or accept Package D or GMC-011–014.

## 3. Problem addressed

Governance can fail in two directions.

Under-governance can expose participants and affected subjects to avoidable harm,
weak Evidence, conflicted review, uncontrolled effects, inadequate correction and
unowned residual risk. Over-governance can delay learning, exhaust reviewers,
displace scarce Domain expertise, expose protected information, freeze an
inadequate method, privilege well-resourced participants and create ceremonial
compliance without better assurance.

Several attractive shortcuts hide the choice:

- every important project receives the same controls;
- maximum review is treated as maximum safety;
- a “pilot,” “internal,” “AI-assisted” or “deterministic” label substitutes for
  consequence analysis;
- the absence of an observed incident substitutes for Evidence of adequate control;
- compliance, document count or approval count substitutes for assurance;
- low price or short execution time substitutes for low total cost;
- governance labor is treated as free or external to the method; or
- uncertainty is used either to demand unlimited precaution or to justify no
  precaution.

Package C prevents these substitutions while preserving competent Domain,
organizational, legal and external authority.

## 4. Compatibility boundary

This profile operationalizes without reopening accepted distinctions among:

- risk, uncertainty, harm, consequence, Value and Responsibility;
- sensitivity, severity, likelihood, exposure and affected distribution;
- reversibility, recoverability, correction and remedy;
- evidence quality, assurance activity, compliance and successful outcome;
- price, budget, resource use, direct cost and total cost;
- recommendation, assessment, risk acceptance, Authorization and execution;
- project-process guidance, Domain policy, organizational policy and external law;
- participant, reviewer, approver, authority and affected subject;
- unknown, disputed, not assessed, not applicable and failed; and
- proposed profile, accepted profile, operational configuration and observed effect.

It proposes no new Foundational concept, universal risk ontology, mandatory
workflow, certification scheme or numerical risk model. Domain criteria, legal
duties, organizational tolerances, decision authority and actual configuration
remain with competent owners.

## 5. Working terms

| Term | Working meaning in v0.1 | Must remain distinct from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| assurance | proportionate activities and conditions used to support justified reliance, control, challenge and correction for a declared use | truth, safety, compliance, authorization or favorable outcome |
| assurance configuration | declared set of evidence, review, independence, authorization, containment, monitoring, challenge, correction and other controls for an identified work configuration | universal workflow, maturity level or successful implementation |
| consequence profile | multidimensional account of plausible beneficial, adverse, direct, indirect, delayed, cumulative, distributed and residual effects with Evidence and uncertainty | one risk score, forecast certainty or accepted Value |
| assurance burden | resources, delay, constraint, exposure, cognitive work and obligations introduced by an assurance configuration and attributed to identified Bearers | mere document count or automatically excessive governance |
| governance opportunity cost | material foregone work, learning, response, participation or alternative use of capability caused by a governance choice | direct financial price alone |
| governance-caused risk | plausible adverse consequence introduced or amplified by governance design, operation, omission, delay or rigidity | proof that governance should be removed |
| escalation | governed strengthening or broadening of assurance in response to material evidence or changed conditions | punishment, automatic stop or admission of prior negligence |
| de-escalation | governed narrowing or removal of assurance whose burden is no longer justified, subject to preserved duties and residuals | convenience, waiver by silence or denial of risk |
| non-compensable condition | declared condition that cannot be erased by favorable values on other dimensions for the stated decision | universally absolute prohibition outside competent authority |
| residual uncertainty | material uncertainty remaining after proportionate inquiry or control | accepted residual risk, harmlessness or permission to proceed |
| risk acceptance | attributable decision by competent authority to tolerate a declared residual exposure for a declared purpose and Scope | semantic truth, assurance adequacy or transfer of consequences to others |

These are profile-local working terms and do not amend accepted SymK vocabulary.

## 6. GMC-009 core rule — consequence-proportional assurance

For material work, the assurance configuration must be justified against the
declared consequence profile of the actual proposed configuration.

The relation has the bounded form:

> **For work configuration W, intended use U, affected horizon H and consequence
> profile C, assurance configuration A is claimed proportionate under Evidence E,
> uncertainty Q, applicable duties D, authority R and review conditions T.**

This claim does not establish that A is sufficient, minimal, compliant or optimal.
It makes the basis, alternatives, unknowns, Bearers and decision authority available
for challenge.

## 7. Material consequence profile

A consequence-proportional assessment considers, where material:

1. intended and plausible actual uses, reliance and misuse;
2. affected subjects, beneficiaries, Bearers and excluded parties;
3. type and severity of possible benefit, harm, burden or lost capability;
4. breadth, concentration and distribution of effects;
5. duration, latency, accumulation and affected time horizon;
6. reversibility, recoverability, detectability and correction latency;
7. uncertainty, disagreement, Evidence quality and missing Evidence;
8. external, cross-system, cross-Domain and dependency effects;
9. privacy, security, confidentiality, safety and access exposure;
10. legal, ethical, professional, contractual and organizational duties;
11. authority, Responsibility, standing, consent, refusal and challenge;
12. precedent, path dependence, lock-in, replication and scaling potential;
13. transition, migration, abandonment, repair and remedy conditions;
14. plausible indirect, delayed, cumulative, side and residual consequences; and
15. consequences of action, inaction, delay and alternative action.

Not every factor applies to every case. Omission of a plausibly material factor
requires rationale; checklist completion does not establish substantive adequacy.

## 8. No universal scalar or automatic aggregation

Package C does not define one universal likelihood-times-impact score, traffic-light
classification, assurance level or threshold.

Quantification may support a competent decision when its construct, data, unit,
baseline, comparator, uncertainty, affected distribution, authority and limitations
are declared. A number does not eliminate qualitative Evidence, contested standing,
unknowns or non-compensable conditions.

Aggregation must not silently permit a favorable average to erase:

- a material failed or unknown dimension;
- concentrated harm to a smaller affected group;
- an applicable legal, ethical or professional constraint;
- absent authority, consent, competence or correction capacity;
- irreversible or poorly recoverable effects; or
- material disagreement hidden by a consensus label.

## 9. Bidirectional calibration rule

The assessment must test both:

1. **assurance deficiency:** what material consequence remains insufficiently
   prevented, detected, challenged, contained, corrected or owned; and
2. **assurance excess or misfit:** what material delay, cost, exposure, exclusion,
   rigidity, fatigue, displacement or lost learning the assurance configuration
   creates without commensurate benefit.

The two tests are independent. Evidence of over-governance does not prove the work
is safe; Evidence of under-governance does not prove every additional control is
useful.

## 10. Assurance-response families

A proportionate configuration may select, combine or omit response families with
recorded rationale:

- narrower Scope, data, population, duration, access or permitted effects;
- stronger Evidence acquisition, validation or counterexample testing;
- competent review, conflict disclosure or actual independence;
- explicit Authorization, risk acceptance or affected-subject participation;
- sandboxing, isolation, rate limits, staged exposure or reversibility controls;
- provenance, versioning, traceability and handoff preservation;
- monitoring, anomaly detection, incident response and stop conditions;
- challenge, dissent, appeal, correction, repair and remedy routes;
- expiry, renewal, reassessment and revalidation;
- substitution of a less burdensome or more effective method; or
- decision not to proceed.

No family is universally required or sufficient. Control selection follows the
actual consequence and function configuration, not the prestige of a method or
participant.

## 11. Escalation rule

Escalation is required for review when material evidence indicates that the current
assurance basis may no longer be adequate, including:

- Scope, use, affected population or external effect expands;
- reversibility, recoverability or correction capacity decreases;
- protected data, consequential access or irreversible action becomes involved;
- material uncertainty, disagreement, anomaly or counterevidence increases;
- actual consequence exceeds or differs from the declared profile;
- a shared dependency defeats claimed independence;
- a trial becomes relied upon, replicated, integrated or production-like;
- a legal, professional, contractual or organizational duty changes;
- monitoring or challenge becomes unavailable; or
- cumulative exposure crosses a declared boundary.

Escalation may strengthen controls, narrow the work, pause, return the method for
reassessment or seek competent authority. Package C itself does not choose the
response or create stop authority.

## 12. De-escalation rule

De-escalation is eligible when evidence shows that a control is materially
ineffective, duplicative, misdirected or more harmful than a credible alternative,
or when the configuration and consequence profile have genuinely narrowed.

A de-escalation record must identify:

1. the control changed or removed;
2. the evidence and comparison supporting the change;
3. the affected consequence dimensions and Bearers;
4. continuing external and internal duties;
5. residual uncertainty, dissent and non-compensable conditions;
6. replacement controls or reason none is warranted;
7. monitoring and reopening triggers; and
8. competent decision authority.

Convenience, schedule pressure, lack of observed incidents, prior expenditure or a
low-risk label does not by itself justify de-escalation.

## 13. Uncertainty and precaution

Uncertainty increases the need for explicit treatment; it does not determine one
automatic response.

Where consequence could be severe, distributed, difficult to detect or poorly
reversible, uncertainty may justify narrower exposure, additional Evidence,
independent challenge, staged trials or a decision not to proceed. Where excessive
delay also creates material harm, a bounded action with monitoring and retrospective
obligation may be more proportionate than waiting for unattainable certainty.

Precaution does not establish maximum control, zero risk, unlimited review or
authority to override affected subjects. Action under uncertainty does not establish
that uncertainty was immaterial.

## 14. Applicable floors and non-compensable conditions

Package C cannot trade away applicable external law, competent organizational
authority, professional duty, rights, consent, safety conditions or mandatory
controls. Their interpretation and applicability remain with competent authority.

A project may declare additional non-compensable conditions for a bounded decision.
Each condition requires a source, Scope, affected subject, decision effect and review
route. Labels such as “critical,” “ethical” or “zero tolerance” do not create the
underlying authority by themselves.

## 15. Strategic understatement, inflation and gaming

The assurance assessment must challenge configurations that:

- decompose one consequential undertaking into nominally small tasks;
- call production reliance a pilot, experiment or internal tool;
- place material effects outside the declared system boundary;
- transfer cost or harm to unrepresented Bearers;
- use the absence of data as a low-risk score;
- select a favorable time horizon or comparator;
- overstate risk to block challenge, competition or change;
- overstate assurance to market, certify or authorize a result;
- multiply artifacts, gates or reviewers to create an appearance of rigor; or
- keep controls because removing them would expose prior waste or error.

Independent challenge means practical access to evidence, time and refusal—not
merely a second name in an approval field.

## 16. GMC-010 core rule — governance belongs in total cost and risk

Every material assurance comparison must include governance activity within the
method's bounded total-cost and consequence analysis.

Governance is not presumed wasteful. It may prevent harm, improve Evidence, preserve
correction capacity, support legitimate reliance and reduce total cost. But its
benefits, burdens and risks require evidence; they are not established by the word
“governance,” by procedural completion or by good intent.

## 17. Governance-cost profile

Where material, identify:

1. design, documentation, review, approval and audit labor;
2. coordination, meeting, queue, waiting and decision latency;
3. Evidence acquisition, verification, monitoring and retention cost;
4. tooling, integration, access-control and compliance cost;
5. transition, training, interpretation and handoff burden;
6. scarce Domain, scientific, engineering and affected-subject attention;
7. opportunity cost of delayed or displaced work and learning;
8. reviewer fatigue, cognitive load and decision-quality degradation;
9. privacy, security and confidentiality exposure created by review or retention;
10. participation barriers and unequal burden across people or organizations;
11. rigidity, vendor or method lock-in and suppressed alternatives;
12. correction, appeal, incident, repair and remedy burden;
13. ceremonial-compliance and proxy-gaming cost;
14. costs transferred beyond the project or provider boundary; and
15. residual cost after the governance configuration ends.

Cost claims identify objects, families, Bearers, boundaries, counterfactuals,
horizons, allocation, uncertainty, exclusions and distribution. “Total” means
bounded material completeness, not omniscience.

## 18. Governance-caused risk profile

Review plausible governance-caused risks, including:

- decision delay during a time-sensitive condition;
- evidence expiration while approval is pending;
- false confidence produced by formal compliance;
- diffusion of Responsibility across many reviewers;
- concentration of authority in process owners;
- expert disengagement and rubber-stamping caused by review overload;
- suppression of dissent by consensus or sign-off pressure;
- leakage of protected information through evidence demands;
- exclusion of smaller or less-resourced participants;
- method lock-in and continuation by procedural inertia;
- fragmented understanding caused by handoff multiplication;
- optimization for auditable proxies instead of the objective; and
- inability to adapt after conditions change.

Identifying a governance-caused risk does not justify removing the relevant control.
It creates a requirement to compare mitigation, redesign or alternatives.

## 19. Comparative assurance-configuration test

For material work, compare credible assurance configurations proportionately. The
candidate set may include the current arrangement, stronger or narrower controls,
different sequencing, changed Scope, a reversible trial, a different method, delayed
action, urgent bounded action and non-execution.

Candidates are assessed on common declared questions such as:

- consequence prevention and containment;
- Evidence and defect-detection capability;
- actual independence and conflict management;
- correction, reversibility, repair and remedy;
- time to responsible decision or Answer;
- direct and total governance cost;
- affected-subject access, distribution and burden;
- adaptability, learning and alternative preservation;
- legal, professional and organizational compatibility; and
- residual uncertainty and ownership.

Comparison may be qualitative, quantitative or mixed. It does not require a full
parallel implementation when that would be disproportionate.

Among configurations credibly supported as adequate for the declared consequence
profile and applicable floors, the recommendation should prefer the least
burdensome configuration, with its residual uncertainty and rejected alternatives
visible. “Least burdensome adequate” is a challengeable comparative claim, not a
universal optimum, permission to weaken duties or automatic decision rule.

## 20. Composition with accepted Package A

Package A supplies the accepted objective, credible method candidates, comparative
method record and proposed Trial or Delivery Release. Package C assesses the
assurance implications of the actual proposed method and execution envelope.

Package C may recommend that Package A:

- narrow or stage a release;
- add Evidence, review, monitoring, stop or correction conditions;
- compare a less burdensome assurance configuration;
- return a method whose total governance cost defeats its claimed fitness; or
- treat changed consequence as a material change.

Package C does not accept the objective, select the method, issue the release,
authorize execution or establish outcome achievement.

## 21. Composition with accepted Package B

Package B supplies the actual functions, participants, overlaps, handoffs,
dependencies, authorities and effects. Package C evaluates which assurance measures
are warranted for that configuration.

Package C may require stronger functional independence, conflict control,
provenance, review or monitoring where consequence warrants it. It must also test
whether added separation creates handoff loss, delay, false independence or
ceremonial review. An assurance requirement does not confer semantic, Domain or
decision authority on a reviewer, standard, AI system or deterministic control.

## 22. Interface with Package D

Package C supplies Package D with inspectable:

- consequence assumptions and unknowns;
- selected assurance configuration and alternatives;
- escalation, de-escalation and expiry conditions;
- predicted governance benefits, costs and risks;
- affected Bearers and distributions;
- residual uncertainty, dissent and risk acceptance; and
- observable indicators for control performance and governance-caused harm.

Package D may return observations that challenge Package C calibration. A signal,
incident or absence of incident does not automatically prove assurance adequacy,
failure or the correct response. Package D remains unaccepted.

## 23. Minimum assurance decision record

A material Package C assessment records proportionately:

1. identified work, version, Scope, Context, Domain and intended use;
2. Package A objective, method and release identities when applicable;
3. Package B function/participant/handoff configuration when applicable;
4. affected subjects, beneficiaries, Bearers and standing;
5. consequence profile with Evidence, uncertainty and disagreement;
6. applicable external and internal duties and competent authorities;
7. non-compensable conditions and assessment gaps;
8. current and credible alternative assurance configurations;
9. comparative assurance benefits, limitations, costs and risks;
10. selected or recommended configuration and rejected alternatives;
11. independence, conflict, challenge and refusal conditions;
12. residual uncertainty, exposure and risk-acceptance owner;
13. escalation, de-escalation, expiry and reassessment triggers;
14. monitoring, correction, repair and remedy routes;
15. dissent, exclusions and unresolved questions; and
16. decision status, authority, date and lineage.

Record size follows materiality. A compact record may satisfy this rule; many pages
may fail it.

## 24. SPServices medical-property stress test

The SPServices plan sought a useful set of medical-document properties. It committed
substantial work to a deterministic extraction and migration path before comparing
that route adequately with AI–Human vocabulary development.

Package C treats the configurations differently rather than assigning “medical” one
automatic assurance level:

| Configuration | Material consequence profile | Proportionate assurance direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| read-only inspection of public or synthetic documents | limited external effect; reversible; discovery uncertainty remains | compact record, bounded access, provenance and spot review may suffice |
| AI-assisted candidate-property generation | broad candidate space; hallucination and omission possible; no deployment effect by itself | preserve model/input/version provenance, alternatives and uncertainty; competent medical review before acceptance |
| medical-expert adjudication of candidate vocabulary | consequential semantic decision; expertise, conflict, coverage and dissent matter | competence evidence, independent challenge where warranted, decision lineage and unresolved-case preservation |
| deterministic schema and API validation | repeatable formal checks; semantic adequacy not established | automated tests plus mapping to accepted properties; avoid duplicate ceremonial semantic approval |
| migration using protected clinical documents or credentials | privacy, security and operational exposure | narrowed access, authorization, logging, containment, expiry and incident response |
| production reliance on accepted properties | wider affected horizon, path dependence and correction cost | Delivery Release, monitoring, versioning, rollback/correction and competent risk acceptance |

The deterministic route may be useful for verification, migration and enforcement,
but its development cost, delayed learning and lock-in belong in total-cost analysis.
The AI–Human route may be faster for semantic discovery, but speed does not establish
coverage, correctness, authority or safe deployment.

Package C would have challenged the original plan before large execution because:

1. an expensive governance and implementation path was being treated as assurance;
2. comparative evidence for the AI–Human alternative was available from prior
   successful vocabulary work;
3. the cost of delayed Domain learning and displaced expert attention was material;
4. low-risk, reversible comparative trials were feasible; and
5. production and protected-data controls could be separated from exploratory
   candidate discovery rather than applied uniformly.

This stress test does not select an SPServices method or accept any medical property.

## 25. Multi-Domain stress-test suite

Package C v0.1 must remain usable in at least these materially different cases:

1. **Documentation correction:** a reversible typo in a non-authoritative internal
   note should not require production-grade review merely because it is in SymK.
2. **Clinical terminology:** a property used in patient-facing or regulated
   decisions may require competent Domain adjudication, affected-horizon analysis,
   versioning and correction routes.
3. **Emergency security response:** delay can increase harm; bounded urgent action
   may be proportionate with explicit authority, containment and retrospective
   completion.
4. **Exploratory research:** uncertain hypotheses need room for reversible learning;
   publication, reliance and protected-data exposure require separate controls.
5. **Community knowledge:** concentrated burden, standing, consent, contested
   interpretation and exclusion may resist numeric aggregation.
6. **Infrastructure migration:** technically reversible changes may still have broad
   dependency, outage, credential and recovery consequences.
7. **Small open-source project:** a large review structure may exclude contributors
   and consume the project without materially improving assurance.
8. **Irreversible archival deletion:** low implementation complexity does not offset
   irreversibility, broad affected horizon or absent recovery.
9. **Mandated regulated method:** Package C cannot waive the mandate, but may expose
   duplicate controls, residual gaps and governance-caused risks.
10. **Automated high-volume operation:** individually small effects may accumulate,
    scale, distribute or become difficult to detect and correct.

The suite passes only if the profile changes the questions and candidate controls
without pretending to determine the locally competent answer.

## 26. Escalation and de-escalation test suite

| Case | Required Package C behavior |
| --- | --- |
| sandbox trial becomes routinely relied upon | require reassessment; “trial” label does not preserve low exposure |
| scope narrows to synthetic data and reversible outputs | permit evidence-bearing de-escalation while preserving applicable duties |
| one severe non-compensable condition emerges | prevent favorable averages from erasing it |
| monitoring repeatedly shows a control detects nothing because it observes the wrong object | compare redesign or removal; do not count activity as assurance |
| no incident has yet occurred | preserve uncertainty; absence of signal is not proof of adequacy |
| reviewer queue causes evidence to expire | treat delay and lost Evidence as governance-caused consequences |

| independent reviewers share the same hidden dependency | reject formal reviewer count as actual independence |
| controls become cheaper through automation | reassess semantic coverage; lower direct cost does not prove adequate assurance |

## 27. Under-governance counterexamples

1. A public-data prototype begins influencing clinical decisions without a changed
   release or consequence assessment.
2. A low likelihood estimate hides irreversible harm and weak detectability.
3. A Domain expert approves a vocabulary without access to rejected candidates or
   uncertainty.
4. An automated test passes the schema while accepted medical meaning is mapped
   incorrectly.
5. A pilot uses protected data and production credentials despite limited output
   distribution.
6. A project splits one cumulative exposure into transactions below a review
   threshold.
7. An organization transfers remediation cost to patients, contributors or a later
   team outside the project boundary.
8. Urgency is declared by the same party whose schedule caused the emergency.
9. A compliance certificate substitutes for Context-specific applicability.
10. A reversible code change creates an irreversible public or legal consequence.

## 28. Over-governance and governance-misfit counterexamples

1. A typo correction waits for a full multi-role review and blocks more material
   work.
2. Three reviewers inspect the same summary without primary Evidence or practical
   refusal.
3. An exhaustive deterministic taxonomy is built before testing whether experts can
   use the properties.
4. A demand for complete certainty prevents a reversible comparison that would
   reduce uncertainty.
5. Retention “for audit” exposes protected information beyond its useful horizon.
6. A mandatory template forces false precision and hides unknowns in a numeric score.
7. Repeated approvals diffuse Responsibility until no one owns residual exposure.
8. A control blocks small contributors while adding no capability unavailable from
   a lighter alternative.
9. Review delay makes a security fix arrive after exploitation.
10. Teams optimize gate completion while the outcome-facing objective deteriorates.

## 31. Prohibited inferences — what v0.1 does not mean

1. Greater consequence does not automatically require more documents, gates or
   participants.
2. More governance activity does not by itself establish lower risk or better
   assurance.
3. Less governance activity does not by itself establish efficiency or
   proportionality.
4. Compliance does not by itself establish safety, semantic adequacy or successful
   outcome.
5. Absence of an incident does not by itself establish adequate assurance.
6. Presence of an incident does not by itself establish that every prior assurance
   decision was negligent or useless.
7. A pilot, prototype, sandbox, internal or read-only label does not by itself
   establish low consequence.
8. Low direct price, compute or labor does not by itself establish low total cost.
9. High governance cost does not by itself justify weaker control.
10. Severe uncertainty does not automatically require either maximum control or no
    action.
11. A quantitative score does not erase qualitative Evidence, distribution,
    unknowns or non-compensable conditions.
12. A low likelihood does not erase severe, irreversible or poorly detectable harm.
13. Reversibility of implementation does not establish reversibility of every
    external consequence.
14. Independent reviewer count does not establish actual independence, competence
    or substantive review.
15. Risk acceptance does not transfer burden, Responsibility or standing from
    affected subjects.
16. Urgency does not manufacture Authority or erase completion obligations.
17. De-escalation does not establish that the earlier control was irrational.
18. Escalation does not establish failure, blame or a required stop.
19. A non-compensable condition in one decision does not become a universal
    prohibition automatically.
20. Governance cost borne outside the project does not cease to be material.
21. An accepted assurance configuration does not establish truth, Value, outcome or
    compliance in operation.
22. Package C does not own Domain semantics, objective selection, method selection,
    role assignment, risk tolerance or external law.
23. Package C acceptance gives GMC-009–010 no independent universal force outside
    the exact accepted profile and creates no policy or standard.
24. Later classification eligibility does not open 2.4 or 2.6.
25. Package C is not the only or necessarily highest-priority remaining SymK
    improvement.

## 33. Reopening conditions

Reassess Package C if:

- it becomes a universal risk score, fixed assurance ladder or checklist;
- projects maximize documentation instead of comparing assurance effects;
- projects systematically understate risk or externalize governance cost;
- de-escalation becomes a convenience waiver;
- escalation becomes automatic punishment or blocks challenge;
- numerical aggregation erases affected distribution or non-compensable conditions;
- compact records fail to expose material uncertainty or authority;
- governance-cost analysis becomes an excuse to avoid applicable duty;
- Domain tests reveal that the factor set is misleading or materially incomplete;
- Package A or B interfaces create circular authority;
- Package D establishes incompatible observability or lifecycle semantics;
- a later constitutional, policy, method, standard or jurisdiction decision changes
  the profile's assumptions; or
- SPServices or another derived project produces material counterevidence.

This accepted profile does not:

- assess or accept risk for any real project;
- assign an assurance configuration, reviewer, approver or affected-subject role;
- establish compliance with law, regulation, ethics, professional duty or policy;
- accept an objective, select a method or issue a Trial or Delivery Release;
- authorize data access, system change, deployment, publication or reliance;
- open, complete or bypass 2.4, 2.6 or any evolution stage;
- revise DR-014, DR-016, accepted Package A, accepted Package B or `SYMK-GP-001`;
- create a schema, application, metric, threshold, certification or enforcement
  mechanism;
- select an SPServices method or accept/reject a medical-document property;
- accept Package D or GMC-011–014; or
- establish that the GMC set exhausts SymK improvement.
