# PLC_META — 1.2 Market Research Template

## Purpose

Provide a **standard structure** for market research in PLC_1 that:

- Maps the **problem space**, segments, and alternatives.
- Identifies **where the real gaps are**.
- Adds a **lightweight “go-to-market angle” lens** using the adoption curve  
  (vanguardists → early adopters → mainstream).
- Feeds directly into **1.6 Discovery Outcomes** (Go / No-Go / Internal-only / Consulting-first).

This is **not** a full GTM plan and **not** a TAM/SAM/SOM thesis.  
It’s a grounded view of **who we can realistically start with** and whether that makes sense for this idea and for you.

---

## Mandatory sections

Each project-specific `market_research.md` must cover at least:

### 1. Market context & problem space

- Where does this product sit in the broader landscape?
- What concrete pains / risks / inefficiencies are we addressing?
- How do people usually “solve it today” (processes, scripts, existing tools)?

**Goal:** anchor the idea in reality, not in buzzwords.

---

### 2. Target segments (macro → micro)

- **Macro segments** (e.g., MSPs, SaaS mid-market, enterprises, law firms, AWS partners, etc.).
- **Micro / niche segments** inside those (e.g., “MSPs offering DRaaS on AWS”,  
  “SaaS vendors under SOC 2 pressure”, “AWS consulting boutiques doing DR projects”).

For each relevant segment:

- Size / density (qualitative is fine).
- Regulatory / business pressure that makes this pain **non-optional**.
- How close this is to your **actual network and experience**.

---

### 3. Buyer personas (summary)

Condensed overview (details live in 1.3 Stakeholder Map):

- Strategic buyer (title(s)).
- Technical influencer(s).
- Risk / compliance / finance stakeholders.

For each, briefly:

- What **success** looks like to them.
- What they **fear** if DR/product fails.
- How this product concept could make them look **good**.

---

### 4. Alternatives & competitive landscape

Split into at least:

- **Platform-native options** (e.g., AWS-native).
- **Third-party tools / vendors**.
- **Manual / “good enough” solutions** (scripts, spreadsheets, ad-hoc consultants).

For each main alternative:

- What it does **well enough**.
- Where it **does not care** (gaps aligned with your idea).
- Any clear **lock-in** or switching friction.

The objective is not an exhaustive grid; it’s to see where you’d **actually pry** into reality.

---

### 5. Differentiation & gap thesis

Synthesize:

- What **capability** (not feature) you bring that the alternatives don’t.
- Why that matters to your chosen segments in the **next 1–3 years**, not in some sci-fi future.
- What has to be **true in the environment** (cloud trends, regulation, tooling) for this gap to stay real.

If this section is hand-wavy, that’s a signal for 1.6.

---

### 6. Segment attractiveness & prioritization

For each candidate segment:

- Attractiveness dimensions (qualitative is enough):
  - Pain intensity.
  - Budget / willingness to pay.
  - Ease of access (network, channels).
  - Fit with your current **skills and reputation**.
- A simple scoring or ranking:
  - “Primary beachhead segment(s)”
  - “Secondary segments (future waves)”
  - “Deprioritized for now”

The result should be **one or two concrete starting segments**, not a shopping list.

---

### 7. GTM Angle — Early Market Lens (required, but lightweight)

This is the new piece that connects 1.2 to 1.6.

#### 7.1 Adoption stage hypothesis

For the **primary starting segment(s)**, state explicitly:

- Are they more like:
  - **Vanguardists** (love crazy tools, tolerate rough edges)?
  - **Early adopters** (pragmatic but willing to be first if value is clear)?
  - **Early majority / mainstream** (need proof, references, stability)?
- What this implies for:
  - How polished the product must be at first.
  - How much **hand-holding / consulting** is expected.
  - Tolerance for “we’re still figuring it out”.

#### 7.2 First 10–20 real users

Answer concretely:

- “Who are the first 10–20 **real humans / organizations** that could say yes?”
- Are they:
  - Close to your **current network**?
  - Accessible through consulting, talks, OSS, partners, content, or something else?

If you can’t name realistic archetypes here, 1.6 should be suspicious.

#### 7.3 How we’d reach them (high-level)

In 5–10 lines, max:

- Primary **GTM motion**:
  - Consulting-led, partner-led, self-service SaaS, community/OSS first, etc.
- Why this motion fits your **current constraints**:
  - Solo vs team,
  - Time available,
  - Capital,
  - Reputation / brand.

No CAC, no funnels. Just: “How would we *actually* get in front of early adopters?”

#### 7.4 Feasibility for *this* architect / team

This is the kill-switch:

- Is this GTM angle **realistic for you**, not for some imaginary VC-backed startup?
- If not, mark explicitly:
  - “Great idea, but requires a different commercial vehicle (larger team, funding, or strong partner).”

This answer is allowed to kill the idea as a **product**, even if the market looks attractive in theory.

---

### 8. Risks & unknowns

List the top **3–5**:

- Structural risks (platform vendors moving in, regulation changes).
- GTM risks (hard-to-reach early adopters, dependence on a single channel).
- Knowledge gaps that prevent a solid decision in 1.6.

Mark which ones:

- Must be addressed **before** going into architecture,
- Can be revisited **later** during PLC_2.

---

### 9. Suggested machine view

Optionally, maintain a parallel `market_research.json` with:

- Segments and their scores,
- Main competitors and gaps,
- GTM angle summary (segment IDs, adoption stage, GTM motion, feasibility).

This allows future tools / AI agents to:

- Compare different products’ markets,
- Recommend reusable GTM patterns,
- Flag when a product has **no credible early segment**.

---

## Constraints

- Keep the whole market research doc in the **5–10 pages** range for a serious product.  
  Less is fine; more is probably overfitting.
- The **GTM Angle (section 7)** must stay **short and sharp**, not turn into a business plan.
- If you cannot write a convincing section 7 without hand-waving, that is **evidence** for 1.6, not a failure to “write better.”

---

## How 1.2 feeds 1.6

When PLC_1.6 evaluates:

- **Business size fit**,
- **Consulting leverage**,
- **Career leverage**,
- **Go / No-Go / Internal-only / Consulting-first**,

it should lean heavily on:

- Sections **2, 4, 6** (segments, alternatives, prioritization),
- And especially **7 (GTM Angle)** for:
  - “Is there a plausible early market?”  
  - “Is the GTM path realistic for this architect/team?”  
  - “Is this a product, a consulting accelerator, a career bet, or something we should kill now?”

That’s the whole point: use market research to **stop bad paths early**, not to justify every idea.
