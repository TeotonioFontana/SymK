## 1. Question

> What kind of problem is each tension, and what relationship may SymK legitimately have to it without either evading foreseeable consequences or claiming authority over an entire external field?

## 2. Problem classes

Each tension may occupy more than one class.

| Code | Class | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| C | Internal constitutional | Concerns coherence among SymK identity, purpose, jurisdiction, axioms, or constitutional authority |
| F | Foundational conceptual | Concerns universal meanings or distinctions necessary for SymK coherence |
| E | Engineering | Concerns design obligations, trade-offs, evaluation, implementation, or operational feedback |
| D | Domain/project | Requires domain Ontology, Logic, standards, professional practice, or project-specific judgment |
| O | Organizational | Depends on institutional strategy, authority, policy, resources, roles, or local practice |
| S | Empirical-social | Requires evidence about people, labor, institutions, markets, education, inequality, or social consequences |
| X | Externally governed | Depends materially on law, regulation, professional authority, ethics, science, or another field not owned by SymK |

Classification describes the problem. It does not decide which pole should prevail.

## 3. SymK relationship classes

| Code | Relationship | SymK responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| U | Universal internal jurisdiction | SymK must establish a universal governing commitment because its identity or coherence depends on it |
| M | Minimal foundational jurisdiction | SymK must define a boundary or distinction but must not claim the full surrounding field |
| P | Project-declaration jurisdiction | SymK requires projects to declare and govern the matter; substantive content remains project-owned |
| R | Consequence-review jurisdiction | SymK requires foreseeable effects, affected parties, feedback, and challenge to remain visible |
| I | Imported constraint | A legitimate external authority supplies substantive requirements that SymK or a project must trace and respect |
| V | Empirical validation dependency | No stable position is justified without observational, comparative, or operational evidence |
| L | Local or outside universal scope | The ultimate choice belongs to a domain, organization, deployment, profession, or society rather than universal SymK |

A tension may combine these relationships. For example, SymK may hold minimal foundational and consequence-review jurisdiction while substantive policy remains local and externally constrained.

## 9. Jurisdiction distribution

The classification yields five broad groups.

- TN-001, TN-007, TN-009, TN-014 through TN-017, TN-020 through TN-023, TN-026, and TN-028 through TN-029.

## 10. Boundary findings

### 10.1 Foreseeability creates review responsibility, not total jurisdiction

When SymK-derived systems foreseeably affect labor, formation, access, dependency, power, or harm, SymK cannot declare the consequences irrelevant. It can require review, evidence, affected-party visibility, and challenge without claiming authority to dictate universal policy.

### 10.2 Acknowledgment is weaker than doctrine but stronger than silence

For empirical-social tensions such as displacement and selection, SymK's appropriate relationship may be to require explicit assumptions, consequence monitoring, and project or organizational decisions under imported authority. This is a real obligation even when SymK does not choose the social outcome.

### 10.3 External authority does not remove internal responsibility

Compliance with law or professional rules does not prove epistemic or ethical adequacy. Conversely, SymK compatibility does not authorize violation of applicable external authority.

### 10.4 Multiple classifications do not imply multiple artifact owners

A tension may cross conceptual, engineering, organizational, and external fields. The owning layer of each resulting artifact must still be declared according to `SYMK-2X-DR-005`.

## 11. Dossier consequences

The candidate dossier clusters remain justified, but their scope is now bounded:

1. **Professional transformation:** SymK review and formation obligations; labor policy and distributive remedies remain empirical, local, and external.
2. **Value under responsibility:** SymK must reject unconditional optimization; domain thresholds and safeguards require project and imported authority.
3. **Authority and legitimacy:** SymK governs distinctions and challengeability; legal, professional, institutional, and social legitimacy are not reducible to SymK.
4. **Shared Knowledge and power:** SymK governs semantic and consequence visibility; access, privacy, and forgetting require project and external authority.
5. **Inheritance and external authority:** SymK governs compatibility and lineage; external authorities retain practical jurisdiction over action.

## 12. Accepted 2.0.5.2 conclusion

> **The thirty tensions do not belong to one authority or one kind of inquiry. SymK has universal or minimal jurisdiction where its own coherence, epistemic commitments, responsibility, or compatibility would otherwise fail. It has project-declaration and consequence-review jurisdiction where derived systems must make choices and expose foreseeable effects. It imports law, professional standards, ethics, and other legitimate external constraints without claiming ownership of them. Empirical-social and organizational outcomes remain outside universal SymK doctrine except for the duties of explicitness, evidence, affected-party visibility, feedback, and governed challenge.**

This classification constrains the minimum dispositions considered in 2.0.5.3.
