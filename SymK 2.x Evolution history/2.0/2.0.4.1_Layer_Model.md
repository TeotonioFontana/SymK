# 2.0.4.1 — Layer Model

**Status:** Substep-Accepted  
**Parent work package:** `2.0.4 — Layer-Boundary Analysis`  
**Decision under preparation:** `SYMK-2X-DR-005`
**Accepted by/date:** Project steward, 2026-08-18

## 1. Question

> Which layers are necessary to distinguish SymK authority, derived-project authority, engineering decisions, implementation choices, and operational authority without treating them as one simple hierarchy?

## 2. Finding: one ladder is insufficient

The current proposed normative hierarchy correctly separates Constitution, foundational concepts, project Constitution, Engineering Charter, standards, decisions, and implementation. It nevertheless combines three different relationships:

1. **jurisdictional reach** — universal SymK commitments versus domain and local commitments;
2. **realization depth** — meaning versus design, implementation, and operation;
3. **authority kind** — constitutional, semantic, design, technical, legal, institutional, or evidentiary authority.

These relationships often point in the same direction, but not always. A local law may prevent an operational deployment even though it has no authority to redefine the SymK concept of Knowledge. An implementation may expose evidence that defeats a foundational assumption even though implementation does not possess semantic authority. A SymK standard may govern several projects without becoming a constitutional commitment.

The layer model must therefore use two axes and recognize cross-cutting authorities.

## 3. Axis A — Jurisdictional reach

| Reach | Governing question | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Universal SymK | What must hold across every SymK-compatible project? | Constitutional commitments; universal concept invariants |
| Derived domain | What must hold for a particular domain project? | LexBrain legal ontology; sshConnectivity connectivity concepts |
| Product or system | What must hold for a particular engineered system? | Composer obligations; LibScan interfaces; one diagnostic service |
| Organization | What must hold for an adopting institution? | Approval policy; access authority; risk tolerance; professional roles |
| Deployment | What must hold in this concrete operating context? | Applicable jurisdiction; configured controls; retained data; current operators |

Movement down this axis narrows scope. Narrower layers may add or strengthen commitments within their jurisdiction. They do not acquire authority to redefine broader commitments silently.

## 4. Axis B — Realization depth

| Depth | Governing question | Characteristic artifacts |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutional | What must remain true for identity and compatibility? | Constitution; jurisdiction articles; ratified axioms |
| Conceptual and semantic | What does a governed concept mean, and which distinctions must be preserved? | Concept specifications; principles; vocabularies; semantic models |
| Engineering governance | Which design obligations translate the governing commitments? | Engineering Charters; binding standards; quality and assurance obligations |
| Specification and architecture | Which structures and behaviors are selected, and why? | Requirements; capability specifications; protocols; models; ADRs; reference architectures |
| Implementation | How is the selected design concretely realized? | Code; schemas; prompts; rules; models; pipelines; infrastructure |
| Operation | How is the realization authorized, configured, used, monitored, and changed here? | Deployment configuration; operating procedures; access decisions; local records |

Movement down this axis increases concreteness. A lower realization may preserve, specialize, strengthen, or operationalize a higher commitment. It may not silently supply missing philosophical or domain meaning.

## 5. The combined authority map

The two axes yield the following principal layers for SymK governance.

| ID | Principal layer | Primary authority | Typical reach | Must not silently do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S0 | Epistemic sources and inquiry | Evidentiary and critical | Any | Acquire normative force merely through publication or prestige |
| S1 | SymK Constitution | Universal constitutional | Universal SymK | Dictate complete domain ontology, product design, or implementation |
| S2 | SymK governed conceptual and engineering corpus | Universal semantic and methodological, according to declared status | Universal or cross-project | Claim constitutional force without selection and ratification; import domain contingencies as universals |
| P1 | Derived-project Constitution | Domain constitutional | Derived domain | Contradict or redefine inherited SymK commitments silently |
| P2 | Derived-project governed conceptual corpus | Domain semantic and methodological | Derived domain | Allow product schemas or local practice to define domain meaning accidentally |
| E1 | Project Engineering Charter and binding project standards | Engineering-governance | Project or system | Create philosophical or domain commitments merely as design preferences |
| E2 | Specification and architecture | Design and decisional | Product or system | Treat selected structures as the only legitimate realization of the governing concepts |
| I1 | Implementation | Realization and technical execution | Concrete system | Acquire semantic or constitutional authority because it runs or is widely deployed |
| O1 | Organization and deployment | Local authorization and operation | Organization or deployment | Universalize local policy, law, risk tolerance, or practice |

### 5.1 Why S0 is shown but not ranked

Foundation papers, research, professional experience, operational observations, and challenges are epistemic sources. They can support or defeat decisions at any layer, but they do not command another layer merely by existing. Their influence must pass through the governance process of the layer being changed.

S0 is therefore part of the model but not a superior normative level.

### 5.2 Why S2 and P2 are necessary

A Constitution cannot contain every mature concept, principle, method, and semantic distinction without becoming unstable and unreadable. Conversely, those artifacts must not gain constitutional status merely by being called foundational.

The same distinction is required inside derived projects. A project Constitution establishes domain identity and binding commitments; a project conceptual corpus develops the domain Ontology, Logic, concepts, and methods beneath those commitments.

### 5.3 Why E1 and E2 are separate

An Engineering Charter states durable design obligations. Architecture and specifications select contextual solutions under those obligations. Without this separation, every architectural choice risks becoming a project-wide rule, while every charter risks degenerating into a collection of current implementation decisions.

### 5.4 Why operation is not merely implementation

The same implementation can be deployed under different laws, institutions, authorities, configurations, populations, and stakes. Operational authorization therefore belongs to a distinct layer. Successful execution does not itself establish legitimate use.

## 6. Cross-cutting authorities

Some forces are not SymK layers and must not be inserted into the hierarchy as though SymK governed them.

| Cross-cutting authority | How it enters | What it can do | What it does not do automatically |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reality and observed consequence | Evidence returned from research, implementation, and operation | Challenge any claim or design | Ratify a replacement commitment without governance |
| Law and regulation | Imported constraint at project, organization, or deployment scope | Prohibit, require, or condition action | Define universal SymK concepts |
| Professional standards and institutions | Imported authority declared by the derived project | Establish competence, reliance, and responsibility requirements | Become universal across unrelated domains |
| Ethical and social constraints | Internal minimum plus imported or locally governed duties | Constrain purpose, design, and use | Supply one complete universal moral theory |
| Technology and physical possibility | Feasibility evidence and implementation constraint | Limit realizable designs | Determine what ought to be built or what a concept means |

An imported authority may have decisive practical precedence within its jurisdiction. This is not semantic precedence over SymK.

## 7. Four directions of movement

The model recognizes four different movements that later substeps must define more precisely.

### 7.1 Downward constraint

Higher or broader commitments constrain more concrete and narrower realizations.

```text
SymK Constitution
    → SymK concepts
    → project Constitution and concepts
    → Engineering Charter
    → architecture
    → implementation
    → operation
```

### 7.2 Downward enrichment

Narrower layers add domain, product, organizational, and deployment meaning that the broader layer neither contains nor pretends to contain.

### 7.3 Upward evidence

Research, domain work, implementation, and operation return evidence that may confirm, qualify, or challenge any broader or more abstract layer.

### 7.4 Lateral import

Law, professional standards, scientific knowledge, organizational policy, and technical standards enter at the layer and scope where their authority applies. Their source and jurisdiction must remain traceable.

## 8. Precedence is jurisdiction-qualified

The model rejects a simplistic rule that the higher numbered or more abstract artifact always wins.

The correct rule is:

> A governing commitment has precedence over a realization only within its declared jurisdiction. A narrower authority may impose stronger local constraints without redefining broader meaning. When two applicable authorities conflict, the conflict must be classified by jurisdiction, authority kind, and scope before precedence is decided.

Examples:

- A LexBrain ontology may add `LegalJurisdiction` without asking SymK to define Brazilian law.
- A court policy may prohibit use of a technically valid Composer feature without making the feature conceptually non-SymK.
- Evidence that a SymK invariant causes systematic epistemic harm may challenge the invariant but does not silently amend it.
- A database schema may require one identifier representation without defining the universal SymK concept of Identity.

## 9. Layer identification test

To locate a commitment provisionally, ask:

1. **Reach:** Must it hold universally, for one domain, one product, one organization, or one deployment?
2. **Kind:** Is it constitutional, semantic, methodological, design, realization, operational, or evidentiary?
3. **Necessity:** What fails if the commitment is absent at that level?
4. **Source:** Is its authority internal to SymK, inherited, imported, or local?
5. **Change:** At what cadence may it safely evolve?
6. **Challenge:** Which evidence can contest it, and which governance process may change it?

This is an initial identification test. The complete placement test belongs to `2.0.4.5`.

## 10. Compatibility test for the model

The layer model is adequate only if it can explain all of the following without contradiction:

- SymK defines universal epistemic commitments without defining Brazilian legal ontology.
- LexBrain defines legal concepts without redefining SymK Knowledge.
- Composer and LibScan share LexBrain concepts without being forced into identical architectures.
- an organization can impose stricter privacy or authorization rules without changing the project Constitution;
- an implementation can vary without changing conceptual identity;
- operational failure can challenge an architectural decision or a SymK commitment through evidence;
- an external legal command can control deployment without becoming a SymK axiom.

The current model passes these cases provisionally. It remains subject to review during ownership, relationship, conflict, and placement analysis.

## 11. Accepted 2.0.4.1 conclusion

> **SymK governance is not a single document ladder. It is a jurisdiction-qualified layer model operating across two axes: reach, from universal SymK to concrete deployment; and realization depth, from constitutional meaning to operation. Each layer possesses a distinct kind of authority. Broader and more abstract commitments constrain compatible lower realizations within their jurisdiction; narrower layers enrich rather than silently redefine them. Evidence travels upward, while external authorities enter laterally at their applicable scope.**

This conclusion is accepted as the layer-model component under preparation for `SYMK-2X-DR-005`. It does not yet decide the complete ownership matrix or interlayer relationship vocabulary.
