# 2.0.5.3 — Minimum SymK Disposition

**Status:** Substep-Accepted  
**Parent work package:** `2.0.5 — Governing Tension Register`  
**Depends on:** Substep-Accepted `2.0.5.1` and `2.0.5.2`  
**Decision under preparation:** `SYMK-2X-DR-006`
**Accepted by/date:** Project steward, 2026-08-18

## 1. Question

> What is the least SymK must require for each registered tension to remain governable without pretending to resolve questions outside SymK's jurisdiction?

## 2. Disposition types

| Code | Minimum disposition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| G | Universal guardrail | SymK establishes a limit that no compatible project may silently cross |
| M | Minimal distinction | SymK preserves concepts or poles that must not be collapsed, without supplying a complete theory |
| D | Project declaration | Each affected project states its scoped balance, standards, authority, and known consequences |
| R | Consequence review | Foreseeable effects, affected parties, feedback, and challenge must remain visible |
| A | External-authority trace | Applicable law, professional standards, ethics, science, or institutional authority must be identified and respected |
| V | Empirical validation | Claims must remain provisional until supported by appropriate observational or operational evidence |
| N | Non-universalization | SymK explicitly refuses to turn a domain, organizational, or social choice into universal doctrine |
| T | Preserved tension | Both poles remain visible because premature resolution would conceal a material cost or uncertainty |

A tension may require several dispositions. These codes identify minimum obligations, not final document forms or implementation protocols.

## 3. Universal disposition rules

The following rules apply across the complete register.

### 3.1 No optimization by erasure

No pole may be eliminated merely because the other is easier to measure, automate, formalize, standardize, or monetize.

### 3.2 No doctrine by consequence alone

Foreseeable consequences create duties of review, evidence, affected-party visibility, and challenge. They do not automatically authorize SymK to prescribe universal social or organizational policy.

### 3.3 No deferral by silence

When SymK lacks substantive jurisdiction, it must still identify the responsible project, organization, profession, external authority, or empirical inquiry where the decision belongs.

### 3.4 No compatibility without disclosure

A material unresolved tension that affects a compatibility claim, design obligation, authorization, or foreseeable consequence must remain visible at the relevant layer.

### 3.5 No finality without evidence

Operational success, failure, and social consequence may mature or challenge every disposition. Stage acceptance does not convert a tension into a permanently settled balance.

## 9. Social-question restraint

The dispositions for TN-014, TN-015, TN-016, TN-023, and TN-029 deliberately stop short of universal social prescriptions.

This restraint is not indifference. SymK requires:

- explicit assumptions;
- identifiable affected parties;
- foreseeable-consequence analysis;
- evidence rather than convenient narratives;
- legitimate decision authority;
- challenge and revision paths;
- and traceable imported law, ethics, and professional standards.

SymK does not thereby decide:

- how many professionals an economy should employ;
- whether all intermediate roles must be preserved;
- how opportunity should be distributed;
- which learners deserve concentrated investment;
- or what compensation society owes for technological displacement.

Those questions remain available for ethical, political, legal, economic, educational, organizational, and empirical deliberation.

## 10. Disposition sufficiency tests

### 10.1 Anti-evasion test

Could a project claim that a foreseeable consequence is outside SymK and therefore need not be examined?

**Result:** No. R requires consequence visibility even when substantive policy is external.

### 10.2 Anti-imperialism test

Could SymK use consequence review to prescribe universal employment or education policy?

**Result:** No. N, A, V, and the accepted jurisdiction boundary prevent that expansion.

### 10.3 Operational-value test

Can a project derive actionable obligations from the dispositions?

**Result:** Yes at the minimum level: declare scope and trade-offs, identify authority and affected parties, preserve evidence and challenge, and import applicable constraints. Exact methods remain later work.

### 10.4 Compatibility test

Can two projects choose different legitimate balances?

**Result:** Yes, when each preserves universal guardrails, governs domain choices, respects external authority, and declares consequences and incompatibilities.

### 10.5 Maturation test

Can evidence from daily use reopen a disposition?

**Result:** Yes. V and R connect the register to operational maturation under `SYMK-2X-DR-005`.

## 11. Candidate constitutional consequences

The dispositions suggest—but do not yet ratify—the following constitutional directions:

1. optimization outcomes remain jointly constrained rather than independently maximized;
2. affected parties and foreseeable consequences cannot be omitted from responsible cooperation;
3. authority, capability, truth, acceptance, and responsibility remain distinct;
4. challenge is part of durable governance;
5. derived projects must declare domain balances and imported authorities;
6. operational evidence may challenge every layer;
7. social and organizational choices are not universalized merely because SymK-derived systems influence them.

Whether these directions become axioms, jurisdiction articles, principles, project duties, or evaluation gates belongs to `2.2`, `2.3`, `2.5`, and `2.6`.

## 12. Accepted 2.0.5.3 conclusion

> **SymK's minimum response to a material tension is governed explicitness proportional to its jurisdiction: universal guardrails where SymK coherence would otherwise fail; minimal distinctions where concepts must not collapse; project declarations where domains must choose; consequence review where systems foreseeably affect participants or society; traceable import of legitimate external authority; empirical validation where outcomes remain uncertain; explicit non-universalization where policy belongs elsewhere; and preservation of residual tension until evidence and legitimate authority support a mature balance.**

These dispositions bound the complex dossiers in 2.0.5.4 and the final `SYMK-2X-DR-006` proposal.
