# 2.0.4.4 — Conflict and Feedback Direction

**Status:** Substep-Accepted  
**Parent work package:** `2.0.4 — Layer-Boundary Analysis`  
**Depends on:** Substep-Accepted `2.0.4.1` through `2.0.4.3`  
**Decision under preparation:** `SYMK-2X-DR-005`
**Accepted by/date:** Project steward, 2026-08-18

## 1. Question

> When commitments, meanings, designs, implementations, local decisions, evidence, or external authorities appear incompatible, where does the conflict belong and in which directions may constraint, evidence, and change travel?

## 2. Scope

This substep defines:

- categories of apparent and substantive conflict;
- the direction in which a conflict must be routed;
- which layer may change which artifact;
- how operational and domain evidence can challenge higher layers;
- how external authority constrains a project without becoming SymK doctrine;
- and the minimum compatibility consequence of unresolved conflict.

It does not define ratification bodies, voting, deadlines, exception approval procedures, appeal mechanics, or the final compatibility-status vocabulary. Those mechanisms belong to `2.3` and `2.6`.

## 3. Conflict is not every difference

The following are normally compatible when explicit and correctly placed:

- instantiation;
- specialization preserving parent meaning;
- local strengthening;
- domain extension outside the parent's jurisdiction;
- alternative projection preserving applicable obligations;
- alternative implementation satisfying the same specification;
- stricter local policy within legitimate organizational authority.

A difference becomes a conflict when two applicable commitments, meanings, authorities, or realizations cannot be honored together within overlapping scope, or when one layer assumes authority belonging to another.

## 4. Conflict classes

| Class | Meaning | Initial direction |
|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary conflict | The same term carries materially different meanings, or different terms conceal the same meaning | Return to the semantic owners at S2 or P2; clarify mappings and scopes |
| Scope conflict | Statements appear incompatible because domain, time, population, task, stakes, or jurisdiction are not aligned | Return to the owners of both statements; normalize scope before asserting contradiction |
| Version conflict | Artifacts rely on different parent or dependency versions | Route to the adopting and owning layers for migration or compatibility review |
| Projection conflict | A representation omits, strengthens, distorts, or contradicts its source | Route first to the projection owner; challenge the source only if the loss reveals a source defect |
| Jurisdiction conflict | A layer governs a decision outside its legitimate reach or authority kind | Route to the owners of the affected boundaries; remove or explicitly reclassify the overreach |
| Contradiction | Applicable propositions cannot both hold in overlapping scope | Each owner governs its own artifact; compatibility remains unresolved until one scope or commitment changes |
| Weakening or bypass | A lower layer evades an applicable inherited commitment while claiming compatibility | Route downward for correction or upward as an explicit challenge; no silent exception |
| External-authority collision | SymK or project commitments conflict with applicable law, professional duty, institutional authority, or another legitimate external constraint | Restrict or suspend affected use locally; route project/SymK implications to their respective owners |
| Operational invalidation | Observed behavior, failure, or consequence defeats an assumption, requirement, or claimed capability | Contain risk locally; challenge the nearest adequate owning layer with evidence |
| Responsibility conflict | Capability, causation, authorization, accountability, and consequence-bearing are allocated incompatibly | Route to project, Engineering Charter, and organizational owners according to each authority |

## 5. The nearest-adequate-owner rule

> **A conflict should first be examined at the nearest layer that both owns the contested decision and is capable of correcting the demonstrated cause. It must move upward when the evidence challenges a parent commitment or recurs beyond the narrower layer's jurisdiction.**

This avoids two opposite errors:

- turning every implementation defect into a constitutional crisis;
- trapping systemic counterevidence inside implementation or operations to protect higher assumptions.

Examples:

- A parser defect belongs initially to I1.
- Repeated parser defects caused by an ambiguous interface contract challenge E2.
- Repeated cross-implementation failures caused by an Engineering Charter obligation challenge E1.
- Cross-domain evidence that a universal semantic distinction is incoherent challenges S2.
- Evidence that a universal constitutional commitment causes unavoidable incompatibility or harm challenges S1.

## 6. Ownership limits during conflict

No layer may amend an artifact owned by another layer merely because the conflict affects it.

- I1 may patch code but not rewrite E2 requirements silently.
- E2 may revise architecture but not redefine a P2 domain concept silently.
- P2 may revise a domain concept but not amend S2 universal meaning silently.
- P1 may suspend its compatibility claim but not amend S1.
- O1 may prohibit or condition local use but not rewrite the project Constitution.
- S1 or S2 may revise their own artifacts through governance but may not erase domain evidence to preserve authority.

Where two owners must change, each change occurs under its own authority with a shared conflict record and explicit dependency ordering.

## 7. Direction rules

### 7.1 Downward constraint

Accepted higher or broader commitments constrain lower realizations within their jurisdiction. A lower layer must comply, narrow its claim, declare a challenge, or state incompatibility.

Downward precedence is not infallibility. A credible challenge may require caution, containment, or suspension while review occurs.

### 7.2 Downward impact after change

When an owning layer changes an artifact, dependent layers receive an impact obligation, not an automatic rewrite.

Each dependent owner determines whether to:

- adopt the new version;
- migrate with changes;
- remain temporarily on the prior version;
- declare an exception or incompatibility;
- or challenge the change.

Exact migration mechanics belong to later governance stages.

### 7.3 Upward evidence

Evidence may originate at S0, P2, E1, E2, I1, O1, or from affected external parties. It may challenge any layer whose commitment explains or materially contributes to the observed issue.

Evidence has epistemic force, not automatic amendment authority.

### 7.4 Lateral import

External law, professional standards, scientific findings, technical limits, and institutional decisions enter at their applicable scope. They may constrain action decisively without becoming concepts or axioms owned by SymK.

### 7.5 Lateral coordination

Peer projects or sibling components may exchange evidence, mappings, and compatible artifacts. Neither acquires authority over the other merely through reuse. Shared parent implications are returned to the parent owner.

## 8. Minimum feedback packet

For evidence to move beyond its originating layer, it must preserve enough context to be evaluated. At minimum it should identify:

- originating system, project, organization, or inquiry;
- observed event, result, or consequence;
- affected artifact, statement, decision, and version;
- relevant domain, time, population, task, stakes, and environment;
- provenance and method of observation;
- expected behavior or governing assumption;
- actual behavior and material difference;
- affected participants and consequences;
- plausible alternative explanations;
- reproducibility or recurrence evidence where available;
- immediate containment already taken;
- and the layer believed capable of correction.

This is an information requirement, not the final challenge-record schema.

## 9. Feedback strength

Not every observation justifies the same upward movement.

| Evidence pattern | Default implication |
|---|---|
| Isolated implementation error | Review I1; no automatic architectural or conceptual consequence |
| Recurrent error across one implementation family | Review E2 assumptions and specifications |
| Recurrent failure across independent implementations of one project | Review E1, P2, or P1 according to cause |
| Recurrent failure across different SymK-derived domains | Review S2 and possibly S1 |
| Single severe harm with a credible causal path | Immediate containment and direct challenge to every plausibly causal owning layer; recurrence is not required |
| Successful operation | Supporting evidence within scope, never proof of universal validity |
| Absence of observed failure | Weak evidence unless observation coverage and detection capacity are demonstrated |

Severity, causal credibility, and affected rights may justify direct escalation even without repetition.

## 10. Compatibility during unresolved conflict

The minimum rule is:

> **A material unresolved conflict must be visible in every affected compatibility claim. Disclosure is necessary but does not itself make the conflict compatible.**

Pending later status design, an affected artifact or project must not present unqualified compatibility when it:

- knowingly contradicts an applicable inherited commitment;
- cannot demonstrate preservation of a material parent meaning;
- operates under an unresolved exception;
- or depends on a challenged commitment whose failure would make the compatibility claim materially misleading.

Safe inquiry and unaffected operations may continue when their independence from the conflict is stated and justified.

## 11. External authority and operational precedence

An external authority can have decisive precedence over action without possessing semantic precedence over SymK.

For example:

- a court may prohibit an AI-assisted procedure in one proceeding;
- privacy law may forbid a data flow permitted by the reference architecture;
- a professional regulator may require human authorization for a class of decisions;
- a safety standard may require stronger controls than a project baseline.

The operational response is to comply, restrict, redesign, or decline deployment within the authority's jurisdiction. The conceptual response is separate: determine whether the external constraint exposes a local requirement, a domain principle, or evidence that challenges SymK itself.

## 12. Conflict patterns across the lineage

### 12.1 Child conflicts with parent

The child corrects its statement, narrows its compatibility claim, or raises an evidenced challenge. It does not silently reinterpret the parent.

### 12.2 Parent changes after child adoption

The child receives an impact review obligation. It does not silently inherit the new version and is not silently made incompatible retroactively.

### 12.3 Sibling projects disagree

Domain-specific difference is permitted. A parent conflict exists only when both claim incompatible interpretations of the same inherited commitment within overlapping scope.

### 12.4 Organization conflicts with project design

The organization may decline or constrain adoption. If it changes the design, the result must be represented as a governed local variant with an explicit compatibility impact.

### 12.5 Implementation contradicts documentation

The discrepancy is a conformance conflict, not an invitation to let running code become the source of truth automatically. The owning layers determine whether implementation or specification changes.

## 13. Anti-suppression and anti-inflation rules

### Anti-suppression

No layer may conceal, downgrade, or localize evidence merely to protect the authority, reputation, or stability of a higher artifact.

### Anti-inflation

No participant may present an isolated defect or disagreement as a universal refutation without evidence connecting it to the higher commitment.

Together these rules preserve both epistemic humility and controlled evolution.

## 14. Direction test

When a conflict appears, ask:

1. What are the exact statements, behaviors, or authorities in tension?
2. Do their scopes, versions, terms, and jurisdictions actually overlap?
3. Is the relationship a permitted specialization, strengthening, extension, or alternative projection rather than conflict?
4. Which layer owns each contested artifact?
5. What is the nearest layer capable of correcting the demonstrated cause?
6. Does the evidence implicate a parent commitment or only a realization?
7. Is immediate local containment required regardless of eventual conceptual resolution?
8. Which compatibility claims become qualified while the conflict remains unresolved?
9. Which dependent layers require impact notice if a governing artifact changes?

## 15. Accepted 2.0.4.4 conclusion

> **Conflict and feedback in SymK are jurisdiction-directed rather than governed by blind hierarchical precedence. Differences must first be tested for vocabulary, scope, version, projection, and jurisdiction before being classified as substantive contradictions. Conflicts are routed to the nearest adequate owner, while evidence must move upward whenever it challenges a parent commitment or exceeds the narrower layer's jurisdiction. Each layer may change only the artifacts it owns; external authorities may control action within their jurisdiction without redefining SymK; and material unresolved conflicts must remain visible in affected compatibility claims.**

This conclusion is accepted as the conflict-and-feedback component under preparation for `SYMK-2X-DR-005`.
