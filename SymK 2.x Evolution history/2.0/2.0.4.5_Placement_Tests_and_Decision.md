## 1. Purpose

Consolidate the accepted layer, ownership, relationship, conflict, and feedback models; apply them to representative SymK and derived-project cases; and determine whether they are sufficient to support a proposed layer-boundary decision.

This substep does not design the later governance or engineering mechanisms assigned to `2.3`, `2.5`, `2.6`, `2.7`, or `2.9`.

## 2. Consolidated model

### 2.1 Principal layers

| ID | Layer | Primary authority |
|---|---|---|
| S0 | Epistemic sources and inquiry | Evidence, criticism, alternatives, and candidate conclusions; non-normative until governed selection |
| S1 | SymK Constitution | Universal constitutional identity, purpose, jurisdiction, and compatibility commitments |
| S2 | SymK governed conceptual and engineering corpus | Universal semantic and methodological authority according to declared artifact status |
| P1 | Derived-project Constitution | Domain constitutional identity, purpose, boundary, and axioms |
| P2 | Derived-project governed conceptual corpus | Domain Ontology, Logic, concepts, methods, and product-level specializations |
| E1 | Engineering Charter and binding project standards | Durable project engineering obligations |
| E2 | Specification and architecture | Contextual design, formal projections, capability contracts, interfaces, and decisions |
| I1 | Implementation | Concrete technical realization and execution evidence |
| O1 | Organization and deployment | Local authorization, policy, configuration, practice, and operation |

### 2.2 Two axes

- **Jurisdictional reach:** universal SymK → derived domain → product/system → organization → deployment.
- **Realization depth:** constitutional → conceptual/semantic → engineering governance → specification/architecture → implementation → operation.

### 2.3 Cross-cutting authorities

Reality, observed consequences, law, regulation, professional standards, scientific findings, ethics, and physical or technological limits enter at their applicable scope. They may constrain action or challenge SymK without becoming artifacts owned by SymK.

### 2.4 Interlayer relationships

Inheritance, specialization, instantiation, composition, role adoption, projection, implementation, local adoption, and challenge are distinct and noninterchangeable.

### 2.5 Direction rule

Constraint moves downward within jurisdiction; narrower layers enrich meaning and design; evidence moves upward; external authority enters laterally; each layer changes only artifacts it owns.

## 3. Placement tests

### Test 1 — Constitutional identity

**Candidate:** “SymK exists to make the engineering of Domain Intelligence Amplifiers systematic, governable, reusable, and evolvable.”

**Placement:** S1.

**Reason:** It states universal purpose and identity conditions for SymK compatibility.

**Result:** Pass. The model distinguishes constitutional purpose from methods, products, and implementations.

### Test 2 — Foundation Paper

**Candidate:** The Knowledge Foundation Paper, including its philosophical survey, working definition, candidate commitments, and open questions.

**Placement:** S0 as a governed epistemic source.

**Reason:** It preserves inquiry and supports constitutional or conceptual selection but cannot ratify its own conclusions.

**Result:** Pass. “Foundation” no longer silently means constitutional authority.

### Test 3 — Mature SymK concept

**Candidate:** The future canonical SymK concept of Knowledge.

**Placement:** S2 under S1 constraints.

**Reason:** Its universal meaning and semantic distinctions must remain reusable across projects without expanding the Constitution into a complete ontology.

**Result:** Pass. The model preserves both semantic authority and constitutional restraint.

### Test 4 — Human and machine concept projections

**Candidate:** Human-semantic and machine-semantic expressions of a mature SymK concept.

**Placement:** S2 only when governed as projections within the S2 concept package; otherwise the projection belongs to the layer that owns its purpose and use.

**Reason:** Format does not determine authority. The concept package question and exact alignment mechanism remain deferred to `2.1/2.5`.

**Result:** Pass with deferred mechanism.

### Test 5 — Knowledge Object

**Candidate:** A universal conceptual inventory called `KnowledgeObject`.

**Placement:** S2 if explicitly governed as a reference model or conceptual projection; not automatically a mandatory schema.

**Derived placements:** A LexBrain legal specialization belongs to P2; a Composer record contract to E2; database tables and classes to I1.

**Result:** Pass. Conceptual authority, domain specialization, contract, and implementation remain distinct.

### Test 6 — A5 disposition

**Candidate:** Philosophical Jurisdiction currently expressed as A5.

**Placement:** S1 as a Constitutional Jurisdiction Article rather than a fundamental axiom, according to Stage-Accepted `SYMK-2X-DR-004`.

**Result:** Pass. Artifact classification follows governing function rather than historical label.

### Test 7 — Domain ontology

**Candidate:** LexBrain concepts of legal authority, precedent, jurisdiction, procedural posture, and legal validity.

**Placement:** P2 under P1 and inherited S1/S2 constraints.

**Reason:** These concepts belong to the legal domain and must not contaminate universal SymK semantics.

**Result:** Pass.

### Test 8 — Composer

**Candidate:** Composer as a LexBrain functionality.

**Placement:** Its product identity and domain-specific concepts belong to P2 at declared product scope; durable design obligations to E1; capabilities and architecture to E2; code and models to I1; office-specific authorization to O1.

**Reason:** Composer does not become a new top-level philosophy merely because it has a substantial specification.

**Result:** Pass. The model supports subproject layers without bypassing LexBrain.

### Test 9 — LibScan dependency

**Candidate:** Composer relies on LexBrain's LibScan capability.

**Placement:** Shared legal concepts remain P2; the capability contract and integration architecture are E2; implementations are I1.

**Relationship:** Composition and implementation dependency, not concept inheritance from the LibScan codebase.

**Result:** Pass.

### Test 10 — Formal ontology

**Candidate:** An OWL ontology expressing legal concepts.

**Placement:** E2 by default as a formal architectural projection; P2 only if expressly governed as a normative part of the domain concept corpus.

**Reason:** Formal precision and file format do not confer semantic authority.

**Result:** Pass. Formal-language selection remains deferred to `2.5/2.9`.

### Test 11 — Standards and reference models

**Candidate:** A coding standard, semantic standard, or reference architecture.

**Placement:** Determined by reach and governing decision: cross-project SymK standard at S2; binding project standard at E1; contextual architecture at E2; local procedure at O1.

**Result:** Pass. The model does not assign authority by the word “standard” or “reference.”

### Test 12 — Alternative implementation

**Candidate:** Two systems preserve the same project concepts and contracts using a relational database and a graph database respectively.

**Placement:** Both implementations belong to I1.

**Compatibility:** Architecture may differ without constitutional or semantic incompatibility unless a binding E1/E2 requirement is violated.

**Result:** Pass. The model does not universalize a preferred technology.

### Test 13 — Machine-learning model or prompt

**Candidate:** A trained model, prompt, retrieval configuration, or agent workflow.

**Placement:** E2 for its governed behavioral contract and architecture; I1 for concrete weights, prompt text, configuration, and runtime realization; O1 for authorized deployment settings.

**Result:** Pass. Model behavior does not define Knowledge, Intelligence, or legal authority by implementation accident.

### Test 14 — External legal constraint

**Candidate:** Applicable privacy law forbids a data flow permitted by a reference architecture.

**Placement:** External authority imported laterally at P1/P2, E1/E2, or O1 according to scope.

**Immediate effect:** Restrict, redesign, or decline the affected deployment.

**Conceptual effect:** Separate review determines whether the law creates a local constraint, domain principle, or evidence challenging SymK.

**Result:** Pass. Practical precedence is distinguished from semantic ownership.

### Test 15 — Organizational selection and labor policy

**Candidate:** An organization chooses to invest primarily in a small group of high-potential professionals while automating intermediate work.

**Placement:** O1 organizational strategy and policy, constrained by imported law, professional ethics, and applicable SymK/project consequence duties.

**Reason:** The strategy may be compatible, harmful, or unlawful depending on context; it is not universal SymK doctrine.

**Result:** Pass. The model records consequence review without universalizing one labor policy.

### Test 16 — Operational failure

**Candidate:** Composer repeatedly produces formally valid but professionally misleading legal analyses.

**Initial route:** O1 containment and I1 investigation.

**Possible escalation:** E2 if the behavior follows the specification; E1 if driven by a design obligation; P2/P1 if caused by legal conceptual assumptions; S2/S1 if evidence implicates universal SymK commitments.

**Result:** Pass. The nearest-adequate-owner rule permits controlled escalation without suppression.

### Test 17 — Parent evolution

**Candidate:** SymK revises an S2 concept after LexBrain adopted an earlier version.

**Effect:** LexBrain receives an impact-review obligation. It does not migrate automatically and is not retroactively rewritten.

**Options:** adopt, migrate, remain temporarily pinned, challenge, or declare incompatibility according to later governance.

**Result:** Pass with detailed protocol deferred to `2.6`.

### Test 18 — Mixed document

**Candidate:** One file contains philosophical argument, a normative concept definition, an API schema, and deployment instructions without status boundaries.

**Result:** Fail placement.

**Required correction:** Declare an authority manifest with section-level ownership or split the artifact.

The model correctly rejects an artifact whose authority cannot be determined.

## 4. Compatibility tests

The consolidated model must satisfy all of the following.

| Compatibility question | Result | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Can SymK govern universal epistemic commitments without owning legal ontology? | Pass | S1/S2 versus P1/P2 jurisdiction |
| Can LexBrain specialize SymK without using identical terminology or architecture? | Pass | Specialization and projection distinctions |
| Can Composer inherit through LexBrain rather than bypassing it? | Pass | Multi-generation lineage |
| Can LibScan and Composer share concepts without sharing implementation? | Pass | P2 shared meaning; E2/I1 independent realization |
| Can an organization reject deployment of a compatible system? | Pass | O1 authorization is distinct from project compatibility |
| Can law control action without becoming a SymK axiom? | Pass | Lateral import and practical precedence |
| Can implementation evidence challenge SymK? | Pass | Upward evidence and nearest-adequate-owner routing |
| Can SymK change without silently rewriting stable projects? | Pass | Versioned adoption and downward impact review |
| Can unresolved conflict remain visible without stopping unrelated inquiry? | Pass | Qualified compatibility and scoped continuation |
| Can a data structure remain a projection rather than the concept itself? | Pass | S2/P2 meaning separated from E2/I1 projection |

## 5. Placement test for future use

For any artifact, commitment, or dispute, determine:

1. **Governed decision:** What decision does it control—identity, meaning, engineering obligation, design, realization, or operation?
2. **Reach:** Must it hold universally, for a domain, product, organization, or deployment?
3. **Authority source:** Is its authority constitutional, semantic, adopted, architectural, technical, external, local, or merely evidentiary?
4. **Primary owner:** Which single layer is accountable for status, change, lineage, and challenge?
5. **Parent commitments:** Which versions and constraints apply?
6. **Relationship:** Is the movement inheritance, specialization, instantiation, composition, role adoption, projection, implementation, local adoption, or challenge?
7. **Permitted additions:** What may the target add within its jurisdiction?
8. **Forbidden effects:** Which meanings or authorities must it not silently alter?
9. **Evidence path:** What evidence can challenge it, and where must that evidence travel?
10. **Conflict effect:** Which compatibility claims must be qualified while a material conflict remains unresolved?

If the owner, authority, or relationship cannot be stated, the artifact is not ready for canonical or binding status.

## 8. Accepted decision

### SYMK-2X-DR-005 — Layer authority, ownership, relationships, and feedback

**Status:** Stage-Accepted

**Question:** How are responsibilities divided among SymK and lower or narrower layers?

**Proposed position:**

> **SymK governance operates through a jurisdiction-qualified layer model across two axes: reach, from universal SymK to concrete deployment; and realization depth, from constitutional meaning to operation. Every governed artifact has one declared primary owning layer determined by the decision it governs, its jurisdiction, and its authority source. Lower and narrower layers may inherit, specialize, instantiate, compose, project, implement, and locally adopt governing artifacts within explicit scope, but may not silently redefine or acquire ownership of their sources. Constraint travels downward within jurisdiction; domain and contextual meaning enrich downward; evidence travels upward; and external authority enters laterally at its applicable scope. Conflicts are routed to the nearest adequate owner, each layer changes only artifacts it owns, and material unresolved conflicts remain visible in affected compatibility claims.**

**Dissent and uncertainty:**

- exact layer granularity;
- exact inheritance and adoption boundary;
- formal status of semantic projections;
- compatibility and exception statuses;
- multi-parent project lineage;
- and the mature relation between A6, the jurisdiction article, and later derived-project governance.

**Affected artifacts:** Constitution hierarchy, A6, conflict protocol, concept and standard structure, Project Constitution and Engineering Charter framework, project lineage, conformance records, and repository placement.

**Authority:** Stage-Accepted for continuation of SymK 2.0; not constitutional ratification.

## 9. Accepted 2.0.4.5 conclusion

The consolidated model passes the representative placement and compatibility tests. No unresolved question currently prevents a controlled 2.0 layer-boundary baseline, provided that the exact formal, procedural, and implementation mechanisms remain deferred to their declared stages.

`SYMK-2X-DR-005` completes 2.0.4 and supplies the layer-boundary baseline for the remaining SymK 2.0 consolidation work.

## 10. Operational maturation condition

The Stage-Accepted model is a controlled baseline for use, not a claim that authority relationships can be completed through abstract analysis alone.

Further maturation depends on daily development and operation exposing:

- ambiguous ownership;
- friction among inherited and local commitments;
- authority claimed without adequate jurisdiction;
- consequences that reveal defective concepts, rules, designs, or policies;
- and cases in which the nearest adequate owner cannot resolve the problem without challenging a parent layer.

Such experience must return as traceable evidence. Consequences may challenge any authority, including SymK, but they do not silently amend the challenged artifact. The responsible owning layer must review the evidence and govern any correction.

This creates the continuing cycle:

```text
governed baseline
    → daily use
    → observed consequence
    → authority challenge
    → governed review
    → revised or reaffirmed baseline
```

The model should therefore be evaluated through `2.7 — Grounding Validation` and later sustained operation. Recurrent authority challenges should be treated as evidence that either a lower layer is exceeding its jurisdiction or the parent model is incomplete, incoherent, or overreaching. Neither explanation is presumed in advance.
