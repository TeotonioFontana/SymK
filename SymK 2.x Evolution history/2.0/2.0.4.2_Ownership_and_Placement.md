# 2.0.4.2 — Ownership and Placement

**Status:** Substep-Accepted  
**Parent work package:** `2.0.4 — Layer-Boundary Analysis`  
**Depends on:** Substep-Accepted `2.0.4.1 — Layer Model`  
**Decision under preparation:** `SYMK-2X-DR-005`
**Accepted by/date:** Project steward, 2026-08-18

## 1. Question

> Which layer has primary governance responsibility for each kind of SymK or project artifact, and how should mixed or ambiguous artifacts be placed?

## 2. Meaning of ownership

In this analysis, **ownership** means primary governance responsibility. The owning layer has the duty and authority to:

- state the artifact's purpose and jurisdiction;
- define or select its content through the appropriate process;
- assign status and version;
- approve changes and supersession;
- preserve lineage, evidence, dissent, and known limitations;
- answer challenges within its jurisdiction;
- and declare its effects on dependent layers.

Ownership does not mean intellectual property, exclusive authorship, infallibility, or immunity from challenge. Several participants may contribute evidence and content, but one governance layer must remain accountable for the artifact's authority.

## 3. Primary-placement rule

> **Every governed commitment and artifact must declare one primary owning layer, its jurisdictional reach, its authority kind, and any dependent projections.**

An artifact may serve several audiences, but it must not exercise several incompatible kinds of authority invisibly. If a document contains constitutional, conceptual, engineering, and implementation material, those parts must be separated or individually marked and governed.

This prevents a foundation paper from ratifying its own candidate axiom, an ontology from becoming a Constitution, or a database schema from becoming a conceptual definition.

## 4. Ownership matrix

| Artifact or commitment | Primary layer | What that layer owns | Permitted dependencies or projections | Misplacement warning |

| Universal constitutional axiom or jurisdiction article | S1 | Normative statement, authoritative interpretation, scope, conflict status | Conceptual derivations, project mappings, tests | A foundation paper may propose but not ratify it |
| Foundation paper and research survey | S0 — epistemic sources and inquiry | Evidence, arguments, alternatives, candidate conclusions, dissent | Constitutional-source maps and later governed selection | “Foundational” does not mean normatively superior |

## 5. Placement of the Knowledge-related artifacts

The Knowledge example can now be classified without selecting a formal language or implementation.

| Artifact | Owner | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Universal SymK meaning of Knowledge | S2 | Universal semantic responsibility beneath constitutional constraints |
| Constitutional commitment concerning Knowledge's role | S1 | Defines what must remain true for SymK compatibility |
| Knowledge Foundation Paper | S0 | Preserves inquiry, alternatives, evidence, and candidate commitments |
| Universal semantic obligations for representing Knowledge-related material | S2 | Cross-project meaning and distinctions |
| A reference `KnowledgeObject` conceptual inventory | S2 if expressly governed as a reference model | Illustrates a projection without becoming the concept or mandatory schema |
| LexBrain concept of Legal Knowledge | P2 | Adds legal-domain meaning and restrictions |
| LexBrain formal ontology of Legal Knowledge | E2 by default, with explicit mapping to P2 | Formal architectural projection of the domain concept |
| Composer knowledge-record contract | E2 | Product-specific interface and structural contract |
| Composer database tables or classes | I1 | Concrete realization |
| Law-office approval and reliance policy | O1 | Local professional and institutional authorization |

This classification resolves the initiating 2.0.4 question provisionally:

> SymK owns the universal meaning and semantic obligations of its concepts through S2. Derived projects own domain specializations through P2. Formal models and data structures belong to the layer that selects or realizes the projection, unless a specific formal model has itself been governed as part of a higher-layer concept specification.

## 6. Projection placement rule

A projection does not always belong to one fixed layer merely because of its format. An OWL ontology, JSON schema, diagram, or natural-language document must be placed by **authority and use**, not file extension.

For example:

- an OWL artifact may be an S2 normative semantic projection, a P2 domain ontology, or an E2 application model;
- Markdown may contain an S1 Constitution, an S0 research note, an E2 specification, or an O1 procedure;
- JSON may be an E2 interface contract, an I1 configuration artifact, or an O1 deployment record.

Format never determines jurisdiction.

## 7. Mixed-artifact rule

When one package legitimately contains material from several layers, it must provide an authority manifest that identifies:

- the primary layer of the package;
- the layer and status of each normative section or child artifact;
- which content is explanatory, evidentiary, normative, illustrative, generated, or operational;
- precedence inside the package;
- and separate change authorities where needed.

If those distinctions cannot be made clear, the package should be split.

## 8. Adoption is not ownership transfer

When a project adopts a SymK standard or concept:

- SymK retains responsibility for the source artifact;
- the project owns its adoption decision, selected version, specialization, and local conformance status;
- the project does not acquire authority to rewrite the source for other projects;
- SymK does not acquire authority over the project's domain additions merely because they refer to SymK.

The same distinction applies when an organization adopts a project system. Local authorization does not transfer ownership of the project Constitution, and project compatibility does not authorize local use automatically.

## 9. Placement test

For any artifact or commitment, ask in order:

1. **What decision does it govern?** Identity, meaning, engineering obligation, design, realization, or operation?
2. **Who must comply?** Every SymK project, one domain, one product, one organization, or one deployment?
3. **What gives it authority?** Ratification, governed concept status, project adoption, design decision, implementation, external law, or local authorization?
4. **What may it change?** A universal commitment, domain concept, project constraint, architecture, code, or local configuration?
5. **What must it not change?** Which parent meanings or authorities remain outside its jurisdiction?
6. **Who approves revision or supersession?** If this cannot be answered, ownership is undefined.
7. **What evidence may challenge it?** If no challenge route exists, governance is incomplete.

## 10. Counterexamples

### 10.1 Implemented schema presented as universal ontology

A database table named `knowledge` is treated as the definition of Knowledge. This is misplaced: I1 has silently assumed S2 authority.

### 10.2 Foundation paper treated as ratified norm

A candidate commitment is enforced because it appears in a Foundation Paper. This is misplaced: S0 has silently assumed S1 authority.

### 10.3 Local legal rule generalized across domains

A Brazilian legal retention rule is placed in SymK Core. This is misplaced: an imported O1/P-level constraint has been universalized into S2.

### 10.4 Project domain concept forced into all projects

LexBrain's `LegalAuthority` becomes a mandatory SymK universal concept. This is misplaced: P2 has silently expanded into S2.

### 10.5 Reference architecture treated as the only compatible realization

A project is declared incompatible merely because it uses a different database or ontology language while preserving all governing commitments. E2 has silently claimed constitutional authority.

## 11. Accepted 2.0.4.2 conclusion

> **Every SymK-related artifact must have one declared primary owning layer determined by the decision it governs, its jurisdictional reach, and its authority source—not by format, location, authorship, or implementation. The owner is responsible for status, meaning within jurisdiction, change, lineage, and challenge. Lower layers may adopt, specialize, project, strengthen, or realize higher commitments, but adoption does not transfer ownership and projection does not confer authority to redefine the source. Mixed artifacts must expose their internal authority boundaries or be split.**

This conclusion is accepted as the ownership-and-placement component under preparation for `SYMK-2X-DR-005`.
