# 2.0.5.4 — Complex Tension Dossiers

**Status:** Substep-Accepted  
**Parent work package:** `2.0.5 — Governing Tension Register`  
**Depends on:** Substep-Accepted `2.0.5.1` through `2.0.5.3`  
**Decision under preparation:** `SYMK-2X-DR-006`
**Accepted by/date:** Project steward, 2026-08-18

## 1. Purpose

Examine five tension clusters whose consequences cross SymK concepts, engineering, professional practice, organizations, external authority, and society.

The dossiers do not solve the complete controversy. Each determines:

- why the tension matters to SymK;
- which legitimate concerns must remain visible;
- the minimum universal SymK obligation;
- decisions required from derived projects and organizations;
- external authorities and evidence that must be imported;
- prohibited simplifications;
- and the later stage or practice through which maturation must occur.

## 2. Dossier A — Professional transformation

**Tensions:** TN-014, TN-015, TN-016, TN-023  
**Themes:** amplification, displacement, formation, selection, dependency, and deskilling

### 2.1 Why the cluster exists

The value proposition of a Domain Intelligence Amplifier is not neutral toward professional organization. If a highly skilled professional can answer more complex questions faster and at lower total cost, work previously distributed across several people may be compressed into a smaller professional system.

That compression can:

- increase the productive reach of advanced professionals;
- reduce routine and intermediate effort;
- lower organizational cost;
- make high-quality capability available in new contexts;
- displace tasks and roles;
- concentrate opportunity and authority;
- remove practice through which future experts once developed;
- and create dependency on systems that participants may become less able to challenge.

These are connected consequences. Treating displacement, formation, selection, and dependency separately would conceal the causal chain among them.

### 2.2 Legitimate concerns that must remain visible

**Amplification and efficiency matter because:**

- scarce expertise can address more problems;
- repetitive work can consume attention better used for judgment;
- organizations may produce better answers with fewer resources;
- and professionals can operate at a higher level of complexity.

**Professional continuity and formation matter because:**

- present experts are not automatically replaced by future experts;
- intermediate work may provide observation, repetition, correction, and gradual responsibility;
- challenge capacity depends on maintained competence;
- selection systems can miss late, unconventional, or context-dependent potential;
- and concentrated opportunity can reproduce institutional privilege.

### 2.3 Minimum SymK obligation

SymK must require affected projects and organizations to make explicit:

- which tasks, roles, and learning opportunities are expected to change;
- which capabilities remain necessary for responsible challenge and decision;
- how those capabilities will be acquired, exercised, evaluated, and recovered;
- who defines selection criteria and under what authority;
- who is affected by exclusion, displacement, or dependency;
- what evidence supports the projected organizational effect;
- and how observed consequences can challenge the design or policy.

SymK does not require preservation of every existing role. It does require that productivity gains not be described as pure amplification when their realization depends on hidden displacement, capability erosion, or transferred burdens.

### 2.4 Project and organizational decisions

Derived projects must identify domain-critical competencies, retained human or institutional judgment, failure recovery, supervision, and formation effects.

Organizations must decide:

- staffing and task allocation;
- selection and investment strategy;
- professional development pathways;
- acceptable dependency;
- transition and support measures;
- and local responsibility for consequences.

Those decisions remain subject to law, professional standards, ethics, collective arrangements, and organizational authority.

### 2.5 Evidence required for maturation

- changes in task distribution rather than job-title speculation alone;
- longitudinal development of novice and intermediate capability;
- ability of professionals to detect, explain, and recover from system failure;
- concentration of opportunity, authority, and economic benefit;
- performance of selected and excluded groups over time;
- new formation pathways created as well as old pathways removed;
- and accounts from affected professionals, learners, clients, and institutions.

### 2.6 Prohibited simplifications

- “Amplification means nobody is replaced.”
- “Displacement proves the system is socially unacceptable.”
- “Only exceptional people matter” as universal SymK doctrine.
- “Equal opportunity requires identical investment.”
- “Automation of a task proves the associated capability is no longer needed.”
- “A human remains nominally present, therefore deskilling is impossible.”
- “The market will resolve formation automatically.”

### 2.7 Preserved dissent

It remains open whether future professional systems will create stronger alternative formation pathways or structurally weaken expertise reproduction. It also remains open whether concentrated investment is a necessary feature of exceptional organizations, a contingent strategy, or a source of unacceptable exclusion in particular contexts.

### 2.8 Destination

- Education, Training, Intelligence, responsibility, and amplification concepts → `2.1`.
- Constitutional duties concerning formation and consequence → `2.2`.
- Project and organizational declarations → `2.6`.
- LexBrain and sshConnectivity evidence → `2.7`.
- Continuing empirical maturation → daily development and operation.

## 3. Dossier B — Value under responsibility

**Tensions:** TN-011, TN-013, TN-030  
**Themes:** actionable answers, uncertainty, speed, cost, validation, and harm prevention

### 3.1 Why the cluster exists

SymK promises practical value: better answers to complex questions, faster and at lower total cost. Yet complex professional questions often involve incomplete evidence, contested standards, irreversible consequences, and unequal exposure to error.

An answer can be faster and cheaper because waste has been removed—or because validation, context, dissent, or responsibility has been suppressed. Conversely, a system can invoke caution so broadly that it never produces useful action.

### 3.2 Legitimate concerns that must remain visible

**Action, speed, and cost matter because:**

- delayed answers can themselves cause harm;
- inaccessible expertise has little practical value;
- excessive validation can make a system unusable;
- and lower total cost is central to scalable amplification.

**Humility, validation, and prevention matter because:**

- fluency and confidence do not establish Knowledge;
- high-stakes errors may be difficult to repair;
- affected parties may bear costs not included in the system's accounting;
- and learning after harm is not an adequate substitute for proportionate safeguards.

### 3.3 Minimum SymK obligation

Every relevant project must treat answer quality, time, total cost, uncertainty, validation, responsibility, and foreseeable consequence as jointly constrained dimensions.

It must declare:

- the domain meaning of a “better” answer;
- evidence and validation appropriate to stakes;
- when delay is itself a risk;
- which costs are counted and which actors bear them;
- conditions for refusal, escalation, or qualified answers;
- and safeguards proportionate to foreseeable harm.

No single dimension may silently become the optimization objective for the whole system.

### 3.4 Project and external authority

Projects and professions define substantive answer thresholds. Organizations authorize reliance. Law, regulation, professional ethics, safety disciplines, and affected-party rights constrain acceptable risk.

SymK governs the explicit relationship among these authorities; it does not supply one universal quality score or acceptable-risk formula.

### 3.5 Evidence required for maturation

- answer quality under domain-defined evaluation;
- time-to-answer and time-to-safe-action;
- complete lifecycle cost, including validation, correction, training, transition, and harm;
- rates and severity of unsupported confidence, refusal, escalation, and undetected failure;
- distribution of benefits and burdens;
- and consequences of both action and delay.

### 3.6 Prohibited simplifications

- “Faster is always better.”
- “Cheaper per answer means lower total cost.”
- “More validation is always safer.”
- “A refusal is a failure to answer.”
- “Operational learning justifies preventable harm.”
- “Compliance proves responsible use.”
- “Accuracy alone defines answer quality.”

### 3.7 Preserved dissent

The constitutional place of “lower total cost” remains open. It may be part of purpose, derived value, or a project success dimension. The mature relation among epistemic quality, professional adequacy, timeliness, cost, and harm remains domain- and evidence-dependent.

### 3.8 Destination

- Knowledge, responsibility, and answer concepts → `2.1`.
- Constitutional compression of value and safeguards → `2.2`.
- Project success and assurance obligations → `2.6`.
- Domain validation → `2.7`.

## 4. Dossier C — Authority and legitimacy

**Tensions:** TN-008, TN-012, TN-017, TN-018, TN-022, TN-029  
**Themes:** truth, authority, expertise, collective capability, roles, responsibility, legitimacy, and challenge

### 4.1 Why the cluster exists

Domain Intelligence Amplifiers distribute epistemic and causal work. One participant may retrieve evidence, another infer, another critique, another authorize, and another bear consequences. The resulting capability may belong partly to the cooperative organization rather than any one participant.

This distribution creates a danger: performance, authority, and responsibility may be collapsed into whichever participant appears most intelligent, most senior, most automated, or most institutionally powerful.

### 4.2 Necessary distinctions

SymK must preserve distinctions among:

- truth;
- epistemic warrant;
- expertise and demonstrated capability;
- institutional acceptance;
- legal or professional authorization;
- role assignment;
- causal contribution;
- decision authority;
- accountability;
- consequence-bearing;
- obligation to repair;
- and standing of affected parties to question or contest.

No one relation entails all the others.

### 4.3 Minimum SymK obligation

Every significant decision arrangement must make visible:

- which participants performed which epistemic and operational roles;
- evidence supporting competence and reliance;
- who was authorized to decide or act;
- who may challenge and through which route;
- who bears accountability and repair duties;
- which affected parties have standing;
- and whether system-level Intelligence attribution obscures any individual or institutional responsibility.

Authority remains effective but consequentially challengeable. Challenge supplies evidence to the authority's owning layer; it does not silently reverse the decision or amend the governing artifact.

### 4.4 Project and external authority

Projects define domain roles and competence evidence. Organizations assign local authority. Professions, courts, regulators, contracts, and law determine many substantive authorizations and duties. Ethical and political legitimacy may exceed both technical performance and legal permission.

SymK requires these sources to remain distinct and traceable without claiming to replace them.

### 4.5 Evidence required for maturation

- comparative performance and failure evidence by role and context;
- cases where formal authority diverges from relevant expertise;
- cases where system-level attribution obscures causal contribution;
- effectiveness and accessibility of challenge mechanisms;
- consequences experienced by affected parties;
- repair after error;
- and recurrent situations in which an owning authority cannot adequately correct the consequences of its decisions.

### 4.6 Prohibited simplifications

- “The most capable participant should decide.”
- “The authorized participant is epistemically correct.”
- “The AI caused it, therefore the organization is not responsible.”
- “A human approved it, therefore responsibility is settled.”
- “Collective Intelligence means collective legal personality.”
- “Affected parties determine truth.”
- “Challenge destroys stable authority.”
- “Authority may disregard consequences because it followed procedure.”

### 4.7 Preserved dissent

It remains open when collective or Amplified Intelligence is literally a bearer of Knowledge or agency rather than a description of distributed capability. It also remains open which affected-party rights are universal ethical minima and which depend on law, profession, institution, or political legitimacy.

### 4.8 Destination

- Intelligence, collective attribution, responsibility, and role concepts → `2.1`.
- Universal constitutional distinctions and challenge commitments → `2.2`.
- Authority and challenge mechanics → `2.3`.
- Project roles and compatibility → `2.6`.
- Consequential authority evidence → `2.7` and daily operation.

## 5. Dossier D — Shared Knowledge and power

**Tensions:** TN-002, TN-019, TN-026  
**Themes:** semantic alignment, plural representation, unequal access, memory, privacy, forgetting, tacit Knowledge, and familiarity

### 5.1 Why the cluster exists

Knowledge is the medium of cooperation, but “shared” cannot mean that every participant receives identical content, representation, access, or internal understanding. Different Intelligences require different projections, and legitimate duties may restrict access. At the same time, control over representation, memory, and access allocates epistemic and institutional power.

Not all Knowledge can be made explicit. Skills, judgment, familiarity, and situated experience may resist complete serialization. A system centered only on durable explicit assets may privilege what is easy to encode and those who control the encoding.

### 5.2 Legitimate concerns that must remain visible

**Sharing and durability matter because:**

- cooperation must survive participants and sessions;
- claims need provenance, challenge, and revision;
- and cumulative work requires recoverable meaning.

**Plurality, restriction, and tacitness matter because:**

- participants have different capacities and legitimate access;
- confidentiality and privacy may forbid universal visibility;
- deletion or expiry may be required;
- representations suppress dimensions;
- and important competence may exist primarily in practice and relationship.

### 5.3 Minimum SymK obligation

Systems must declare:

- what is shared and with whom;
- which semantic relationship makes cooperation possible;
- which representations exist and what each omits;
- who controls creation, access, transformation, restriction, retention, and deletion;
- which grounds and dissent are unavailable to which participants;
- what practical or experiential Knowledge is not captured;
- and how exclusion or asymmetry affects reliance and responsibility.

Shared Knowledge therefore means governed semantic relation sufficient for cooperation, not forced informational equality or universal access.

### 5.4 Project and external authority

Projects define domain representations and access-relevant roles. Organizations govern local access and retention. Privacy, confidentiality, evidentiary rules, professional secrecy, intellectual property, security, and affected-party rights are imported from legitimate authorities.

### 5.5 Evidence required for maturation

- semantic failures across representations;
- decisions made without access to material grounds or dissent;
- who benefits from and controls canonicalization;
- effect of access restrictions on accountability;
- loss caused by deletion or indefinite retention;
- performance differences between explicit instruction and practice-based formation;
- and testimony from participants whose Knowledge is systematically difficult to encode.

### 5.6 Prohibited simplifications

- “Shared means everyone sees everything.”
- “Access restriction is merely a technical permission.”
- “The canonical representation contains the complete meaning.”
- “If it cannot be serialized, it is not Knowledge.”
- “Retention always supports learning.”
- “Deletion always destroys accountability.”
- “Equal representation format produces equal understanding.”

### 5.7 Preserved dissent

The minimum semantic alignment required for cooperation remains unsettled. The balance among durable lineage, privacy, justified forgetting, institutional memory, and affected-party rights cannot be fixed without domain and legal evidence.

### 5.8 Destination

- Knowledge, representation, context, and epistemic asset concepts → `2.1/2.5`.
- Universal projection and responsibility commitments → `2.2`.
- Project access and semantic declarations → `2.6`.
- Domain and organizational evidence → `2.7`.
- Technical representation and validation → `2.9`.

## 6. Dossier E — Inheritance and external authority

**Tensions:** TN-021, TN-024, TN-025, TN-027, TN-028  
**Themes:** project autonomy, cross-project coherence, law, local duty, unresolved incompatibility, ownership, and practical authority

### 6.1 Why the cluster exists

SymK needs inherited commitments to remain a coherent discipline rather than a label applied to unrelated systems. Derived projects and organizations nevertheless encounter domain truth, law, professional authority, technical constraints, and consequences that SymK cannot predefine.

The resulting authority structure is not a simple hierarchy. A law may control deployment without defining SymK meaning. An implementation may expose a constitutional defect without possessing constitutional amendment authority. One artifact owner may remain accountable while several external authorities legitimately constrain the artifact.

### 6.2 Legitimate concerns that must remain visible

**Inheritance and coherence matter because:**

- compatibility must mean more than inspiration;
- silent redefinition destroys cumulative learning;
- and shared concepts and obligations enable reuse.

**Autonomy and external authority matter because:**

- domains supply meanings SymK does not own;
- law and professional authority can decide legitimate action;
- local institutions bear responsibilities that cannot be transferred upward;
- and operational consequences may reveal parent error or overreach.

### 6.3 Minimum SymK obligation

Projects, subprojects, and adopters must declare:

- inherited artifact and version;
- specialization, projection, implementation, and local adoption relationships;
- one accountable owner for each governed artifact;
- contributing and external authorities;
- known conflict, exception, and compatibility effects;
- evidence challenging parent or local commitments;
- and the responsible route for review.

No owner may amend another layer's artifact silently. No external authority is transformed into SymK doctrine merely because compliance is mandatory. No implementation becomes conceptually authoritative merely because it operates successfully.

### 6.4 Continuity under conflict

Safe inquiry and unaffected operation may continue during a conflict when scope, independence, authority, and risk are explicit. A material unresolved conflict must qualify affected compatibility claims.

Continuity is not permission to normalize the conflict. Suspension is not automatically required for every theoretical disagreement. The responsible operational authority contains immediate risk while each artifact owner governs its own correction.

### 6.5 Evidence required for maturation

- repeated project exceptions or challenges to the same parent commitment;
- conflicts among legal, professional, project, and organizational authorities;
- operational costs of version pinning and migration;
- cases where one-primary-owner improves or obscures accountability;
- implementation conventions that repeatedly outperform or contradict formal standards;
- and consequences of continuing, qualifying, or suspending operation during unresolved conflict.

### 6.6 Prohibited simplifications

- “The parent always wins.”
- “Local law redefines universal SymK meaning.”
- “The project is autonomous, therefore inheritance is optional.”
- “Disclosure makes every exception compatible.”
- “One owner means one source of all authority.”
- “Running code is the source of truth.”
- “A canonical document may ignore operational evidence.”
- “Any conflict requires stopping all work.”

### 6.7 Preserved dissent

The exact status of qualified compatibility, temporary exceptions, incompatible-but-lineaged projects, and multi-parent composition remains open. The mature disposition of A6 also remains open: its content may be axiomatic, jurisdictional, procedural, or distributed across several artifact types.

### 6.8 Destination

- Axiom and constitutional classification → `2.2`.
- Ratification, challenge, exception, and compatibility statuses → `2.3`.
- A6 and legacy disposition → `2.4`.
- Project Constitution, Engineering Charter, and inheritance protocol → `2.6`.
- Grounding evidence → `2.7`.
- Engineering conformance and lowering → `2.9`.

## 7. Cross-dossier dependencies

The dossiers are separable for analysis but not independent:

- professional dependency affects authority and legitimacy;
- cost reduction can drive displacement and reduced formation;
- unequal access to Knowledge can concentrate expertise and authority;
- external law can constrain both information sharing and professional automation;
- and operational harm challenges both value claims and governing authority.

The consolidated register must preserve these links without merging the dossiers back into one unmanageable social theory.

## 8. Dossier adequacy tests

### 8.1 Jurisdiction test

Does each dossier distinguish SymK obligations from project decisions, organizational choices, and external authority?

**Result:** Pass.

### 8.2 Consequence test

Can a project avoid examining a foreseeable effect by calling it social or external?

**Result:** No. Each dossier retains explicit review and evidence duties.

### 8.3 Non-universalization test

Does any dossier prescribe universal labor, selection, education, privacy, legitimacy, or legal policy?

**Result:** No. Substantive choices are routed to their legitimate authorities.

### 8.4 Practicality test

Can later project governance derive declaration and evidence obligations from each dossier?

**Result:** Yes. Each identifies decisions, authorities, affected parties, evidence, and challenge paths.

### 8.5 Dissent test

Are unresolved positions and uncertainty preserved?

**Result:** Yes. Each dossier states unresolved empirical or normative questions without treating them as defects in documentation.

## 9. Accepted 2.0.5.4 conclusion

> **The most socially consequential SymK tensions require bounded dossiers rather than universal solutions. SymK must preserve their causal connections, require explicit project and organizational decisions, expose affected parties and foreseeable consequences, import legitimate external authority, demand empirical maturation, and protect challenge. It must not transform these duties into universal labor, education, selection, privacy, legitimacy, or legal doctrine.**

These dossiers supply the complex-case foundation for the consolidated register and `SYMK-2X-DR-006` in 2.0.5.5.
