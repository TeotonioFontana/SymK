# SymK 2.0 — Identity and Jurisdiction

**Stage status:** Release candidate under review
**Target version:** 2.0  
**Current version form:** `2.0-rc.1`
**Normative effect:** DR-001–DR-007 have provisional Stage-Accepted program authority; canonical migration is authorized and proceeds step-by-step; final 2.0 acceptance remains pending

## Central question

> **What is SymK, why does it exist, and where does its authority begin and end?**

## Objective

SymK 2.0 establishes a coherent relationship among:

- the Product Vision;
- SymK’s identity as an engineering discipline;
- its cooperative purpose;
- its epistemological center;
- the minimal Intelligence concept required to identify participants;
- the boundary between SymK and derived projects;
- the boundary between philosophy and engineering;
- and the questions intentionally deferred to later versions.

## Questions 2.0 must answer

- What is SymK?
- Why does it exist?
- What problem does it address?
- What does SymK govern?
- What does SymK engineer?
- Who or what can participate?
- Why are Knowledge and Intelligence within its scope?
- Which philosophical fields constrain SymK?
- What belongs to SymK itself?
- What belongs to LexBrain, sshConnectivity, and other projects?
- What does SymK explicitly not claim?
- What remains unresolved after 2.0?

## Out of scope

Version 2.0 will not finalize:

- the definitions of Knowledge or Intelligence;
- the complete theory of Education and Training;
- the full Knowledge Engineering lifecycle;
- detailed Entity, Relationship, Identity, Context, or Representation models;
- the final A0–A6 ratification decision;
- project constitutions or Engineering Charter specifications;
- corpus-wide migration;
- implementation architecture, coding standards, or software.

These questions may be examined only far enough to establish identity and jurisdiction, then deferred explicitly.

## Acceptance principle

Completion means that remaining uncertainty is explicit, bounded, traceable, and assigned to the correct stage. It does not mean philosophical consensus or finality.
