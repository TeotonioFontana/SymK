# 2.0.4 — Layer-Boundary Analysis

**Status:** Stage-Accepted
**Decision:** `SYMK-2X-DR-005 — Stage-Accepted`
**Accepted by/date:** Project steward, 2026-08-18

## 1. Purpose

Define how authority and responsibility are allocated among SymK, derived projects, engineering specifications, implementations, organizations, and operational deployments.

The objective is to determine:

- where a commitment or artifact belongs;
- what a lower layer inherits from a higher layer;
- what a lower layer may add or specialize;
- what no layer may silently redefine;
- and how evidence, conflict, and challenge move between layers.

This work package determines **placement and interlayer relationships**. It does not finalize the content or technical mechanism of the artifacts being placed.

## 2. Explicit exclusions

The following questions are outside 2.0.4 and are assigned to later stages:

- mature definitions of Knowledge, Intelligence, Representation, Context, or other foundational concepts — `2.1`;
- the final anatomy of Canonical Concept Specifications or dual-lane Concept Packages — `2.1/2.5`;
- selection of first-order logic, OWL, SHACL, or another formal language — `2.5/2.9`;
- the exact protocol for project inheritance, specialization, version pinning, and compatibility declarations — `2.6`;
- validation of the framework through LexBrain and sshConnectivity — `2.7`;
- compilation or lowering from ontologies into schemas, rules, code, or storage — `2.9`.

These subjects may be used as boundary tests, but they shall not be designed or settled in this work package.

## 4. Initial layer hypothesis

| Layer | Owns | Must not silently own |
|---|---|---|
| SymK Constitution | Universal purpose, jurisdiction, epistemic constraints, minimal participant commitments, and constitutional inheritance | Mature concept detail, domain ontology, product policy, or implementation choices |
| SymK foundational concepts and corpus | Governed universal meanings, arguments, distinctions, principles, methods, and standards according to their declared authority | Constitutional authority merely through publication; domain-specific meaning |
| Derived-project Constitution and conceptual layer | Domain axioms, domain Ontology and Logic, domain concepts, and explicit specialization of inherited commitments | Redefinition, weakening, or bypass of SymK commitments without challenge |
| Engineering Charter | Design obligations translating constitutional and conceptual commitments into project engineering constraints | New philosophical commitments created accidentally by architecture |
| Specification and architecture | Testable structures, interfaces, protocols, semantic projections, and system models | Silent constitutional or conceptual interpretation |
| Implementation | Concrete realization and operational evidence | Authority to define a concept because code or storage exists |
| Organization and deployment | Local authorization, policy, risk treatment, law, professional practice, and operating decisions | Universalization of local constraints or practices |

This table is the initial layer summary accepted through `SYMK-2X-DR-005`; the complete two-axis model and its qualifications are recorded in the five 2.0.4 substep artifacts.

## 5. Boundary evidence from the Knowledge projection test

The Knowledge/FOL/OWL discussion is retained only as a boundary probe. It supports four provisional conclusions:

1. Conceptual authority and technical ownership are different.
2. A derived project may specialize inherited meaning and owns its domain additions.
3. A formalization, ontology, schema, or data structure is a governed projection located at an appropriate engineering layer.
4. No projection, formal language, schema, or implementation acquires authority to redefine its governing concept merely through implementation or use.

The test does **not** select a formal language, ratify a Concept Package architecture, or prescribe an ontology-lowering pipeline.

## 6. Initial placement example

| Question | Provisional responsible layer |
|---|---|
| What does Knowledge mean universally in SymK? | SymK foundational concept layer |
| Which distinctions must every SymK-derived project preserve? | SymK foundational concept layer under constitutional constraint |
| What does legal Knowledge require? | LexBrain conceptual layer |
| Which legal-domain design obligations follow? | LexBrain Engineering Charter |
| How is a legal concept represented in an ontology? | LexBrain specification and architecture |
| Which formal or ontology profile is used? | Project architecture under later SymK standards, if any |
| How is an ontology lowered into validation or code artifacts? | Engineering specification and implementation |
| Which persistence structure is used? | Implementation |
| Which institution may rely on the result and under what legal duties? | Project, organization, and deployment under imported authority |

This example tests placement only. Its substantive answers remain revisable in their destination stages.

## 7. Questions retained inside 2.0.4

- What makes a project “SymK-derived” rather than merely inspired by SymK at the jurisdictional level?
- Which categories of commitments constrain lower layers automatically, and which require explicit adoption?
- Where do organization-specific policies belong?
- What is the minimum distinction among inheritance, specialization, projection, and implementation?
- How does operational evidence return to challenge higher layers?
- Which kinds of silent conflict invalidate or qualify a compatibility claim?
- How is authority allocated when subprojects create additional layers?

## 8. Expected decision

`SYMK-2X-DR-005` establishes a layer-responsibility matrix and placement tests sufficient to guide later work. It does not prematurely settle concept content, formal-language selection, project-governance protocols, or implementation architecture. Its further maturation depends on evidence from daily development, operational consequence, and governed authority challenge.
