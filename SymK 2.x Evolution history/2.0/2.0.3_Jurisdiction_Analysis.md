## 1. Central question

> **Which questions must SymK govern for Domain Intelligence Amplifiers to remain coherent, trustworthy, and reusable—and which questions belong to derived projects, organizations, deployments, or external disciplines?**

Jurisdiction means responsibility to make necessary commitments explicit and govern their consequences. It does not mean ownership of an academic field or authority to settle every controversy within it.

## 2. Why jurisdiction must follow identity and purpose

The accepted identity defines SymK as the engineering discipline for creating Domain Intelligence Amplifiers. The accepted purpose is to make their engineering systematic, governable, reusable, and evolvable across working domains.

Therefore, a field enters SymK only to the degree necessary to:

1. define that class of systems coherently;
2. protect the accepted value chain;
3. preserve epistemic accountability, responsibility, and learning;
4. enable inheritance and comparison across domains;
5. prevent implementation or local practice from silently redefining universal commitments.

Relevance alone does not create SymK jurisdiction.

## 3. Jurisdiction relationship types

| Relationship | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Constitutive jurisdiction | SymK must establish universal commitments because its identity or purpose cannot be expressed without them. |
| Minimal foundational jurisdiction | SymK must govern a bounded concept or distinction but does not claim the whole surrounding field. |
| Project-declaration jurisdiction | SymK requires derived projects to declare and govern domain-specific commitments; SymK does not supply their substantive content. |
| Imported constraint | An external discipline, law, or institution supplies constraints that SymK and projects must respect and trace. |
| Evidence source | A field informs claims and revisions but does not determine SymK meaning by itself. |
| Consequence-review obligation | SymK must expose and evaluate foreseeable effects without prescribing a universal social solution. |
| Outside universal scope | The matter is neither necessary nor universal; it belongs to project, organization, deployment, or society. |

One field may occupy more than one relationship depending on the question.

## 4. Jurisdiction entry test

For any proposed SymK commitment, ask in order:

1. **Identity necessity:** Would SymK cease to describe Domain Intelligence Amplifiers coherently without it?
2. **Purpose necessity:** Is it required to preserve systematic, governable, reusable, or evolvable engineering?
3. **Universality:** Does the commitment hold across working domains, or does its content vary materially by domain?
4. **Level:** Is the issue constitutional, conceptual, engineering, organizational, or operational?
5. **Authority:** Who possesses legitimate expertise or authority to settle it?
6. **Consequence:** Would omission permit false certainty, hidden harm, or silent semantic change?
7. **Minimality:** What is the smallest commitment SymK must make?
8. **Challengeability:** What evidence could revise the boundary?

If a commitment fails identity, purpose, and universality, it does not belong in universal SymK merely because it is important.

## 5. Stage-proposed jurisdiction map

### 5.1 Epistemology

**Relationship:** Constitutive jurisdiction over the epistemic requirements of Domain Intelligence Amplifiers; broader Epistemology remains outside exhaustive scope.

SymK must govern distinctions and conditions involving:

- Knowledge, claims, evidence, grounds, representations, and epistemic status;
- truth claims versus institutional authority;
- scope, context, provenance, uncertainty, disagreement, and defeaters;
- responsible reliance, validation, challenge, revision, and consequence feedback;
- limits on what a system may claim to know or warrant.

SymK does not settle every theory of Knowledge, truth, justification, testimony, disagreement, or social Epistemology. It selects only what its engineered system class requires, preserving alternatives and dissent where necessary.

### 5.2 Knowledge Engineering

**Relationship:** Constitutive engineering jurisdiction.

SymK must govern the design, construction, evaluation, and evolution of epistemic conditions and their artifacts. This includes acquisition, representation, reasoning arrangements, validation, application, memory, feedback, and revision.

SymK does not manufacture Knowledge or truth. Knowledge Engineering is central but does not alone exhaust SymK, because SymK additionally governs Intelligence cooperation, constitutional inheritance, professional responsibility, and cross-domain reuse.

### 5.3 Intelligence and Amplified Intelligence

**Relationship:** Minimal foundational jurisdiction.

SymK must govern enough to identify candidate bearers, attribute scoped capabilities from evidence, distinguish capacity from performance, and describe cooperation and amplification. It must distinguish Intelligence from authority, authorization, consciousness, moral status, wisdom, and responsibility.

SymK does not provide a complete philosophy of mind, theory of consciousness, psychometrics, cognitive architecture, or general intelligence theory.

### 5.4 Cooperation

**Relationship:** Minimal foundational and engineering jurisdiction.

SymK must distinguish cooperation from mere communication, coordination, compliance, collusion, or coexistence. It must govern how different contributions, roles, challenge, memory, and feedback are organized to produce Amplified Intelligence.

SymK does not define a complete social theory of cooperation or declare cooperation intrinsically good regardless of ends and consequences.

### 5.5 Domain reasoning and Logic

**Relationship:** SymK holds minimal meta-logical jurisdiction; derived projects hold substantive domain-logic jurisdiction.

SymK requires reasoning assumptions, inference forms, uncertainty treatment, contradictions, applicability, limitations, and validation to be explicit and testable. It governs the distinction between formal consequence and epistemic or professional acceptance.

LexBrain governs legal reasoning commitments. IaaC governs infrastructure reasoning. sshConnectivity governs diagnostic and operational reasoning. SymK does not impose one universal reasoning formalism.

### 5.6 Ontology

**Relationship:** SymK holds minimal constitutional and meta-ontological jurisdiction; derived projects hold domain-ontology jurisdiction.

SymK necessarily refers to systems, participants, Knowledge-related objects, representations, relationships, contexts, capabilities, and histories. It must govern the distinction between conceptual commitments and implementation schemas.

Derived projects define what exists or matters in their working domains. A database schema, API, document form, or AI vocabulary must not become either SymK ontology or domain ontology by implementation accident.

### 5.7 Representation and semantics

**Relationship:** Constitutive engineering jurisdiction.

SymK must govern representation as purpose-dependent projection, preserve semantic lineage across representations, and expose material assumptions, exclusions, transformations, and losses.

It does not mandate one representation language, knowledge graph, database, document format, or model architecture.

### 5.8 Ethics and responsibility

**Relationship:** Minimal internal normative jurisdiction plus imported constraints.

Because the accepted purpose explicitly preserves responsibility, SymK must govern minimum distinctions among capability, role, authority, authorization, contribution, accountability, affected-party standing, consequence, and repair.

Substantive duties come from professional ethics, law, institutional authority, and project-specific consequence analysis. SymK does not claim a complete moral theory or determine every permissible domain action.

### 5.9 Education and Training

**Relationship:** Minimal foundational and engineering jurisdiction; pedagogy remains external or project-specific.

SymK must recognize that persistent systems form habits and that future expert capability cannot be assumed. It must preserve the relation between Education, repeated Training, challenge, judgment, and responsible participation.

SymK does not prescribe curricula, credentials, universal selection criteria, schooling policy, or one theory of human or machine learning.

### 5.10 Professional practice

**Relationship:** Project-declaration jurisdiction and imported authority.

Derived projects must identify professional standards, competence, decision rights, answer thresholds, and responsibilities. SymK requires these to be explicit and traceable but does not define good lawyering, infrastructure architecture, medicine, or another profession universally.

### 5.11 Cognitive science, psychology, and AI research

**Relationship:** Evidence sources.

These fields supply evidence about capacity, learning, performance, bias, cognition, interaction, and system behavior. Their findings may challenge SymK concepts. No empirical field alone determines the constitutional meaning of Knowledge, Intelligence, responsibility, or cooperation.

### 5.12 Software, systems, data, and AI engineering

**Relationship:** Realization disciplines and imported engineering constraints.

They build dependable components and provide mature methods. SymK specifies epistemic and cooperative requirements that implementations must realize. Implementation technologies do not gain authority to define SymK concepts merely because they are operationally necessary.

### 5.13 Law, regulation, security, privacy, and institutional policy

**Relationship:** Imported constraints, mostly governed at project, organization, and deployment levels.

SymK requires constraints and authority to be explicit, versioned, and traceable to their source and jurisdiction. It does not universalize one country’s law, one organization’s risk posture, or one profession’s authorization model.

### 5.14 Economics, labor, organization design, and selection

**Relationship:** Consequence-review obligation; substantive choices usually outside universal scope.

SymK must not hide foreseeable effects such as professional displacement, concentration of productive power, loss of apprenticeship pathways, dependency, deskilling, and selective investment in future experts.

It does not prescribe one labor policy, organization pyramid, hiring doctrine, distributional outcome, or universal model of merit. Derived projects and organizations must govern their choices and affected-party consequences.

### 5.15 Ultimate domain purposes

**Relationship:** Outside SymK universal jurisdiction; mandatory project declaration.

Justice, clinical health, infrastructure resilience, secure connectivity, scientific discovery, commercial return, and similar ends belong to the relevant project and its communities. SymK governs how a Domain Intelligence Amplifier reasons and remains accountable; it does not define the ultimate good of every domain.

## 7. Layer allocation

```text
SymK
├── identity and purpose
├── universal epistemic constraints
├── minimal Intelligence, amplification, cooperation, and responsibility concepts
├── representation and semantic-evolution discipline
├── cross-domain engineering and evaluation requirements
└── constitutional inheritance and challenge

Derived project
├── domain purpose and affected parties
├── domain Ontology
├── domain Logic and reasoning
├── professional standards and answer thresholds
├── project axioms and Engineering Charter
└── SymK specialization, exception, or challenge

Organization and deployment
├── participant selection and formation
├── permissions and authorization
├── risk appetite and operating policy
├── technology and workflow choices
├── labor and commercial model
└── responsibility for actual decisions and consequences
```

External disciplines and authorities may constrain every layer according to their legitimate jurisdiction.

## 8. Boundary conflict protocol

When ownership is disputed:

1. identify the exact commitment rather than the general field;
2. apply the jurisdiction entry test;
3. distinguish universal meaning from domain content and local implementation;
4. identify legitimate external authority and evidence;
5. place the commitment at the lowest level that can govern it without losing a universal requirement;
6. record any inherited constraint, specialization, exception, or constitutional challenge;
7. preserve dissent when the boundary remains uncertain.

Higher abstraction does not automatically defeat stronger domain evidence. Lower implementation does not silently acquire conceptual authority.

## 9. Counterexamples and compatibility tests

1. **Universal legal ontology in SymK:** legal norm types are placed in SymK Core. Fails universality; belongs to LexBrain.
2. **Schema-defined reality:** an implementation table becomes the definition of Knowledge. Fails conceptual/representation separation.
3. **Logic-free project:** a project claims reasoning capability but cannot state inference assumptions or validation. Fails mandatory project declaration.
4. **Ethics excluded entirely:** a system claims SymK compatibility while responsibility and affected parties are treated as irrelevant. Fails the accepted purpose.
5. **Complete moral theory in SymK:** one contested ethics is constitutionalized beyond system necessity. Fails minimality.
6. **Human cognition universalized:** one model of human intelligence defines every participant. Fails non-anthropocentric minimal scope.
7. **Labor consequence concealed:** an amplifier is designed to replace intermediate work but the project refuses consequence analysis. Fails review obligation, though SymK does not dictate the labor policy.
8. **External law traced:** a jurisdiction requires human professional authorization; the project records it as a scoped imported constraint. Compatible.
9. **Domain challenge returned:** operational legal evidence shows a universal SymK distinction is defective and triggers constitutional review. Compatible.
10. **Interdisciplinary correction:** cognitive-science evidence changes an Intelligence attribution standard through governance. Compatible; jurisdiction is not intellectual isolation.

## 10. A5 classification

The existing A5 combines:

- a statement about SymK’s philosophical center;
- a minimal Intelligence commitment;
- allocation of Ontology and Logic;
- a limitation on exhaustive coverage;
- and a rule for derived-project responsibility.

These are scope and allocation rules governing how all axioms and artifacts are interpreted. They do not express one irreducible substantive commitment of the same kind as epistemic humility or constitutional inheritance.

### Stage-accepted disposition

Reclassify A5 from an axiom into a **Constitutional Jurisdiction Article**. Preserve its exposition, sources, tests, and dissent, but organize the content as:

1. jurisdiction purpose;
2. jurisdiction relationship types;
3. universal SymK commitments;
4. derived-project obligations;
5. imported constraints and evidence;
6. matters outside universal scope;
7. boundary conflict and challenge procedure.

This reclassification does not demote jurisdiction. It gives jurisdiction the correct constitutional function: defining the field on which every axiom operates.

### Axiom-admission rule exposed by the reclassification

Importance alone does not make a statement axiomatic. A constitutional commitment should remain an axiom only when it is:

1. fundamental to SymK identity or compatibility;
2. irreducible without losing essential meaning;
3. universal across the intended SymK scope;
4. independent rather than derivable from other axioms plus governed definitions;
5. substantive rather than primarily definitional, jurisdictional, procedural, methodological, or architectural;
6. necessary at the highest normative level rather than fully governable through a more appropriate constitutional article or lower artifact.

“Can be expressed another way” does not mean that an axiom cannot be paraphrased. It means its full constitutional work can be performed more accurately by another artifact class without losing an irreducible commitment.

A5 fails the axiom-admission test because it combines scope definition, field classification, layer allocation, and interpretive limitation. All are constitutionally important, but their proper form is a jurisdiction article governing the axiom set.

## 11. Proposed constitutional jurisdiction statement

> **SymK governs the universal epistemic and engineering commitments necessary to create Domain Intelligence Amplifiers, including bounded concepts of Knowledge, Intelligence, Amplified Intelligence, cooperation, representation, reasoning accountability, responsibility, learning, and evolution. Derived projects govern their domain purposes, Ontologies, Logic, professional standards, and substantive reasoning under inherited SymK constraints. Organizations and deployments govern local authority, policy, technology, labor, and action. External disciplines and institutions retain authority within their legitimate fields. No layer may silently redefine another’s commitments, and every boundary remains open to evidenced challenge.**

This is proposed scope language, not an axiom statement.

## 12. Consequences for accepted identity and purpose

- “Engineering discipline” gains a bounded field rather than claiming all collaboration or intelligence.
- “Governed domain Knowledge” requires SymK epistemic constraints plus project domain content.
- “Domain Intelligence Amplifier” requires minimal universal system concepts but project-specific Ontology and Logic.
- “Better” depends on domain standards and consequences; SymK governs disclosure and accountability, not the final domain metric alone.
- “Responsibility” requires minimum universal distinctions and imported professional/legal duties.
- “Learning” gives SymK minimal Education and feedback jurisdiction without importing complete pedagogy.
- “Lower total cost” creates evaluation and consequence obligations without giving SymK authority over universal labor policy.

## 13. Prohibited jurisdiction interpretations

- SymK owns or completes Epistemology.
- Every philosophically relevant question belongs in the SymK Constitution.
- Ontology and Logic are absent from SymK or supplied entirely by SymK.
- Derived projects may define Knowledge or Intelligence however convenient.
- Ethical responsibility is entirely outside SymK because domains provide professional rules.
- SymK may impose one professional code, labor structure, selection model, or political theory universally.
- Implementations determine concepts because working code is concrete.
- Jurisdiction boundaries prevent external evidence or constitutional correction.

## 14. Preserved dissent and uncertainty

1. **Epistemology language:** “Constitutive jurisdiction over epistemic conditions” may still sound broader than the intended engineering accountability.
2. **A5 classification:** Keeping A5 as an axiom would make philosophical limitation maximally visible; reclassification may appear to weaken it.
3. **Ethical minimum:** The boundary between universal responsibility and domain moral authority remains unsettled.
4. **Education:** Its role may be constitutive to sustained Amplified Intelligence rather than merely minimal.
5. **Professional scope:** Civic, scientific, artistic, or nonprofessional amplifiers may require a broader project-owner model.
6. **Labor review:** Requiring consequence review without substantive duties may be too weak; prescribing duties may exceed SymK jurisdiction.
7. **Ontology:** SymK’s own minimum ontology cannot be finalized before 2.1 and 2.5.
8. **Logic:** The minimum universal constraints on contradiction, uncertainty, and nonmonotonic revision require later formalization.
