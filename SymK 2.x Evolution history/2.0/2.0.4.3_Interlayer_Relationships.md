# 2.0.4.3 — Interlayer Relationships

**Status:** Substep-Accepted  
**Parent work package:** `2.0.4 — Layer-Boundary Analysis`  
**Depends on:** Substep-Accepted `2.0.4.1` and `2.0.4.2`  
**Decision under preparation:** `SYMK-2X-DR-005`
**Accepted by/date:** Project steward, 2026-08-18

## 1. Question

> Which relationships allow commitments and artifacts to move among SymK, derived projects, engineering, implementation, and operation without confusing constraint, meaning, representation, realization, or authority?

## 2. Governing distinction

Interlayer movement is not one generic operation called inheritance. Different relationships preserve different things and confer different authority.

The minimum vocabulary must distinguish:

- **inheritance** of governing commitments;
- **specialization** of meaning within a narrower scope;
- **instantiation** of a general concept in a particular case;
- **composition** of several concepts or artifacts;
- **role adoption** within a context;
- **projection** into another representation;
- **implementation** of a selected design;
- **local adoption** by an organization or deployment;
- **challenge** supported by evidence.

The exact protocols, serialization formats, and compatibility algorithms remain deferred to later stages.

## 3. Relationship matrix

| Relationship | Source → target | What it preserves or establishes | What it may add | What it does not confer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inheritance | Governing parent → declared child | Applicable parent commitments, precedence, lineage, and challenge duties | Child commitments within narrower jurisdiction | Ownership of the parent; permission to weaken it silently |
| Specialization | General concept → narrower concept | Parent meaning and all applicable invariants | Domain restrictions, distinctions, relations, and stronger conditions | Identity with the parent; permission to contradict it |
| Instantiation | Concept or specification → particular | Classification under the governing concept or satisfaction of a specification | Particular values, evidence, context, and history | Proof that every broader epistemic or normative claim is true |
| Composition | Several concepts or artifacts → composite | Declared contribution of each component | Relations, coordination, and emergent composite behavior | Subtype status; transfer of one component's authority to the whole |
| Role adoption | Governed role → participant-in-context | Duties, permissions, and expectations of that role in scope | Contextual assignment, term, competence evidence, and limits | Permanent identity; authority outside the role and context |
| Projection | Concept or artifact → representation | Selected meaning or structure declared by the mapping | Format-specific structure and operational constraints | Exhaustive equivalence; authority to redefine the source |
| Implementation | Specification or architecture → realization | Selected behavior and constraints to the extent demonstrated | Technology, optimization, internal mechanisms, and operational instrumentation | Conceptual, constitutional, or local authorization authority |
| Local adoption | External or project artifact → organization/deployment | Selected version, applicability, and local commitment to use | Local controls, stronger restrictions, exceptions, and authorization | Ownership of the source; universal validity; automatic compatibility |
| Challenge | Evidence-bearing claimant → governed artifact | Traceable objection, affected scope, and reasons for reconsideration | Counterevidence, alternative, requested correction, or incompatibility declaration | Automatic amendment, exemption, or victory over the challenged authority |

## 4. Inheritance

### 4.1 Meaning

Inheritance is a governance relationship created when a child claims lineage or compatibility with a governing parent. It brings applicable parent commitments into the child's constraint set.

### 4.2 Minimum obligations

A child must declare:

- the parent artifact and version;
- the scope of inheritance;
- applicable inherited commitments;
- specializations and local additions;
- known incompatibilities and active challenges;
- and the status of its compatibility claim.

### 4.3 Boundary

Inheritance is not copying, directory placement, naming similarity, or automatic synchronization with every future parent version. A child inherits a declared version until governed migration occurs.

## 5. Specialization

### 5.1 Meaning

Specialization creates a narrower concept or commitment that preserves the governing meaning while adding domain or contextual determination.

```text
SymK Knowledge-related concept
    → LexBrain legal specialization
    → Composer product specialization
```

### 5.2 Valid specialization

A valid specialization may:

- narrow scope;
- add domain distinctions;
- impose stronger conditions;
- define domain relationships;
- and expose dimensions left open by the parent.

It must not make a parent invariant false while continuing to claim compatibility.

### 5.3 Boundary

Class-subclass notation may express part of specialization, but the governance relationship is not reducible to software or ontology inheritance. Meaning, scope, dissent, responsibility, and compatibility remain governed even when no technical class exists.

## 6. Instantiation

Instantiation applies a concept or specification to a particular entity, event, claim, capability, or artifact.

An object may instantiate a project concept without instantiating every broader colloquial use of the same word. Conformance evidence must identify which definition and version are being instantiated.

Instantiation does not turn classification into truth. A record typed as `KnowledgeAttribution` is an instance of the represented class under the chosen model; its type declaration alone does not establish that Knowledge has actually been achieved.

## 7. Composition

Composition combines several governed elements without asserting that the composite is a subtype of any component.

For example, a legal opinion may compose claims, evidence, representations, reasoning steps, authority assessments, and responsibility records. It is not thereby a subtype of Knowledge, Evidence, or Intelligence.

Composition must preserve:

- component identity and provenance where material;
- the relationship through which each component contributes;
- responsibility for the composite;
- and any new behavior or risk produced by the combination.

## 8. Role adoption

A participant adopts or is assigned a role within a governed context. Role adoption separates what a participant **is** from what it is authorized or expected to **do here**.

For example:

- a lawyer may act as validator for one legal proposition;
- an AI system may act as proposer or critic;
- a court may act as institutional authority;
- a machine process may act as structural verifier.

No role assignment establishes general competence, Intelligence, truth, responsibility, or authority outside its declared context.

## 9. Projection

Projection expresses selected dimensions of a governed source in another representation or structure.

Every material projection must identify:

- its source and source version;
- its purpose and audience;
- the dimensions preserved;
- transformations and added constraints;
- excluded or unexpressed dimensions;
- known semantic loss;
- and its authority status.

Projection may be human-readable, formal, graphical, executable, statistical, or physical. No format is intrinsically closer to the concept in a way that grants automatic precedence.

## 10. Implementation

Implementation concretely realizes an accepted specification or architecture. Its conformity is established by evidence, not by existence.

Implementation may introduce technically necessary choices, but material choices must remain traceable to:

- inherited constraints;
- project or architectural decisions;
- imported requirements;
- or explicitly declared implementation discretion.

An implementation discovery may challenge its design or governing concept through evidence. It does not silently amend them.

## 11. Local adoption

An organization or deployment adopts a declared version of a project artifact, standard, system, or policy for use within a local scope.

Adoption must distinguish:

- technical availability;
- project compatibility;
- organizational approval;
- professional authorization;
- and legal permissibility.

None entails all the others automatically.

## 12. Challenge

Challenge is the governed upward or lateral relationship through which evidence contests an inherited commitment, concept, design, implementation, or local decision.

A challenge must identify:

- the challenged artifact and version;
- the conflicting statement or consequence;
- the affected scope;
- evidence and reasoning;
- immediate risk or incompatibility;
- and the authority responsible for resolution.

Challenge preserves revisability without allowing silent divergence. Detailed challenge statuses and procedures belong to `2.3` and `2.6`.

## 13. Relationship direction map

```text
S0 Evidence and inquiry
    ───────────── challenge ────────────┐
                                        │
S1 SymK Constitution                    │
    │ inheritance                       │
S2 SymK concepts and corpus             │
    │ inheritance + specialization      │
P1/P2 Project Constitution and concepts │
    │ translation into obligations      │
E1 Engineering Charter                  │
    │ projection + design selection     │
E2 Specification and architecture       │
    │ implementation                    │
I1 Implementation                       │
    │ local adoption                    │
O1 Organization and deployment ─ evidence
```

Composition and role adoption operate laterally within any layer whose jurisdiction defines the relevant composite or role.

## 14. Multi-generation lineage

A subproject does not bypass its intermediate parent.

```text
SymK
    → LexBrain
        → Composer
            → one organizational deployment
```

Each generation owns its additions and adoption decisions, preserves its direct parent mapping, and remains traceable to higher ancestors. A conflict may originate at any generation and challenge the layer where the contested commitment is owned.

Compatibility with a child does not imply compatibility with every ancestor unless the complete lineage is declared and passes review.

## 15. Prohibited equivalences

The following equivalences are rejected:

- copying = inheritance;
- subclass syntax = complete semantic specialization;
- classification = truth;
- composition = subtype;
- capability = role authority;
- representation = represented concept;
- implementation = conformance;
- deployment = authorization;
- adoption = ownership;
- challenge = amendment;
- generated artifact = authoritative source;
- popularity or repeated use = ratification.

## 16. Relationship identification test

For any claimed interlayer relationship, ask:

1. What is the source artifact and version?
2. What is the target artifact and owning layer?
3. Is the target accepting constraints, narrowing meaning, representing, realizing, adopting, or contesting?
4. Which source commitments must remain preserved?
5. Which additions belong to the target?
6. Which authority, if any, is transferred? The default answer is none.
7. What evidence demonstrates the relationship?
8. What incompatibilities or semantic losses are declared?

## 17. Accepted 2.0.4.3 conclusion

> **Interlayer movement in SymK must use explicit, noninterchangeable relationships. Inheritance transmits applicable governing constraints; specialization narrows and enriches meaning; instantiation applies a concept to a particular; composition combines without implying subtype; role adoption assigns contextual duties and authority; projection represents selected dimensions; implementation realizes a design; local adoption authorizes scoped use; and challenge returns evidence without silently amending the challenged artifact. Each relationship must preserve source and target ownership, scope, version lineage, and known incompatibilities.**

This conclusion is accepted as the interlayer-relationship component under preparation for `SYMK-2X-DR-005`.
