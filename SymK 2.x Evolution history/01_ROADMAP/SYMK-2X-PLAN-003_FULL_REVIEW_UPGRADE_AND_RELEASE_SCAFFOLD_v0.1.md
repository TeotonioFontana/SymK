## 5. Project-name and identity fitness route

The name issue is material because DR-017 Stage-Accepts an identity broader than the historical Human–AI symbiotic protocol, while accepted Knowledge Engineering semantics deny that SymK engineers Knowledge or truth directly.

### 5.1 Required 2.1.11 review

The review must test at least four alternatives:

1. retain “SymK” and retain “Symbiotic Knowledge” as its current expansion;
2. retain “SymK” as an opaque proper name while retiring the historical expansion;
3. reinterpret the name under a semantically defensible expansion; or
4. adopt a successor framework name while preserving “SymK” as historical/project lineage or a bounded specialization.

### 5.2 Evaluation criteria

- fidelity to DR-017 and exact accepted concepts;
- clarity about the engineered object and bounded Knowledge Engineering jurisdiction;
- avoidance of Human–AI dyad, symmetry, mutual-benefit or Knowledge-as-artifact implications;
- newcomer comprehension;
- continuity, provenance and migration burden;
- distinction from DIA, implementations and derived projects;
- stable spoken/written use and identifier strategy;
- international/search/domain/trademark considerations after a semantic shortlist exists; and
- consequences for Constitution, packages, repositories, document identifiers and releases.

### 5.3 Gate rule

Before final 2.1 Stage-Acceptance, the project steward must either make a named identity/name decision or explicitly defer it with a stable placeholder, downstream owner, consequences and a rule preventing misleading expansion. Formal 2.2 may not pretend the issue is absent.

Physical renaming, if selected, is not performed by the semantic decision alone. 2.4 governs authority/status disposition; 2.8 governs controlled path, identifier, link and publication migration; 3.0 uses the accepted release identity.

## 8. Universal entry checklist

Every stage opening must identify:

- explicit opening authority and date;
- accepted predecessor checkpoint and unresolved inherited issues;
- exact frozen inputs and hashes;
- stage question, scope and exclusions;
- work packages, evidence products and tests;
- authority and decision limits;
- stop, reopening and escalation conditions;
- affected artifacts and derived projects;
- identifier continuity; and
- the fact that opening is not completion or acceptance.

## 9. Universal exit checklist

Every stage checkpoint must demonstrate:

1. complete work-package disposition;
2. decision and evidence traceability;
3. counterexample and compatibility review;
4. preserved dissent, tensions and deferred questions;
5. affected-artifact and project impact analysis;
6. canonical consistency and manifest verification;
7. exact decision object and competent authority;
8. explicit non-effects;
9. stage summary and next-stage transfer; and
10. no implied approval of the successor stage.
