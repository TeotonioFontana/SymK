# SymK 2.x Evolution Governance

## 1. Objective

This governance prevents the 2.x consolidation from becoming a sequence of silent rewrites. It governs the history of change; it does not pre-ratify the resulting SymK content.

## 2. Unit of controlled evolution

A material evolution item is any proposal that changes:

- SymK identity, purpose, or jurisdiction;
- a constitutional statement or authoritative interpretation;
- a governed concept or its scope;
- a normative hierarchy or inheritance relation;
- compatibility outcomes for a material class of projects;
- or the status, authority, or disposition of an existing artifact.

## 3. Required decision record

Every material decision must record:

1. identifier and title;
2. stage and status;
3. question;
4. historical formulations;
5. constitutional and foundational sources;
6. evidence and counterexamples;
7. alternatives considered;
8. adopted position and reasoning;
9. rejected or deferred alternatives;
10. consequences for existing documents;
11. consequences for derived projects;
12. preserved dissent and confidence;
13. deciding or ratifying authority;
14. effective version and date;
15. supersession or amendment lineage.

## 4. Change flow

```text
Question
  → source and evidence collection
  → alternatives
  → adversarial review and compatibility tests
  → proposed decision
  → recorded dissent and consequences
  → stage acceptance
  → controlled canonical migration
  → post-migration verification
```

No canonical migration should precede a proposed decision. No stage acceptance should erase unresolved dissent.

## 5. Authority

Until SymK 2.3 constitutes formal authority, all 2.0 conclusions remain **Proposed** or **Stage-Accepted Baselines**, not constitutionally Ratified norms. The human project steward may accept a stage baseline for continued work, but this provisional authority and its limits must be explicit.

## 6. Traceability

Every changed canonical artifact must cite its evolution decision identifiers. Every decision must point to the affected canonical artifacts. Git commits may supplement but never replace semantic lineage.

## 7. Dissent

Dissent is preserved when a materially different interpretation remains reasonable, the evidence is incomplete, or the adopted position carries known risk. Dissent must state what future evidence would strengthen, weaken, or resolve it.

## 8. Scope discipline

A stage may discover questions belonging to later stages. Such questions go to `DEFERRED_QUESTIONS.md`; they must not be solved incidentally merely because a current document touches them.

## 9. No silent cleanup

Renaming, relocating, merging, or reclassifying an artifact can change perceived authority. Such changes require a recorded disposition when they affect meaning, lineage, or discoverability.
