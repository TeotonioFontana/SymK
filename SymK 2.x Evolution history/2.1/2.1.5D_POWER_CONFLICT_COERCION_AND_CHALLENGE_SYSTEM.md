## 1. Question and analytical result

> How must SymK represent power, asymmetry, conflict, dissent, coercion, domination, manipulation, suppression, and challenge so that neither a cooperative label nor formal permission conceals constrained participation, formative control, affected-party exclusion, or failure to correct and repair?

Stream D supports a relational and layered power system rather than a binary opposition between free cooperation and non-cooperation.

The broadest working anchor is:

> **Power is a Context-, Scope-, relation-, and time-dependent capacity of an identified subject or organized arrangement to shape another subject's or a shared system's available options, agenda, participation, interpretation, conduct, conditions, distribution, attribution, exposure to consequences, or possibility of challenge and revision. Power may exist without current exercise, operate intentionally or structurally, produce different effects from those intended, and be legitimate, illegitimate, mixed, contested, or unresolved. Power does not by itself entail authority, domination, agency, responsibility, Intelligence, or successful control.**

Within that broad field:

> **Coercion is the material shaping of conduct through a credible threat of serious adverse consequence, force, or deliberate control or exploitation of constrained alternatives such that practical refusal is substantially impaired. Coercion is an occurrence attribution distinct from its justification, legality, authority, effectiveness, and responsibility.**

> **Domination is a durable or structurally reproduced power relation in which one subject or arrangement possesses materially consequential capacity for arbitrary, insufficiently answerable, or practically unchallengeable control over another's options, participation, standing, interpretation, development, or exposure to consequences, whether or not that capacity is exercised in every episode.**

> **Dissent is an attributable nonconcurrence with a prevailing, proposed, authorized, or recorded position that remains identifiable rather than being converted into silence, consensus, error, disloyalty, or absence.**

> **An effective challenge path is a governed possibility through which a materially relevant question, objection, counterevidence, refusal, impact claim, or frame challenge can enter consideration with practical access, protection, standing or representation, reception, reasoned response, preservation, and the capacity to produce review, restriction, correction, revision, or repair when warranted. It does not guarantee agreement or revision.**

The Stream D result is:

1. disagreement, dissent, bounded competition, material conflict, and unequal roles can remain compatible with actual Cooperation;
2. their compatibility depends on whether the shared undertaking, plural material contributions, mutual orientation, and relational connection persist at the claimed level;
3. coercion, domination, manipulation, suppression, or affected-party exclusion may coexist with the thin occurrence of Cooperation among some participants but defeat or materially restrict responsible SymK qualification;
4. complete compulsion may reduce a supposed participant to an actor, instrument, or constrained contributor when no contribution and orientation remain attributable at that subject's own level;
5. formal permission to challenge is insufficient without practical capacity, protection, uptake, response, preservation, and consequence;
6. authority, constraint, dependence, influence, asymmetry, and role differentiation must not be collapsed into coercion or domination; and
7. power analysis exposes interfaces for 2.1.6 but does not allocate responsibility, settle legitimate authority, or create constitutional duties.

All definitions are analytical candidates only. Final concept classification, responsibility semantics, constitutional force, and DR-013 remain unresolved.

## 2. Inherited constraints

Stream D inherits without reopening:

1. Cooperation is a relational process in the Product Vision value chain, not the final objective, an unconstrained good, or a participant trait.
2. Actual cooperative occurrence, governed qualification, outcome/value, attribution, assessment, status, authorization, and record remain distinct.
3. Participant, contributor, actor, resource, source, beneficiary, affected party, authority, assessor, Bearer, learner, and responsible party remain distinct roles.
4. A cooperative unit is an analytical boundary, not automatically a collective Intelligence, responsible party, legal person, or authority.
5. Unequal capability, contribution, benefit, Knowledge, role, or dependency does not alone establish domination or defeat Cooperation.
6. Knowledge and Intelligence do not entail authority, responsibility, cooperative fitness, challengeability, Education, or correct action.
7. Formation is value-neutral; architectures may create formative conditions and influence without automatically producing Training, Education, habit, or another effect.
8. Education and Training are distinct; compliance, credential, performance, or repetition does not establish Education.
9. The epistemic lifecycle is recursive; disagreement, challenge, consequence, correction, revision, preservation, and decay can reopen prior claims, statuses, and arrangements.
10. Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views must remain aligned where material, including power that one view makes visible and another suppresses.
11. Detailed agency, legitimate authority, duty, responsibility, accountability, remedy, liability, and legal or moral standing remain principally assigned to 2.1.6.
12. Domain-specific labor law, professional authority, institutional governance, pedagogy, sanction, consent, and conflict-resolution rules remain owned by applicable authorities and derived projects within the universal minimum.

## 3. The power-level separation

Statements such as “the participant had power,” “the system coerced the user,” “challenge was allowed,” or “the authority corrected the process” conceal different levels.

| Level | Working question | Prohibited substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Power source or basis | Which capability, resource, role, dependency, rule, infrastructure, Knowledge asymmetry, social position, or threat supports possible influence or control? | Possessing a resource proves exercised or legitimate power |
| Power capacity or configuration | Who or what can shape which options, agendas, participation, interpretations, conduct, distributions, consequences, or revisions within Scope? | Capacity proves intention, exercise, effect, authority, or responsibility |
| Power exercise or intervention | What direction, framing, ranking, withholding, incentive, sanction, threat, exclusion, design, refusal, or other act occurred? | An act produced its intended effect or was authorized |
| Exposure and practical alternatives | What options, dependencies, vulnerabilities, costs, refusal conditions, and protective resources confronted the affected subject? | Formal choice establishes a meaningful alternative |
| Power mechanism | Through which informational, technical, economic, institutional, legal, social, physical, psychological, or formative relation could influence occur? | Plausible mechanism proves coercion or effect |
| Power effect | What changed in conduct, options, attention, belief, participation, burden, distribution, standing, formation, or challenge? | Any compliance proves consent, Cooperation, Training success, or legitimacy |
| Qualification | Is the relation constrained, influential, authoritative, dependent, coercive, dominating, manipulative, suppressive, exploitative, justified, or unresolved under stated criteria? | One label settles every normative or legal question |
| Attribution and assessment | Who makes and evaluates the power claim using what evidence, alternatives, Scope, and uncertainty? | Assessment creates the underlying relation |
| Governance response | Was the relation permitted, authorized, restricted, challenged, suspended, corrected, revised, repaired, remedied, or prohibited? | Governance status proves truth, legitimacy, correction, or repair |
| Record and Projection | Which Representation preserves sources, capacities, exercises, alternatives, effects, qualification, dissent, responses, consequences, and lineage? | A permission flag, consent field, audit log, or policy is the actual power relation |

Different levels may coexist and conflict. A lawful authority may issue an ineffective direction. An interface designer may exercise formative power without direct contact. A participant may experience coercion even when the controller denies coercive intent. A record may state that challenge is available while implementation makes it unusable.

## 4. Power

### 4.1 Relational rather than possessed absolutely

Power is never exhausted by a subject label. The same participant may have strong epistemic influence, weak formal authority, high technical control, severe economic dependency, and effective challenge standing in different relations. A model, person, institution, policy, interface, market, or hybrid arrangement may shape different objects through different mechanisms.

A material power attribution should identify:

1. the proposed power holder or organized source;
2. the affected subject, group, relation, environment, or undertaking;
3. the object that may be shaped;
4. the source and mechanism of capacity;
5. Context, Scope, time, dependencies, and alternatives;
6. whether capacity was exercised and through which act or arrangement;
7. intended and observed effects, countereffects, resistance, and uncertainty;
8. affected parties and distributions of benefit, burden, risk, and standing;
9. authority, justification, challenge, correction, repair, and responsibility claims separately; and
10. the boundary and view through which the attribution is made.

### 4.2 Power without exercise and exercise without success

A credible sanction may shape conduct without being imposed. A gatekeeper can possess agenda power without excluding an item in the current meeting. A formally weak participant may influence a decision through trusted Knowledge. Conversely, an authority can direct and fail, a threat can be resisted, and a recommendation can be ignored.

SymK must therefore preserve:

```text
power basis -> possible capacity -> possible exercise -> possible effect
```

No arrow is automatic, and qualification or responsibility does not follow merely from a later arrow.

### 4.3 Productive, protective, and harmful power

Power can enable inquiry, protect vulnerable participants, coordinate emergencies, allocate scarce attention, interrupt unsafe action, preserve dissent, and require correction. It can also conceal alternatives, impose burdens, exploit dependency, erase identity, train silence, and prevent repair. Calling all power harmful makes protective governance unintelligible; calling power neutral hides patterned consequences and formative effects.

## 5. Constraint, influence, authority, dependence, and vulnerability

| Distinction | Stream D working meaning | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Constraint | A condition that limits, channels, delays, prices, or removes possible action or participation | May be physical, technical, informational, economic, institutional, legal, social, temporal, or self-imposed; does not require a controller or threat |
| Influence | A relation that changes the likelihood, salience, interpretation, preference, or conduct of another through reasons, information, example, framing, design, expectation, incentive, reputation, or pressure | May be transparent or concealed, welcome or resisted, legitimate or manipulative; need not substantially impair refusal |
| Authority | Recognized normative, institutional, legal, professional, or procedural standing to decide, direct, permit, prohibit, allocate, or assess within Scope | Does not entail Knowledge, truth, correctness, effective control, Intelligence, unlimited jurisdiction, legitimacy of every act, or responsibility for every effect |
| Authorization | A governed permission for an identified subject to perform an act or occupy a role within conditions | Does not prove capability, occurrence, outcome, authority over other questions, or responsible exercise |
| Dependence | A relation in which a subject's capability, options, continuity, welfare, standing, or participation materially relies on another subject, resource, system, or arrangement | May be mutual, asymmetric, chosen, inherited, beneficial, risky, or unavoidable; does not alone establish coercion or component identity |
| Vulnerability | Material susceptibility to loss, burden, harm, exclusion, misrepresentation, or inability to recover when a dependency, threat, error, or power is exercised | Does not imply weakness, incapacity, consent, participant status, or responsibility for exploitation |
| Incentive | A prospective benefit, burden, reward, or cost that changes reasons or expected consequences | May influence without coercing; severity, alternatives, dependency, source, transparency, and cumulative effect matter |
| Sanction | An adverse consequence attached to conduct or status under an asserted rule or power | May be authorized, unauthorized, proportionate, coercive, protective, punitive, or contested; the label does not settle justification |

The existence of authority does not eliminate coercion analysis. An authorized sanction may still substantially impair practical refusal; whether it is justified is a separate question. Conversely, a demanding rule, professional obligation, scarcity, interface limitation, or unattractive alternative is not automatically coercion.

## 6. Asymmetry and justified role differentiation

Asymmetry is descriptive: participants differ in capability, Knowledge, access, visibility, resources, authority, contribution, dependency, risk, benefit, replaceability, or challenge capacity. Formal equality can coexist with severe practical asymmetry, and unequal roles can coexist with responsible Cooperation.

Stream D does not decide which asymmetries are finally justified. It proposes a qualification test for later governance. A role or power differentiation is more supportable when its claim identifies:

- a bounded undertaking and function rather than origin-based privilege;
- relevant capability, evidence, law, need, or protective purpose;
- declared Scope, limits, duration, dependencies, and conflicts of interest;
- proportionality between the differentiation and its purpose or consequences;
- separation of capability, role, authority, authorization, and responsibility;
- meaningful review, challenge, appeal, reassessment, and termination conditions;
- effects on nonparticipants and affected parties;
- alternatives considered, including less restrictive arrangements;
- and evidence that practical implementation does not contradict the declared basis.

Failure of one or more conditions does not mechanically prove domination. It makes the justification incomplete or challenged. Exact legitimacy, duty, and authority standards belong to 2.1.6 and applicable Domains.

## 7. Coercion

### 7.1 Working evidence profile

A material coercion attribution should examine:

1. the conduct or participation sought;
2. the source of the threat, force, dependency control, or constrained alternative;
3. the adverse consequence and its credibility, severity, proximity, and cumulative character;
4. the affected subject's actual alternatives, resources, time, information, dependency, and vulnerability;
5. whether refusal, delay, appeal, representation, or exit was practically available;
6. whether the source deliberately created, controlled, exploited, or merely encountered the constraint;
7. resulting conduct and whether it remained attributable as the subject's own contribution;
8. authority and asserted justification separately from occurrence;
9. other affected parties and displaced burdens; and
10. uncertainty, resistance, counterevidence, consequence, and repair.

No single consent checkbox, contract, threat phrase, sanction, dependency, or subjective report settles every case. Human evidence of experienced compulsion, scientific evidence about alternatives and effects, and engineering evidence of gates, defaults, penalties, and access must be reconciled.

### 7.2 Coercion and participation

Coercion does not yield one universal occurrence rule:

- If a subject performs only as the controller's instrument and no materially relevant contribution or mutual orientation remains attributable at the subject's level, that subject may be an actor or constrained contributor rather than a Cooperation Participant.
- If the subject retains a material contribution and sufficient orientation despite impaired refusal, the thin cooperative occurrence may remain supportable, while responsible qualification is challenged or fails.
- Coercion among internal members does not disappear because their institution participates externally.
- Coercion of a nonparticipant or affected party can disqualify responsible qualification even when immediate participants cooperate voluntarily.

This preserves diagnosis without sanitizing coercion or defining every compelled relation as cooperation.

### 7.3 Justification is separate

Emergency restraint, legal compulsion, protective intervention, professional duty, and enforcement may be argued to be justified in some Domains. The occurrence of coercive pressure, its authorization, its justification, its proportionality, its responsible source, and its consequences remain separate claims. Stream D neither bans nor authorizes any class by label.

## 8. Domination

Domination concerns a standing relation of control rather than one successful command. Indicators may include:

- unilateral capacity to alter essential options or status;
- practical immunity from challenge, review, correction, or exit;
- dependence intentionally produced or exploited without adequate protection;
- arbitrary or undisclosed decision criteria;
- agenda, classification, memory, or representation controlled by one side;
- retaliation, replaceability, surveillance, or opacity that chills dissent;
- inability of affected subjects to obtain reasons, representation, or remedy;
- institutional reproduction across personnel changes;
- and cumulative formative effects that normalize inferiority, silence, concealment, or helplessness.

Power asymmetry, hierarchy, expertise, coordination, dependency, or authority alone does not establish domination. A relation becomes more domination-like as materially consequential control becomes arbitrary, structurally durable, insufficiently answerable, and practically unchallengeable.

An unexercised dominating capacity can shape conduct through anticipation. Conversely, one severe coercive act may occur without a durable domination relation. Both claims require their own evidence and Scope.

## 9. Manipulation, suppression, agenda control, and exploitation

### 9.1 Manipulation

Manipulation is purposive steering that materially relies on concealment, deception, bypass of reflective consideration, or exploitation of a known vulnerability in ways that impair informed and revisable participation. Persuasion, teaching, interface design, recommendation, and behavioral influence are not automatically manipulation. Relevant distinctions include transparency, grounds, ability to inspect and contest, alignment between declared and operative purpose, dependency, vulnerability, and consequence.

### 9.2 Suppression

Suppression is the prevention, reduction, penalization, erasure, or material exclusion of expression, evidence, disagreement, participation, memory, or challenge. A contribution can be suppressed before expression, during reception, through ranking or omission, after recording, or by retaliation. Restricting irrelevant, abusive, unsafe, unlawful, or duplicative material may be justified, but the restriction, authority, criteria, evidence, appeal, and loss must remain visible.

Silence is therefore ambiguous. It may express agreement, abstention, uncertainty, fear, incapacity, exclusion, strategic withdrawal, or successful suppression.

### 9.3 Agenda control

Agenda control shapes which questions, alternatives, evidence, harms, affected parties, time horizons, and decision criteria can enter the undertaking. It may be necessary for bounded work, but it becomes a material power issue when exclusions are concealed, self-protective, practically unchallengeable, or presented as absence of disagreement.

### 9.4 Exploitation

Exploitation is a qualification claim that a subject or arrangement takes unfair or insufficiently answerable advantage of another's contribution, dependency, vulnerability, constrained alternatives, exclusion, or exposure to consequences. Unequal benefit is not sufficient by itself. The standard of unfairness, entitlement, remedy, and responsibility belongs to later governance and Domains; Stream D preserves the relation and evidence needed to ask the question.

## 10. Disagreement, dissent, conflict, and competition

| Distinction | Working meaning | Relation to Cooperation |
|---|---|---|
| Difference | Non-identity of perspective, capability, representation, interest, role, value, or position | Constitutive possibility for plural contribution; neither conflict nor benefit follows automatically |
| Disagreement | Incompatibility or nonconvergence among claims, judgments, interpretations, preferences, predictions, or decisions | Compatible and often epistemically productive; does not require hostility or action |
| Dissent | Identifiable nonconcurrence relative to a prevailing, proposed, authorized, or recorded position | Preserved dissent can be a material contribution and lifecycle input; silence or minority status must not erase it |
| Contestation | Active questioning of a claim, rule, classification, objective, role, authority, procedure, or frame | May instantiate challenge; can strengthen, delay, redirect, or suspend an undertaking |
| Conflict | Material incompatibility among goals, interests, claims, roles, values, actions, resource uses, identities, or consequences such that pursuit or resolution affects another | Can occur within, across, or outside Cooperation; it does not entail harm, coercion, breakdown, or non-cooperation |
| Competition | Structured pursuit of scarce, exclusive, or comparative success | Compatible when bounded and subordinate to a wider shared undertaking; may become an independent or adversarial relation when the wider orientation collapses |
| Opposition | Conduct directed against another proposal, act, control, or outcome | Can be a contribution when oriented to the shared undertaking or protection of affected parties |
| Refusal | Nonperformance, withdrawal, withholding, or rejection of a requested contribution, role, classification, or condition | May interrupt Cooperation, protect its responsible qualification, or expose that participation was never meaningful |
| Breakdown | Material failure of the relational organization needed for the claimed cooperative occurrence or continuing relation | Must be distinguished from conflict inside a relation; may be local, temporary, partial, or terminal |
| Suspension/termination | Governed or actual pause/end of an episode, role, participation, or relation | Does not rewrite earlier Cooperation, conflict, coercion, contribution, or consequence |

### 10.1 Conflict is not harm

Conflict can be peaceful, principled, productive, irresolvable, destructive, or latent. Harm can occur without conflict through neglect, exclusion, compliant error, or structural design. Suppressing visible conflict can increase hidden harm.

### 10.2 Dissent is not mere error

Dissent can be mistaken, unsupported, strategic, disruptive, or abusive, but its minority or unauthorized status does not establish those properties. It requires epistemic and governance assessment rather than conversion into a status of error. Preservation does not require adoption.

### 10.3 Competition can be nested

Competing hypotheses, teams, suppliers, models, or designs may contribute to a wider inquiry when rules, evidence, integration, affected parties, and shared orientation remain visible. If incentives make defeating rivals the operative undertaking and evidence, safety, or affected parties become obstacles, the wider Cooperation claim may fail even if coordination continues.

## 11. Compatibility with Cooperation

Conflict, competition, asymmetry, disagreement, or dissent remain compatible with actual Cooperation when the evidence still supports:

1. identifiable plural participants at stable explanatory levels;
2. a bounded shared undertaking that remains operative despite differences;
3. material contributions from more than one participant, including possible critique, refusal, prevention, or correction;
4. mutual orientation sufficient to connect the contributions to the undertaking and relevant participant relations;
5. relational organization rather than coincidence, isolated opposition, or post hoc aggregation; and
6. a declared boundary explaining which relation is cooperative and which relation is conflictual, competitive, coercive, or external.

They become incompatible with the claimed occurrence when, within that Scope, no common undertaking remains, other parties cease to be contributors and become only obstacles or targets, contributions no longer orient to the shared undertaking, or the relational organization has broken down. The same subjects can cooperate in one undertaking while competing or conflicting in another.

Responsible SymK qualification requires additional, consequence-proportional evidence concerning:

- relevant power sources, asymmetries, dependencies, vulnerabilities, and practical alternatives;
- basis and limits of roles, authority, sanctions, and exclusions;
- dissent, affected parties, hidden curricula, agenda control, and displaced burdens;
- practical challenge, representation, review, correction, restriction, exit, and repair;
- outcome, value, harm, benefit, and uncertainty independently from occurrence;
- and historical change, retaliation, decay, and unresolved conflict.

Coercion, domination, manipulation, suppression, exploitation, or collusion need not be erased from the occurrence description to defeat responsible qualification. A status can be `actual relation, responsible qualification failed or challenged` rather than the misleading alternatives `not cooperation` or `acceptable cooperation`.

## 12. Compatibility with Education and Formation

### 12.1 Conflict and dissent as formative material

Disagreement and conflict can expose assumptions, interests, identities, consequences, and power. They may support Learning or Education when the learner can examine grounds, encounter material difference, test conduct, receive response, revise judgment, and participate in correction or repair. Exposure to conflict alone proves no formative effect or educational achievement.

### 12.2 Challengeability and Education

The Stream C Education candidate requires challengeability. Stream D refines this: a curriculum, trainer, institution, model, interface, norm, classification, or educational objective must be practically contestable at an appropriate level. The learner need not personally hold every capability if a governed arrangement supplies accessible representation and effective challenge. Ceremonial human oversight, inaccessible appeal, or retaliation does not satisfy the condition.

### 12.3 Coercive Training and limited Learning

A Training program can produce reliable behavior through threat, dependency, surveillance, reward control, or suppression. A narrow trained effect, conditioning, socialization, adaptation, or learned avoidance may occur. If the program destroys practical challenge capacity, conceals grounds, prevents revision, or forms compliance as the only safe response, it does not thereby establish Education and may contradict the current educational profile.

### 12.4 Integration versus assimilation

Integration preserves relevant identity, difference, perspective, language, dissent, sources, and the capacity to revise relations. Assimilation makes participation conditional on erasure or unchallengeable conformity. Some common practices and representations may be needed for shared work; the power, necessity, alternatives, loss, affected parties, and revision path determine whether the arrangement remains supportable.

### 12.5 Formative power and hidden curriculum

Objectives, examples, rankings, defaults, response timing, access, metrics, rewards, sanctions, tolerated violations, role permissions, and memory practices may exert formative influence. Their existence does not prove an effect. Where effects are claimed, Stream C's learner, mechanism, persistence, transfer, alternative-explanation, and level evidence remain required.

## 13. The challenge system

Challengeability must not be represented as one Boolean property. Stream D distinguishes:

| Layer | Question | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|
| Notice and legibility | Can the subject identify the claim, rule, objective, decision, system boundary, evidence, authority, and available path? | Opaque criteria or invisible automated action |
| Formal permission | Is objection, dissent, refusal, appeal, or frame challenge allowed in stated governance? | Policy forbids challenge |
| Access and opportunity | Can the subject reach the path in time, language, format, location, and technical conditions? | Channel exists but is inaccessible or too late |
| Practical capacity and support | Does the subject have information, skill, time, resources, assistance, representation, and alternatives needed to challenge? | Permission exists but dependency or complexity makes use impossible |
| Protection | Can challenge occur without disproportionate retaliation, exclusion, loss, surveillance, or formative penalty? | Speaking is formally allowed but predictably punished |
| Standing or representation | Can the issue enter consideration from a participant, affected party, source, proxy, advocate, assessor, or other qualified relation? | Nonparticipants bearing harm are declared irrelevant |
| Reception and preservation | Is the challenge received, acknowledged, attributable, preserved, and protected from silent deletion or consensus rewriting? | Dissent disappears from the record |
| Evaluation | Are grounds, counterevidence, alternatives, conflicts, and consequences substantively examined under declared criteria? | Challenge is counted but not considered |
| Reasoned response | Are acceptance, rejection, restriction, deferral, or uncertainty explained within authority and Scope? | Authority repeats status without reasons |
| Corrective consequence | Can a warranted challenge change a claim, status, reliance, role, intervention, implementation, sanction, or undertaking? | Review can never alter the operative system |
| Repair or remedy interface | Can prior harm, exclusion, dependency, lost standing, or damaged relation be addressed beyond future correction? | Rule changes but burden remains entirely with affected parties |
| Appeal, recurrence, and learning | Can unresolved dissent, new evidence, repeated failure, or consequence reopen the matter? | One closure makes the frame permanently immune |

These layers support four distinct attributions:

1. **challenge permission** — a declared allowance;
2. **challenge capacity** — a subject's or represented arrangement's practical ability;
3. **effective challenge path** — a governed path capable of substantive uptake and consequence;
4. **successful challenge** — a particular challenge produced an appropriate response, which may be reasoned rejection as well as correction or revision.

Actual revision is not required merely because a challenge exists. A supportable rejection may preserve challengeability if grounds, authority, counterevidence, dissent, appeal, and consequences remain visible. Conversely, one accepted challenge does not prove the path is generally effective.

## 14. Voice, refusal, exit, and resistance

Voice, refusal, exit, and resistance are related but non-substitutable:

- **voice** expresses a position inside or toward the undertaking;
- **refusal** withholds a requested act, role, classification, or condition;
- **exit** ends or reduces participation or dependency;
- **resistance** opposes a power exercise, arrangement, or consequence;
- **representation** enables another subject or institution to advance a challenge where direct capacity is absent;
- **whistleblowing** discloses concealed material concern outside ordinary channels when internal paths are ineffective or implicated.

Meaningful exit is not universally possible or sufficient. Essential services, employment, public systems, professional duties, monopoly infrastructure, irreversible effects, and nonparticipant harms can make “leave if you disagree” a concealment of power. Effective internal challenge and external review may be necessary even where exit exists. Exact rights and duties remain for 2.1.6 and applicable authorities.

Refusal can be a material contribution when it prevents harm or exposes false premises. It can also end the contribution needed for the current cooperative occurrence. The assessment must preserve both effects rather than equating cooperation with obedience.

## 15. Affected-party standing and exclusion

An affected party may bear risk, burden, exclusion, lost opportunity, surveillance, displacement, or another consequence without participating in the undertaking. Nonparticipation does not erase material relevance.

Stream D establishes only a standing interface:

> A supportable responsible-cooperation or Education assessment must identify materially affected parties and determine whether their evidence, interests, burdens, and challenge require direct participation, representation, consultation, review, protection, or another governed path proportionate to consequence and practical capacity.

This statement does not decide who has legal standing, veto power, decision authority, remedy, or responsibility. It prevents the participant boundary from becoming a mechanism for excluding consequences.

Affected-party exclusion can occur through missing data, inaccessible language, agenda rules, model classifications, proxy selection, timing, confidentiality, cost, technical interfaces, or the claim that only contributors matter. Engineering systems must preserve the exclusion and declared loss where complete inclusion is impossible.

## 16. Hidden curriculum and formative power

A declared curriculum may teach challenge while the operative environment trains silence. Stream D applies the following diagnostic sequence:

```text
declared norm or objective
    compared with operative interfaces, incentives, metrics, examples, sanctions, defaults, and tolerated conduct
        yields a possible hidden-curriculum hypothesis
            tested for exposure, mechanism, learner-level effect, persistence, transfer, and consequence
                then assessed for Training, socialization, conditioning, habit, Learning, or Education
```

No architecture automatically educates or trains. Yet formative power cannot be dismissed because it is indirect or unintended. Material assessment should ask:

- who selected the objective, examples, reward, sanction, metric, default, and exception;
- which conduct is easy, visible, rewarded, delayed, costly, penalized, or impossible;
- what happens after dissent, refusal, error, disclosure, or harm;
- whose perspective is represented, translated, ranked, or absent;
- whether the system forms concealment, dependency, deference, haste, false certainty, challenge, repair, or another disposition;
- and whether power effects differ across participant and affected-party levels.

The subject exercising design or institutional power, the technical actor executing a rule, the learner affected, the assessor, the authority, and the responsible party remain separate.

## 17. Substrate- and level-sensitive power

| Level | Possible power and challenge evidence | Boundary warning |
|---|---|---|
| Individual | Expertise, persuasion, control of resources, threat, sanction, role, reputation, care, refusal, dissent, testimony, appeal, or resistance | Human intention or reported consent does not settle structural alternatives or effects |
| Artificial component or model | Ranking, filtering, framing, generation, prediction, access mediation, or behavior that shapes decisions | Model output does not by itself establish authority, intention, agency, coercion, responsibility, or a stable power holder |
| Configured artificial system | Persistent gates, policies, memory, optimization, adaptive control, monitoring, sanctions, or interaction design attributable to the configured system | Institutional design and operator dependencies must not be hidden inside “the AI decided” |
| Collective or team | Norms, peer pressure, agenda, selection, review, informal sanctions, representation, dissent protection, or group control | Member power and team-level power require separate attribution; majority status is not consensus or legitimacy |
| Institution | Law, policy, credentials, employment, ownership, infrastructure, persistent roles, memory, sanction, appeal, remedy, and external review | Legal identity or authority does not prove epistemic correctness, internal Cooperation, practical challenge, or responsible exercise |
| Distributed arrangement | Protocol control, standards, network effects, access, moderation, interoperability, forkability, dependency, and dispersed governance | Absence of one controller does not imply absence of power, domination, responsibility interfaces, or affected parties |
| Hybrid | Coupled human–artificial agenda, recommendation, review, authorization, monitoring, feedback, and challenge paths | Ceremonial human approval does not absorb system power or responsibility; component effects must not be copied automatically to the hybrid |

Power can be distributed without disappearing. A relation may have no single controller while defaults, dependencies, protocols, markets, and institutions jointly constrain options. Attribution must identify the lowest organized explanatory level without omitting constitutive coupling.

## 18. Alternatives considered

### A — Define Cooperation so coercion, domination, collusion, or harm can never occur within it

**Rejected.** This morally sanitizes harmful plural capacity, hides internal and external relations, and makes responsible qualification impossible to diagnose. The occurrence/qualification/outcome separation is retained.

### B — Any coercion eliminates Cooperation automatically

**Rejected as a universal occurrence rule.** Complete compulsion may defeat participant status, but impaired refusal can coexist with material contribution and mutual orientation. Coercion normally challenges responsible qualification and requires exact level evidence.

### C — Explicit consent or unrestricted exit is necessary for every cooperative occurrence

**Rejected as universal.** Emergency, institutional, professional, public, and legally structured relations may lack a discrete consent event or unrestricted exit. Participation basis, practical alternatives, challenge, authority, and consequence remain mandatory evidence where material.

### D — Conflict, competition, or dissent proves cooperative failure

**Rejected.** Each can contribute to a bounded shared undertaking. The test is whether the relational nucleus persists and whether responsible qualification survives, not whether harmony or consensus appears.

### E — Formal challenge permission is sufficient

**Rejected.** Permission without access, capacity, protection, standing, reception, evaluation, response, preservation, and possible consequence is ceremonial.

### F — Every influence or asymmetric role is manipulation or domination

**Rejected.** Reasons, expertise, coordination, protection, bounded authority, and differentiated roles can be supportable. Their source, Scope, alternatives, answerability, effect, and challenge must remain visible.

### G — Authority eliminates the need to analyze coercion or power

**Rejected.** Authority, authorization, coercion occurrence, justification, responsibility, and outcome are distinct. Legitimate authority may constrain; unauthorized actors may exercise substantial power.

### H — Challengeability requires every challenge to change the decision

**Rejected.** Effective challenge requires substantive entry and possible consequence, not automatic success. Reasoned rejection can be supportable; silent immunity cannot.

### I — Distributed or technical power has no bearer and therefore no governance relevance

**Rejected.** Power may be attributable to configured systems, institutions, protocols, or hybrid arrangements without anthropomorphic intention. Responsibility allocation remains a later question.

## 20. Three-view preservation obligations

### 20.1 Human View

The Human View should explain:

- how participants and affected parties experience and interpret power, alternatives, dependency, conflict, safety, refusal, voice, challenge, response, and repair;
- what the undertaking, authority, role, sanction, norm, and hidden curriculum mean to them;
- which identities, languages, perspectives, harms, and unresolved dissent risk erasure;
- why a relation is described as influential, coercive, dominating, manipulative, suppressive, protective, or justified;
- and what the account cannot establish by testimony or narrative alone.

### 20.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View should preserve, where material:

- observable capacities, interventions, alternatives, incentives, dependencies, threats, behavior, effects, distributions, and temporal patterns;
- methods for distinguishing influence from coercion, authority from effect, disagreement from breakdown, and silence from agreement;
- counterfactual, comparative, longitudinal, behavioral, institutional, network, and consequence evidence;
- sampling, measurement, population/system boundary, uncertainty, competing explanations, and defeat conditions;
- and evidence of chilling, selection, adaptation, hidden curriculum, retaliation, transfer, repair, or recurrence.

No one survey, output metric, consent event, complaint count, sanction record, or behavioral compliance score defines power or challengeability universally.

### 20.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View should eventually preserve:

- power source, holder/configuration, affected subject, object, mechanism, exercise, alternatives, effect, qualification, authority claim, and record separately;
- participant, affected party, represented subject, assessor, authority, operator, designer, beneficiary, and future responsible party distinctly;
- roles, permissions, gates, defaults, rankings, exclusions, incentives, sanctions, dependencies, appeals, time limits, representation, and escalation paths;
- challenge notice, access, protection, reception, evaluation, response, consequence, dissent, appeal, correction, and repair interfaces;
- Context, Scope, time, versions, evidence, uncertainty, view mismatch, Projection loss, and lineage;
- and enough composition to attribute distributed and hybrid power without assigning every effect to the visible model or final approver.

No schema, ontology, status vocabulary, risk score, consent model, workflow, access-control system, audit format, or implementation is selected here.

### 20.4 Alignment test

A system fails alignment when human evidence reports practical compulsion, scientific evidence measures only performance, and engineering records preserve only `consent=true`, `authorized=true`, or `challenge_available=true`. The mismatch, omitted alternatives, affected use, current status, uncertainty, and correction path must remain explicit.

The vision's requirements that disagreement survive canonicalization, consequences challenge authority, roles remain revisable, responsibility remain visible, and persistent systems preserve challenge capacity are supported. Cooperation remains the process rather than the objective. Better answers, speed, or cost cannot sanitize coercion, suppression, affected-party exclusion, or responsibility loss.
