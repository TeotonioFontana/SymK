## 1. Opening decision

After Stage-Acceptance of revised DR-011, the project steward asked whether SymK should have semantic packages for Knowledge or knowledge objects and then instructed the project to proceed. That instruction formally opens work package 2.1.4 for evidence-controlled analysis of Knowledge Engineering, epistemic conditions, claims, grounds, assets, status, reliance, Representation, Projection, and lifecycle.

The package question is admitted as a boundary test, not as a pre-approved architecture. 2.1.4 must establish which meanings SymK must preserve before 2.5 decides final package anatomy and before 2.9 selects schemas, storage, APIs, formal profiles, or lowering mechanisms.

This opening authorizes analysis. It does not create `symk.semantic.knowledge`, admit `KnowledgeObject`, accept the current `02_CORE_MODEL` scaffold, or authorize runtime storage or derived-project changes.

## 5. Inherited constraints — not questions reopened by default

2.1.4 inherits these Stage-Accepted constraints:

1. Knowledge is a scoped plural epistemic achievement attributable to a Bearer.
2. Knowledge is not identical to a knowledge claim, Knowledge attribution, Knowledge asset, Representation, epistemic status, acceptance, or reliance authorization.
3. Actual bearing, attribution claim, assessment, authority for use, and stored record are distinct.
4. A document, graph, database, model, corpus, or repository does not become Knowledge by storage, structure, naming, or institutional acceptance.
5. Representation exposes selected aspects without becoming identical to its subject.
6. Projection must make additions, omissions, transformations, and semantic loss visible.
7. Domain specializations belong to the relevant semantic authority; legal or medical values do not become SymK universals through implementation.
8. Final package anatomy belongs to 2.5, and formal-language and lowering choices belong to 2.5/2.9.

A contradiction discovered during 2.1.4 must invoke the explicit DR-011 reopening conditions rather than silently weakening these constraints.

## 6. Package and object boundary to be tested

The opening question requires at least four levels to remain separate:

```text
Knowledge
    actual epistemic achievement of a Bearer

Knowledge semantics
    governed meaning, modes, attribution rules, and prohibitions

Epistemic artifacts
    claims, grounds, assessments, assets, records, and representations

Operational instances and projections
    stored records and platform-specific reductions
```

A semantic package may govern meanings at the second and third levels. It does not contain actual Knowledge or replace the Bearer relation. A particular runtime record conforms to, references, or projects those semantics; it is not itself a semantic package.

`KnowledgeObject` is therefore a controlled ambiguity. 2.1.4 must determine whether it has any useful engineering meaning, whether it should be replaced by more precise terms such as `KnowledgeAsset` or `EpistemicRecord`, and whether a Document is best related to Representation or to an epistemic-asset role rather than treated as a specialization of Knowledge.

## 7. Principal analytical hazards

- **Container reification:** treating Knowledge as something stored inside a file, graph, package, or object.
- **Package-instance collapse:** confusing a governed semantic declaration with an individual epistemic record.
- **Claim-truth collapse:** treating asserted, accepted, verified, or authoritative status as truth or Knowledge.
- **Ground-evidence collapse:** treating any attached source as sufficient evidence independently of the claim, method, Context, and assessor.
- **Provenance-warrant collapse:** treating traceability as epistemic adequacy.
- **Formal-validity substitution:** treating schema conformance or valid inference from premises as truth-connected success.
- **Reliance substitution:** treating permission or practical necessity to act as Knowledge.
- **Document specialization error:** making Document a subtype of Knowledge instead of distinguishing its representational and evidentiary roles.
- **Domain leakage:** importing legal, medical, organizational, or platform statuses as universal epistemic statuses.
- **Projection concealment:** losing source, Scope, uncertainty, disagreement, or supersession without declaring it.
- **Lifecycle linearity:** assuming acquisition ends in stable storage instead of challenge, application, consequence, revision, and retirement.
- **Premature implementation:** selecting JSON, OWL, SHACL, Python, MySQL, SharePoint, AWS, or another carrier before the semantic obligations are decided.

## 9. Governed analysis sequence

### A. Direct object and epistemic-artifact boundary

- determine what Knowledge Engineering acts on if it cannot manufacture Knowledge or truth directly;
- distinguish Knowledge, Knowledge semantics, claim, attribution, ground, evidence, asset, record, Representation, and Document;
- test `KnowledgeObject` and the need for a Knowledge semantic package without fixing package anatomy;
- propose initial dispositions subject to later streams.

### B. Epistemic-condition and lifecycle model

- model inquiry, acquisition, construction, expression, preservation, communication, assessment, challenge, application, consequence, revision, supersession, and retirement;
- distinguish processes, states, relations, roles, and artifacts;
- identify where practical and familiarity Knowledge resist artifact-centered models.

### C. Grounds, evidence, provenance, uncertainty, disagreement, and status

- distinguish ground from evidence role, source, provenance, confidence, validation, and authority;
- preserve incompatible claims and typed uncertainty;
- separate truth status, epistemic assessment, governance status, and reliance authorization.

### D. Representation, Projection, Ontology, Logic, and two-lane obligations

- define what representations must preserve for epistemic use;
- distinguish structural validity, logical validity, semantic adequacy, epistemic sufficiency, and operational usefulness;
- establish declared-loss and lineage requirements without choosing a language;
- state the minimum capabilities later semantic packages must provide.

### E. Consolidated stress test and consequences

- apply negative, edge, cross-domain, multi-representation, and runtime-projection cases;
- test Product Vision, DR-009 through DR-011, candidate A1/A4, DQ-001, DQ-018, DQ-022, both semantic lanes, and derived-project ownership;
- record Foundation Paper, constitutional, later-package, project, and engineering consequences;
- preserve dissent, confidence, and reopening conditions.

### F. DR-012 proposal checkpoint

Only after A–E pass may a consolidated Proposed `SYMK-2X-DR-012` be entered in the Decision Register and presented to the project steward. No intermediate analytical note has decision authority.

## 10. Mandatory counterexample set

At minimum, the analysis must explain:

1. a true claim accepted for the wrong reason;
2. a false claim with impeccable provenance and formal schema validity;
3. a richly structured document or graph labeled a `knowledge object`;
4. practical competence not recoverable from its procedure document;
5. two incompatible claims retained in one governed asset;
6. an authoritative source whose assertion is later defeated;
7. a correct generated answer without adequate grounding;
8. a valid inference from false or inapplicable premises;
9. emergency reliance on an uncertain claim without claiming Knowledge;
10. the same claim represented differently in Markdown, JSON, a database, and SharePoint;
11. a platform projection that drops provenance, uncertainty, or supersession;
12. legal and medical document-type assertions sharing a universal relation but not a value vocabulary;
13. one document playing evidentiary, instructional, historical, and operational roles at different times;
14. an asset superseded or retired without rewriting its historical status.
