## 1. Question and analytical result

> What non-empty ethical minimum must SymK itself preserve when responsibility, authority, consent, consequence, standing, challenge, and repair are distributed, without selecting a complete moral, political, or legal theory?

Stream E supports the following working answer:

> **A responsibility arrangement may claim SymK ethical compatibility only when, proportionately to material consequence, it keeps affected subjects and represented interests within view; exposes material Power, dependence, vulnerability, alternatives, foreseeable effects, uncertainty, severity, reversibility, and residual harm; does not manufacture legitimacy, consent, responsibility transfer, or absolution from type, origin, capability, authority, compliance, contract, notice, acceptance, approval, automation, outcome, or record; provides practical, protected, receivable, assessable, and consequential challenge and response paths; and preserves prevention, containment, correction, repair, remedy, review, appeal, and revision interfaces across the relevant lifecycle.**

> **This boundary does not determine a complete account of good, right, justice, autonomy, dignity, welfare, equality, desert, rights, personhood, liability, punishment, or legitimate political authority. It does not require consent for every action, give every affected party a veto, promise that every challenge succeeds, or prescribe one remedy. It requires that a material ethical question is neither erased nor falsely settled and that applicable Domain, organizational, professional, legal, political, and moral authority remains explicit, challengeable within its jurisdiction, and connected to capable response.**

The candidate minimum has seven mutually supporting commitments:

1. **Affected-subject non-erasure:** material consequence does not become irrelevant because a subject lacks a contract, account, role, Participation, recognized voice, legal personality, technical identity, or direct causal Contribution.
2. **Non-privilege and non-substitution:** Human, artificial, collective, institutional, or hybrid type; capability; authority; compliance; consent record; outcome; and status are never sufficient by themselves to establish ethical adequacy or absolution.
3. **Consequence-sensitive due regard:** material Power, dependence, vulnerability, alternatives, foreseeability, uncertainty, severity, reversibility, distribution, and residual effects must be investigated and governed with rigor proportionate to their possible consequences.
4. **No fabricated consent:** notice, access, continued use, silence, compliance, signature, click, contract, or recorded acceptance does not establish understanding, voluntariness, practical refusal, scope, continuing agreement, or valid authorization by itself.
5. **Effective challenge and accountable response:** a material claim, decision, allocation, or consequence must be challengeable through a practical and protected path that can receive Evidence, reach a capable forum, produce a reasoned response, and make correction or other response possible.
6. **Prevention-to-repair continuity:** responsibility cannot end at explanation, blame, punishment, payment, audit, or record. Prevention, containment, correction, repair, remedy, residual harm, recurrence, and revision remain distinct and connected where applicable.
7. **Visible jurisdiction and unresolved conflict:** SymK's minimum does not overwrite legitimate external authority. Competing duties, missing authority, incapable forums, unremediable effects, and unresolved incompatibilities remain visible rather than being converted into fictional discharge.

This is not an independent foundational ethical theory. It is a candidate constitutive qualification boundary for the Responsibility system and a candidate constitutional minimum for later 2.2 selection. It gives DQ-017 a proposal-ready analytical answer, but neither this artifact nor its registration accepts that answer.

## 2. Authority, status, and force

This Stream E artifact is evidence for Stream F. It has no Stage-Accepted semantic or normative authority.

The analysis distinguishes four forms of force:

1. **Analytical necessity:** a distinction is required to diagnose cases without conflation.
2. **Semantic adequacy:** a material SymK claim cannot be represented honestly if the distinction is erased.
3. **Ethical compatibility:** a responsibility arrangement cannot claim the candidate SymK minimum if it violates a candidate commitment.
4. **Constitutional or external obligation:** an accepted source requires conduct, procedure, right, remedy, prohibition, or enforcement.

Stream E establishes analytical necessity and proposes semantic and ethical compatibility conditions. It does not create constitutional or external obligations. Constitutional selection remains 2.2; procedures remain 2.3 and 2.6; external law, regulation, professional standards, and institutions retain their own force.

No use of “must” in a working definition makes the clause accepted. It states what the candidate model requires to remain internally coherent and testable.

## 4. Inherited constraints

Stream E preserves these predecessor boundaries:

1. Knowledge, Intelligence, capability, origin, performance, Participation, Contribution, Cooperation, Power, authority, legal status, approval, and successful outcome do not automatically create responsibility, consent, standing, moral status, or ethical adequacy.
2. Bearer, actor, Participant, Contributor, source, resource, authority, responsible party, beneficiary, affected party, claimant, representative, assessor, and forum are non-substitutable roles.
3. Cooperation can be harmful, coercively constrained, exploitative, collusive, or otherwise irresponsibly qualified without ceasing to be classifiable as a thin occurrence.
4. Power capacity, exercise, mechanism, effect, qualification, recognized authority, governance response, and record remain distinct.
5. Formal challenge permission is not effective challengeability.
6. An affected nonparticipant remains in the consequence horizon without being made a Participant, responsible bearer, or authority.
7. Epistemic warrant, Reliance Authorization, organizational permission, legal authority, ethical justification, and practical effectiveness remain distinct.
8. Agency, action, causal Contribution, control, role, authority, authorization, decision, delegation, and supervision remain typed and level-qualified.
9. Responsibility is a sourced, scoped, phased, challengeable relationship-family candidate; it is not a trait, conserved substance, or one undifferentiated status.
10. Prospective responsibility, duty, obligation, retrospective responsibility, answerability, accountability, blame, sanction, liability, remedy, repair, and correction remain distinct.
11. Allocation is object-first, multi-subject, multi-level, temporal, non-additive, and separate from an allocation record.
12. No Human, artificial component, collective, institution, legal entity, provider, deployer, user, configured system, or hybrid is the universal residual bearer.
13. Distribution, automation, delegation, contract, opacity, update, replacement, or succession does not by itself erase or transfer responsibility.
14. A genuine gap is possible; complexity alone does not establish one, and a record cannot cure one.
15. Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views remain aligned where material, with declared Projection loss.
16. External authorities and derived projects own exact rights, duties, liability, remedies, procedures, standards, and thresholds within their jurisdictions.
17. A0, A1, A2, A4, A6, J1, and the conflict protocol remain unratified candidate artifacts.
18. No concept disposition, DQ answer, constitutional change, Foundation Paper revision, package, formalism, implementation, project change, or DR-014 content is accepted by analysis.

## 5. Why the minimum cannot be empty

Pure jurisdictional pluralism is insufficient. If SymK only records that “the Domain decides,” then a system may:

- exclude affected nonparticipants from every forum;
- call coerced compliance consent;
- hide severe foreseeable consequences behind uncertainty;
- accept a responsibility allocation that no bearer can perform;
- preserve a complete audit trail while providing no answer, correction, repair, or remedy;
- let authority suppress counterevidence;
- move responsibility through contracts and automation until it disappears;
- expand a system boundary for success and shrink it for harm;
- comply with one authority while concealing a material conflict with another; or
- treat an unavailable remedy as proof that no response is required.

Such arrangements would contradict the Product Vision's responsibility and consequence constraints, DR-012's recursive consequence return, DR-013's affected-party and effective-challenge requirements, and Streams C–D's accountability and remedy horizons. SymK therefore needs some normative force of its own.

The minimum remains bounded because it governs preservation, non-substitution, practical challenge, accountable response, and jurisdiction. It does not decide the substantive good, distribute every benefit and burden, rank all values, identify every rights-holder, define just compensation, determine culpability, or replace legitimate law and professional judgment.

## 6. Alternatives tested

### Alternative A — Adopt a complete moral theory

**Rejected.** Deontology, consequentialism, virtue ethics, contractualism, care ethics, capability theory, rights theories, and plural or Domain traditions may contribute legitimate authority or Evidence, but selecting one exceeds SymK's constitutive Knowledge Engineering jurisdiction.

### Alternative B — Legal compliance is the universal ethical minimum

**Rejected.** Legal authority may be decisive for action within jurisdiction but can be incomplete, conflicting, absent, prospective, underenforced, or ethically challenged. Compliance does not establish epistemic warrant, effective accountability, consent, repair, or universal ethical adequacy.

### Alternative C — Consent is the single universal criterion

**Rejected.** Consent may be unnecessary, impossible, invalid, insufficient, limited, revocable, representative, or unable to bind affected third parties. Emergencies, public duties, professional obligations, minors, future populations, essential services, and collective effects defeat a consent-only model.

### Alternative D — Minimize or eliminate all harm

**Rejected as a complete rule.** SymK must preserve harm and consequence analysis, but zero harm is often impossible, “harm” is Domain- and authority-sensitive, benefits and burdens conflict, uncertainty persists, and some justified activities impose risk. No universal optimization calculus is selected.

### Alternative E — Transparency and explanation are sufficient

**Rejected.** Notice without understanding, disclosure without practical refusal, explanation without receptive challenge, and logging without correction or remedy are accountability theater.

### Alternative F — Every affected party receives consent authority or veto

**Rejected.** Affected status supports standing and response, not automatic decision authority, unanimity, veto, ownership, or remedy entitlement. Exact authority belongs to applicable frames.

### Alternative G — Assign one final Human or institution as residual ethical bearer

**Rejected.** It recreates the Human-approval or institutional sink, conflicts with Stream D's object-first allocation, and may assign duties to an incapable or unauthorized bearer. Residual allocation may be legitimate under a source, but it is not universal.

### Alternative H — Pure descriptive semantics with no ethical boundary

**Rejected.** It would let SymK describe responsibility disappearance accurately while still calling the arrangement compatible with its identity and Product Vision.

### Alternative I — Bounded anti-erasure and response minimum

**Retained as the working candidate.** It is strong enough to reject consent theater, affected-party erasure, accountability theater, laundering, suppression, and consequence concealment while remaining neutral among complete theories and exact Domain remedies.

## 7. Ethical-compatibility claim

A material ethical-compatibility claim should identify, proportionately to consequence:

1. the system, undertaking, decision, action, omission, relationship, or responsibility arrangement claimed to be compatible;
2. the claimant, authority, purpose, and version of the claim;
3. Domain, Context, Scope, population, stakes, time, and jurisdiction;
4. affected subjects, represented interests, beneficiaries, burdened parties, and subjects whose status is uncertain;
5. relevant Power, dependence, vulnerability, alternatives, and distribution;
6. foreseeable benefits, burdens, risks, harms, uncertainty, severity, reversibility, persistence, and residual effect;
7. responsibility objects, sources, bearers, levels, allocation forms, retained relations, and gaps;
8. whether consent is required or claimed, by which source, for what object, from whom, and under what conditions;
9. notice, comprehension support, voluntariness, practical refusal, representation, scope, duration, and revocation Evidence;
10. standing, representation, challenge, protection, reception, evaluation, response, review, and appeal paths;
11. prevention, monitoring, containment, correction, repair, remedy, recurrence, and Learning paths;
12. applicable external authorities and unresolved duty or jurisdiction conflicts;
13. Human, Scientific, and Engineering View alignments and material losses;
14. current assessment, dissent, uncertainty, limitations, and reopening conditions; and
15. Provenance, status, history, and Projection loss of the claim itself.

This is an analytical information envelope, not an accepted schema, certification, checklist, score, or workflow. Low-consequence cases can preserve it compactly; severe, irreversible, distributed, or highly uncertain cases require greater depth.

## 8. Affected subject, affected party, and affected interest

### 8.1 Working distinctions

- **Affected subject:** an identified subject or declared level whose conditions, capabilities, opportunities, constraints, resources, relationships, exposure, rights, recognized interests, or experienced consequences are materially changed or placed at material risk.
- **Affected party:** a governed consequence/challenge role occupied by an affected subject, or by a legitimate representative where direct occupation is impossible or inappropriate.
- **Affected interest:** an interest, condition, value, right, need, resource, environment, population concern, or future concern represented as materially affected under a declared frame.
- **Beneficiary:** a subject receiving a benefit; not automatically an affected party's representative or decision authority.
- **Burdened party:** a subject bearing a cost, constraint, risk, loss, exposure, or duty; burden does not by itself determine injustice or remedy.
- **Claimant:** a subject or representative advancing a claim; need not be the only affected party.
- **Representative:** a subject or institution authorized or otherwise recognized to express or protect another subject's or interest's position within Scope.

Affected status is relational and scoped, not an intrinsic permanent trait. It may be direct, indirect, cumulative, distributed, delayed, future, probabilistic, collective, environmental, or mediated through dependence.

### 8.2 Non-erasure rule

The candidate boundary rejects these exclusion inferences:

- no operational role therefore no affected status;
- no account or contract therefore no relevant consequence;
- no direct causal interaction therefore no exposure;
- no present complaint therefore no burden;
- no recognized legal personality therefore no analytical question;
- no technical identifier therefore no representable subject;
- no individual named therefore no collective or diffuse effect;
- no certainty therefore no material risk;
- no available remedy therefore no standing; or
- no capacity for direct speech therefore no representation requirement.

The non-erasure rule does not itself establish moral status, legal personhood, rights, injury, entitlement, standing in court, authority, or remedy. It requires the relevant question and Evidence to remain visible and routed to the proper authority.

### 8.3 Materiality

Materiality depends on the claim and Context. Relevant dimensions include:

- magnitude and severity;
- likelihood and uncertainty;
- duration, recurrence, and accumulation;
- reversibility and reparability;
- distribution and concentration;
- dependence and practical alternatives;
- vulnerability and ability to protect one's interests;
- novelty and detectability;
- whether affected subjects can understand, challenge, or obtain response;
- and whether one View or Projection hides the effect.

No universal scalar threshold is selected. Trivial effects need not receive the same process as severe or irreversible effects, but an owner cannot define materiality solely to exclude inconvenient parties.

## 9. Affected-party standing

### 9.1 Minimum standing interface

The working candidate defines **SymK affected-party standing** as:

> **governed eligibility, arising from material effect or exposure rather than operational role alone, to have the relevant interest represented; to supply Evidence or challenge a material claim, decision, allocation, or consequence; to have the challenge received and assessed by a capable forum; and to receive or access a reasoned response and the applicable correction, review, appeal, repair, or remedy route.**

This is a minimum interface, not a complete bundle of rights.

### 9.2 What standing does not entail

Standing does not automatically entail:

- Participant or Contributor status;
- consent authority;
- authorship, ownership, or access to all information;
- decision authority, equal vote, or veto;
- epistemic correctness;
- success on the merits;
- a particular remedy;
- legal standing in any jurisdiction;
- moral personhood;
- or responsibility for the underlying activity.

### 9.3 Direct and represented standing

Direct participation may be impossible for children, future persons, diffuse populations, incapacitated subjects, nonhuman interests, large collectives, confidential cases, or subjects exposed to retaliation. Representation therefore requires independent analysis of:

1. source and legitimacy of representation;
2. represented subject or interest and boundary;
3. Scope, purpose, duration, and limits;
4. representative capability, independence, resources, and conflicts;
5. communication and consultation where possible;
6. Evidence preservation, dissent, and minority positions;
7. challenge to the representative or mandate;
8. accountability and replacement of the representative; and
9. whether direct and represented paths should coexist.

Representation is neither identity nor ownership. A representative's response does not erase the represented subject's consequences.

## 10. Consent system

### 10.1 Working definition

> **Consent is a scoped, temporally situated relation in which an identified subject, or a sufficiently authorized representative within Scope, communicates voluntary agreement concerning a specified object after a materially adequate opportunity for relevant notice and understanding, under practical conditions in which refusal or withdrawal is not made fictitious by deception, coercion, manipulation, disabling dependence, or concealed consequence.**

This is a candidate universal semantic minimum for a material consent claim, not a universal legal-validity test or a rule that consent is required for every action.

### 10.2 Consent and adjacent distinctions

| Distinction | Working boundary |
|---|---|
| Notice | Information is made available or communicated; does not establish receipt, attention, understanding, agreement, or voluntariness |
| Disclosure | Specified information is exposed; may remain inaccessible, excessive, misleading, untimely, or incomprehensible |
| Receipt | Information reaches a subject or representative; does not establish comprehension |
| Understanding | Material comprehension supported for the relevant decision; not perfect knowledge or agreement |
| Acknowledgment | Recognition that information or a request was received; not consent |
| Compliance | Conformity with a rule, request, command, or condition; may occur without agreement |
| Acceptance event | A click, signature, statement, gesture, or recorded state; Evidence of expression, not the complete consent relation |
| Contract | A governed agreement with force from applicable authority; does not prove moral, ethical, or universal SymK consent |
| Authorization | Permission or mandate sourced from authority; may rely on consent but is not identical to it |
| Assent | Affirmative participation or agreement that may lack the full authority or capacity required by an applicable frame |
| Acquiescence | Non-resistance or apparent acceptance; may reflect dependence, fatigue, uncertainty, or lack of alternatives |
| Silence | Absence of expressed objection; not consent by itself |
| Continued use | Continued interaction; may reflect necessity, lock-in, ignorance, or lack of alternatives |
| Voluntariness | Agreement not produced by disqualifying control over practical alternatives; not absence of every influence or constraint |
| Practical refusal | A materially usable ability to decline, stop, narrow, or choose an alternative without disproportionate undisclosed penalty under the relevant frame |
| Withdrawal or revocation | Later termination or narrowing of consent where the governing relation permits it; cannot always reverse prior effects |
| Acceptance of risk | Agreement concerning specified risk; does not transfer all responsibility or waive every duty or remedy |

### 10.3 Required dimensions of a material consent claim

A material claim should preserve:

1. consenting subject and subject level;
2. capacity, authority, or representative basis recognized by the applicable frame;
3. requesting or relying subject and their authority;
4. exact object, action, data use, relationship, intervention, risk, or decision;
5. purpose, Domain, Context, Scope, duration, population, and version;
6. material expected benefits, burdens, risks, uncertainties, alternatives, and affected third parties;
7. timing, form, accessibility, language, and provenance of notice;
8. Evidence of receipt and materially adequate comprehension support;
9. dependence, asymmetry, incentives, sanctions, coercion, manipulation, deception, urgency, and practical alternatives;
10. form and timing of expressed agreement;
11. specificity, granularity, conditions, expiry, renewal, and change rules;
12. refusal, withdrawal, revocation, and data/effect persistence consequences;
13. representation, conflicts, and dissent where applicable;
14. challenge, response, review, appeal, correction, repair, and remedy paths;
15. external legal, professional, institutional, and Domain authority;
16. current status, uncertainty, disagreement, and limitations; and
17. record, Provenance, version, and Projection loss.

No single field such as `consent=true` can preserve this relation.

### 10.4 Consent is neither universally necessary nor sufficient

Consent may be unnecessary or unavailable under emergency authority, public obligation, legal mandate, professional duty, vital-interest protection, adjudication, investigation, or other recognized frames. Stream E does not authorize those actions. It requires the source, jurisdiction, necessity claim, limits, alternatives, affected parties, later review, and response obligations to remain explicit.

Consent is insufficient when:

- the consenting subject lacks authority over other affected parties;
- a duty is non-waivable under applicable authority;
- material information or consequences were concealed;
- refusal was impractical;
- the object or Context later changed;
- consent exceeded its Scope or duration;
- an arrangement remains exploitative, suppressive, epistemically defective, or irresponsibly governed;
- or prevention, correction, repair, or remedy duties survive agreement.

### 10.5 Consent under dependence

Dependence does not make all consent impossible. It increases the burden of analysis. Essential services, employment, education, health care, public administration, platform lock-in, disability support, and professional reliance may constrain exit while still permitting some voluntary choices.

The analysis must distinguish:

- ordinary cost from disproportionate penalty;
- necessary condition from manufactured dependency;
- transparent incentive from concealed manipulation;
- bounded authority from threat outside authority;
- reasonable burden from disabling practical refusal;
- consent to one object from acceptance of an entire relationship;
- and choice among meaningful alternatives from nominal selection among equivalent constraints.

If refusal is not practically available, the relation should not be described unqualifiedly as consent. Another authority may still justify or permit the action, but its force must be named honestly.

## 11. Challenge system

### 11.1 Challenge definition

Challenge is a recursive relationship through which a claim, decision, allocation, authority, status, representation, operation, consequence, or governing frame is questioned, opposed, tested, or presented with counterevidence by a subject or representative with a route to assessment and response.

Challenge remains distinct from disagreement, dissent, complaint, refusal, review, appeal, adjudication, correction, and remedy.

### 11.2 Effective challenge layers

A material challenge path preserves, proportionately:

1. **notice:** the subject can know the relevant claim, decision, effect, or path;
2. **access:** the channel, Evidence, and format are materially reachable;
3. **capacity:** the challenger can understand and use the path, with support where needed;
4. **standing or representation:** direct or represented eligibility is recognized;
5. **practical alternatives:** challenge is not made fictitious by impossible timing or cost;
6. **protection:** retaliation, coercion, suppression, exposure, or conflict is governed;
7. **reception:** an identifiable subject or forum must receive it;
8. **preservation:** the challenge, Evidence, dissent, and relevant state remain traceable;
9. **evaluation:** a sufficiently capable and independent assessment occurs;
10. **reasoned response:** disposition, grounds, authority, uncertainty, and remaining options are communicated;
11. **possible consequence:** correction, containment, reassessment, escalation, repair, remedy, or policy change is within some capable path;
12. **review and appeal:** further examination exists where the applicable frame requires it;
13. **recurrence:** repeated failures can expose systemic causes rather than reset as isolated events; and
14. **Learning and revision:** consequences can return to responsible and governing layers.

Formal permission satisfies only one fragment. Challenge can be effective even when it does not prevail, but only if the merits are genuinely received and the response path is capable of consequence.

### 11.3 Protection and suppression

Suppression includes concealment, disabling access, retaliation, penalty, strategic delay, procedural exhaustion, hostile complexity, evidence destruction, agenda exclusion, or automated filtering that prevents material challenge from reaching a capable forum.

Protection need not mean anonymity or immunity in every case. It means the arrangement identifies and governs foreseeable retaliation, dependence, confidentiality, safety, conflict of interest, and power imbalance sufficiently for the path to be practical.

An artificial monitor, automated detector, representative, auditor, whistleblower, affected party, or external authority may originate Evidence. Origin does not settle its truth or standing; it cannot justify automatic dismissal.

## 12. Review, appeal, and adjudication

- **Review:** governed re-examination of a claim, decision, process, status, or consequence, possibly by the original forum.
- **Appeal:** a request for reconsideration by a distinct or differently authorized forum under an applicable frame.
- **Reassessment:** renewed evaluation after new Evidence, changed Context, expiry, or detected error.
- **Escalation:** routing to a broader or more capable owner because the current forum lacks authority, competence, independence, or response capacity.
- **Adjudication:** an authoritative determination under a recognized procedure and jurisdiction.

Challenge does not always require appeal. Appeal does not guarantee reversal. Adjudication does not create epistemic truth by authority. A review label is ineffective if the reviewer lacks information, independence, time, competence, or power to produce a relevant response.

Stream E selects no tribunal, deadline, voting rule, standard of review, evidentiary burden, appeal sequence, or enforcement mechanism.

## 13. Prevention, containment, correction, repair, and remedy

### 13.1 Distinctions

| Relation | Working boundary |
|---|---|
| Prevention | Action intended to avoid a material adverse consequence before it occurs or recurs |
| Mitigation | Reduction of likelihood, magnitude, duration, distribution, or exposure without necessarily removing the source |
| Containment | Limitation of immediate spread, use, exposure, or effect while the issue remains unresolved |
| Cessation | Ending the relevant action, use, or condition; may not reverse prior effects |
| Correction | Change to an erroneous claim, decision, representation, procedure, configuration, or system; correction attempt and achieved correction remain distinct |
| Repair | Work directed toward damaged capabilities, relationships, conditions, practices, records, or systems; not identical to correction or compensation |
| Remedy | A governed response available to an affected subject or claimant under an applicable frame; exact content and entitlement are Domain/external questions |
| Redress | A response addressing a wrong, injury, burden, or justified grievance under a recognized frame |
| Restitution or restoration | Return of a resource, status, condition, or opportunity where possible |
| Compensation | Transfer intended to address loss or burden; does not by itself correct cause, restore relation, or prevent recurrence |
| Sanction or punishment | Adverse response to breach or wrongdoing; does not by itself repair, remedy, or prevent |
| Apology or acknowledgment | Recognition of effect, error, or wrong; may contribute to repair but does not complete it automatically |
| Recovery | Regaining a prior or revised condition after disruption |
| Residual harm | Material adverse effect remaining after response or not feasibly reversible |

### 13.2 Response continuity

The candidate minimum does not require every listed response in every case. It requires that the relevant response horizon be examined and that one response not silently substitute for another.

Examples:

- a corrected model does not compensate a person already harmed;
- payment does not repair corrupted institutional practice;
- punishment does not correct a false record;
- disclosure does not contain ongoing exposure;
- an apology does not restore access;
- an audit does not make anyone answerable;
- rollback does not erase data already disclosed;
- and successful appeal does not automatically prevent recurrence.

Where repair or remedy is impossible, the impossibility, cause, residual effect, affected subjects, prevention duty, and external authority remain explicit. Impossibility does not justify a fictional status such as `resolved=true`.

### 13.3 Remedy coverage

Remedy coverage asks whether each material affected-subject and responsibility object has a reachable route to a capable source or forum that can:

- receive the claim;
- preserve Evidence;
- determine its authority or route it;
- assess applicable responsibility and effect;
- stop or contain ongoing harm where authorized;
- correct relevant claims, records, decisions, or systems;
- provide or order an applicable remedy;
- explain limitations and unresolved remainder;
- support review or appeal; and
- return recurrence Evidence to responsible and governing layers.

Coverage is not proof that a remedy is owed, sufficient, successful, or enforceable. Absence of coverage is a typed remedy or governance gap.

## 14. Power, dependence, and vulnerability

### 14.1 Power-sensitive ethical analysis

Ethical compatibility cannot be assessed from stated rules alone. It must preserve:

- who can define the frame, options, defaults, Evidence, metrics, and agenda;
- who can grant or withhold access, resources, authorization, livelihood, service, education, or remedy;
- who can observe, rank, classify, sanction, replace, exclude, or expose;
- who understands the system and who depends on opaque intermediaries;
- who can interrupt, reverse, exit, refuse, challenge, or appeal;
- who benefits and who bears risk or residual harm;
- and how these relations change across time and levels.

### 14.2 Vulnerability

Vulnerability is a relational condition of material exposure combined with limited capacity, information, resources, alternatives, protection, influence, or response access. It is not a judgment of inferiority, incapacity, fault, or permanent identity.

Vulnerability may be:

- situational or durable;
- individual, collective, institutional, ecological, or infrastructural;
- created or intensified by design, policy, market structure, dependency, emergency, disability, poverty, secrecy, expertise asymmetry, or cumulative burden;
- hidden by aggregate success metrics;
- or produced by the very system claiming consent or accountability.

Protective action can itself become paternalistic, coercive, or suppressive. The candidate minimum therefore requires declared authority, purpose, Evidence, alternatives, affected standing, challenge, review, and response; it does not define one universal protective intervention.

### 14.3 Power does not settle responsibility automatically

Power capacity, exercise, causal effect, authority, duty, and responsibility remain distinct. Greater Power may support stronger prospective duties under an applicable source, but Stream E does not create a universal formula from control to duty. Lack of total control does not prove absence of responsibility; lack of responsibility does not erase the affected subject or response gap.

## 15. Foreseeability, uncertainty, severity, and reversibility

### 15.1 Foreseeability

Foreseeability is assessed relative to subject, role, time, information, capability, available Evidence, Domain practice, warning, and reasonable inquiry under a declared frame. Actual occurrence does not prove prior foreseeability; failure to predict an exact mechanism does not erase foreseeable classes of consequence.

The analysis distinguishes:

- known consequence;
- reasonably foreseeable consequence;
- plausible but weakly supported risk;
- deep uncertainty or missing model;
- unforeseeable consequence under the declared Evidence;
- and consequence made unforeseeable through avoidable ignorance, suppression, fragmentation, or failure to investigate.

### 15.2 Uncertainty

Uncertainty is not absence of risk and does not automatically prohibit action. Material uncertainty should preserve:

- what is unknown and why;
- model, measurement, sampling, scenario, behavioral, institutional, and future uncertainty;
- competing explanations;
- affected distributions and tail consequences;
- monitoring and detection capacity;
- reversibility, containment, and response options;
- conditions for reassessment or suspension;
- and which authority may accept residual risk.

Confidence labels, simulations, compliance status, absence of observed failure, and successful pilots do not establish universal safety.

### 15.3 Consequence-sensitive rigor

As credible severity, scale, irreversibility, duration, dependency, vulnerability, or uncertainty increases, the candidate minimum expects stronger preservation of Evidence, alternatives, challenge, independent assessment, monitoring, containment, reversibility, response capacity, and authority. This is a directional proportionality condition, not a universal calculus.

Low consequence can justify lighter process. Severe or irreversible consequence defeats the inference that absent certainty, absent complaint, formal compliance, or nominal consent is sufficient.

### 15.4 Severe and irreversible harm

When credible severe or irreversible harm is possible:

1. the affected population and possible distribution cannot be omitted;
2. uncertainty and competing models must remain visible;
3. a capable authority and response path must be identified;
4. prevention, safer alternatives, limitation, reversibility, monitoring, containment, and recovery must be examined;
5. consent cannot substitute for third-party effects or non-waivable duties;
6. a responsibility or remedy gap cannot be treated as exculpatory;
7. expansion or continued reliance must remain challengeable; and
8. residual harm and unresolved conflict must be disclosed.

Stream E does not decide a universal prohibition threshold or that every unallocable object requires cessation. It rejects only the claim that the unresolved condition is ethically adequate merely because no perfect answer exists.

## 16. Emergency action

Emergency action may occur before ordinary authorization, consent, review, or Evidence thresholds can be satisfied. Urgency does not create unlimited authority or erase later responsibility.

A material emergency claim should preserve:

1. the emergency condition and Evidence available at the time;
2. source and Scope of any emergency authority or asserted necessity;
3. decision subject, actor, affected subjects, and unavailable participants;
4. time, duration, geographic or population boundary, and expiry;
5. feasible alternatives and why ordinary process was impractical;
6. predicted benefits, burdens, uncertainty, severity, and residual risk;
7. measures to limit action to the asserted need;
8. monitoring, containment, reversal, and handoff conditions;
9. contemporaneous record without demanding impossible detail;
10. later notice, answerability, independent review, correction, repair, and remedy;
11. repeated use, normalization, or emergency extension; and
12. unresolved duty or external-authority conflicts.

This model neither authorizes emergency action nor requires prior consent where it is impossible. It prevents “emergency” from becoming permanent exception, retrospective authorization fiction, or erasure of affected-party and repair obligations.

## 17. Conflicting duties and authorities

Professional, organizational, legal, contractual, epistemic, safety, relational, and remedial duties may conflict. Stream E supplies no universal priority rule.

A material conflict record should preserve:

- each asserted duty, object, source, bearer, jurisdiction, Scope, time, and priority claim;
- whether the conflict is semantic, factual, jurisdictional, practical, temporal, or substantive;
- affected subjects, risks, uncertainty, Power, and alternatives;
- possible compatible interpretations, sequencing, narrowing, exception, recusal, refusal, escalation, or containment;
- the nearest adequate owners and external authorities;
- actions taken under urgency and retained answerability;
- dissent and suppressed or unavailable Evidence;
- the selected action and why, without implying every duty was discharged;
- review, appeal, correction, repair, remedy, and reopening paths; and
- every unresolved remainder.

Compliance with one duty does not silently discharge another. An unauthorized but beneficial result does not retrospectively create authorization; a compliant but harmful result does not erase consequence and revision. Material unresolved conflict remains visible in compatibility and reliance claims.

## 18. Responsibility allocation and ethical gaps

### 18.1 No default residual bearer

The candidate ethical minimum does not reverse Stream D by installing a universal Human or institutional residual bearer. It instead requires:

- object-specific identification of prevention, decision, monitoring, answerability, correction, repair, and remedy needs;
- source-specific analysis of which duties exist;
- capable and empowered bearers or forums where the source requires them;
- preservation of retained relations across delegation and distribution;
- affected-subject standing and response coverage;
- and visible gaps where no valid allocation exists.

An applicable authority may create nondelegable or residual duties. Their force comes from that authority, not from universal subject type.

### 18.2 Ethical significance of gaps

A responsibility gap becomes ethically material when it exposes affected subjects, permits foreseeable consequence without capable prevention or response, makes challenge futile, allows responsibility laundering, or supports an unqualified compatibility claim.

The candidate minimum distinguishes:

- **existence of a gap:** a required relation or response lacks a valid capable bearer or forum;
- **cause of the gap:** genuine limits, uncertainty, missing authority, incapacity, fragmentation, outsourcing, suppression, design, or representation;
- **ethical assessment:** whether creation, tolerance, concealment, or continuation is acceptable under the governing frame;
- **response:** prevention, redesign, additional authority, limitation, insurance, fund, representative, forum, suspension, disclosure, or another Domain measure; and
- **residual state:** what remains unresolved or unremediable.

Stream E proposes that concealment or laundering of a material gap violates the minimum. It does not declare every genuine gap impermissible or choose the response.

### 18.3 Allocation artifact and ethical adequacy

A responsibility matrix, policy, contract, audit, model card, consent record, risk register, appeal portal, insurance arrangement, or remedy fund may support ethical adequacy. None establishes it alone.

The arrangement must remain testable against actual capability, Power, practical access, affected distribution, response effectiveness, recurrence, and three-view Evidence.

## 19. Subject type, level, and status

The candidate minimum is substrate-neutral in its prohibitions but not indifferent to evidence.

- Human status does not automatically establish competence, authority, consent, effective oversight, responsibility, or ethical adequacy.
- Artificial capability or agency does not automatically establish moral agency, legal personality, duties, rights, standing, liability, or remedy entitlement.
- Collective or institutional identity does not copy member consent, blame, standing, or responsibility to the whole or vice versa.
- Legal personality can support legal duties, liability, standing, and remedy within jurisdiction without settling moral or operational predicates.
- A hybrid can possess configured capabilities and Power without becoming one moral or legal person.
- An affected party is not made responsible by exposure.
- A representative does not absorb the represented subject's identity, consequences, or every right.
- An incapable or unreachable bearer cannot make accountability effective merely by being named.

If moral or legal status is contested, the uncertainty remains explicit and is routed to competent authority. Type or origin alone cannot close the question in either direction.

## 20. Temporal and lifecycle obligations

Ethical compatibility is not a one-time approval state. The analysis spans:

1. framing and purpose;
2. inquiry and Evidence collection;
3. design and data selection;
4. procurement, contracting, and integration;
5. authorization and deployment;
6. operation and reliance;
7. monitoring and incident detection;
8. challenge and response;
9. correction, repair, and remedy;
10. update, maintenance, and replacement;
11. suspension, retirement, and decommissioning;
12. merger, dissolution, transfer, and succession; and
13. preserved history, recurrence, and Learning.

Consent may expire or lose Scope. Standing may arise after a previously unknown effect. Foreseeability may change with new Evidence. A safe initial deployment may drift. A successor may inherit response capacity without inheriting blame. A correction may leave residual harm. A past lawful action may remain historically challengeable without becoming retroactively unlawful by SymK declaration.

No current status overwrites historical authority, Evidence, dissent, consequence, or response.

## 21. Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views

### 21.1 Human View

The Human View should explain, where material:

- how affected subjects understand the purpose, relation, risk, alternatives, Power, dependence, consent, challenge, response, and consequence;
- which interests, identities, capabilities, rights, duties, experiences, or vulnerabilities are claimed;
- whether notice is intelligible and refusal practical;
- how representation was formed and contested;
- what repair, remedy, fairness, dignity, autonomy, care, or legitimacy mean under the relevant frame;
- which dissent and unresolved conflict remain;
- and what testimony or interpretation cannot establish alone.

One stakeholder statement, terms document, survey, persona, satisfaction score, complaint count, or representative cannot stand for every affected subject.

### 21.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View should preserve, where material:

- evidence of effects, distributions, likelihood, severity, duration, reversibility, accumulation, and residual harm;
- counterfactuals, causal models, uncertainty, confounding, missing data, sensitivity, and competing explanations;
- comprehension, behavioral choice, practical refusal, dependence, coercion, retaliation, challenge access, response latency, recurrence, and repair outcomes;
- population and system boundaries, selection effects, excluded groups, future effects, and monitoring limits;
- methods for identifying vulnerability without reifying it as a trait;
- and the boundary between empirical evidence and normative or legal judgment.

Observed compliance does not prove consent. Absence of complaints does not prove absence of harm. A statistically beneficial aggregate does not erase concentrated burden. A predicted risk does not itself determine the ethically correct action.

### 21.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View should eventually preserve, where material:

- subject, affected-party, representative, authority, responsible bearer, claimant, assessor, and forum roles separately;
- purpose, object, Scope, Context, time, version, population, and jurisdiction;
- notice, accessibility, comprehension support, agreement event, practical refusal, revocation, and consent limitations;
- Power, access, permissions, defaults, dependencies, incentives, sanctions, control, monitoring, and interruption;
- consequence, uncertainty, severity, reversibility, distribution, residual effect, and Evidence;
- challenge notice, access, protection, reception, assessment, response, escalation, review, and appeal;
- prevention, containment, correction, repair, remedy, recurrence, and Learning;
- allocation transfers, retained relations, gaps, succession, and external authority; and
- Human/Scientific/Engineering alignments, Provenance, status, history, dissent, and Projection loss.

No boolean consent field, fairness score, ethics checklist, risk rating, responsibility matrix, audit log, ticket state, appeal button, policy document, explanation interface, or remedy payment can represent the whole relation.

### 21.4 Alignment failures

Alignment fails when, for example:

- Human View reports dependence and opaque consequence, Scientific View measures only acceptance rates, and Engineering View stores `consent=true`;
- Engineering shows a formal appeal path, Human evidence shows retaliation, and Scientific evaluation omits non-use caused by fear;
- Scientific averages show benefit while Human evidence identifies a severely burdened subgroup omitted from the system boundary;
- Human leadership declares responsible oversight, Engineering exposes no interruption capacity, and Scientific evidence shows automation bias;
- or legal compliance is represented as ethical or epistemic validity without preserving its jurisdiction and limitations.

The mismatch must remain visible. No View receives universal precedence outside its authority.
