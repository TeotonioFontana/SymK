## 1. Question and result

> Which distinctions must SymK preserve among claims, Grounds, Evidence, Provenance, assessment, status, uncertainty, disagreement, and permission to rely?

Stream C finds that these meanings form a typed epistemic-governance system. None can serve as a universal substitute for truth, Knowledge, or another member of the system. Their relationships are contextual, scoped, attributable, challengeable, and revisable.

## 2. Minimum distinctions

| Candidate | Working meaning | Must not be treated as |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge claim | Represented content presented as knowable, true, warranted, applicable, or epistemically relevant | truth, Knowledge, acceptance, or authorization merely by assertion |
| Ground | A basis invoked as bearing on a claim, achievement, assessment, or action | automatically sufficient Evidence or proof |
| Evidence | A contextual role in which something bears on a question, claim, assessment, or decision under a method | an intrinsic property independent of question, Context, method, or assessor |
| Counterevidence | Evidence that weakens, defeats, redirects, or qualifies a claim or assessment | proof of the exact opposite in every Scope |
| Provenance | Origin, custody, authorship, transformation, derivation, and lineage information | truth, warrant, quality, authority, or relevance |
| Assessment | A governed evaluation under declared criteria, method, evidence, assessor, authority, Context, and Scope | the predicate or achievement assessed |
| Epistemic status | The result or state assigned by an assessment scheme | truth, Knowledge, or universal acceptance |
| Governance status | A program or institutional state controlling handling, authority, or workflow | epistemic status or truth status |
| Uncertainty | Represented limitation concerning what is known, inferred, measured, modeled, or expected | one scalar probability in all cases |
| Disagreement | Material divergence among claims, interpretations, assessments, methods, values, or actions | error, bad faith, or equal evidential standing by default |
| Reliance authorization | A decision permitting specified use or action under Scope, conditions, risk, and responsibility | Knowledge, truth, safety, or correctness |

## 3. Ground, Evidence, and Provenance

A Ground can be a reason, observation, testimony, record, experience, method, model, precedent, performance, or other basis. It functions as Evidence only relative to a question or assessment practice. The same item may support one claim, undermine another, remain irrelevant to a third, or change role after new information.

Evidence must therefore relate a subject or claim; direction and kind of bearing; source and Provenance; Context and Scope; method or inferential bridge; assessor or attributing source; strength, limitations, uncertainty, and defeaters; time or version; and challenge/revision lineage.

Provenance supports inspection, attribution, reproducibility, responsibility, and challenge. Yet exact lineage does not make false content true or irrelevant material probative. It should distinguish subject origin, representational authorship, custody, transformation, derivation, assessment, status transition, and projection lineage.

## 4. Assessment and status

Assessment relates a subject to criteria under a scheme. The result is scoped to that scheme and authority. Competing assessments may coexist without collapsing into one master status.

The minimum model separates the subject or claim; actual epistemic situation; assessment act; criteria and method; Grounds and Evidence; assessor and authority; epistemic status; governance status; reliance decision; and records or Projections of each. This extends DR-011’s actual-bearing/attribution/assessment/record rule.

## 5. Uncertainty, disagreement, and reliance

Uncertainty may concern measurement, source reliability, model structure, interpretation, missing alternatives, future consequence, applicability, or unresolved conflict. It may be qualitative, interval-based, probabilistic, scenario-based, or not responsibly reducible to one number.

Disagreement records who or what differs, about which proposition or practice, for which reasons, under which methods and Scopes, with what authority and evidence. A consensus Projection must declare excluded, unresolved, or transformed dissent.

Reliance authorization relates an intended use, user or actor, subject, Context, Scope, risk, conditions, safeguards, responsibility, evidence state, and review point. It may reasonably be granted under uncertainty or withheld despite strong evidence. It is downstream from—but not mechanically determined by—epistemic assessment.

## 6. Formal validity and epistemic sufficiency

Formal validity concerns whether a conclusion follows under a declared formal system. Epistemic sufficiency concerns whether Grounds, premises, methods, source conditions, defeaters, uncertainty, and Context support the relevant attribution or reliance. Valid form with false or unjustified premises is not sufficient; well-supported practical Knowledge is not invalid merely because it lacks a formal derivation.

## 7. Counterexample results

The distinctions survive a cryptographically traceable false document; replicated data supporting incompatible models; valid reasoning from unreliable extraction; consensus created by suppressed dissent; regulator-authorized use under residual uncertainty; a correct claim lacking responsible Grounds; poor Provenance with independent corroboration; tacit practical competence; two scheme-relative statuses; and a machine confidence score presented as certainty.

## 8. Working dispositions and obligations

- Knowledge claim, Ground, Evidence, Provenance, Assessment, epistemic status, uncertainty, disagreement, and reliance authorization remain governed supporting distinction or relationship-family candidates.
- Knowledge asset and Epistemic record remain engineering aggregates or Representations, not Knowledge containers.
- Governance status and epistemic status require explicit separation.
- No universal assessment scale, evidence calculus, uncertainty formalism, or reliance threshold is selected.

A later formal or package design must distinguish claims, subjects, Grounds, Evidence roles, sources, transformations, assessments, statuses, authorities, uncertainty, disagreement, reliance decisions, Scope, Context, time, version, and semantic loss without forcing every relation into one numerical model.

Proceed to Stream D: reconcile Representation, Projection, Ontology, Logic, and semantic alignment without reducing the work to an exhaustive two-lane model.
