**Status:** Analytical working proposal — no decision authority
**Date:** 20 August 2026
**Working baseline:** `2.1-dev.29`
**Authority:** 2.1.5 opening instruction `SYMK-2X-EV-107`, control `SYMK-2X-EV-108`, and Stream A–B evidence `SYMK-2X-EV-109`–`110`
**Evidence record:** `SYMK-2X-EV-111`

## 1. Question and analytical result

> How must SymK distinguish formation, learning, instruction, Training, Education, socialization, adaptation, conditioning, habit, reflection, correction, repair, and decay so that a formative activity, environment, process, effect, achievement, attribution, or record cannot substitute for another?

Stream C supports a layered formation concept system rather than one synonym chain.

The broadest working anchor is:

> **Formation is the temporally extended shaping or reorganization of an identified subject's comparatively durable capacities, dispositions, practices, judgments, relationships, or organized patterns through experience, intervention, participation, environment, or self-directed activity. Formation may be intended or unintended, beneficial or harmful, integrative or deformative, and does not by itself entail Education, Training, Knowledge, Intelligence, responsible participation, or improvement.**

Within that broad family:

> **Learning is a comparatively durable change attributable partly to the learner's experience, inquiry, practice, feedback, interaction, or adaptation mechanism rather than solely to a transient condition, external substitution, relabeling, or record change.**

> **Training is a directed and structured formative activity organized toward declared performance or disposition conditions, characteristically through practice, variation, feedback, correction, rehearsal, reinforcement, or adaptation. A Training activity may fail; a trained change or competence requires separate evidence of attributable, sufficiently durable manifestation and transfer within Scope.**

> **Education is a broad, developmental, and challengeable formation through which an identified learner becomes more capable of integrated and responsible participation in a shared world by connecting Knowledge, judgment, conduct, relationship, and the capacity to examine and revise the practices and frame involved. Educational intent, instruction, curriculum, attendance, credential, compliance, or one successful performance does not establish this achievement.**

The Stream C result preserves Education and Training as distinct and potentially reciprocal but rejects universal co-entailment:

- Training can occur without Education.
- Education can occur without a separately directed or repeated Training program.
- Training may stabilize capabilities and dispositions that support Education.
- Education may supply meaning, judgment, relational direction, and challenge to Training.
- Durable practical enactment commonly requires practice and feedback, but **practice is broader than Training** and no universal rule makes repeated Training constitutive of every Education.

The result also corrects the inherited architecture claim:

> **Every persistent architecture may create formative conditions and should be examined for formative influence. It does not automatically train, educate, or produce a habit. Actual formative effects require an identified learner, stable boundary, mechanism, developmental evidence, alternatives, persistence, transfer limits, and consequences.**

These are analytical working candidates only. Final classification, constitutional necessity, and DR-013 remain unresolved.

## 2. Inherited constraints

Stream C inherits without reopening:

1. Cooperation is a relational process rather than SymK's objective, participant trait, or favorable outcome.
2. Participant, learner, Bearer, contributor, actor, resource, source, beneficiary, affected party, authority, and cooperative unit remain distinct.
3. No participant set, team, institution, cooperative unit, or relation becomes a collective Intelligence Bearer automatically.
4. Knowledge and Intelligence do not entail learning, Education, cooperative disposition, authority, responsibility, or correct action.
5. Intelligence does not universally require persistent learning or adaptation.
6. Actual bearing, attribution, assessment, governance status, authorization, and record remain distinct.
7. The epistemic lifecycle is recursive; application, consequence, challenge, correction, revision, preservation, and decay can reopen earlier assessments.
8. Representations and artifacts may support formation but do not contain learning, Education, Training effects, competence, habit, or institutional change.
9. Formation at one level does not copy automatically to members, components, collectives, institutions, distributed arrangements, or hybrids.
10. Detailed formative power, consent, coercion, authority, duty, and responsibility remain principally for Stream D and 2.1.6.

## 3. The formation-level separation

Ordinary statements such as “the system trained the participant,” “the course educated the team,” or “the organization learned” hide several distinct levels.

| Level | Working question | Prohibited substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Formative purpose or objective | What change was sought, by whom, for which learner and undertaking? | Declared objective proves legitimacy or achievement |
| Formative activity or intervention | What instruction, exercise, feedback, redesign, reward, sanction, example, or practice occurred? | Delivered activity proves change |
| Formative environment | Which interfaces, incentives, defaults, roles, metrics, norms, examples, constraints, and tolerated practices shaped exposure and response? | Presence in the environment proves learning or Education |
| Exposure or participation | What did the proposed learner encounter or do, with what duration and variation? | Attendance, access, interaction count, or repetition proves formation |
| Formative process or mechanism | Through which cognitive, behavioral, social, organizational, technical, or relational mechanism could change occur? | Plausible mechanism proves actual effect |
| Formative effect | What comparatively durable change occurred in capacity, disposition, representation, expectation, behavior, practice, relationship, or organization? | Any difference is improvement, learning, Training, or Education |
| Formative achievement | Which qualified result—learned capability, trained performance, habit, Education, integration, repair—has been supportably achieved? | Effect receives the highest-status label available |
| Attribution and assessment | Who claims and evaluates which effect or achievement under what evidence, criteria, Scope, and authority? | Assessment creates the underlying change |
| Governance status or authorization | Is the intervention or attribution proposed, accepted, restricted, challenged, prohibited, or authorized? | Approved curriculum or credential makes the learner educated |
| Formative record | Which Representation preserves purpose, activity, environment, exposure, mechanism, change, evidence, assessment, consequence, and history? | Record, model weights, transcript, certificate, policy, or memory store is the formation itself |

No unqualified `trained`, `educated`, `learned`, or `formed` field can preserve these levels by itself.

## 4. Developmental change and Formation

### 4.1 Developmental change

Developmental change is the widest temporal distinction: a subject differs across time in a way material to its organization, capacity, disposition, practice, relationship, or behavior. Growth, deterioration, drift, redesign, replacement, damage, recovery, and learning can all produce developmental change.

Change alone does not identify the mechanism, value, or continuity of the subject.

### 4.2 Formation

Formation concerns patterned shaping across time. It may arise through:

- deliberate Education or Training;
- informal experience and participation;
- socialization and institutional practice;
- conditioning, incentives, sanctions, or repeated interface use;
- correction, repair, crisis, conflict, or consequence;
- technical retraining, redesign, reconfiguration, adaptation, or component replacement;
- accumulated encounters and relationships.

Formation is neutral with respect to value. A participant can be formed toward concealment, dependency, domination, brittle compliance, collusion, or avoidance as well as toward challenge, judgment, restraint, and repair.

### 4.3 Formative influence is not formative effect

An environment may expose, reward, pressure, or constrain without producing a durable change. The correct progression is:

```text
condition or intervention
    may exert formative influence
        may activate a mechanism
            may produce a formative effect
                may support a qualified achievement attribution
```

No arrow is automatic. This distinction is essential for the inherited hidden-curriculum and persistent-architecture claims.

## 5. Learning

### 5.1 Working conditions

A material Learning attribution should identify:

1. the learner Bearer and stable boundary;
2. baseline capacity, disposition, representation, expectation, behavior, practice, or organization;
3. relevant experience, inquiry, practice, feedback, interaction, or adaptation process;
4. a subsequent comparatively durable change at that learner's level;
5. an attributable connection between process and change;
6. relevant variation, transfer, manifestation, and dependency;
7. competing explanations such as temporary assistance, changed environment, external control, component replacement, memorization, leakage, maturation, injury, or random drift;
8. Scope, Context, time, uncertainty, consequences, and conditions of decay or reversal.

Learning may be beneficial, harmful, accurate, false, narrow, broad, intentional, incidental, individual, artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, or hybrid. None follows from the word alone.

### 5.2 What does not establish Learning

- one changed output;
- a prompt or temporary context window;
- a retrieved document available only during use;
- a record, policy, or file added without integrated use;
- a model, person, or role label changed by an administrator;
- temporary compliance under supervision or threat;
- a new component substituted for the prior learner;
- an institution retaining information that no organized process can interpret or apply.

### 5.3 Learning and Intelligence

Learning may develop or evidence Intelligence but does not entail it. Intelligence may exist without online learning. Learning is a change process or achievement; Intelligence is a multidimensional capacity. An update, adaptation, or improved result does not establish either without predicate-specific evidence.

## 6. Instruction

> **Instruction is an intentional formative contribution designed to enable or direct learning through explanation, demonstration, example, question, task, feedback, counterexample, modeling, guidance, or another structured intervention.**

Instruction is attributed primarily to the activity and instructor role. It may be given by a person, peer, artificial system, institution, environment, artifact, or organized arrangement. Successful delivery, exposure, or comprehension does not establish Learning, Training effect, or Education.

Instruction can be false, manipulative, irrelevant, poorly designed, coercive, or unsuccessful. Its source, grounds, purpose, authority, learner boundary, uncertainty, and consequences remain governed.

## 7. Training

### 7.1 Training activity

Training activity is directed and structured toward a declared capability, disposition, response, or performance condition. Repetition is characteristic but not necessarily identical or mechanically counted. Representative variation, practice, feedback, correction, reinforcement, rehearsal, and evaluation commonly matter.

A Training program can occur even if the learner fails to change. SymK must therefore distinguish “received Training” from “became trained” and from “was authorized or certified.”

### 7.2 Training effect and trained achievement

A trained effect requires evidence that the activity contributed to comparatively durable change. A trained capability or disposition additionally requires manifestation under relevant conditions, transfer appropriate to the target, visibility of dependence on supervision or reward, and declared limits.

Training success does not establish:

- understanding of grounds or purpose;
- ability to challenge the objective;
- responsible participation;
- broad transfer;
- Education;
- Knowledge or Intelligence;
- authorization, responsibility, or moral value.

### 7.3 Machine training terminology

Pretraining, fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, adaptation, retrieval augmentation, prompting, and configuration are different technical processes. Industrial use of *training* does not settle the SymK concept.

- A training run is an engineering activity.
- Changed parameters are an artifact/state difference.
- Changed capability or disposition is an attribution requiring evaluation.
- Retrieval or prompting can change current performance without changing the model Bearer.
- A configured or hybrid system may change while the model remains fixed.
- A new model version may replace rather than educate the prior Bearer.

No technical label establishes Education.

## 8. Education

### 8.1 Working achievement profile

Education is narrower in value and broader in organization than generic Formation or Training. A material Education attribution should support, within Scope:

1. **identified learner continuity:** the subject said to have developed remains sufficiently stable across the process;
2. **developmental breadth:** change is not exhausted by one response, rule, score, or narrow task;
3. **integration:** relevant Knowledge, competence, judgment, conduct, relationship, and self- or system-revision become better organized rather than merely accumulated;
4. **participatory direction:** the change bears materially on participation in a shared world or undertaking;
5. **grounds and limits:** purpose, grounds, conflicts, limitations, and alternatives remain accessible at the learner or governed system level appropriate to the Bearer;
6. **challengeability:** the practice, objective, classification, and formative frame can be questioned and revised through an effective path;
7. **manifestation and transfer:** the change appears across relevant variation rather than only under the training cue, instructor, interface, or reward;
8. **persistence and revisability:** the change is sufficiently durable but not permanent, infallible, or immune to correction;
9. **relational and consequential assessment:** other participants, affected parties, burdens, unintended effects, and repair remain visible;
10. **evidence and uncertainty:** attribution is developmental, multi-source where appropriate, challengeable, and distinct from credential or status.

The exact content of responsible participation remains coordinated with Stream D and 2.1.6. Stream C supplies the formative boundary without selecting one moral theory, curriculum, culture, or pedagogy.

### 8.2 Education is not every positive change

A learner can acquire a correct fact, improve one skill, adapt to an interface, or develop one habit without the broader integration and challengeability required by the Education candidate. These may contribute to Education but do not establish it alone.

### 8.3 Education does not require a school or educator role

Education may occur through inquiry, experience, relationship, consequence, reflection, encounter, self-directed practice, or institutional change. Formal instruction and Training may support it but are not universal necessary activities.

### 8.4 Education and Intelligence

Formation, Learning, and Training do not require an Intelligence attribution in advance. Education requires an organized learner capable, at the level claimed, of the relevant integration, participation, judgment, and revision. That profile may supply evidence about capabilities but does not automatically establish the complete DR-011 Intelligence predicate. When an artifact claims “Education among Intelligences,” the Intelligence attribution remains separate.

## 9. Education and Training: distinct, overlapping, and contingent

| Relationship claim | Stream C result |
|---|---|
| Training is Education | Rejected; directed performance formation can remain narrow, unreflective, coercive, or non-transferring |
| Education is Training | Rejected; broad educational change may arise without a directed repeated Training program |
| Every Education requires repeated Training | Rejected as universal; durable practical manifestation often requires practice, but practice and experience are broader than Training |
| Every Training is educational | Rejected; Training may stabilize harmful, brittle, deceptive, or conformist conduct |
| Training can contribute to Education | Retained; practice, feedback, correction, and variation can stabilize capabilities and dispositions within broader formation |
| Education can govern Training | Retained as a candidate relation; educational judgment can expose purpose, limits, consequences, and revision needs |
| Education without any practice is complete | Rejected for claims involving reliable practical or relational manifestation; propositional or reflective development alone does not prove enacted disposition |
| Training without reflective challenge is always invalid | Rejected; bounded low-risk skills may be trained legitimately, though their purpose, authority, and consequences remain governable |
| Education and Training are reciprocal in every case | Rejected as too strong; reciprocity is contingent on target, learner, Domain, persistence, and consequence |

The candidate A3 wording that Education “becomes effective through repeated Training” therefore requires revision or stronger evidence. The defensible minimum is that relevant practice, feedback, and manifestation matter for durable practical formation, and Training is one governed means.

## 10. Socialization

> **Socialization is formation through which a subject acquires the language, expectations, roles, practices, norms, and patterned responses of a social or institutional order through participation, observation, imitation, reward, sanction, custom, and relationship.**

Socialization is descriptive rather than automatically educational. It may enable communication and membership while reproducing domination, silence, exclusion, prejudice, collusion, or dependency. Successful integration into an order does not establish that the order or integration is responsible, challengeable, or educational.

Education may include, assess, revise, or resist socialization. Assimilation occurs when integration requires erasure of material difference or challenge capacity; Stream D must examine the power conditions.

## 11. Adaptation

> **Adaptation is an adjustment in state, behavior, organization, strategy, or environment-sensitive response that changes fit or performance relative to conditions.**

Adaptation may be:

- transient or durable;
- internal to the learner or produced by external configuration;
- intentional, automatic, selected, designed, or emergent;
- beneficial locally while harmful systemically;
- evidence of Learning, or merely a current response to changed conditions.

Adaptation becomes Learning only when the change and mechanism satisfy the learner-level attribution conditions. It becomes Education only under the broader achievement profile. Intelligence does not require online adaptation, and adaptation does not establish Intelligence.

## 12. Conditioning

> **Conditioning is formation of response tendencies through patterned association, exposure, reward, punishment, reinforcement, avoidance, or control of available consequences.**

Conditioning can contribute to Training, socialization, habit, or adaptation. It does not require understanding, endorsement, or reflection. When it suppresses effective challenge, conceals its objective, exploits severe dependency, or makes revision practically impossible, it conflicts with the Education candidate even if performance becomes reliable.

Not every feedback process is coercive conditioning. Exact consent, necessity, authority, vulnerability, and reversibility tests belong to Stream D and 2.1.6.

## 13. Habit and disposition

> **A Habit is a comparatively durable disposition manifested as a recurring tendency to perceive, attend, interpret, respond, or act in relevant situations without reconstructing the complete deliberation each time.**

Habit is distinct from:

- one repeated act;
- an explicit rule or policy;
- current behavior under supervision;
- a stored parameter or script;
- capability generally;
- virtue, moral approval, or permanent identity.

Habits may be individual, organizational, technical, relational, or hybrid only when the Bearer boundary and recurring disposition are evidenced at that level. Repetition can contribute but is neither necessary in one mechanical form nor sufficient. Habits may conflict, depend on environment, fail to transfer, weaken, be suppressed, or become harmful after conditions change.

## 14. Reflection

> **Reflection is an activity or capability through which a subject or organized system examines experience, grounds, purpose, assumptions, norms, conflicts, effects, limits, and possible revision.**

Reflection may support Learning and Education but does not guarantee change, truth, responsible judgment, or action. It may be distributed: one participant raises challenge, another preserves evidence, and an institution revises the practice. A distributed reflection claim requires the Stream B integration and level rules; a meeting or retrospective record alone is insufficient.

The Education candidate requires an effective reflective or challenge function at the attributed level, not necessarily identical private introspection in every component.

## 15. Feedback, correction, and repair

### 15.1 Feedback

Feedback is information, response, consequence, or signal made available to a subject or system concerning an act, state, process, or result. It may be ignored, misunderstood, manipulative, delayed, incomplete, or harmful. Availability does not establish uptake or change.

### 15.2 Correction

> **Correction is an intervention or process directed toward changing an identified error, deviation, inadequacy, or mismatch relative to declared criteria.**

Correction can target a claim, Representation, performance, habit, procedure, relationship, environment, or assessment. It may be mistaken or coercive. Corrected output does not establish Learning; a subject may depend wholly on external correction each time.

### 15.3 Repair

> **Repair is a relational or system process directed toward addressing damage, breach, lost trust, exclusion, misunderstanding, or impaired cooperative conditions so that a workable relation can be restored, reconstituted, transformed, or responsibly ended.**

Repair is not identical to correction. Correcting a factual error may leave humiliation, dependency, exclusion, or trust damage intact. Repair is not forgetting, forced reconciliation, punishment, deletion, or return to the prior state. It may require acknowledgment, restitution, redesign, changed roles, preserved history, or termination; detailed responsibility belongs later.

### 15.4 Learning from correction and repair

Correction or repair becomes formative only when a relevant Bearer changes comparatively durably. A restored output or one resolved conflict may be successful without producing Learning, habit, Education, or system-level change.

## 16. Decay, loss, drift, and unlearning

> **Formative decay is a decrease in the availability, integration, reliability, transfer, or responsible revisability of a previously supported capacity, disposition, practice, relationship, or organization within Scope.**

Decay may result from disuse, changed incentives, member departure, model or tool replacement, environmental shift, ceremonialization, dependency, overload, trauma, corruption, or lost institutional memory. It does not erase historical achievement.

Loss and decay remain distinct from:

- **drift:** change away from a prior state or criterion, not necessarily loss;
- **supersession:** replacement by a governed successor;
- **retirement:** ending use or authorization;
- **restriction:** narrowing Scope or reliance;
- **forgetting:** loss of availability or recall at a relevant Bearer;
- **unlearning:** a process of weakening or reorganizing a learned pattern, which may be beneficial or harmful;
- **repair:** response to damage rather than the damage or loss itself.

Education, Training, Learning, and habit attributions must remain temporal and challengeable. No status is permanent.

## 17. Learner, participant, and Bearer boundary

### 17.1 Learner

Learner is the Bearer role occupied by the subject at whose level actual Learning or formative change is attributed. The learner may also be a Participant, but participation does not establish Learning and Learning does not require current Cooperation.

### 17.2 Educator, instructor, trainer, designer, and environment

These roles may offer activities or conditions. They do not own the learner's achievement, guarantee success, or acquire unchallengeable authority. A system can be a formative environment without being an educator; a person can instruct without Education occurring; a technical pipeline can train a model without itself becoming educated.

### 17.3 Cooperative unit

The Stream B cooperative unit is not automatically a learner. A cooperative-unit Learning attribution requires change in its own integrated organization, relations, memory, procedures, capabilities, or dispositions—not merely changed member states, new records, one better output, or a replaced component.

### 17.4 Multiple levels

Member, team, institution, distributed arrangement, and hybrid Learning or Education attributions may coexist when separately evidenced. They must identify composition and avoid copying the same change across levels.

## 18. Substrate- and level-sensitive formation

### 18.1 Individual biological learner

Evidence may include changed understanding, competence, judgment, conduct, relationship, transfer, reflection, and durability, qualified by embodiment, development, culture, environment, and dependency. Credential, testimony, or one performance is insufficient.

### 18.2 Artificial learner

The proposed Bearer may be a model, configured agent, service, deployment, adaptive controller, or larger system. Parameter change, memory update, prompt history, retrieval access, tool substitution, and runtime adaptation have different boundaries. Stream C permits literal Learning or Formation only with level-specific evidence; it does not assume artificial Education.

An artificial Education attribution remains especially demanding because integrated participation, grounds/limits, effective challenge, transfer, and revision must be supportable without borrowing human properties from operators or designers.

### 18.3 Collective or team learner

Changed members do not automatically make the team learn. Team-level Learning requires changes in integrated communication, division of work, shared or role-linked memory, correction, selection, practice, or coordination that persist appropriately across member activity.

### 18.4 Institution as learner

An institution may learn when its persistent roles, procedures, memory, incentives, review, and action change through experience at the institutional level. A policy document or database update supplies evidence or a resource; it is not institutional Learning unless the organization can interpret, enact, challenge, and preserve the change.

Institutional Education additionally requires broader integration, participatory direction, challengeability, transfer, and consequence evidence.

### 18.5 Distributed learner

Distributed Learning requires stable coupling, communication, memory, control, feedback, and continuity supporting change at the networked level. Parallel node updates or a shared repository are insufficient without integration.

### 18.6 Hybrid learner

A hybrid may learn when the coupling, roles, feedback, memory, and joint practice change at a stable combined level. A fixed model with changed human practice can support hybrid Learning; it does not establish model Learning. A model update with unchanged dysfunctional coupling may not improve the hybrid relation. Removing a constitutive component may defeat the attribution or reveal dependency rather than decay.

## 19. Formation across Knowledge and relation modes

### 19.1 Propositional formation

Formation may change which propositions a learner accepts, how grounds and defeaters are handled, and how uncertainty or scope is judged. Copying content or passing recall tests does not establish Knowledge or Education. False beliefs and epistemic concealment can also be learned.

### 19.2 Practical formation

Practice, feedback, variation, and correction may develop competence and habit. Procedure possession and one successful performance are insufficient. Training often contributes here, but practical Knowledge, trained capability, and Education remain distinct.

### 19.3 Familiarity formation

Accumulated encounter may develop recognition, discrimination, orientation, and situated judgment. A profile, simulation, or copied record cannot reproduce the encounter automatically. Training and instruction may prepare or interpret experience without substituting for it.

### 19.4 Relational formation

Participants may develop dispositions and practices of mutual orientation, contribution, disagreement, restraint, challenge, correction, and repair. Harmony, politeness, compliance, and consensus do not establish relational Education. The capacity to question the relation and its frame remains material.

### 19.5 Institutional formation

Roles, incentives, routines, memory, review, sanctions, interfaces, and tolerated violations may change institution-level practice. Formation can be positive or deformative. Canonical documents and declared values do not establish manifestation; observed routines do not by themselves establish responsible Education.

## 20. Alternatives considered

### A — Adopt the Education Foundation Paper and A3 unchanged

**Rejected as governing analysis.** The material correctly distinguishes many processes but treats Cooperation as the objective, says every architecture educates or trains, overstates repeated Training as universally constitutive of effective Education, and risks transferring Education to collective or artificial Bearers without sufficient boundary evidence.

### B — Collapse Formation, Learning, Training, Education, and socialization into Learning

**Rejected.** It hides intent, mechanism, breadth, normativity, challengeability, power, transfer, and whether a durable learner-level change occurred.

### C — Reduce Education to instruction, curriculum, credential, or Knowledge acquisition

**Rejected.** It substitutes offered content or governance status for integrated developmental achievement and excludes practical, familiarity, relational, and institutional formation.

### D — Reduce Education to Training plus successful performance

**Rejected.** It cannot distinguish narrow compliance, conditioning, brittle optimization, suppressed challenge, or lack of transfer.

### E — Reserve Education and literal Learning to humans

**Rejected as a universal origin rule.** Substrate and level differences are material, but origin alone neither establishes nor excludes the predicates. Artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, and hybrid claims remain evidence-bearing.

### F — Treat every update, record change, or environment response as Learning

**Rejected.** It confuses bearer change with artifact state, external support, component replacement, transient adaptation, or observation.

### G — Layered formation system with qualified achievements

**Retained as the Stream C working approach.** It distinguishes conditions, activities, exposure, mechanisms, effects, achievements, attributions, statuses, and records while preserving substrate-sensitive levels and recursive consequence.

| Human develops dependency through repeated use while model weights remain fixed | Human Learning or habituation and hybrid formation may occur; model Learning does not follow |
| Institution changes procedures after failure while members remain unchanged | Institutional Learning is possible if roles, memory, enactment, and revision change at that level; member Education does not follow |
| Collective is called a learner because records changed | Record change is insufficient without integrated interpretation, enactment, feedback, and continuity at the collective level |
| Self-modifying artificial system changes while its Bearer boundary shifts | Learning attribution remains unresolved until continuity, component replacement, mechanism, and stable success/failure boundary are established |

| Repeated practice improves one task but fails transfer | Narrow trained effect may exist; broad competence or Education is not established |

The working system distinguishes every required case without artifact reification, permanent status, human-origin privilege, or automatic educational success.

## 22. Three-view preservation obligations

### 22.1 Human View

The Human View should explain:

- who or what is said to have changed and at which level;
- purpose, learner, shared world, formative activity, environment, experience, mechanism, and observed change;
- why the result is called Learning, Training, Education, socialization, adaptation, conditioning, habit, correction, repair, or decay;
- power, challenge, affected parties, alternatives, unintended formation, limits, and consequences;
- what the attribution does not imply.

### 22.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View should preserve, where material:

- baseline and comparison conditions;
- longitudinal, transfer, variation, intervention, counterfactual, ablation, process, and failure evidence;
- methods for distinguishing learner change from temporary support, environmental change, component replacement, measurement practice, maturation, or selection;
- competing explanations, uncertainty, studied population/system boundary, and defeat conditions.

No single score, behavior, credential, weight difference, survey, or output metric defines Formation or Education universally.

### 22.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View should eventually preserve:

- learner and Bearer identity, level, boundary, version, components, dependencies, and continuity;
- formative purpose, target, activity, environment, exposure, mechanism, effect, achievement claim, and record as distinct;
- baseline, observations, variation, transfer, persistence, decay, uncertainty, assessment, status, challenge, and lineage;
- instructor, trainer, educator, designer, operator, participant, authority, assessor, beneficiary, affected party, and responsible party separately where material;
- declared loss when dashboards, certificates, model cards, learning records, policies, or runtime fields omit material distinctions.

No schema, learning theory, assessment instrument, curriculum model, metric, status vocabulary, or implementation is selected here.

### 22.4 Alignment test

A system fails alignment when human materials describe integrated Education, scientific evidence measures only immediate task performance, and engineering records preserve only course completion or model version. The mismatch, affected use, uncertainty, and correction lineage must remain explicit.

## 24. Consequences for governing artifacts

### Product Vision

The vision's concern with Education, Training, habit, and continuing challenge remains justified where persistent systems shape participants. “Shape” must not be read as proof that every system trains or educates. The value chain remains distinct: formation may affect Cooperation and capability but is not the medium, process, target capability, reasoning, answer, or value.

### Candidate A3

A3 preserves important warnings but requires material review:

- “stable cooperation depends upon Education” remains an unproven necessity claim pending Streams D–F and persistent-system analysis;
- “Education becomes effective through repeated Training” is too strong as a universal constitutive statement;
- relevant practice, feedback, manifestation, and habit may be necessary for some durable cooperative dispositions without every case being Training;
- “every persistent architecture is a formative environment” should become a formative-condition and assessment claim, not automatic Education or Training;
- named habits remain candidates, not a universal accepted catalogue.

No A3 text or status changes through Stream C.

### Candidate A4

A4's engineering-of-conditions direction is compatible. Architectures can engineer formative conditions, activities, feedback, and records, but cannot manufacture actual Learning or Education by design declaration, workflow completion, or status. Formative consequence belongs to recursive lifecycle assessment.

### Education Foundation Paper

The future v0.2 paper should:

- replace Cooperation-as-objective language;
- separate formative purpose, activity, environment, exposure, process, effect, achievement, attribution, status, and record;
- weaken “every architecture educates/trains” to a testable formative-condition and influence claim;
- distinguish practice from Training and reject repeated Training as universally constitutive of Education;
- separate learner, Participant, cooperative unit, Bearer, educator, instructor, trainer, designer, authority, assessor, beneficiary, affected party, and responsible party;
- qualify artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, and hybrid attributions through stable boundaries and level evidence;
- preserve harmful formation, decay, transfer failure, power, challenge, and dissent.

Publication remains 2.1.10.

### DR-011 and DR-012

Learning, adaptation, Education, and Training remain distinct from Intelligence. Formation records are Representations. The recursive lifecycle now has a more precise formation interface: consequence and feedback may produce influence, effect, Learning, correction, repair, or decay, but no transition creates an achievement or status automatically.

### 2.1.6 through 2.1.8

- Stream D and 2.1.6 receive power, consent, coercion, formative authority, challenge standing, responsibility, punishment, and repair interfaces.
- 2.1.7 must test whether persistent DIAs create formative conditions, whether any component or cooperative unit learns, and whether capability amplification coexists with dependency or decay.
- 2.1.8 must distinguish feedback signal, correction, Learning, answer improvement, action, consequence, repair, and value.

### Derived projects

Projects will own Domain pedagogy, curricula, professional formation, training methods, target capabilities, evidence thresholds, certification, cultural practices, and operational projections. They must preserve the universal distinctions and cannot infer Learning, Training success, Education, or institutional change from completion, score, deployment, or record alone.

No derived-project artifact changes through Stream C.
