## 1. Question and analytical result

> What minimum makes an organized system a Domain Intelligence Amplifier rather than an ordinary tool, repository, workflow, platform, automation, product, coordinated group, or marketing label, and must every such system contain artificial Intelligence?

Stream C supports the following working answer:

> **A Domain Intelligence Amplifier is an identified, bounded, sufficiently continuous organized System oriented to a declared Domain purpose and class of complex Domain questions, within which plural distinguishable Intelligence Bearers actually cooperate through governed Domain Knowledge, and whose organized operation is evidentially capable of supporting repeatable Amplification of an identified Bearer's relevant Intelligence configuration across relevant variation. The system-class attribution must preserve the system boundary and organization; participant, component, resource, environment, provider, beneficiary, affected-subject, amplified-Bearer, and responsible-party levels; the target capability, baseline, integration, dependencies, failures, uncertainty, challenge, and lifecycle; and the distinction among actual class membership, attribution claim, assessment, governance status, and every product, architecture, certificate, or other Representation.**

The candidate has seven constitutive elements:

1. an identified and justified **System** boundary, organization, Identity, and continuity;
2. a declared **Domain purpose** and bounded class of **complex Domain questions**;
3. plural distinguishable participant **Intelligence Bearers** supported at their own levels;
4. actual **Cooperation** among those Intelligences in a shared epistemic undertaking;
5. **governed Domain Knowledge** as the medium, realized through claims, grounds, methods, Representations, histories, statuses, challenge, application, consequence, and revision rather than stored Knowledge substance;
6. an explicit **Amplified Intelligence target** identifying the proposed amplified Bearer, capability object, baseline, Scope, Context, time, gains, losses, dependencies, and Evidence; and
7. an evidenced, repeatable **system capability to support the Amplification relation** across relevant variation, not merely an aspiration, one successful output, or a feature list.

The definition is not circular. Intelligence is independently governed by DR-011; Knowledge and its epistemic lifecycle by DR-011/DR-012; Cooperation by DR-013; Amplification by Stream A; and the amplified Bearer/profile by Stream B. DIA class membership composes these already independent meanings in one specific organized-system pattern. Amplification does not depend on DIA status, and a valid amplification relation does not automatically create a DIA.

The DIA system and the amplified Intelligence Bearer need not be identical. A DIA may amplify an individual participant, a team, an institution, a configured-system Bearer, or multiple separately evidenced subjects. The DIA may itself also bear Intelligence or Amplified Intelligence, but each attribution requires independent level-specific Evidence.

Artificial Intelligence is not a universal necessary condition. It may occupy participant, component, resource, tool, amplifier, Bearer, assessor, or other roles, but a fully Human, institutional, distributed, or other substrate configuration remains eligible when every constitutive condition is satisfied. Conversely, AI presence does not establish any DIA condition by itself.

These are analytical directions only. Stream D must test integration mechanisms, dependency, Power, Responsibility, and lifecycle before the class can support a decision proposal.

## 3. Why SymK needs a system-class boundary

The phrase “Domain Intelligence Amplifier” can fail in opposite directions.

It can be too broad:

- every search tool becomes a DIA because it improves access;
- every AI product becomes a DIA because it generates answers;
- every team becomes a DIA because members cooperate;
- every repository becomes a DIA because it contains Domain content;
- every automation becomes a DIA because it increases throughput;
- every platform becomes a DIA because it connects participants; or
- every successful project becomes a DIA because the label is strategically useful.

It can also be too narrow:

- only systems containing an LLM count;
- only Human–AI pairs count;
- only one knowledge-graph architecture counts;
- only systems that themselves bear Intelligence count;
- only systems that improve every dimension count;
- only systems producing better answers or lower cost count; or
- only systems already certified as responsible and beneficial count.

Both failures make the Product Vision unusable. The first erases SymK's identity by admitting ordinary tools and organizations. The second turns one implementation, participant type, architecture, success criterion, or normative judgment into the universal system class.

The boundary must therefore be functional and relational without being merely aspirational, technological, architectural, or outcome-defined.

## 4. System class, instance, operation, claim, and status

At least six levels must remain distinct:

1. **DIA system-class concept:** the universal semantic minimum proposed in Stream C.
2. **Candidate system:** an identified organized system proposed as a class member.
3. **Actual class membership:** the candidate system actually satisfies the constitutive conditions within declared Domain, Scope, Context, and time.
4. **DIA operation or episode:** a bounded occasion in which the system is used and its participants may or may not cooperate or achieve amplification.
5. **Class-attribution claim and assessment:** a source claims membership and an assessor evaluates the claim against Evidence and alternatives.
6. **Governance status and record:** an Authority may recognize, restrict, suspend, certify, dispute, expire, or supersede the claim for a declared use; Representations preserve selected information.

These levels can diverge. A valid DIA can have a failed episode. A product can be designed as a DIA candidate without yet satisfying the class. A status may authorize a pilot without proving capability. A real class member may be misclassified. A certificate or registry entry can be wrong or stale.

## 6. Constitutive condition N1 — identified organized System

### 6.1 System rather than collection

The proposed class member must be an organized whole with a justified boundary. Merely placing people, models, documents, tools, and workflows in one platform or organization does not establish a System for DIA purposes.

Relevant organization may include differentiated functions, interfaces, dependencies, constraints, state, communication, coordination, correction, maintenance, lifecycle, or other relations. No single architecture or mechanism is universal.

### 6.2 Boundary stability

The boundary used to claim amplification must remain visible when analyzing failure, dependency, cost, Power, Responsibility, affected subjects, update, replacement, and remedy. The system may have permeable or changing boundaries, but those changes must be governed rather than opportunistic.

### 6.3 System need not be an Intelligence Bearer

DIA is a specific System class. Its system-class membership does not imply that the System itself bears Intelligence. The system may organize Cooperation among participant Intelligences and amplify one or more of them without supporting a whole-level Intelligence attribution.

If the system is also claimed to bear Intelligence or Amplified Intelligence, Stream B's lowest-adequate-level, integration, profile, baseline, and evidence rules apply separately.

## 7. Constitutive condition N2 — Domain purpose and complex-question orientation

### 7.1 Declared Domain

The system must identify a Domain in the DR-010 sense: a bounded field of relevance, practice, inquiry, or competence that contributes to Context and Scope without becoming identical to either.

A dataset category, product market, department name, profession, benchmark suite, user segment, or folder hierarchy may help represent a Domain but does not define it automatically.

### 7.2 Domain purpose

The system requires a declared purpose connecting its organized operation to a Domain undertaking. Purpose is project-governed and may be contested, multi-level, or revised. A mission statement, sales claim, contract, or owner intention does not by itself establish actual organization or class membership.

The universal minimum does not require the purpose to be morally good, legally valid, professionally authorized, or beneficial. Those assessments remain separate and can disqualify SymK compatibility or use without changing a true descriptive capability claim.

### 7.3 Complex-question class

The system must be oriented to a bounded recurring class of Domain questions whose adequate treatment requires more than direct retrieval, fixed execution, or one isolated output. Complexity may involve interdependent claims, uncertainty, conflicting evidence, contextual judgment, multiple perspectives, changing conditions, trade-offs, consequence, or iterative inquiry.

This is a directional boundary, not a universal complexity definition. Domain and project authorities own exact question classes and criteria; 2.1.8 owns full reasoning/answer treatment.

### 7.4 One question is insufficient

One difficult prompt, successful consultation, model output, or emergency episode cannot define the system class. The question orientation must be part of the system's organized capability and evidenced across relevant variation.

## 8. Constitutive condition N3 — plural distinguishable Intelligence participants

### 8.1 Qualified participant Bearers

At least two distinguishable participant subjects must have independent, DR-011-compatible Intelligence attributions at their own levels. Human, artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, and hybrid candidates remain eligible; origin and substrate do not decide qualification.

Two interface accounts, model instances, roles, departments, agents, or components do not establish two Intelligence Bearers by naming.

### 8.2 No property copying

The DIA system's organization does not copy Intelligence to every participant, component, tool, repository, or role. A participant may contribute as a resource, source, operator, affected subject, or conduit without being one of the cooperating Intelligences.

### 8.3 Same subject at multiple levels

Member, team, institution, configured-system, and hybrid claims may coexist only when independently evidenced. The same underlying people or components cannot be double-counted as plural Intelligences merely by relabeling levels.

### 8.4 No mandatory professional title

Skilled professionals are central to the Product Vision and common DIA configurations, but formal professional status is not a universal semantic condition. Domain practitioners, teams, institutions, artificial Bearers, or other Intelligences may satisfy the participant condition. Professional authority and standards remain Domain/external matters.

## 9. Constitutive condition N4 — actual Cooperation among Intelligences

### 9.1 Cooperation, not mere coexistence

The participant Intelligences must actually cooperate within the system's shared epistemic undertaking. Stream C inherits DR-013's thin nucleus:

- plural distinguishable participants;
- a bounded shared undertaking;
- materially relevant contributions;
- mutual orientation;
- relational connection; and
- preserved difference.

### 9.2 Architecture cannot create Cooperation

Messaging, orchestration, workflow, shared storage, access control, routing, parallel execution, common interface, API integration, or Human approval may support Cooperation. None establishes the relational process alone.

### 9.3 Cooperation need not be harmonious or equal

Disagreement, dissent, competition, asymmetry, constrained alternatives, conflict, and Power may coexist with Cooperation. Coercion, manipulation, suppression, extraction, or domination may defeat responsible or qualified cooperation and may challenge whether mutual orientation exists, but Stream C does not decide every case.

### 9.4 Idle and episodic systems

An idle system may retain the organized capability for cooperation if Identity and capability persist. An operational episode may fail to realize Cooperation even when the same system remains a DIA. Repeated non-cooperative operation may defeat the class attribution by showing that the constitutive capability never existed or no longer persists.

## 10. Constitutive condition N5 — governed Domain Knowledge as medium

### 10.1 Knowledge is not a stored ingredient

The system does not contain Knowledge as a substance. Knowledge belongs to qualified Bearers under its mode-specific conditions. Systems operate through Knowledge-related claims, grounds, methods, capabilities, histories, assets, Representations, and practices.

### 10.2 Minimum epistemic governance

The system must make Domain epistemic work governable at a consequence-proportional level. Materially relevant elements include:

- claim and subject identity;
- grounds and Evidence;
- Provenance and transformation;
- Domain, Scope, Context, and time;
- uncertainty, disagreement, counterevidence, and defeaters;
- epistemic status distinct from governance status;
- source, assessor, authority, and intended use;
- application and consequence;
- challenge, correction, supersession, and revision; and
- lineage across Representations and views.

No one technical record must contain every element. The meaning must remain recoverable across the organized system.

### 10.3 Shared medium does not mean unrestricted sharing

Governed availability may be asymmetric, mediated, role-specific, privacy-preserving, security-constrained, or differently represented. Cooperation does not require identical beliefs, universal access, consensus, one canonical document, or one common schema.

### 10.4 Repository and retrieval insufficiency

Content volume, retrieval accuracy, search quality, context-window size, data integration, provenance fields, or repository canonicalization may support the medium. They do not establish actual Knowledge, Cooperation, amplification, or DIA membership by themselves.

## 11. Constitutive condition N6 — explicit Amplified Intelligence target

### 11.1 Target is not class membership

The system must be organized toward Amplification of an identified Intelligence Bearer's relevant capability profile. Purpose or intent alone is insufficient; it identifies the functional target that condition N7 must evidence.

### 11.2 Required target structure

The target must preserve:

- proposed amplified Bearer and level;
- capability object, dimension, specialization, or configuration;
- Domain, Scope, Context, and relevant variation;
- materially comparable baseline or counterfactual;
- expected gains, losses, unchanged and unknown dimensions;
- new or lost integration;
- assistance, resources, dependencies, and alternatives;
- time, persistence, retention, development, and revalidation conditions;
- source, assessor, authority, uncertainty, challenge, and intended use; and
- affected distribution where material.

### 11.3 System and target Bearer may differ

The DIA may amplify:

- an internal participant;
- an external user who participates through the system;
- a team or institution;
- a configured-system Bearer;
- multiple subjects at different levels; or
- the DIA system itself, if an independent whole-level Bearer attribution and baseline pass.

No option is universal.

### 11.4 Target profile is not value proof

The selected capability profile reflects purpose and often value. Its authority and challenge path must be explicit. Target conformity does not establish better answers, lower total cost, responsible operation, ethical compatibility, or benefit.

## 12. Constitutive condition N7 — evidenced system capability to support repeatable Amplification

### 12.1 More than aspiration

The proposed system must have an actual organized capability to support the Stream A Amplification relation for at least one declared target Bearer and capability object across relevant variation. A roadmap, design, feature, architecture, policy, prompt, demo, output, benchmark, certification, or product claim is insufficient.

### 12.2 Two evidence propositions

The class claim requires support for:

1. **system-capability proposition:** the identified system organization can support the relevant cooperative epistemic process across variation; and
2. **amplification proposition:** operation through that system contributes to increased capability of the identified target Bearer relative to the declared baseline.

Evidence for one does not establish the other.

### 12.3 Repeatability

Repeatability does not require identical outcomes, deterministic behavior, perpetual availability, or one universal statistical threshold. It requires evidence that the result is not one lucky, leaked, scripted, cherry-picked, or incomparable episode and that the claimed capability survives materially relevant variation.

### 12.4 Failure evidence

Relevant failure, uncertainty, degradation, and boundary evidence are constitutive to the class attribution's challengeability. A system that only records successes cannot support a mature claim.

### 12.5 Candidate before evidence

A newly designed or configured system may be called a **DIA candidate**, design candidate, or proposed implementation in ordinary language. It should not receive unqualified actual class membership until the system-capability and amplification propositions are supportable. These labels are analytical aids, not accepted status vocabulary.

## 13. Necessary, common, optional, prohibited, and Domain-owned conditions

### 13.1 Necessary universal conditions

| Condition | Why necessary |
|---|---|
| Identified organized System | DIA is a system class, not a feature, artifact, episode, or relation alone |
| Justified boundary, organization, Identity, and continuity | Prevents aggregation, brand identity, and boundary shopping |
| Declared Domain and Domain purpose | “Domain” is constitutive to the class identity |
| Bounded complex-question class | Distinguishes the class from generic productivity and direct lookup |
| Plural distinguishable qualified Intelligence participants | Product Vision and DR-013 require Cooperation among Intelligences |
| Actual Cooperation | A connection or workflow is not the process |
| Governed Domain Knowledge as medium | Distinguishes epistemic system class from ordinary coordination or automation |
| Explicit amplified Bearer and capability target | Prevents circular or beneficiary-based amplification claims |
| Evidenced system capability and Amplification relation across relevant variation | Prevents aspiration, labels, features, or one outcome from defining membership |
| Challengeable class attribution with dependencies, failures, uncertainty, time, and lineage | Prevents status and implementation from becoming self-validating |

### 13.2 Common but not universal conditions

Common DIA configurations may include:

- skilled professionals or Domain practitioners;
- artificial Intelligence components or participants;
- persistent repositories and memory;
- structured knowledge representations;
- retrieval and search;
- reasoning support;
- orchestration and workflow;
- facilitation, moderation, supervision, or review;
- feedback and adaptation;
- institutional roles and governance bodies;
- versioning, audit, observability, and system cards;
- distributed infrastructure and external services; or
- Education, Training, onboarding, and formation activities.

Frequency, usefulness, or Product Vision salience does not make an item universally necessary.

### 13.3 Optional realization mechanisms

The class does not require a particular:

- LLM, model, agent, planner, optimizer, or algorithm;
- knowledge graph, database, vector store, document repository, or file format;
- centralized, distributed, federated, local, cloud, embodied, or analog architecture;
- synchronous, asynchronous, conversational, workflow, deliberative, or batch interaction;
- Human-in-the-loop, Human-on-the-loop, AI-in-the-loop, or automated-control arrangement;
- interface, API, ontology, logic, schema, protocol, programming language, or product stack;
- memory, learning, adaptation, autonomy, or persistence mechanism;
- metric, threshold, benchmark, capability profile, or certification method; or
- business model, ownership, organizational, contractual, or legal form.

### 13.4 Prohibited defining substitutions

No class definition may substitute:

- AI for Intelligence plurality;
- connection for Cooperation;
- content or retrieval for governed Domain Knowledge;
- system output for participant capability;
- automation for Amplification;
- architecture for system class;
- product, platform, workflow, or project for System identity;
- benchmark, score, adoption, certification, or compliance for actual membership;
- Human approval for epistemic adequacy or Responsibility;
- better/faster/cheaper answers for Amplified Intelligence;
- intended benefit for actual value; or
- a wide system boundary for missing integration or Evidence.

### 13.5 Domain- and external-authority-owned conditions

The universal class does not own:

- the actual Domain boundary and purpose;
- the exact complex-question class;
- Domain Knowledge claims, standards, methods, and evidence criteria;
- participant competence, licensure, professional role, or authority;
- capability dimensions, specializations, target profiles, weights, and thresholds;
- acceptable performance, robustness, uncertainty, risk, and total cost;
- security, privacy, safety, legal, contractual, regulatory, and ethical requirements;
- actual Responsibility, standing, consent, remedy, or governance allocation;
- architecture, components, providers, deployment, operation, maintenance, and retirement; or
- empirical class validation and certification procedures.

These authorities may add constraints but may not silently redefine the universal DIA minimum outside their jurisdiction.

## 14. DIA, Amplifier, amplified Bearer, and system Bearer

Four roles must remain separate:

| Role | Working meaning | Non-entailment |
|---|---|---|
| DIA system | Organized system satisfying the specific class conditions | Need not itself bear Intelligence or be the target Bearer |
| Amplifier | Intervention/resource/relation/arrangement contributing to an amplification relation | Need not satisfy the DIA class |
| Amplified Bearer | Subject whose capability configuration increases | Need not include the whole DIA boundary |
| Intelligence-bearing system | System independently satisfying DR-011 at its own level | Need not be a DIA or amplified |

One actual subject may occupy several roles when separately evidenced. No role transfers through naming.

Examples:

- a DIA amplifies an individual professional while the whole system lacks a whole-level Intelligence attribution;
- an artificial participant acts as one amplifier within a fully configured DIA;
- a training intervention amplifies a Human but is not a DIA because plural Intelligence Cooperation and governed Domain Knowledge are absent;
- an intelligent autonomous system is not a DIA when it does not organize Cooperation among Intelligences through governed Domain Knowledge; or
- a DIA system also becomes an amplified configured-system Bearer after an update, but only under a separate same-Bearer baseline and profile claim.

## 15. Architecture, product, project, platform, workflow, and tool boundary

### 15.1 Architecture

Architecture represents organization and realization choices. Multiple architectures may instantiate the class, and one architecture may support DIA and non-DIA systems. An architecture diagram does not establish actual organization, Cooperation, Knowledge governance, or amplification.

### 15.2 Product

A product is an offered or governed artifact/service arrangement. One product may contain multiple system instances, integrate into a larger DIA, provide only one component, or falsely claim DIA status. Product identity and system Identity are not interchangeable.

### 15.3 Project

A project is an undertaking that may design, operate, study, govern, or use a DIA. The project is not automatically the system. A project can fail to create a DIA, and a DIA can persist across projects.

### 15.4 Platform

A platform may host, connect, or supply multiple systems. Shared infrastructure and interfaces do not make every tenant one system or every application a DIA.

### 15.5 Workflow

A workflow is an organized sequence or control Representation. It may contribute to Cooperation and system operation, but steps and handoffs do not establish participant Intelligence, mutual orientation, governed Knowledge, or Amplification.

### 15.6 Tool

A tool may be an amplifier, resource, component, interface, or participant only when qualified. A useful tool is not a DIA merely because a user becomes more effective.

### 15.7 Marketing label and certificate

Branding, vendor claims, standards labels, maturity ratings, audits, certifications, and registry entries are claims or governance statuses. They neither create nor guarantee class membership.

## 16. Artificial Intelligence necessity — DQ-016 analysis

### 16.1 Candidate universal answer

Stream C supports the following proposal-ready but non-authoritative answer:

> **No. Artificial Intelligence is not constitutive of every Domain Intelligence Amplifier. A DIA requires Cooperation among plural qualified Intelligence Bearers through governed Domain Knowledge toward evidenced Amplification within a Domain; the substrate and origin of those Bearers are not fixed. Artificial Intelligence may be a participant, component, resource, tool, amplifier, Bearer, assessor, or no part of a particular DIA. Its presence neither establishes nor is universally required for class membership.**

### 16.2 Product Vision interpretation

The Product Vision's phrase “professionals, AI, and other Intelligences” identifies a salient heterogeneous future and explicitly places AI among possible Intelligences. The 2.1.7 opening separately requires artificial Intelligence as possible participant/component/resource/Bearer to be distinguished from a necessary component and warns against AI essentialism. Read together, the authoritative direction supports inclusion without universality.

### 16.3 Fully Human case

A Human team or institution can satisfy the candidate class if plural qualified Intelligence Bearers cooperate through governed Domain Knowledge around complex questions and the organized system evidentially supports amplification. Ordinary teamwork, committees, professional associations, or institutions do not qualify automatically.

### 16.4 Fully artificial or non-Human case

A configuration without Human participants is not excluded by origin, but every Intelligence, Cooperation, Domain Knowledge, target Bearer, system-capability, evidence, affected-subject, and Responsibility boundary still applies. Stream C does not assert that any actual fully artificial configuration satisfies them.

### 16.5 AI-rich false positive

An AI-rich product that generates, retrieves, automates, scales, optimizes, or recommends without plural Intelligence Cooperation, governed Domain Knowledge, and evidenced capability Amplification is not a DIA under the candidate minimum.

## 17. Human and professional participation

### 17.1 Human participation is not universally constitutive

The non-privilege principles and substrate-neutral Intelligence concept prevent Human origin from being a universal class condition. A particular constitutional, legal, professional, safety, or project rule may require Human participation or Authority for a declared use without redefining the universal class.

### 17.2 Professional relevance remains central

The Product Vision seeks increased reasoning capacity for skilled professionals across projects, organizations, technologies, and generations. This remains a principal intended application and later consequence test. It does not make professional title, employment relation, licensure, or one occupational structure a universal semantic condition.

### 17.3 Affected Humans remain visible

The absence of a Human participant does not remove Humans or other subjects from the affected-subject and Responsibility horizon. Nonparticipant effects remain material to governance, compatibility, and use.

## 18. Class membership, success, failure, and lifecycle

### 18.1 Class membership is capability, not episode success

A DIA is identified partly through an organized capability to support repeatable amplification. It need not amplify every participant in every episode or produce a correct answer every time. Failure evidence remains part of the capability assessment.

### 18.2 Persistent failure

Repeated failure across the claimed Scope may show:

- the system never satisfied the class;
- the system lost a constitutive capability;
- the Domain, Scope, or target was overstated;
- dependencies or conditions changed;
- an episode failed while class capability remains; or
- the Evidence is indeterminate.

No one interpretation follows automatically.

### 18.3 Degraded, suspended, and historical membership

A system may remain the same identified subject while its class capability is degraded, suspended, contested, expired, or lost. Historical truth that it once qualified does not guarantee current membership. These are analytical state distinctions, not accepted status vocabulary.

### 18.4 Update and successor

Component, participant, model, data, process, provider, purpose, Domain, target, or governance change may require revalidation. If Identity continuity fails, a successor receives a new class attribution rather than inheriting the predecessor's status.

### 18.5 Retirement and records

A retired system can remain historically classifiable while no longer operating. Its archives, model weights, documents, certificates, or records are not themselves the DIA unless they independently satisfy the system conditions.

## 19. Capability, responsible operation, compatibility, and value

### 19.1 Descriptive class versus responsible qualification

A system may satisfy the descriptive DIA class while operating irresponsibly, unlawfully, coercively, unsafely, unfairly, or harmfully. Erasing the descriptive capability claim because of adverse consequences would create outcome-based ontology; treating class membership as responsible would create capability-based ethics.

Stream D must determine how responsible amplification and SymK-compatible DIA qualifications relate to the descriptive class under DR-014.

### 19.2 Epistemic governance remains constitutive

Governed Domain Knowledge is part of the descriptive system class, not a complete ethical judgment. A system that merely produces answers without recoverable epistemic governance fails the class even if outcomes are beneficial.

### 19.3 Better, faster, lower total cost

These are constrained value objectives downstream of Amplified Intelligence. None is necessary to every episode or sufficient for class membership. A valid DIA can produce a worse answer, operate slowly, cost more, or cause harm in a case. Repeated failure may defeat its claimed capability or value, but the relations remain distinct.

### 19.4 SymK-compatible DIA

“SymK-compatible DIA” is a possible later qualification requiring the accepted class plus applicable constitutional, Responsibility, epistemic, challenge, lifecycle, project, and implementation obligations. Stream C does not create that status or decide whether the phrase belongs in DR-015, 2.2, or 2.5.

## 20. Evidentiary test layers

### 20.1 Layer 1 — semantic completeness

Can the claim identify the system, Domain, purpose, complex-question class, participant Intelligences, Cooperation, governed Domain Knowledge, target Bearer/profile, baseline, system contribution, relevant variation, dependencies, evidence, uncertainty, challenge, status, and lineage?

Missing material elements defeat an unqualified claim before performance evidence is interpreted.

### 20.2 Layer 2 — condition-specific actuality

Does each constitutive condition actually hold, independently of the claim, architecture, status, or record?

### 20.3 Layer 3 — cross-condition coherence

Do the system boundary, participant levels, Domain, Scope, time, baseline, target Bearer, Cooperation occurrence, Knowledge governance, and amplification Evidence refer to the same materially stable configuration?

### 20.4 Layer 4 — counterexample and alternative explanation

Can content, lookup, luck, leakage, aggregation, automation, external control, unequal comparison, post hoc boundary expansion, hidden Human labor, provider dependence, or selective reporting explain the observed result?

### 20.5 Layer 5 — lifecycle validity

Does the attribution remain valid after updates, participant change, failure, outage, provider change, boundary change, new counterevidence, challenge, or revised Domain conditions?

### 20.6 Layer 6 — use and compatibility qualification

Which reliance, deployment, certification, constitutional, professional, legal, ethical, or project decision may the assessment govern? This layer does not create the actual class membership.

The layers provide the conceptual part of DQ-011. Operational methods, thresholds, and validation remain 2.7 and relevant Domain/external authority.

## 21. Candidate approaches considered

### A — DIA as any system that improves a user's performance

**Rejected.** Assistance, tools, training, access, luck, output, and one-dimensional performance can improve without the system-class conditions.

### B — DIA as any AI-enabled Domain product

**Rejected.** AI presence, Domain branding, product identity, and task automation do not establish participant Intelligence, Cooperation, governed Knowledge, or amplification.

### C — DIA as a Human–AI pair

**Rejected as universal.** It privileges one participant pattern, admits non-cooperative tool use, excludes fully Human or other configurations, and hides wider system dependencies.

### D — DIA as any configured system that bears Amplified Intelligence

**Rejected.** The DIA need not itself be the amplified Bearer; the approach also omits Domain purpose, plural Intelligence Cooperation, and governed Knowledge.

### E — DIA as any Knowledge Engineering system

**Rejected.** Knowledge Engineering may create epistemic conditions without plural Intelligence Cooperation or actual Amplification.

### F — DIA as a certified architecture or product category

**Rejected.** Representations, standards, and governance statuses do not create actual system organization or capability.

### G — DIA only when it produces better, faster, cheaper, responsible answers

**Rejected as the class definition.** It collapses capability, answer, value, cost, Responsibility, and outcome and makes failures change ontology opportunistically.

### H — Specific organized-system class composing Domain, complex-question, Intelligence-participant, Cooperation, governed-Knowledge, target-Bearer, and evidenced-amplification conditions

**Retained as the Stream C working direction.** It preserves the Product Vision value chain, avoids circularity and architecture essentialism, and separates actual membership from use, compatibility, and outcome.
