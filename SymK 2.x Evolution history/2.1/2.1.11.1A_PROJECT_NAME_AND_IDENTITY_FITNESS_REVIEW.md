## Review question

> Does “SymK,” including its historical expansion “Symbiotic Knowledge,” still identify the Stage-Accepted DR-017 discipline and framework accurately enough for constitutional, project, corpus and release use?

## Why the question is material

The historical name arose from a Human–AI symbiotic-cooperation protocol. DR-017 now identifies a broader responsible Knowledge Engineering discipline and framework centered analytically on governed knowledge undertakings involving heterogeneous Intelligence Bearers, supporting participants, institutions, representations and engineered systems.

The accepted system also distinguishes Knowledge from claims and artifacts and states that Knowledge Engineering engineers epistemic conditions rather than Knowledge or truth directly. A project name that implies a fixed dyad, automatic mutual benefit, or directly engineered Knowledge could therefore misstate identity, object or jurisdiction.

## Alternatives that must remain open

| Alternative | Continuity benefit | Principal risk |
|---|---|---|
| Retain “SymK” and “Symbiotic Knowledge” | Maximum historical continuity | Historical expansion may materially misdescribe current identity |
| Retain “SymK” as an opaque proper name; retire the expansion | Preserves recognition and identifiers | Requires disciplined explanation and may retain misleading associations |
| Adopt a new defensible expansion | Preserves acronym | Risks reverse-engineering language to fit letters |
| Adopt a successor framework name; preserve SymK as lineage or bounded specialization | Maximizes semantic transparency | Highest migration, recognition and provenance burden |
| Defer decision under a controlled placeholder | Allows more evidence | Semantic debt may leak into Constitution and packaging |

No alternative is preferred by this scaffold.

## Required evidence

- exact DR-001–018 identity and concept statements;
- all four completed Human readings and name-related findings;
- historical uses and expansions of “SymK” across governed and legacy sources;
- newcomer comprehension evidence;
- constitutional and jurisdictional consequences;
- affected repository paths, identifiers, links, packages and derived projects;
- costs and risks of semantic retention versus physical rename;
- a later external distinctiveness/domain/trademark check for any semantic shortlist; and
- dissent, uncertainty and reopening conditions.

## Tests

1. **Identity fidelity:** Does the name fit DR-017 without explanatory contradiction?
2. **Object fidelity:** Does it avoid implying that Knowledge or truth is manufactured directly?
3. **Participant neutrality:** Does it avoid fixing Human–AI, dyadic, substrate or origin roles?
4. **Cooperation fidelity:** Does it avoid implying automatic symmetry, benefit, consent or successful Cooperation?
5. **Jurisdiction:** Does it avoid claiming all Knowledge Engineering, Intelligence or social cooperation?
6. **Distinctiveness:** Can it identify the framework apart from DIA, implementations and derived projects?
7. **Newcomer comprehension:** What does an informed newcomer infer before reading corrective material?
8. **Lineage safety:** Can history, citations and accepted decisions remain discoverable?
9. **Migration feasibility:** Can documents, identifiers, repositories, packages and releases change without authority drift?
10. **Future fitness:** Does the result remain usable through 3.0 and later revision?

## Permitted dispositions

- retain current name and expansion with reasons and non-entailments;
- retain the proper name but formally retire the expansion;
- select a successor name through an exact decision;
- defer under an exact placeholder, owner, deadline/gate and consequence rule; or
- return the question for additional evidence.
