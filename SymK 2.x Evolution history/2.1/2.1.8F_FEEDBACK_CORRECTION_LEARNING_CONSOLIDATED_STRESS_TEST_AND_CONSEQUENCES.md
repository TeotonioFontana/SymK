Stream F supports the following working result:

> **Feedback is a scoped information-or-consequence return relation in which an identified signal, Representation, observation, assessment, challenge, complaint, anomaly, outcome, or consequence is made available to an identified receiving subject or organized system in relation to an identified prior state, question, answer, target, standard, expectation, decision, action, policy, or governing object. Feedback does not by itself establish reception, interpretation, evidentiary relevance, uptake, response, correction, update, capability change, Learning, Formation, improvement, Authority, or value.**

> **Correction is a scoped response-and-change family directed at an identified error, defect, deviation, falsehood, omission, incompatibility, harm-producing condition, or governance failure under declared criteria. Correction proposal, Authorization, action, actual changed state, verification, repair, remedy, recurrence prevention, and record are distinct. Overwrite is not correction: the prior object, use, consequence, and responsibility lineage remain visible.**

> **Learning retains DR-013's Stage-Accepted meaning: comparatively durable change at an identified Learner level linked to experience, inquiry, practice, feedback, interaction, or adaptation and manifested beyond the immediate support condition. Receipt, parameter change, update, changed output, behavior change, score gain, correction, adaptation, or improvement does not establish Learning automatically. System Learning additionally requires a stable Learner boundary, system-level experience and mechanism, integrated durable change, manifestation under relevant variation, and controlled competing explanations.**

> **The consequence-return path is recursive rather than a linear promotion pipeline. Observation may generate a signal; a signal may be interpreted and assessed as Evidence or counterevidence for a specified claim; feedback may be transmitted and received; uptake may inform response; correction, update, repair, decision, policy, concept, or constitutional review may follow under competent Authority; behavior or capability may change; Learning or Formation may be assessed; and later consequences may reopen any earlier layer. > **The consolidated value-chain minimum preserves Question, Reasoning, Answer, Answer Quality, constrained Value, professional decision/action, Domain outcome, consequence, Feedback, Correction, and Learning as non-substitutable relations linked by explicit claims, Evidence, Authority, Responsibility, affected horizon, time, cost, distribution, challenge, and lineage. ## 3. Accepted feedback, correction, and Learning baseline

Stream F applies rather than reopens these DR-012 through DR-014 commitments:

1. The epistemic lifecycle is recursive: consequence, challenge, and new inquiry may enter at any phase.
2. Observed effects become contributions requiring interpretation and may challenge earlier layers or Views; they do not amend them automatically.
3. Feedback is a governed supporting information/consequence relation under DR-013.
4. Feedback does not establish reception, uptake, correction, Learning, Formation, or improvement.
5. Uptake is a governed supporting response relation distinct from exposure and favorable change.
6. Correction is a governed supporting response/change family directed at an identified error or deviation.
7. Repair, recovery, remedy, containment, cessation, compensation, punishment, suppression, and correction remain distinct.
8. Learning is a governed supporting developmental-change concept requiring comparatively durable experience-linked change at an identified Learner level.
9. Formation is the Stage-Accepted Foundational temporally extended shaping or reorganization of comparatively durable capacities, dispositions, practices, judgments, relationships, or organized patterns.
10. Learning, Training, Education, Formation, improvement, Intelligence, Knowledge, value, and responsible action do not entail one another universally.
11. System Learning requires stable Learner Identity, system-level experience and mechanism, integrated durable change, manifestation under relevant variation, and controlled competing explanations.
12. Selection, replacement, redesign, retrieval, current Context, external operator change, environmental change, or component change may explain output differences without system Learning.
13. Effective challenge requires more than permission: notice, access, capacity, protection, representation or standing, reception, evaluation, reasoned response, possible correction, repair or remedy, appeal, recurrence treatment, and Learning remain distinct.
14. Responsibility includes prevention-to-repair continuity where applicable but no one response discharges every Responsibility relation.
15. Historical preservation forbids revision, correction, supersession, withdrawal, restriction, or retirement from rewriting earlier states, uses, effects, or authority.

## 4. Unified semantic spine

The consolidated spine is:

```text
Domain situation or problem
    ↓ framing / challenge / affected horizon
governed Question and answer obligation
    ↓ inquiry / Evidence acquisition
Reasoning and adjacent operations
    ↓ product / support / trace relation
Answer content-to-question relation
    ↓ multidimensional assessment
Answer-Quality and responsible-answer profile
    ↓ temporal / cost / plural evaluative assessment
constrained Value profile
    ↓ recommendation / decision support
authorized professional decision or Action
    ↓ execution / causal process
Domain outcome
    ↓ direct / indirect / delayed / distributed effects
Consequences
    ↓ observation / signal / measurement / challenge
Feedback relation
    ↓ interpretation / assessment / uptake / Authority
Response / Correction / Repair / Remedy / Update
    ↓ durable change assessment
Learning / Formation / capability and practice change
    ↺ may challenge Question, Knowledge, reasoning, answer, quality,
      value, decision, Responsibility, policy, concept, or Constitution
```

Every arrow is a typed, scoped, temporal, challengeable relationship. None is automatic promotion, causal proof, Authority transfer, status transition, or value validation.

## 7. Observation, data, and registered occurrence

An **observation** is an occurrence in which an identified subject or organized arrangement registers selected aspects of a state, event, relation, behavior, or change under a declared mode, Context, and time.

Observation does not entail:

- measurement validity;
- complete access;
- unbiased sampling;
- correct interpretation;
- causal relevance;
- evidentiary relevance;
- feedback transmission;
- affected-subject representation; or
- truth of a later claim.

Data may represent recorded observations, generated states, labels, transactions, or transformations. Data origin and structure do not give intrinsic evidentiary force.

## 8. Signal, measurement, Evidence, and counterevidence

### 8.1 Signal

A **signal** is an event, state, pattern, message, score, report, Representation, or consequence treated as potentially informative about an identified object under a declared interpretive relation.

Availability, salience, volume, recency, dashboard placement, alert severity, or reward magnitude does not establish relevance or validity.

### 8.2 Measurement

A **measurement** is a method-governed assignment or estimation concerning an identified construct, object, unit, scale, instrument, procedure, population, Context, and time with uncertainty and validity conditions.

Measurement is not the measured construct. A precise measurement can be invalid; a valid measure can be incomplete; an indicator can be useful without being exhaustive.

### 8.3 Evidence

Evidence retains DR-012's contextual-role meaning. The same signal may support one claim, challenge another, and remain irrelevant to a third.

### 8.4 Counterevidence and defeaters

Counterevidence may rebut content, undercut support, reveal measurement defect, expose changed applicability, challenge causality, or restrict reliance. It should identify the relation affected rather than mark the whole system “invalid” without analysis.

## 9. Feedback

### 9.1 Working boundary

Feedback is an information-or-consequence return relation among:

- a source or originating event;
- an identified signal or consequence;
- a prior object, target, standard, expectation, decision, action, process, policy, or state;
- a transmission or availability relation;
- an identified receiver or receiving system;
- a potential interpretive, evaluative, regulatory, corrective, adaptive, or formative role;
- Domain, Scope, Context, time, and uncertainty; and
- record, challenge, and lineage.

### 9.2 Feedback is relational

An event is not feedback intrinsically. An outcome can be feedback to one system and merely an unobserved consequence to another. A complaint can be feedback to a provider, Evidence in a review, challenge in governance, and data in an Engineering system under separate claims.

### 9.3 Feedback can be absent or blocked

Consequences may fail to return because they are unobserved, delayed, inaccessible, suppressed, strategically filtered, unrepresented, outside the system boundary, or unable to reach a competent forum. A missing signal does not establish no consequence.

### 9.4 Feedback can be harmful or misleading

Feedback may be noisy, biased, manipulated, coercive, privacy-invasive, strategically gamed, delayed, misinterpreted, or directed toward a harmful objective. Feedback is value-neutral as a relation.

## 10. Challenge, complaint, anomaly, alert, rating, and reward

| Family | Working distinction | Non-entailment |
|---|---|---|
| challenge | Scoped contest of a claim, decision, allocation, rule, status, use, or consequence with a requested or possible response | Does not establish challenger correctness or reversal |
| complaint | Communication asserting dissatisfaction, harm, defect, unfairness, breach, or requested response | Does not establish falsity, liability, or immateriality if unverified |
| anomaly | Observed or inferred deviation from an expected pattern or model | Does not establish error, cause, harm, or need for correction |
| alert | Generated notice that declared conditions may require attention | Does not establish event truth, severity, or response success |
| rating | Ordinal, categorical, or numeric assessment under a declared instrument | Does not establish quality, value, or affected distribution |
| reward | Signal or consequence used in selection, adaptation, control, or evaluation | Does not establish Evidence validity, benefit, Learning, or improvement |
| audit finding | Method-bound assessment against declared criteria | Does not establish complete quality, legality, or correction |
| incident report | Representation of an event, effect, response, or investigation | Does not establish causal completeness or Responsibility allocation |

Protected challenge requires notice, access, practical capacity, protection, competent reception, Evidence access, reasoned response, possible corrective consequence, appeal, recurrence handling, and preserved lineage proportionate to materiality.

## 11. Transmission, receipt, interpretation, and uptake

### 11.1 Transmission or availability

Feedback may be sent, published, logged, displayed, exposed, or made accessible. Transmission success does not establish receipt by the relevant subject or system.

### 11.2 Receipt

Receipt is an occurrence in which the feedback reaches an identified receiver or receiving interface. Logging, delivery confirmation, inbox presence, or API success may be Evidence but not understanding.

### 11.3 Interpretation

Interpretation relates signal to meaning, object, cause, standard, or possible response. It can be correct, mistaken, partial, strategic, disputed, or unavailable.

### 11.4 Uptake

Uptake is integration or use of feedback or challenge in an identified inquiry, assessment, decision, practice, process, or organized state. Uptake can occur without agreement, correction, favorable change, capability gain, or Learning.

### 11.5 Rejection, deferral, and non-use

Feedback may be rejected, deferred, quarantined, ignored, misunderstood, overridden, or used for another purpose. Each state requires separate grounds, authority, consequence, and record where material.

## 12. Response and correction

### 12.1 Response

A response may acknowledge, explain, investigate, dispute, accept, restrict, decide, act, compensate, correct, repair, escalate, or decline. Response occurrence does not establish adequate response.

### 12.2 Correction object

Correction may target:

- question framing or assumptions;
- data, label, source, or Evidence;
- reasoning method, premise, inference, model, or process;
- answer content, Representation, coverage, status, or use;
- quality assessment, metric, threshold, or benchmark;
- decision, Authorization, action, workflow, or policy;
- cost allocation, value claim, causal attribution, or outcome interpretation;
- software, model, configuration, interface, or system;
- role, Authority, Responsibility allocation, or record; or
- harm-producing condition, recurrence path, or residual effect.

Correcting one object does not correct every linked object automatically.

### 12.3 Correction stages

Material correction may distinguish:

1. defect or error claim;
2. assessment and Evidence;
3. correction proposal;
4. Authority and decision;
5. corrective action;
6. actual changed state;
7. verification and monitoring;
8. communication to prior users and affected subjects;
9. repair, remedy, compensation, and residual response;
10. recurrence prevention and policy review; and
11. versioned historical record.

### 12.4 Failed and harmful correction

A correction can be incomplete, wrong, unauthorized, late, inaccessible, suppressive, harmful, or create new defects. “Fixed” status and changed output are not verified correction.

## 13. Update, adaptation, behavior change, and capability change

**Update** is a versioned change to data, content, model, rule, parameter, configuration, status, record, practice, or organized state. It may be externally imposed and transient.

**Adaptation** is change relative to conditions. It can be temporary, harmful, selected externally, or explained by current Context rather than Learning.

**Behavior change** is changed conduct or output pattern. It may result from coercion, incentive, interface, policy, replacement, fatigue, current Context, or random variation.

**Capability change** is changed capacity across relevant variation. It requires evidence beyond one output or Context and remains distinct from use, performance, Intelligence, Amplification, Learning, and value.

Update, adaptation, behavior change, and capability change can overlap. None alone establishes correction, Learning, Formation, or improvement.

## 14. Learning, Formation, and improvement

### 14.1 Learning

Learning requires:

- an identified stable Learner boundary and level;
- a comparatively durable change;
- a material link to experience, inquiry, practice, feedback, interaction, or adaptation;
- an identified capacity, disposition, practice, judgment, relationship, or organized pattern changed;
- manifestation beyond the immediate support condition under relevant variation;
- Evidence, uncertainty, and competing-explanation control; and
- version, assessment, challenge, and lineage.

Learning can be beneficial, harmful, narrow, brittle, misdirected, or value-neutral. It does not entail improvement.

### 14.2 Formation

Formation is broader than Learning and may include intended or unintended shaping through environment, participation, intervention, or self-directed activity. Feedback can be a formative condition without producing a formative effect.

### 14.3 System Learning

System Learning requires the DR-013 stable-system-Learner conditions. Member Learning, component updates, provider changes, replacement, redesign, retrieval, or changing input distribution cannot be copied to the system whole.

### 14.4 Improvement

Improvement is a comparative evaluative claim under a declared profile, baseline, subjects, purpose, criteria, authority, uncertainty, distribution, and consequence horizon. Learning can occur without improvement; improvement can occur through replacement or redesign without Learning by the claimed system.

## 15. Recursive feedback and correction lifecycle

Potential events include:

- outcome or consequence occurrence;
- observation or missed observation;
- signal formation and transmission;
- measurement and integrity review;
- receipt, interpretation, and assessment;
- Evidence or counterevidence assignment;
- challenge, complaint, anomaly, alert, or investigation;
- acknowledgment, reasoned response, decision, or non-action;
- correction, restriction, withdrawal, rollback, repair, remedy, or recurrence prevention;
- update, behavior change, capability change, Learning, or Formation assessment;
- question reframing, answer revision, quality reassessment, value/cost reconsideration, responsibility review, or policy change;
- concept, constitutional, Foundation Paper, or project review under its own authority; and
- monitoring, revalidation, supersession, retirement, and historical retention.

The lifecycle can branch, loop, stall, reverse, fork, merge, or remain unresolved. A recorded transition may be false; an actual change may occur without recorded authorization.

## 16. Consequence-to-governance return paths

The return path begins with a possible consequence and ends only at the authority competent for the affected object. Return is recursive, but authority is not transitive.

### 16.1 Consequence-to-validation return

An observed consequence may:

1. raise or revise a question about an answer, method, system, use, or claim;
2. supply a signal requiring integrity and relevance assessment;
3. become Evidence or counterevidence only under an identified Ground and assessment;
4. challenge a validation result, benchmark, model, threshold, or applicability claim;
5. cause revalidation, restriction, expiry, suspension, or supersession under competent authority; and
6. require retention of the prior result and the reason for change.

A favorable consequence does not validate the preceding answer or method automatically. An adverse consequence does not falsify it automatically. Causal Contribution, counterfactuals, confounding, implementation, Context, affected distribution, and time remain material.

### 16.2 Consequence-to-Responsibility return

An observed consequence may activate inquiry into prospective, role, causal, capacity, Authority, response, correction, repair, remedy, oversight, or continuity relations. It does not assign Responsibility, blame, liability, duty, or sanction by itself.

The return path must preserve:

- the affected subject and claimed harm or benefit;
- the relevant Bearer, level, object, phase, standard, and jurisdiction;
- causal and non-causal factual links;
- Authority, control, capability, dependence, and opportunity to respond;
- prior notice, Authorization, decision, action, omission, and reliance relations;
- challenge, answer, correction, repair, remedy, and residual consequence; and
- uncertainty, conflict, allocation, status, and lineage.

### 16.3 Consequence-to-policy return

Consequences can motivate policy review, but they do not create policy. A policy return must identify the policy object, owner, scope, authority, affected subjects, Evidence, alternatives, distribution, transition, enforcement, exceptions, correction, and appeal. Local operational convenience cannot silently become universal SymK policy.

### 16.4 Consequence-to-concept return

Repeated anomaly, contradiction, ambiguity, false classification, missing distinction, or unusable boundary may challenge a concept. It does not revise the canonical concept directly. The return path is:

`consequence -> observation -> Evidence assessment -> concept challenge -> governed analysis -> decision -> versioned concept change -> dependent-artifact review`.

The path preserves the former concept, the claim that it failed, the Evidence and counterevidence, the decision authority, the new concept version, and unresolved effects.

### 16.5 Consequence-to-constitutional return

A consequence can expose conflict with a candidate or accepted constitutional commitment. Constitutional change nevertheless requires its own jurisdiction, Evidence, dissent, authority, decision, versioning, and migration. No project outcome, popularity signal, market result, optimization, or system behavior acquires constitutional authority through repetition.

### 16.6 No automatic promotion

No return path permits automatic promotion from:

- occurrence to observation;
- observation to signal;
- signal to measurement;
- measurement to Evidence;
- Evidence to conclusion;
- consequence to attribution;
- attribution to Responsibility;
- complaint to defect;
- anomaly to failure;
- response to correction;
- change to improvement;
- update to Learning;
- practice to policy;
- project convention to universal concept;
- candidate proposition to constitutional commitment; or
- accepted concept to every implementation.

## 17. Cross-stream interface audit

### 17.1 Question to reasoning

Reasoning is answer-directed only relative to an identified Question version and answer obligation. A reasoning episode can also examine, decompose, reframe, reject, or replace a Question. Reframing must not be reported as answering the prior Question without a relation and lineage record.

### 17.2 Reasoning to answer

Reasoning may contribute to an Answer but does not entail one. An Answer can incorporate retrieval, testimony, computation, measurement, judgment, or prior Knowledge without one unified reasoning episode. Correct Answer content does not prove the claimed reasoning process; sound reasoning does not guarantee a correct Answer under incomplete Evidence.

### 17.3 Answer to quality

An Answer Quality profile assesses an identified Answer-to-Question relation under intended use, dimensions, Evidence, assessors, and authority. It is neither contained intrinsically in the text nor reduced to correctness, fluency, confidence, adoption, outcome, or one score.

### 17.4 Quality to time, cost, and Value

Quality dimensions constrain temporal and cost comparisons, but they do not determine Value. Faster or lower-cost production is not improvement if the compared answer obligation, review condition, affected horizon, or quality profile changes silently. Greater quality can still have low or negative Value for a particular purpose; a useful answer can remain epistemically weak or responsibly unusable.

### 17.5 Answer, decision, action, outcome, and consequence

An Answer may inform a recommendation; a recommendation may inform a decision; an authorized decision may cause or constrain action; action and omission may contribute to outcomes and consequences. Each transition requires its own object, subject, Authority, Evidence, and lineage. No downstream state retroactively manufactures the upstream predicate.

### 17.6 Consequence to feedback, correction, and Learning

A consequence can become the object of observation and feedback. Feedback can be transmitted, received, interpreted, rejected, or used. Use can prompt response, correction, update, capability change, or Learning. Every arrow is contingent, evidence-bearing, and independently challengeable.

### 17.7 Consolidated value-chain form

The universal conceptual chain is therefore not a pipeline with guaranteed promotions. It is a governed, branching relational system:

`Domain situation/problem -> governed Question -> inquiry/reasoning and adjacent operations -> Answer -> quality/use assessments -> recommendation/decision/action -> outcome/consequence -> observation/feedback -> challenge/response/correction/Learning -> revised questions, answers, practices, policies, concepts, or systems`.

Every node may be absent, plural, distributed, contested, unknown, failed, superseded, or not applicable. Every arrow may carry uncertainty, transformation, Power, Authority, cost, delay, loss, and affected consequences.

## 34. Alternatives considered

1. **Treat Question as a supporting compound of Scope, Context, and intent.** Rejected because what is sought and the answer obligation remain irreducible and structurally necessary.
2. **Treat Complex Question as Foundational.** Rejected provisionally because complexity is a relational qualification of Question rather than an independent universal object.
3. **Treat Reasoning as any transformation from input to output.** Rejected because it collapses computation, retrieval, generation, and causal process claims.
4. **Treat Reasoning as inference only.** Rejected because material consideration-sensitive processes include but are not exhausted by inference.
5. **Require a verbatim internal chain for every reasoning claim.** Rejected because such traces may be unavailable, misleading, unsafe to disclose, or not causally faithful.
6. **Treat Answer as any response.** Rejected because response occurrence does not establish the Question relation.
7. **Treat Answer as a governed compound only.** Rejected provisionally because Answer Identity remains stable before quality, use, decision, and consequence profiles.
8. **Treat correctness as complete Answer Quality.** Rejected because relevance, completeness, support, uncertainty, safety, and other dimensions remain independent.
9. **Adopt one universal Answer Quality scalar.** Rejected because aggregation hides non-applicability, distribution, disagreement, critical failure, and authority.
10. **Use response latency as speed.** Rejected because inquiry, verification, coordination, correction, and lifecycle clocks can reverse the comparison.
11. **Use price or direct resource consumption as total cost.** Rejected because Bearers, indirect costs, transfer, externalization, opportunity, harm, remedy, and residuals matter.
12. **Treat Value as a compound with no universal anchor.** Rejected provisionally because scoped evaluative claims share an irreducible relation despite plural value theories.
13. **Adopt one universal utility function.** Rejected because claimant, affected subjects, authority, horizon, distribution, non-commensurability, and non-compensable conditions vary.
14. **Let favorable outcome validate the whole chain.** Rejected because luck, intervention, changed Context, confounding, and distribution break the inference.
15. **Treat every returned signal as feedback and Evidence.** Rejected because source, object, transmission, integrity, relevance, assessment, and governance status differ.
16. **Treat any update or improved score as Learning.** Rejected because stable Learner, comparative durability, experience link, capacity change, manifestation, and competing-explanation controls are required.
17. **Make correction overwrite the prior state.** Rejected because challenge, effect, Responsibility, repair, recurrence, and historical accountability require lineage.
18. **Defer every concept to Domain authority.** Rejected because universal non-substitution, identity, evidence, authority, distribution, correction, and lifecycle boundaries are necessary for interoperability and governance.
