## 1. Question and analytical result

> What minimum boundary must SymK establish among actual reasoning, inference, derivation, computation, calculation, search, retrieval, association, simulation, generation, deliberation, judgment, explanation, justification, presentation, and their traces before it evaluates an answer?

Stream B supports the following working answer:

> **Reasoning is a scoped and temporally situated process in which an identified subject or organized arrangement treats materially relevant considerations as bearing on a question, problem, aim, or possible conclusion, and relates, transforms, weighs, tests, or coordinates them through challengeable moves under one or more methods, norms, rules, models, competencies, or practices. A reasoning episode may terminate in an intermediate conclusion, judgment, plan, explanation, answer candidate, qualified suspension, or reframing. It is not established by a fluent output, a correct result, a displayed chain, a formal derivation, computation, search, retrieval, simulation, generation, deliberation, Human review, or use of an intelligent system alone.**

> **Inference is a licensed or claimed support relation—or an operation instantiating such a relation—from identified premises or prior states to a conclusion under a declared inference regime. A valid derivation demonstrates conformity to the selected formal rules; it does not by itself establish premise truth, semantic adequacy, real-world truth, relevance, robustness, applicability, epistemic warrant, actual causal process, responsible reliance, or Knowledge. Inference may occur within reasoning, implement part of it, represent it, or proceed mechanically without establishing the broader reasoning claim.**

> **A reasoning trace, proof, derivation, rationale, log, explanation, or retrospective account is a Representation or Projection of selected process, support, or communicative relations. Its faithfulness, completeness, provenance, causal connection, epistemic role, audience fitness, security, and limits require separate Evidence. SymK should require consequence-proportional inspectability of material grounds, assumptions, methods, uncertainty, alternatives, tests, conclusions, and defeaters—not presume that verbatim private or inaccessible internal states are necessary, available, safe to disclose, or faithfully reproduced by an explanation-shaped output.**

This result separates at least six states:

1. **actual reasoning episode:** the temporally bounded process that occurred;
2. **inference or other operation:** a move, relation, computation, retrieval, search, simulation, generation, comparison, evaluation, or selection that may contribute;
3. **reasoning product:** an intermediate or terminal conclusion, judgment, candidate answer, plan, or qualified suspension;
4. **reasoning Representation:** a proof, derivation, trace, rationale, explanation, diagram, log, transcript, or reconstruction;
5. **reasoning claim and assessment:** assertions and evaluations about whether reasoning occurred and how adequate it was; and
6. **governance status, Authorization, reliance, and record:** governed positions that neither create the process nor guarantee its quality.

The working model does not require one psychological, computational, logical, philosophical, or architectural theory of reasoning. It establishes a universal minimum for preserving the object, Bearer or arrangement, question or aim, material inputs, methods, moves, uncertainty, alternatives, conclusions, process Evidence, assessment, and limits of a reasoning claim. Domain, scientific, professional, legal, and engineering authorities retain their substantive methods and validity criteria above that minimum.

No concept disposition is accepted here. Stream C must analyze answer, output, recommendation, decision, and action without treating any reasoning result as an answer automatically.

## 3. Why SymK needs this boundary

Without the boundary, incompatible claims can all be described as “reasoning”:

- a search engine returns a source whose author performed the reasoning;
- a calculator executes an algorithm without evaluating the question or premise applicability;
- a rule engine derives a conclusion from declared premises under a formal regime;
- a model generates a plausible explanation after its output is fixed;
- a professional recognizes a case through cultivated but partly tacit judgment;
- a team distributes framing, retrieval, inference, checking, challenge, and synthesis across members;
- an institution follows a procedure whose rationale no current participant understands;
- an artificial system exposes tool calls but not a faithful account of how candidate outputs were selected;
- a benchmark rewards the expected answer despite leakage, memorization, or invalid support;
- an authorized reviewer signs a conclusion without independently reasoning through it; or
- a favorable outcome makes an earlier weak process appear retrospectively sound.

Calling all of these reasoning would erase distinctions needed for attribution, evaluation, Responsibility, and improvement. Calling only explicit formal derivation reasoning would exclude practical judgment, defeasible inquiry, interpretation, diagnosis, planning, analogical comparison, distributed work, and embodied competence. SymK therefore needs a middle boundary: strong enough to block output- and trace-based inflation, but open enough to remain substrate-, architecture-, method-, and Domain-neutral.

The boundary is also prior to answer quality. A wrong answer can follow competent reasoning when material Evidence was unavailable. A correct answer can follow luck, leakage, copying, memorization, or an invalid route. A high-quality explanation can communicate support without reproducing the actual causal process. A poor explanation can accompany sound but tacit or privacy-protected reasoning. These combinations must remain representable.

## 4. Conceptual layers

| reasoning capacity | A capability attributed to a Bearer across relevant variation | One reasoning episode, performance, answer, or score |
| reasoning disposition or competence | A tendency or learned ability to reason appropriately under relevant conditions | Guaranteed exercise in the current case |
| reasoning episode | A bounded occurrence in which considerations bear through organized moves on a question, problem, aim, or conclusion | Its output, transcript, declared method, or later explanation |
| reasoning process pattern | A recurring or modeled organization of activities across episodes | The actual causal history of one episode |
| reasoning method or practice | A declared way of relating and evaluating considerations | Correct application, adequacy, or successful result |
| inference regime | Rules, semantics, permissions, defaults, probabilities, norms, or practices governing support moves | Real-world truth or universal validity |
| inference relation | A licensed or claimed relation between premises or prior states and a conclusion | The occurrence of a cognitive or computational process |
| inference operation | An act or execution that produces or licenses a conclusion under a regime | A complete reasoning episode |
| computation or calculation | Rule- or algorithm-governed state transformation | Reasoning, interpretation, applicability, or warrant by itself |
| search or retrieval | Exploration for candidates or access to an identified item | Inference, understanding, evaluation, or answer adequacy |
| simulation or generation | Production of model-conditioned trajectories, states, or content | Evidence of real-world fit, reasoning, truth, or novelty |
| deliberation | Consideration of alternatives, reasons, constraints, priorities, and consequences | Decision, Authorization, consensus, or correct judgment |
| judgment | A situated assessment, conclusion, classification, or selection under relevant standards and uncertainty | Decision, action, status, or truth by authority |
| reasoning product | Intermediate or terminal conclusion, judgment, plan, hypothesis, or answer candidate | The process that produced it or an accepted answer |
| trace, proof, derivation, rationale, explanation, or log | Representation of selected process, support, formal, causal, or communicative relations | The complete actual process or automatic Evidence of it |
| reasoning claim | Assertion that a subject or arrangement reasoned in a stated way | Actual reasoning or an accepted assessment |
| reasoning assessment | Evidence-bearing evaluation of occurrence, fidelity, validity, adequacy, relevance, robustness, or use | The actual predicate, conclusion truth, or unlimited Authorization |
| governance status and record | Controlled position and durable Representation | Process occurrence, quality, truth, Knowledge, or Responsibility |

The layers may align without collapsing. An actual reasoning episode may instantiate a method, include inferences and computations, produce a conclusion, be partially represented in a trace, receive an assessment, and be authorized for a use. Each relation still requires its own support.

## 5. Working reasoning anchor

### 5.1 Minimum positive conditions

A material actual-reasoning claim should identify, as applicable:

- the **claimed reasoner or organized reasoning arrangement** and its boundary;
- the **question version, problem, aim, or candidate conclusion** to which the episode responds;
- the **Domain, Scope, Context, time, stakes, and affected horizon**;
- the **considerations** treated as bearing, including observations, claims, premises, assumptions, Evidence, models, examples, memories, testimony, constraints, goals, values, or experienced features;
- the **roles** those considerations play rather than merely their presence;
- the **method, norm, rule, model, competence, practice, or inference regime** under which moves are made;
- the **material moves or relations**, at a granularity proportionate to consequence;
- the **alternatives, disagreements, uncertainty, exceptions, and defeaters** considered or omitted;
- the **intermediate and terminal products**, including suspension or reframing;
- the **dependencies**, resources, tools, participants, data, environments, and support conditions;
- the **Evidence that the claimed process occurred**, distinct from Evidence supporting its conclusion;
- the **assessment criteria, assessor, authority, result, limitations, and intended reliance**;
- the **representations and projections** used to expose the process and their declared losses; and
- the **lineage, challenge, correction, expiry, and reopening conditions**.

These conditions do not require a maximal record for every low-consequence episode. They specify the semantic questions that must remain answerable when material.

### 5.2 Consideration-sensitive organization

Reasoning requires more than temporal succession. A source followed by an output is not enough. The claim must state how one or more considerations were treated as bearing on another state, option, conclusion, or course under some norm, competence, or method.

The bearing need not be deductive or fully explicit. It may be probabilistic, defeasible, analogical, causal, interpretive, diagnostic, practical, normative, spatial, embodied, perceptual, or socially distributed. But a claimant cannot use “reasoning” as an unexplained label for any state transition.

### 5.3 Challengeable does not mean fully verbalized

A process can be challengeable when material sources, assumptions, methods, boundary conditions, alternatives, tests, conclusions, uncertainty, and limitations are inspectable, even if not every internal state can be faithfully verbalized. Conversely, a long verbal chain may be unfaithful, incomplete, post hoc, irrelevant, strategically edited, or generated without governing the result.

SymK's minimum is epistemic and governance inspectability, not universal demand for private introspection, proprietary disclosure, privileged material, unsafe secrets, or a fictional complete causal transcript.

## 7. Reasoner, Bearer, and organized-arrangement boundary

### 7.1 No output-owner default

The person, model, account, organization, service, or product named on an output is not automatically the reasoner. Authorship, ownership, signature, delivery, interface visibility, legal personality, and product branding are separately governed relations.

### 7.2 Lowest adequate organized level

Reasoning should be attributed at the lowest organized level that explains the material consideration-sensitive process without excluding constitutive integration. This applies the DR-010 Bearer role and the DR-015 level discipline without assuming that reasoning attribution equals Intelligence attribution.

Separate claims may be supportable at multiple levels:

- a Human reasons through the interpretation and judgment while using retrieved material;
- an artificial component executes an inferential operation;
- a configured Human–artificial arrangement distributes search, critique, comparison, and synthesis;
- a team reasons collectively through structured challenge and integration;
- an institution preserves a reasoning practice through roles, procedures, memory, and review; or
- a source author performed reasoning that a later retriever merely imports.

No reasoning, conclusion, Intelligence, Authority, Responsibility, or quality transfers among these levels automatically.

### 7.3 Constitutive, contributing, enabling, and environmental roles

A component may be:

- **constitutive** of the claimed organized episode;
- a **Contributor** supplying a material move or consideration;
- an **enabler** providing access, compute, interface, translation, or coordination;
- a **source** of a claim or prior reasoning product;
- an **assessor or challenger** of the process;
- an **environmental condition**; or
- an **affected subject** without participating in the process.

Indispensability under one configuration does not alone identify the reasoner. Removal and substitution tests may support causal analysis but do not settle semantic or normative attribution by themselves.

### 7.4 Distributed and collective reasoning

Collective reasoning requires more than aggregated individual outputs. A group-level claim should preserve differentiation of contributions, communication or transformation paths, conflict handling, integration, memory, correction, and a group-level product or disposition not adequately attributed to one member alone.

No universal requirement says every Contributor must understand the whole process, agree with the conclusion, or possess the resulting Knowledge. Distributed incompleteness, dissent, and asymmetric access must remain visible.

### 7.5 Reasoning and Intelligence remain distinct

One actual reasoning episode may manifest a capability but does not establish stable Intelligence across relevant variation. An Intelligence Bearer may possess reasoning capability without exercising it in the current episode. A narrow formal or computational process may instantiate inference without supporting a broader Intelligence attribution. Reasoning-quality and capability-change claims therefore require separate evidence.

## 8. Episode Identity, version, and lineage

### 8.1 Episode boundary

A reasoning episode is bounded by a material combination of reasoner or arrangement, question or aim, Domain, Scope, Context, time, inputs, method or regime, and process continuity. The boundary must not be widened after success to include helpful resources and narrowed after failure to exclude them.

### 8.2 Continuation, revision, and successor

The same episode may continue through ordinary elaboration, verification, challenge, or correction when lineage remains intact. A successor episode should be recorded when a material change occurs in:

- question Identity or governing aim;
- Bearer, organized arrangement, or system boundary;
- premise or assumption set;
- inference regime, model, method, or tool configuration;
- decisive Evidence or excluded alternatives;
- conclusion object or answer obligation;
- material Scope, Context, jurisdiction, time, or affected horizon; or
- the claimed causal/process history.

A revised conclusion does not erase the earlier episode. A copied trace does not create process continuity. Re-executing the same code is a new occurrence even if it instantiates the same derivation pattern.

### 8.3 Reuse

Prior derivations, analyses, models, and judgments may be reused as sources or sub-results. Reuse requires declared source/version, applicability, assumptions, dependency, and current review. Importing a prior reasoning product is not evidence that the importing subject repeated or understood the source process.

## 9. Reasoning and inference

### 9.1 Inference as relation and operation

SymK should distinguish:

1. **semantic or logical consequence:** a relation that holds under a declared semantics independently of whether anyone computes it;
2. **licensed inferential move:** a move permitted by a rule, model, norm, or practice;
3. **inference operation:** an occurrence that applies or approximates such a move;
4. **inference claim:** an assertion that a support relation or operation holds;
5. **derivation Representation:** an ordered expression of moves; and
6. **inference assessment:** evaluation of conformity, strength, reliability, warrant, applicability, or use.

These are not interchangeable. A consequence may hold without being derived. A derivation may be executed without reflective reasoning. A person may reason well while leaving parts implicit. A displayed derivation may fail to match the actual route.

### 9.2 Reasoning may exceed inference

Reasoning may include framing, identifying relevant considerations, interpreting terms, choosing models, generating alternatives, seeking counterevidence, diagnosing failures, weighing uncertainty, judging applicability, challenging premises, comparing consequences, and suspending conclusion. These operations are not all inference relations, though they may govern which inferences matter.

### 9.3 Inference may be narrower than reasoning

A database rule, theorem prover, type checker, classifier, probabilistic updater, or spreadsheet formula may execute a licensed transition. Whether to call the larger activity reasoning depends on the claim boundary and positive conditions, not on a universal privilege for Human consciousness or artificial computation.

## 10. Inference families and regime plurality

Potential inference families include:

- deductive and proof-theoretic;
- inductive and enumerative;
- abductive and explanatory;
- probabilistic, Bayesian, statistical, and likelihood-based;
- causal and counterfactual;
- analogical and case-based;
- default, defeasible, non-monotonic, and exception-sensitive;
- interpretive, hermeneutic, and meaning-oriented;
- diagnostic and classificatory;
- practical, planning, and means–end;
- normative, ethical, legal, and rule-governed;
- spatial, temporal, qualitative, or diagrammatic; and
- learned-pattern, hybrid, ensemble, or simulation-supported.

This is an open map, not a universal taxonomy. A single episode may combine families. Their semantics, standards, and evidence differ. SymK's minimum is to prevent an inference label from concealing which regime, premise roles, assumptions, uncertainty, and defeat conditions apply.

No family is universally superior. Deduction can preserve false premises. Induction can generalize beyond supported populations. Abduction can prefer a persuasive but wrong explanation. Probabilistic inference can be calibrated under the wrong model. Analogy can import irrelevant similarities. Legal or practical reasoning can be valid within a norm system yet inapplicable outside its jurisdiction. Learned-pattern inference can succeed without inspectable claim-level provenance and fail under shift.

## 11. Derivation, proof, and formal validity

### 11.1 Derivation

A derivation is a Representation of expressions related by declared rules in a formal or semi-formal system. It may be machine-checked, Human-produced, reconstructed, compressed, or translated. Its identity depends on the expressions, rules, semantics where applicable, versions, and transformation lineage.

### 11.2 Proof

“Proof” is Domain- and regime-sensitive. In formal mathematics it may denote a derivation satisfying a formal system. In science, law, engineering, and ordinary language it may denote very different evidentiary accomplishments. SymK must not universalize one use or allow the word to manufacture certainty.

### 11.3 Formal validity

Formal validity establishes that, under a specified semantics or proof system, a conclusion follows from premises or that a derivation conforms to rules. It does not establish:

- that premises are true, complete, current, admissible, or responsibly obtained;
- that symbols preserve intended Domain meaning;
- that the chosen formalism fits the question;
- that model conditions hold in the actual Context;
- that omitted exceptions or affected consequences are immaterial;
- that the actual process used the displayed derivation;
- that the conclusion answers the governed question;
- that reliance or action is authorized; or
- that the result is Knowledge.

### 11.4 Soundness terminology

“Soundness” has different uses. In ordinary deductive argument it commonly combines validity with true premises. In formal systems, abstract interpretation, static analysis, testing, and scientific practice it may denote different no-false-negative, no-false-positive, preservation, or adequacy properties. Every material soundness claim must identify its object, meaning, regime, premises or model, proof or Evidence, Scope, and limitations.

## 12. Computation and calculation

Computation is a rule-, algorithm-, mechanism-, or learned-procedure-governed transformation of states or Representations. Calculation is a computation interpreted as producing a quantitative or otherwise rule-determined result. Both can be exact relative to their inputs and procedure while epistemically or practically inadequate.

Examples include:

- arithmetic over the wrong units;
- optimization of the wrong objective;
- a correct formula applied outside its assumptions;
- a simulation run with inapplicable boundary conditions;
- a deterministic transformation of outdated source data;
- a numerical result whose precision exceeds input support; or
- a reproducible pipeline that omits an affected population.

Computation may instantiate inference, support reasoning, automate a subtask, or produce a candidate consideration. It does not by itself establish interpretation, premise selection, model fitness, reasoning, judgment, answer correspondence, or value. Conversely, reasoning may occur without numerical calculation or an explicit computational architecture.

## 13. Search, retrieval, and access

### 13.1 Search

Search explores a declared or implicit candidate space under query, ranking, navigation, heuristic, or interaction conditions. A successful search finds something useful; it does not establish that the space was adequate, the result is true, or the query preserved the governed question.

### 13.2 Retrieval

Retrieval accesses or returns an item, passage, record, claim, model, case, or prior result. It can completely satisfy a bounded lookup when the source is authoritative and the question asks for that source content. Even then, answer adequacy depends on identity, currency, applicability, and correspondence—not on retrieval being redescribed as inference.

### 13.3 Imported reasoning

A retrieved source may contain reasoning. The source author, team, or system may bear that process attribution. The retriever may reason in selecting, comparing, interpreting, applying, or challenging it, but mere access does not transfer authorship, understanding, competence, Knowledge, or Responsibility.

### 13.4 Query and result mediation

Ranking, embeddings, indexing, filters, access permissions, personalization, and interface presentation shape what is found. These may contain computations or learned inferences, but their presence does not settle whether the end-to-end inquiry reasoned about the Domain question. Material omissions and inaccessible alternatives remain possible defeaters.

## 14. Association, pattern recognition, simulation, and generation

### 14.1 Association and recognition

Association connects states or features; pattern recognition identifies similarity, category, or regularity. Both may be constitutive of expert judgment or learned inference, but neither is automatically reasoning. The material claim must identify what norm, consideration role, reliability, Context, and conclusion relation the association supports.

### 14.2 Simulation

Simulation generates model-conditioned states, trajectories, or outcomes. It can test consequences within a declared model, support counterfactual exploration, expose sensitivity, or generate Evidence about implementation behavior. It does not establish that the model represents the world adequately, that probabilities are calibrated, or that simulated consequences will occur.

### 14.3 Generation

Generation produces candidate text, images, plans, hypotheses, proofs, explanations, code, or other Representations. Candidate generation may support reasoning by expanding alternatives, but generation alone does not establish that alternatives were evaluated, that content is grounded, or that the actual generation process matches an explanation.

### 14.4 Novelty and creativity

Novelty, surprise, elegance, originality, usefulness, or creative value do not establish reasoning. A reasoned product may be conventional; a novel product may be stochastic, copied, recombined, accidental, or unsupported. Creativity and reasoning may interact but require distinct claims.

## 15. Deliberation and judgment

### 15.1 Deliberation

Deliberation is consideration of alternatives, reasons, constraints, goals, values, uncertainty, and consequences in relation to a possible conclusion, choice, or course. It may be individual, dialogical, adversarial, collective, institutional, artificial, or hybrid.

Deliberation does not guarantee balanced participation, complete alternatives, correct weighting, legitimate values, consensus, decision, Authorization, or action. Power can shape which reasons and affected subjects enter the process.

### 15.2 Judgment

Judgment is a situated assessment, classification, conclusion, or selection under relevant standards, competence, Context, and uncertainty. It may be a reasoning product, an operation within reasoning, or a practical competence manifestation. A judgment can be expert but wrong, authorized but weakly supported, well supported but unauthorized for decision, or correct without creating Knowledge.

### 15.3 Judgment and decision

A judgment may inform a decision. A decision is a governance act under Authority and may incorporate legal, ethical, strategic, resource, timing, or other non-epistemic constraints. A sound judgment does not make the decision; an authorized decision does not make the supporting judgment true.

### 15.4 Tacit and embodied judgment

Professional and practical judgment may depend on tacit discrimination, perception, bodily skill, familiarity, and cultivated responsiveness not exhausted by propositions. SymK should preserve performance Evidence, exemplars, comparative cases, challenge, limits, and accountable use without demanding a fabricated complete verbal chain.

## 16. Explanation, justification, rationale, and presentation

### 16.1 Explanation is relation- and audience-sensitive

An explanation may address why an event occurred, how a mechanism works, why a conclusion is supported, why a decision was made, how a process proceeded, or how to understand a representation. These are different objects.

A good causal explanation may not justify reliance. A good justification may not describe the causal process. A process explanation may omit reasons. An audience-appropriate explanation may simplify while preserving material meaning, or distort through omitted assumptions.

### 16.2 Justification and rationale

A justification offers grounds intended to support acceptability, belief, reliance, or action under a stated standard. A rationale records reasons presented for a conclusion, choice, or decision. Either may be contemporaneous or retrospective, sincere or strategic, complete or selective, causally operative or post hoc.

### 16.3 Presentation

Presentation organizes content for an audience through wording, order, visual form, interface, emphasis, and style. It can improve comprehension and challenge, or conceal uncertainty and alternatives. Fluency, clarity, formality, citation density, verbosity, confidence, and polished structure do not establish reasoning or truth.

### 16.4 Explanation-shaped output

An explanation-shaped output is evidence that an explanation Representation exists. It is not automatically Evidence that:

- the stated steps occurred;
- they causally produced the conclusion;
- all material considerations are shown;
- the conclusion is valid or true;
- the source understood the explanation;
- the explanation is faithful to internal computation;
- the selected audience can responsibly rely on it; or
- the output is an answer to the governed question.

## 17. Actual process and Representation evidence

### 17.1 Process-evidence families

Evidence that a reasoning process occurred may include, with different strengths and limits:

- contemporaneous notes, sketches, calculations, annotations, intermediate artifacts, and revisions;
- observed dialogue, challenge, comparison, and response;
- validated system instrumentation, event records, tool calls, data lineage, and configuration history;
- reproducible derivations or computations linked to the episode;
- controlled intervention, ablation, perturbation, replay, and counterfactual tests;
- architecture and procedure evidence showing possible process organization;
- participant testimony or self-report;
- behavioral signatures across varied cases;
- error patterns and sensitivity to changed premises;
- independent review of source use, assumptions, methods, and conclusion support; and
- output characteristics.

No family is universally decisive. Architecture shows possible mechanisms, not necessarily the actual path. Logs can be incomplete, mutable, misunderstood, or too coarse. Self-report can be sincere but inaccurate. Reproduction can establish a valid alternative route rather than the original one. Output behavior underdetermines process because multiple routes can produce the same result.

### 17.2 Trace identity and fidelity

A trace claim must identify:

- the episode and version represented;
- how trace events are generated and linked to actual operations;
- which levels of the system or group are covered;
- selection, compression, redaction, reconstruction, and transformation;
- ordering and time semantics;
- missing, inaccessible, or unverifiable states;
- integrity and provenance controls;
- intended audience and use; and
- known fidelity limits and defeaters.

### 17.3 Process inspectability without total disclosure

High-consequence use may require stronger process Evidence, but proportionality remains material. Privacy, confidentiality, privilege, safety, security, intellectual property, participant dignity, and adversarial misuse can justify restriction. Equivalent assurance may use independent review, controlled access, validated summaries, reproducibility, tests, or formal checks where complete disclosure is impermissible.

Restriction must not be converted into an unchallengeable claim. The claimant should state what cannot be exposed, why, what substitute Evidence is offered, who can assess it, and which reliance remains unauthorized.

### 17.4 No universal verbatim-chain requirement

SymK should not require a universal verbatim record of every internal step. Some reasoning is tacit, parallel, distributed, embodied, stochastic, privacy-sensitive, or not faithfully reportable. Some systems can generate convincing chains that are not causally connected to their result. Governance should target material epistemic relations and proportionate process Evidence rather than fetishize a single trace form.

## 18. Premise, assumption, Evidence, rule, model, and conclusion relations

### 18.1 Role typing

An object can occupy different roles in different episodes. A measurement may be Evidence for one claim, a premise in one argument, an input to a model, and an object of challenge elsewhere. Role typing must not reclassify the object permanently.

At minimum, distinguish:

- **premise:** a proposition treated as an input to a support or derivation relation;
- **assumption:** a condition provisionally taken, often without being established inside the current episode;
- **Evidence:** a contextual role in which something is used to support, test, challenge, restrict, or differentiate a claim;
- **rule or norm:** a licensed transformation, evaluation, or conduct relation;
- **model:** a selective Representation or organized structure used to describe, predict, explain, simulate, classify, or intervene;
- **constraint:** a condition limiting admissible states, moves, answers, or actions;
- **goal or value consideration:** a state or evaluation relevant to practical reasoning without becoming an epistemic premise automatically;
- **intermediate conclusion:** a product used as a later consideration;
- **terminal conclusion:** the episode's declared endpoint, not automatically a final answer;
- **defeater:** a consideration that rebuts, undercuts, restricts, suspends, or changes the force of another relation; and
- **unknown or unresolved item:** a material gap that may remain explicit rather than being silently filled.

### 18.2 Source, content, and role

A source's Authority, reliability, reputation, or governance status does not determine the truth or role of every content item it supplies. The same content can have several sources; the same source can supply claims of different quality; an authoritative source may control a legal status without settling an empirical proposition.

### 18.3 Dependency graph rather than linear chain

Reasoning is often branching, recursive, defeasible, parallel, cyclic in revision, and distributed across levels. A single linear chain may be an audience Projection. The governed model should permit:

- multiple supporting and opposing considerations;
- shared sub-results;
- alternative routes;
- mutually constraining interpretations;
- assumption dependencies;
- model comparisons;
- challenge and correction loops;
- unresolved conflict; and
- explicit residual uncertainty.

No graph form is mandated here. The requirement is that a linear presentation not erase material dependency or defeat relations.

## 19. Validity, truth, warrant, relevance, robustness, and use

The following dimensions must be evaluated separately where material:

| Dimension | Governing question | What it does not establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| syntactic conformity | Does the Representation satisfy declared formation rules? | Semantics, validity, truth, or reasoning |
| derivational correctness | Were declared transformation rules applied correctly? | Premise truth, model fit, or answer adequacy |
| formal validity | Does the conclusion follow under the selected formal semantics or proof regime? | Soundness, relevance, applicability, or Knowledge |
| premise acceptability | Are premises true, supported, licensed, or appropriately assumed for the use? | Completeness or valid inference |
| semantic adequacy | Do terms and formal objects preserve intended Domain meaning? | Empirical truth or responsible use |
| epistemic warrant | Is reliance on the conclusion supported by appropriate grounds, process, competence, and defeat handling? | Final truth, Authority, or beneficial outcome |
| truth or accuracy | Does the claim appropriately correspond to its object under the relevant truth conditions? | Good process, explanation, completeness, or value |
| relevance | Does the consideration or conclusion bear on the governed question? | Truth or sufficient weight |
| completeness or coverage | Are material parts, exceptions, alternatives, uncertainties, and consequences addressed? | Correctness of included parts |
| calibration | Do stated confidence or probabilities track outcomes under the declared conditions? | Causal adequacy, relevance, or individual-case truth |
| robustness | Does support survive relevant perturbation, alternative specifications, and boundary changes? | Universal transfer or responsible deployment |
| applicability | Do the premises, model, population, jurisdiction, time, and conditions fit the current case? | Correct execution or Authorization |
| explanatory adequacy | Does the account answer the relevant why/how question for its audience and purpose? | Actual process fidelity or justification |
| practical adequacy | Does the reasoning support the intended task under declared constraints? | Truth, ethical legitimacy, or responsible use |
| responsible use | Are Authority, Responsibility, affected subjects, risk, challenge, correction, and consequence conditions satisfied? | Truth or value by governance declaration |

These dimensions may conflict. A formally valid, well-explained derivation may be irrelevant. A robust statistical pattern may be causally misunderstood. A true conclusion may lack a responsible acquisition path. A justified conclusion may later be defeated. A responsibly restricted result may remain uncertain.

## 20. Uncertainty, alternatives, and defeaters

### 20.1 Typed uncertainty

Material uncertainty may concern:

- question Identity or framing;
- source identity, integrity, or currency;
- premise truth or applicability;
- data quality, sampling, measurement, or missingness;
- model structure, parameter, boundary, or shift;
- inference strength or regime choice;
- interpretation and semantic alignment;
- process occurrence or trace fidelity;
- alternative explanations or methods;
- conclusion truth, precision, or stability;
- transfer to another Context, population, time, or jurisdiction;
- affected consequence; or
- assessor disagreement and governance status.

One confidence label should not collapse these.

### 20.2 Alternative paths and conclusions

Reasoning adequacy may require considering alternative framings, explanations, premises, models, methods, inferences, conclusions, and no-conclusion states. The burden is consequence-proportional; it is not an infinite search requirement.

Failure to consider an alternative matters when it could materially change the support, Scope, conclusion, affected consequence, or responsible use. An unexamined alternative does not always defeat a result, but it must not be silently represented as excluded.

### 20.3 Defeaters

A **rebutting defeater** supports an incompatible conclusion. An **undercutting defeater** weakens the connection between grounds and conclusion. An **applicability defeater** shows that premises, model, or rule do not fit the case. A **process defeater** challenges whether the claimed reasoning occurred or was faithful. A **governance defeater** restricts reliance or action without necessarily changing truth.

These categories are working analytical aids, not a closed formal taxonomy.

### 20.4 Suspension and revision

Reasoning may responsibly terminate in suspension, a conditional conclusion, a set of live alternatives, a request for Evidence, or reframing. Such outcomes are not failures merely because they lack a single confident answer. Later Evidence may reopen the episode or create a successor; the earlier uncertainty and reliance history must remain recoverable.

## 21. Relation to question, inquiry, answer, and action

### 21.1 Question and reasoning

Reasoning must be evaluated against the governed question version or declared aim it actually addresses. A coherent process can reason about the wrong question. A question may be answered by direct observation, reliable testimony, or authoritative retrieval without a substantial new reasoning episode by the answerer. Reasoning and answering therefore do not entail each other.

### 21.2 Inquiry and reasoning

An inquiry may include framing, search, retrieval, observation, experiment, dialogue, computation, reasoning, review, and communication. Some bounded inquiries may complete through retrieval. Some reasoning occurs outside a formal inquiry. The record should preserve which operations occurred rather than label the whole workflow reasoning.

### 21.3 Reasoning product and answer candidate

A conclusion becomes an answer candidate only when it is presented as responding to the governed question. Stream C must establish answer Identity, correspondence, content, Scope, status, adoption, revision, and challenge. Stream B does not decide those boundaries.

### 21.4 Reasoning and action

Reasoning can support recommendation, planning, decision, or action, but it does not allocate Authority or Responsibility. Action can occur without reasoning, contrary to reasoning, or for legitimate constraints not contained in the reasoning result. Outcomes cannot retrospectively manufacture sound reasoning.

## 22. Proportionality and process assurance

The required process Evidence should increase with material combinations of:

- consequence, irreversibility, and affected-subject exposure;
- uncertainty, novelty, and model or Domain shift;
- contested assumptions, values, law, or jurisdiction;
- reliance on opaque, stochastic, distributed, or changing components;
- dependence, concentration, lock-in, and failure severity;
- difficulty of correction, repair, remedy, or recurrence prevention;
- power asymmetry and limited effective challenge;
- required reproducibility, explainability, or professional accountability; and
- disagreement among Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views.

Proportionality does not lower the semantic standard. It changes the evidence and representation burden. A low-stakes arithmetic check may need little lineage. A high-consequence professional recommendation may require sources, assumptions, alternatives, model limits, independent review, uncertainty, and protected-access audit.

More trace is not always more assurance. Voluminous low-fidelity logs can obscure decisive assumptions. A concise proof can establish derivational correctness but not applicability. A well-designed test can be stronger process Evidence than a narrative. Assurance should target the material claim and failure mode.

## 23. Candidate approaches considered

| Approach | Strength | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| Any information transformation is reasoning | Simple and substrate-neutral | Collapses storage, copying, parsing, routing, calculation, and retrieval into reasoning |
| Only conscious Human reflection is reasoning | Protects phenomenological and intentional aspects | Excludes artificial, collective, tacit, embodied, institutional, and formally implemented cases by stipulation |
| Only explicit logical inference is reasoning | Precise and checkable | Excludes framing, relevance, analogy, diagnosis, defeasible judgment, interpretation, and practical reasoning |
| Correct answer proves reasoning | Easy to measure | Fails luck, leakage, memorization, copying, shortcut, and wrong-question cases |
| Displayed chain proves reasoning | Promotes inspectability | Reifies post hoc, selective, reconstructed, or generated Representations |
| Architecture or model class proves reasoning | Supports system description | Infers actual process from possible mechanism and invites origin privilege |
| Domain-specific definitions only | Respects methodological pluralism | Leaves Product Vision reasoning semantically uncontrolled across Domains |
| Universal exhaustive process transcript | Maximizes apparent audit detail | Impossible or misleading for tacit, parallel, stochastic, distributed, private, secure, and non-reportable processes |
| Proposed consideration-sensitive, process-evidenced minimum | Preserves plural methods, attribution, process/product/trace separation, challenge, and proportionality | Requires later operational profiles and leaves hard borderline cases open |

The last approach is provisionally preferred because it satisfies the accepted structural and epistemic constraints without selecting one reasoning theory or architecture.

## 27. Human, Scientific, and Engineering View obligations

### 27.1 Human View

The Human View should expose, where material:

- the question or aim in comprehensible terms;
- reasons, assumptions, uncertainty, alternatives, exceptions, and limitations;
- who contributed, challenged, judged, authorized, and is affected;
- which explanation addresses causal process, support, decision rationale, or communication;
- where tacit judgment or inaccessible process remains;
- which disagreement and value considerations are unresolved; and
- how to question, correct, appeal, or obtain a qualified no-answer.

It must not turn narrative fluency, professional confidence, interface order, or a reason label into evidence of process quality.

### 27.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View should expose, where material:

- construct definitions and operationalization;
- premise and model assumptions;
- Evidence design, source quality, sampling, measurement, and uncertainty;
- inferential regime, validation, sensitivity, alternatives, and defeaters;
- process-attribution method and fidelity limits;
- reproducibility and transfer boundaries;
- disagreement and negative results; and
- what would restrict, defeat, or reopen the assessment.

It must not convert statistical significance, formal validity, benchmark performance, reproducibility, consensus, or publication into truth, relevance, causal adequacy, or universal applicability.

### 27.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View should expose, where material:

- episode, input, source, model, configuration, tool, and code versions;
- operations, dependencies, boundaries, ordering, retries, failure paths, and human interventions;
- trace-generation and integrity mechanisms;
- test, validation, monitoring, and rollback relations;
- access restrictions, redactions, and equivalent assurance paths;
- distinction among actual events, reconstructed traces, explanations, and projections;
- latency, resource, failure, and observability limits; and
- lineage, supersession, retention, and correction behavior.

It must not treat event availability, system architecture, deterministic replay, tool calls, dashboards, trace volume, model type, or schema completeness as semantic or epistemic authority.

### 27.4 Cross-view alignment

The Views need not contain identical fields or granularity. They must preserve the same material episode Identity, question version, Bearer or arrangement boundary, consideration roles, method/regime, conclusion, uncertainty, assessment, restrictions, and lineage. Every material transformation or omission must be declared.

A View disagreement is itself governable Evidence. It may indicate translation loss, construct mismatch, invalid measurement, inaccessible process, contested interpretation, or legitimate plural standards. It is not resolved automatically in favor of the most formal, technical, authoritative, or user-friendly View.

## 28. Working concept consequences

Stream B provisionally supports these consequences:

1. **Reasoning** remains a candidate independently anchored process concept.
2. **Domain reasoning** is a Domain-qualified application, not a separate universal process kind by name alone.
3. **Reasoning episode** is a bounded occurrence with identity and lineage.
4. **Reasoner or reasoning Bearer** is an attribution role whose level and boundary require Evidence.
5. **Reasoning capacity** belongs to Intelligence/capability analysis and is not one episode.
6. **Inference** is a plural, regime-relative relation/operation family.
7. **Derivation and proof** are Representations with Domain- and regime-sensitive statuses.
8. **Computation and calculation** are operation families that may instantiate or support inference.
9. **Search and retrieval** are inquiry operations, not reasoning by definition.
10. **Association, recognition, simulation, and generation** may support reasoning without establishing it.
11. **Deliberation** is an activity family considering alternatives and reasons without making a decision.
12. **Judgment** is a situated assessment or product without automatic Authority.
13. **Explanation** is an object-, purpose-, and audience-relative relation commonly carried by a Representation.
14. **Justification and rationale** record offered support or reasons without guaranteeing causal fidelity.
15. **Reasoning trace and log** are Engineering Projections with explicit fidelity and coverage claims.
16. **Premise, assumption, Evidence, rule, model, constraint, conclusion, and defeater** are contextual roles rather than permanent object kinds.
17. **Validity, soundness, warrant, truth, relevance, completeness, calibration, robustness, applicability, and responsible use** are non-scalar assessment dimensions.
18. **Reasoning process Evidence** and **conclusion Evidence** are independently necessary where material.
19. **Process inspectability** is consequence-proportional and compatible with governed restriction, but restriction cannot eliminate challenge.
20. **Output, answer, value, outcome, feedback, and Learning** remain later and independent boundaries.

## 30. Deferred-question treatment and derived-project probes

### 30.1 DQ-014

Stream B adds a second prerequisite to “How should answer quality, speed, and total cost be measured and traded off?”:

> Comparisons must distinguish reasoning episode and product; inference, computation, retrieval, simulation, generation, deliberation, judgment, explanation, and presentation; question-version correspondence; premise and assumption state; source and model versions; Evidence available; uncertainty and alternatives; process Evidence; validation burden; human and system dependencies; and correction/review time and cost.

Therefore:

- a faster inference is not automatically faster reasoning or time to responsible answer;
- a cheaper model call or retrieval is not lower total reasoning-system cost;
- a correct answer does not validate the process;
- a longer explanation is not deeper reasoning;
- trace volume is not assurance quality;
- omitted verification cannot be counted as pure speed or cost improvement;
- reusable prior reasoning must carry applicability and review cost; and
- privacy/security protection and equivalent assurance may create legitimate trade-offs.

This is substantial non-final progress. No metric, threshold, weighting, optimization, total-cost model, or trade-off is accepted.

### 30.2 LexBrain translation probe

A legal or doctrinal workflow may retrieve an authoritative source, identify issues, interpret language, analogize cases, distinguish facts, apply rules, weigh conflicting authorities, construct arguments, and recommend action. Stream B requires these to remain distinct:

- retrieving a precedent is not applying it;
- a formally structured syllogism does not settle interpretive or jurisdictional applicability;
- an explanation of a recommendation may differ from the actual analysis path;
- legal Authority of a source does not make every factual premise true;
- professional judgment does not itself allocate decision Authority; and
- a later favorable ruling does not retroactively validate the reasoning.

No LexBrain artifact, legal method, conclusion, or professional rule is changed.

### 30.3 IaaC translation probe

An infrastructure-as-code workflow may parse desired state, retrieve module documentation, calculate dependencies, generate a plan, simulate changes, validate constraints, and obtain Human approval. Stream B requires:

- dependency calculation not be equated with complete operational reasoning;
- plan output remain distinct from actual state and authorized change;
- provider schemas and validators remain versioned assumptions;
- generated explanations remain separate from tool execution traces;
- hidden environmental conditions and rollback paths remain defeaters; and
- approval remain distinct from technical validity and responsible operation.

No architecture, module, policy, deployment, or implementation is selected.

### 30.4 sshConnectivity translation probe

A connectivity diagnosis may retrieve configuration, execute tests, observe errors, compare hypotheses, infer a likely fault, and recommend remediation. Stream B requires:

- command output remain observation rather than diagnosis;
- one successful connection not establish a robust causal model;
- a timeout not identify one cause;
- cached or copied remediation not establish reasoning by the current operator;
- diagnostic uncertainty and alternative layers remain explicit; and
- a correct recommendation not authorize execution.

No sshConnectivity schema, runtime, diagnosis, or command is changed.

## 33. Confidence and reopening conditions

**Confidence: High** that actual reasoning, inference, computation, retrieval, deliberation, judgment, explanation, product, trace, claim, assessment, status, and record must not substitute for one another; that output underdetermines process; and that validity does not establish premise truth, real-world truth, relevance, applicability, warrant, or responsible use.

**Confidence: High** that fluent or correct output, displayed chains, formal derivations, logs, architecture, origin, Human review, and DIA use cannot individually establish reasoning.

**Confidence: Medium–High** in the consideration-sensitive process anchor, six-state separation, minimum claim envelope, lowest-adequate-organized-level rule, typed process/conclusion Evidence, and consequence-proportional inspectability.

**Confidence: Medium** that Reasoning will pass final Foundational admission; that Inference will remain supporting; and that the proposed role map will survive answer, quality, value, outcome, and feedback analysis.

**Confidence: Low to Medium** in universal episode-continuity criteria, collective and institutional reasoning thresholds, reasoning attribution to narrow non-Intelligence systems, tacit-process assurance, and the exact minimum for restricted traces.

The model must be reopened if later analysis shows that:

- consideration sensitivity cannot distinguish reasoning from ordinary information processing across major Domains;
- the anchor wrongly requires explicit questions, aims, representations, or conclusions;
- non-representational, embodied, affective, intuitive, or tacit reasoning cannot fit without distortion;
- the lowest-adequate-level rule systematically privileges individuals, components, or widest systems;
- distributed and collective episodes cannot be bounded without importing one architecture;
- reasoning cannot be attributed independently of Intelligence or Agency;
- inference cannot be governed as a plural supporting family;
- computation, retrieval, simulation, generation, deliberation, judgment, or explanation proves independently Foundational;
- process occurrence cannot be evidenced without requiring inaccessible or unsafe internal disclosure;
- material assurance cannot survive privacy, privilege, security, or proprietary restrictions;
- trace fidelity cannot be distinguished from explanation quality or output behavior;
- Domain methods require validity or soundness relations the model cannot preserve;
- premise, assumption, Evidence, rule, model, conclusion, and defeater roles cannot remain contextual;
- Stream C cannot distinguish reasoning product from answer;
- Stream D shows answer quality necessarily includes a single reasoning-quality status;
- Stream E shows speed, cost, or value is constitutive of Reasoning rather than separately assessed;
- Stream F shows feedback or correction changes the Identity of every reasoning episode;
- Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views cannot preserve the same material process claim;
- derived-project grounding produces systematic false-positive or false-negative reasoning attributions;
- formalization cannot preserve uncertainty, alternatives, implicit moves, restricted evidence, and trace loss; or
- the model conflicts with the Product Vision or Stage-Accepted DR-009 through DR-015.
