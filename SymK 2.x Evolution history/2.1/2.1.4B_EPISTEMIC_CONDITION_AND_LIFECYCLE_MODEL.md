## 1. Question and result

> If Knowledge Engineering acts on epistemic conditions rather than manufacturing truth or Knowledge, what lifecycle does it govern and what changes across that lifecycle?

Stream B supports a recursive lifecycle rather than a linear pipeline. Knowledge Engineering may design, construct, govern, evaluate, and revise conditions at any point in the pursuit, expression, assessment, application, challenge, or preservation of Knowledge. No lifecycle transition by itself creates truth, Knowledge, authority, or responsible reliance.

## 2. Working definition

An **Epistemic Condition** is a condition that materially affects the pursuit, possible achievement, attribution, expression, preservation, assessment, application, challenge, or revision of Knowledge within declared Scope and Context.

The distinction is supporting rather than a synonym for Knowledge. A condition may be enabling, constraining, evidentiary, representational, institutional, social, technical, procedural, material, or environmental. Its effect must be stated relative to an epistemic purpose, subject, Bearer or candidate Bearer, Context, Scope, and lifecycle activity.

## 3. Recursive lifecycle

| Activity | Governing question | Typical conditions | Prohibited inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame | What question, subject, Context, Scope, and stakes are established? | vocabulary, boundary, affected parties, authority, assumptions | An explicit frame is not necessarily neutral or complete |
| Inquire or acquire | How are observations, testimony, records, skills, or resources sought? | access, methods, instruments, sampling, competence, incentives | Acquisition or extraction does not entail Knowledge |
| Represent or claim | What content is made available, by whom, in which form, and with what loss? | language, notation, model, record, interface, provenance | A claim or Representation is not the achievement it concerns |
| Ground and evaluate | What bears on the claim or achievement, under which method and criteria? | Grounds, Evidence, counterevidence, validity tests, expertise, disagreement | Formal validity, evidence, acceptance, or status alone is not epistemic sufficiency |
| Integrate | How is the result related to prior Knowledge, conflict, uncertainty, and alternatives? | Ontology, Logic, explanatory relations, contradiction handling | Consistency with a corpus does not guarantee truth or Knowledge |
| Authorize reliance or apply | Who may rely, decide, act, or withhold action, within which Scope? | decision rules, responsibilities, risk tolerances, safeguards | Authorization or successful use does not retroactively create Knowledge |
| Observe consequence | What effects, failures, harms, benefits, and surprises follow? | monitoring, feedback, affected-party testimony, outcomes | A favorable outcome is not sufficient proof of the claim or method |
| Challenge and revise | How may claims, assessments, methods, frames, and conditions be contested? | dissent, replication, appeal, correction, versioning | Revision does not imply every earlier result was worthless or false |
| Preserve, supersede, or retire | What remains available, authoritative, inspectable, or deprecated? | lineage, archival integrity, status transitions, migration records | Storage, preservation, or current status is not Knowledge |

The activities can overlap, repeat, branch, or return to an earlier frame. Consequence evidence may reopen assessment; disagreement may change framing; a new Representation may reveal semantic loss; and application may expose a hidden dependency.

## 4. Condition relationships

Epistemic conditions may:

- enable or constrain inquiry without determining its result;
- strengthen or weaken Grounds without becoming truth makers;
- preserve, transform, simplify, or obscure meaning in Representation;
- distribute access, authority, risk, competence, and responsibility unevenly;
- make disagreement visible or suppress it;
- support reliable challenge, correction, and revision; and
- alter what responsible reliance requires in a particular Context.

Every material condition claim therefore needs direction, subject, Context, Scope, lifecycle location, asserted effect, evidence, uncertainty, authority, and lineage. “Good epistemic conditions” is not an unanalyzed global status.

## 5. Knowledge modes

The DR-011 plural Knowledge minimum is preserved. The lifecycle must accommodate propositional, practical, experiential or acquaintance-like, and possible collective or distributed modes without forcing them into one artifact pattern.

- Propositional Knowledge may emphasize claims, Grounds, Evidence, inference, and challenge.
- Practical Knowledge may emphasize embodied competence, performance across variation, correction, and consequence.
- Experiential or acquaintance-like Knowledge may resist complete transfer through a record or description.
- Collective or distributed candidate cases require a stable Bearer boundary and cannot be inferred from aggregation alone.

The modes may interact, but copying a Representation cannot be assumed to transfer the corresponding achievement.

## 6. Acquisition, extraction, construction, and governance

**Acquisition** is a broad activity by which a Bearer may come to know or gain epistemic resources. **Extraction** derives content or structure from a source. **Construction** creates or arranges artifacts and conditions. None is a synonym for Knowledge production.

Knowledge Engineering may construct an index, claim graph, evidence record, curriculum, review process, or interactive environment. These interventions can improve conditions for inquiry and responsible reliance, but their success must be evaluated without reifying Knowledge as stored content.

Epistemic governance assigns statuses, roles, authorities, processes, and challenge rights. It remains distinct from truth and Knowledge attribution. Responsibility, authorization, and consequence allocation remain substantively assigned to 2.1.6 and 2.1.8.

## 7. Counterexample results

The model survives a true claim produced by luck; a well-grounded claim later defeated; valid inference from false premises; an accepted institutional status that proves mistaken; successful action based on an inaccurate model; a skilled practice not fully articulable; copied files that do not transfer understanding; a Representation omitting minority objection; precise provenance with weak relevance; and a preserved obsolete record still valuable as lineage evidence.

## 8. Working disposition and next action

- `Epistemic Condition` remains a governed supporting distinction candidate.
- `Knowledge Engineering` remains a foundational concept candidate whose direct object is epistemic conditions.
- The lifecycle is a governed relationship and activity model, not one mandatory sequence.
- Lifecycle, governance, epistemic, truth, and reliance statuses must remain separately expressible.

Proceed to Stream C: distinguish Ground, Evidence, Provenance, epistemic status, uncertainty, disagreement, governance status, and reliance authorization.
