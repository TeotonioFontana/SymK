## 5. Inherited hypotheses — not accepted conclusions

The v0.1 materials make several strong proposals that must be treated as hypotheses:

1. Knowledge is an epistemic achievement involving truth-connected, competence-bearing, or direct relation and responsible reliance within Scope.
2. A knowledge claim, knowledge asset, Representation, and governed epistemic status are distinct from Knowledge.
3. Intelligence is a substrate-realized capacity of an organized System, expressed across a relevant range and involving some adaptation beyond prior cases.
4. Performance is evidence for capability and is not identical to Intelligence.
5. Intelligence, competence, skill, agency, autonomy, rationality, understanding, consciousness, wisdom, moral standing, and authority are not interchangeable.
6. Knowledge and Intelligence are distinct but reciprocal: Intelligence participates in producing, challenging, interpreting, and applying Knowledge; Knowledge may scaffold, preserve, or amplify capability.
7. Neither participant type nor artifact type establishes epistemic standing in advance.

The work package must be willing to reject, narrow, split, or defer any of these hypotheses.

## 6. Principal analytical hazards

- **Hidden circularity:** “Knowledge is what Intelligence possesses” paired with “Intelligence is what produces or uses Knowledge.”
- **Representation reification:** treating a stored claim, document, graph, model, or status as Knowledge.
- **Performance substitution:** treating one output, test, or score as Intelligence.
- **Bearer drift:** including tools and collaborators for capability attribution but excluding them for responsibility or failure.
- **Factivity/reliance collapse:** treating operational acceptance as truth, or factivity as sufficient authorization for use.
- **Skill/capability collapse:** treating a procedure document as competence or accumulated skill as transparent evidence of acquisition capacity.
- **Anthropocentric or substrate privilege:** defining Intelligence through human resemblance or denying it from origin alone.
- **Metaphysical overreach:** making consciousness, intrinsic teleology, moral status, or complete theories of truth necessary without showing SymK-level need.
- **Domain leakage:** allowing legal, medical, artificial-system, or organizational criteria to become universal SymK criteria.
- **Premature formalization:** choosing a schema, ontology, logic, score, or Python class before semantic disposition.

## 8. Governed analysis sequence

### A. Independent Knowledge anchor

- identify the problem Knowledge must solve in the accepted SymK identity;
- test factivity, epistemic achievement, responsible reliance, plural modes, and possible bearers;
- distinguish Knowledge from claim, asset, Representation, information, data, belief, status, authorization, and acceptance;
- attempt definitions that do not use Intelligence as a necessary definitional term.

### B. Independent Intelligence anchor

- identify the problem Intelligence must solve in the accepted SymK identity;
- test capacity, organization, realization, adaptation, relevant range, and possible bearers;
- distinguish Intelligence from substrate, cognition, competence, skill, performance, agency, autonomy, rationality, understanding, consciousness, moral standing, and authority;
- attempt definitions that do not use Knowledge as a necessary definitional term.

### C. Bearer and attribution model

- test individual, artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, and hybrid candidates;
- require stable Identity, justified System boundary, declared Context and Scope, evidence, resources, assistance, time, and revision conditions;
- distinguish the represented subject, attribution claim, attribution decision, and Bearer.

### D. Reciprocal consistency

- add typed relationships only after A–C;
- distinguish constitutive dependency, enabling dependency, developmental dependency, evidentiary dependency, and operational use;
- determine whether reciprocity is universal, mode-specific, contingent, or partly rhetorical;
- reject any relation that erases the independent counterexamples of either concept.

### E. Stress tests and consequences

- apply negative, edge, and cross-domain cases;
- test DQ-001, DQ-002, DQ-012, the Knowledge/reliance tension, and the Intelligence-attribution tension;
- record consequences for candidate A1/A2/A4, the Product Vision value chain, the Foundation Paper revision ledger, later work packages, and derived projects;
- record dissent, confidence, and reopening conditions.

### F. DR-011 proposal checkpoint

Only after A–E are complete may a consolidated Proposed DR-011 be entered in the Decision Register and presented to the project steward. The proposal must distinguish accepted predecessor constraints from new positions and must not revise canonical concept or Foundation Paper artifacts directly.

## 9. Mandatory counterexample set

At minimum, the analysis must explain:

1. a true claim accepted for the wrong reason;
2. a false but operationally relied-upon claim;
3. a document or graph with rich provenance but no established Knowledge;
4. competent practice that cannot be reduced to a procedure representation;
5. a correct one-off performance caused by memorization, leakage, luck, or external assistance;
6. stable capability that fails to manifest under adverse conditions;
7. a fixed specialized system with high competence but no online learning;
8. a coordinated group that aggregates outputs without system-level integration;
9. a collective or hybrid capability whose boundary changes opportunistically;
10. an intelligent contribution lacking agency, autonomy, authority, consciousness, or moral standing;
11. an organization holding durable distributed epistemic resources no member possesses in full;
12. the same represented property or assertion used in two domains without acquiring universal epistemic status.
