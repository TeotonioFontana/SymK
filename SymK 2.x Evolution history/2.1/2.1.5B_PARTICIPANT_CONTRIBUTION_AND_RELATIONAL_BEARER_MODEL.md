## 1. Question and analytical result

> Who or what may participate in Cooperation, how are contributions and mutual orientation attributed, and when may a collective, institution, distributed arrangement, or hybrid be treated as one participant without inventing a collective Bearer or erasing its members and dependencies?

Stream B supports the following working answer:

> **A Cooperation Participant is a subject occupying a scoped relational role in an actual cooperative occurrence when a materially relevant contribution and mutual orientation to the shared undertaking are supportable at that subject's own explanatory level. Participation is episode-, relation-, Context-, Scope-, and time-dependent. It is not established by presence, membership, interaction, designation, output, compliance, benefit, Intelligence attribution, or organizational status alone.**

The participant role does not itself entail Intelligence, agency, autonomy, consent, responsibility, authority, authorship, legal personality, moral standing, or permanent cooperative disposition. When the stronger Product Vision claim is that Cooperation occurs **among Intelligences**, each subject presented as one of those Intelligences requires a separate DR-011-compatible Intelligence attribution. Supporting contributors, resources, actors, beneficiaries, and affected parties may also be present without becoming Intelligence participants by implication.

The analysis also rejects a default “Bearer of Cooperation” model:

> **Actual Cooperation is a polyadic relational process among participants, not a unary property borne by one participant or automatically by their aggregate. The cooperative occurrence or continuing relation may itself be the subject of an attribution, assessment, status, and record. A team, institution, distributed system, or hybrid may separately participate as an organized unit, or bear Intelligence or a cooperative capability, only when level-specific criteria and evidence support each attribution.**

This preserves both plural participation and system-level organization without reifying every group, platform, workflow, or successful output. The result is an analytical candidate for continuation, not a final concept disposition.

## 2. Inherited constraints

Stream B inherits without reopening:

1. Cooperation is an actual scoped relational process, not SymK's objective, a favorable outcome, participant trait, or permanent status.
2. Its thin nucleus requires plural distinguishable participants, a bounded shared undertaking, material contributions, mutual orientation, relational connection, and preserved difference.
3. Actual occurrence, governed qualification, and outcome/value assessment are separate.
4. Every actual Intelligence and Knowledge instance has a Bearer; actual bearing, attribution, assessment, authority, and record remain distinct.
5. Bearer is a governed supporting attribution role, not a universal Entity class.
6. Cooperation among participant Bearers does not automatically create a collective Intelligence Bearer.
7. Attribute predicates at the lowest organized level that explains them without omitting constitutive integration.
8. The boundary used to claim success must remain visible when analyzing failure, dependencies, cost, authority, responsibility, and consequences.
9. Different levels may receive separately evidenced attributions; predicates do not copy automatically among member, component, collective, institution, distributed system, or hybrid.
10. Detailed agency, authority, consent, responsibility, accountability, and liability remain principally assigned to 2.1.6.

## 3. Relational roles that must remain distinct

| Role | Stream B working meaning | What it does not entail |
|---|---|---|
| Participant | Subject whose contribution and mutual orientation are attributable at the selected level within a cooperative occurrence or continuing relation | Intelligence, agency, consent, authority, responsibility, benefit, authorship, or moral standing |
| Contributor | Subject whose act, restraint, capability, resource, information, or other input materially enters the shared undertaking | Participation; a source may contribute without entering the cooperative relation |
| Actor | Subject or organized mechanism that performs an act or produces an event in the process | Control, understanding, mutual orientation, authorization, responsibility, or participant status |
| Resource | Something made available to, used by, or depended upon in the undertaking | Contribution at its own level, participation, Bearer status, or authority |
| Source | Origin of a claim, datum, testimony, design, code, observation, or other represented contribution | Current participation, Knowledge, correctness, authorship of later transformations, or consent to use |
| Beneficiary | Subject intended or observed to receive a benefit | Participation, contribution, consent, responsibility, or exclusive standing |
| Affected party | Subject bearing a material consequence, burden, risk, exclusion, or benefit | Participation or contribution; nonparticipation does not remove governance relevance or challenge standing |
| Authority | Subject or institution with recognized standing to decide, direct, authorize, prohibit, or assess within Scope | Participation, epistemic correctness, contribution, Intelligence, or responsibility for every consequence |
| Bearer | Subject that actually satisfies an attributed predicate within Scope | Participant status; the predicate borne must be identified separately |
| Represented subject | Subject about which a claim, profile, record, or model is made | Source, owner, participant, assessor, or Bearer of the represented predicate |
| Assessor | Subject evaluating an attribution or process under declared criteria | Authority to decide every use or correctness of the assessment |
| Maintainer or operator | Subject preserving or operating an artifact or system | Authorship, Knowledge, participant status, semantic authority, or responsibility for all uses |

These roles may overlap in a particular case, but no overlap is universal and no role transfers automatically.

## 4. Participant as a relational role

### 4.1 Participation is actual before it is declared

A policy, contract, roster, user account, team membership, platform label, workflow assignment, or “Human/AI partnership” statement may declare expected participation. It does not establish that participation occurred. Actual participation requires the contribution and orientation conditions to be satisfied within the relevant occurrence or continuing relation.

### 4.2 Participation is not a permanent property

The same subject may participate in one episode, remain only a member or available resource in another, become an affected party in a third, and exit the relation later. A prior cooperative episode does not create a permanent `CooperativeParticipant` type or trait.

### 4.3 Episode participation and continuing-relation membership differ

An ongoing cooperative relation may preserve a participant roster, expectations, readiness, monitoring, or role-distributed availability even when not every member acts in each episode. SymK should therefore distinguish:

- actual participant in a specific occurrence;
- declared, eligible, assigned, or standing participant in a continuing arrangement;
- inactive member whose governed availability or restraint may or may not itself be a material contribution;
- former or excluded participant preserved in history.

Membership in a continuing arrangement cannot be used to fabricate an episode-level contribution.

### 4.4 Participation does not require identical internal states

Human intention, institutional purpose, artificial goal representation, protocol-constrained responsiveness, and hybrid organization may realize orientation differently. Stream B does not adopt consciousness, belief, intention, autonomy, or moral agency as universal participant conditions. It requires evidence that the contribution is organized with reference to the shared undertaking and at least one other participant or contribution at the level claimed.

### 4.5 Direct awareness is not universally necessary

Participants in mediated, asynchronous, institutional, or distributed cooperation need not know every other participant individually. They must nevertheless be connected through a shared undertaking and a relational organization that makes their contributions responsive, composable, constrained, or available to one another. Unknowing upstream sources whose work is merely appropriated remain sources or affected parties unless participation at their level is independently established.

## 5. Qualified participant-attribution test

A material Participant attribution should make the following recoverable:

| Condition | Required question | Failure prevented |
|---|---|---|
| Subject identity | Which subject is claimed to participate, and what preserves its continuity? | A label, account, model name, or organization name substituting for Identity |
| Selected level | Is the subject an individual, component, configured system, team, institution, distributed arrangement, hybrid, or another organized level? | Opportunistic movement among levels |
| Boundary | Which people, components, artifacts, tools, data, procedures, and environments are inside or outside the proposed participant? | Assistance counted for success but excluded from review |
| Shared undertaking | What bounded undertaking is the participation relative to? | Generic “collaboration” without a joint object of work |
| Material contribution | What did the subject contribute at the selected level, and why was it material? | Presence, membership, volume, prestige, or output count becoming contribution |
| Mutual orientation | How was the contribution organized with reference to the undertaking and another participant or contribution? | Coincidence, post hoc aggregation, or externally imposed combination becoming participation |
| Relational connection | Through what response, handoff, complementarity, constraint, integration, correction, or governed composition did it enter the process? | Parallel work in one container becoming cooperation |
| Dependencies and resources | Which capabilities, people, tools, representations, infrastructure, data, authority, or environment were required? | The visible component receiving the whole attribution |
| Scope and time | In which Domain, Context, Scope, episode, period, and version does the attribution apply? | Permanent or universal participant status |
| Evidence and alternatives | What supports the attribution, and what competing descriptions—actor, resource, source, member, beneficiary, or affected party—remain? | Participant terminology chosen by rhetoric |
| Change conditions | Which membership, model, tool, procedure, interface, or environmental changes preserve, restrict, or replace the attribution? | Identifier continuity masking a changed subject |
| Assessment and lineage | Who assesses the attribution, with what authority, uncertainty, challenge path, and history? | Self-designation becoming fact or status |

No single field such as `participant=true`, role name, login state, signature, message count, output score, or organizational membership can satisfy this test.

## 6. Contribution model

### 6.1 Working definition

> **A Contribution is a typed, scoped relation in which something attributable to a subject materially enters a shared undertaking by enabling, supplying, interpreting, challenging, constraining, coordinating, deciding, executing, monitoring, correcting, preserving, repairing, or otherwise changing the undertaking's available process or result.**

Contribution does not mean beneficial contribution. It may be mistaken, incomplete, counterproductive, harmful, or later defeated. Its quality, legitimacy, authority, originality, responsibility, and outcome value require separate assessments.

### 6.2 Material causal influence is not sufficient

An attacker, outage, weather event, or hidden bias may materially change an undertaking. That causal influence is not automatically a contribution because it may lack relational entry or orientation toward the undertaking. Causal contribution, causal responsibility, and participation require further treatment in 2.1.6.

### 6.3 Contribution need not be a visible artifact

A contribution may consist of:

- a claim, question, ground, observation, model, design, or proposal;
- practical execution or skilled judgment;
- critique, counterexample, disagreement, refusal, restraint, or boundary protection;
- access, translation, mediation, coordination, monitoring, or repair;
- memory, continuity, institutional procedure, or maintained availability;
- an artificial or technical transformation, search, inference, generation, or control action;
- a resource made available under a participant's organized control.

The artifact or output representing a contribution is not necessarily the contribution, contributor, or participant.

### 6.4 Contribution attribution levels

The same event may support different contribution claims:

- an individual drafted a paragraph;
- a model generated candidate text;
- a configured system retrieved sources and transformed the draft;
- a team selected, challenged, and adopted the result;
- an institution issued the final position.

Each claim requires its own level, relation, evidence, and Scope. The same work must not be counted repeatedly as independent contributions when higher-level statements merely summarize lower-level work. Composition and derivation must remain explicit.

### 6.5 Negative and corrective contributions

Refusal, dissent, challenge, correction, and prevention can be material contributions when oriented to the shared undertaking. Output volume therefore cannot serve as a universal contribution measure. A participant who prevents a harmful action may contribute more materially than one who produces many artifacts.

## 7. Mutual orientation without anthropomorphic transfer

Stream A left mutual orientation necessary but substrate-sensitive. Stream B proposes that orientation evidence may be supported through different mechanisms:

| Candidate level | Possible evidence of orientation | Insufficient by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Individual | Expressed understanding, responsive conduct, uptake, handoff, correction, intentional contribution, or reliable role performance | Self-description, attendance, message production, or agreement |
| Artificial executing system | State or behavior conditioned on the undertaking and other contributions; maintained task model; responsive selection, transformation, correction, or control within the configured system | Input/output exchange, model branding, fluent language, or operator intention attributed to the model |
| Collective/team | Integrated division of work, shared or role-linked state, selection, feedback, correction, and output attributable to team organization | Co-location, common employer, voting, averaging, or combined files |
| Institution | Persistent roles, procedures, memory, authorized interfaces, review, and revision through which institutional contributions respond to others | Legal existence, policy statement, official seal, document retention, or one employee's act |
| Distributed arrangement | Stable coupling, protocols, memory, coordination, feedback, and continuity across nodes | Network connection, shared repository, parallel execution, or common data format |
| Hybrid | Reciprocal human–artificial feedback and role organization that materially shapes a combined contribution beyond occasional tool use | A human using software, a model producing output, or a convenient boundary drawn after success |

These are evidence categories, not necessary implementation features or proof of Intelligence. Direct mental intention is not universal; pure external orchestration and post hoc aggregation remain insufficient.

## 8. Candidate participant patterns

### 8.1 Individual participant

An individual may participate when a contribution and mutual orientation are attributable to that individual within Scope. Human origin, professional title, presence, signature, or final approval does not establish meaningful participation. Assistance and dependencies remain visible.

### 8.2 Artificial participant

An artificial participant attribution must identify whether the proposed subject is a model, configured agent, runtime, service, toolchain, deployment, or another organized system. A model can be an actor or resource while the configured system or hybrid is the participant. Conversely, a component may participate at its own level if its organized contribution and orientation are independently supportable.

Artificial origin neither proves nor disproves participation. Consciousness, moral standing, and responsibility are not inferred. The Product Vision's stronger “Intelligence” label requires a separate attribution under DR-011.

### 8.3 Collective or team participant

A collection of members does not participate as one unit solely because members contribute. A team-level participant attribution requires evidence that team organization selects, composes, constrains, revises, or presents a contribution at the team level. Internal member cooperation and external team participation are separate relations.

A team may participate externally without being established as a collective Intelligence Bearer. If the claim is instead that individual Intelligences cooperate internally, no collective Intelligence attribution is needed.

### 8.4 Institutional participant

An institution may participate through persistent roles, procedures, memory, authorized acts, review, and revision that make a contribution attributable beyond one member. Legal identity or authority alone is insufficient. An employee's act may be personal, institutional, unauthorized, or mixed; exact authority and responsibility belong to 2.1.6.

Institution-level cooperation with another institution does not establish that internal relations among employees were cooperative or responsible. Internal coercion can coexist with an externally coordinated institutional contribution and remains visible in qualification and consequence review.

### 8.5 Distributed participant

A distributed arrangement may participate as one unit when coupling, communication, memory, coordination, control, and continuity support a contribution at that level. Physical dispersion is not the issue. A shared repository, protocol, or network connection without integration is insufficient.

Open and asynchronous communities can support distributed participation when contributors orient to a governed shared undertaking through maintained interfaces, review, issue tracking, norms, and integration—even if every member does not know every other member.

### 8.6 Hybrid participant

A hybrid may participate as one organized unit when human and artificial components form stable, identity-relevant feedback and role relations that produce a contribution not adequately attributed to one component alone. Occasional tool use does not suffice.

The analysis must choose and declare whether:

- human and artificial systems are distinct participants cooperating with one another;
- their integrated hybrid is one participant cooperating externally;
- or both levels are separately relevant.

The same contributions cannot be counted at every level without composition and lineage. Internal cooperation does not automatically make the hybrid an Intelligence Bearer; hybrid Intelligence and later Amplified Intelligence require separate evidence.

### 8.7 Artifact, repository, and infrastructure

A static document, dataset, repository, network, or infrastructure service is normally a resource, Representation, source, or maintained component rather than a participant. Passive containment, availability, or technical necessity does not establish contribution and orientation at that artifact's own level.

This is not a metaphysical ban. If an organized executing system exists at a larger level, that system may receive a qualified participant attribution. The record or storage medium does not inherit it.

## 9. Cooperative unit and relational-bearer boundary

### 9.1 Cooperative unit

For analysis, a **cooperative unit** is the scoped configuration of participants, shared undertaking, contributions, relations, resources, dependencies, affected parties, and temporal boundary under review. It is an analytical system boundary, not automatically a Bearer, participant, Intelligence, legal person, or responsible party.

### 9.2 Cooperation is polyadic

The primary semantic form is relational:

```text
participants P1 ... Pn
    contribute C1 ... Cn
    through relations R
    toward shared undertaking U
    within Context/Scope/time S
```

No single participant “contains” the Cooperation. The relation or occurrence can possess Identity, provenance, lifecycle, assessment, and Representation without becoming a collective Intelligence.

### 9.3 Three meanings hidden by “the team cooperates”

The statement may mean:

1. members cooperate with one another inside the team;
2. the team acts as one participant in Cooperation with an external participant;
3. the team bears a cooperative capability, disposition, status, or reputation.

These claims require different evidence. None entails the others.

### 9.4 Internal and external relations do not transfer automatically

An institution may cooperate externally while its internal work is coercive or fragmented. Members may cooperate internally while the institution exploits an affected party. A hybrid may be internally coordinated while neither component has meaningful challenge capacity. Every relevant relation must retain its own participant set, undertaking, qualification, and consequence assessment.

### 9.5 Cooperative capability is not actual Cooperation

A subject may be assessed as capable, disposed, prepared, or authorized to cooperate without cooperating in the current episode. Actual Cooperation does not permanently grant that capability or status. Formation analysis must later distinguish developed disposition from current performance.

## 10. Level, boundary, dependency, and change rules

1. **Explanatory-level rule:** select the lowest organized level that explains the contribution and orientation without omitting constitutive integration.
2. **No default largest system:** the whole platform, organization, or Human–AI arrangement is not automatically the participant because it contains every dependency.
3. **No default visible component:** the person or model producing the visible output does not automatically receive the whole contribution.
4. **Stable success/failure boundary:** retain the participant boundary when evaluating errors, burden, hidden labor, cost, correction, and consequences.
5. **Resource disclosure:** dependencies remain visible even when they do not become components of the participant.
6. **Multi-level permission:** allow distinct, separately evidenced attributions at individual, component, team, institution, distributed, and hybrid levels.
7. **No duplicate credit:** identify composition, derivation, overlap, and omitted contributions when summarizing lower-level work at a higher level.
8. **Temporal validity:** membership, models, prompts, tools, procedures, authority, interfaces, and environments may preserve, restrict, or replace the participant.
9. **Relation continuity:** a continuing cooperative relation may survive participant changes if its undertaking, organization, lineage, and identity conditions persist; constitutive participant loss may instead end or replace it.
10. **Historical preservation:** entry, exit, exclusion, replacement, and changed boundaries do not rewrite earlier participation or consequences.

## 11. Asymmetry, dependence, and standing

Unequal capability, contribution, benefit, knowledge, authority, or power does not by itself defeat participation or Cooperation. It does require visibility.

A small contribution may be material. A participant may depend heavily on another. A coordinator may contribute without producing domain content. A beneficiary may contribute nothing. An affected party may have no participant role yet possess stronger grounds for challenge than some participants.

Stream B therefore rejects these implications:

```text
larger output        -/-> larger contribution
greater capability  -/-> greater participant standing
participant status  -/-> authority or responsibility
benefit              -/-> participation or consent
affected-party role  -/-> non-relevance because nonparticipant
dependency           -/-> component identity or justified control
formal equality      -/-> equal practical power
```

The rules for justified authority, consent, duty, challenge standing, responsibility, accountability, and liability remain for 2.1.6. Stream B establishes only that these cannot be inferred from participant or contributor labels.

## 12. Attribution, assessment, status, and record

| Level | Question | Prohibited substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Actual participation | Did this subject contribute with mutual orientation at the claimed level? | Declared role or membership creates participation |
| Participant attribution | Who claims that which subject participated in what undertaking, level, Scope, and period? | Claim proves occurrence |
| Participant assessment | What evidence and criteria support the level, boundary, contribution, and orientation? | Output, interaction count, or type label settles the assessment |
| Governance designation | Was the subject assigned, admitted, excluded, authorized, or recorded as a participant? | Designation creates actual participation or responsibility |
| Contribution attribution | What material contribution is attributed to whom or what, through which composition and dependencies? | Visible artifact or final approver receives all credit |
| Cooperation attribution | Did the required plural participant relations form an actual cooperative occurrence? | Participant attributions alone prove mutual orientation or the complete relation |
| Record | Which Representation preserves claims, evidence, assessment, status, composition, challenges, and history? | Stored participant graph becomes the relation or collective Bearer |

## 13. Alternatives considered

### A — Every interacting component is a participant

**Rejected.** It turns messages, dependencies, infrastructure, databases, and passive artifacts into participants and collapses coordination, causation, resource use, and Cooperation.

### B — Only autonomous, conscious, intentional, or morally responsible agents participate

**Rejected as the universal SymK rule.** It pre-decides agency, mind, and responsibility; excludes institutional, artificial, distributed, and hybrid cases before evidence; and anthropomorphizes the participant boundary.

### C — Every Intelligence Bearer is a participant, and every participant is an Intelligence

**Rejected.** Intelligence is a capacity, participation is a relational occurrence, and neither attribution entails the other. The stronger Product Vision statement requires both claims separately where applicable.

### D — Teams and institutions participate whenever members act in their name

**Rejected.** It reifies organizations, erases unauthorized or personal acts, and copies member predicates to the whole without level-specific integration.

### E — Only individual participants are real; collective and hybrid statements are shorthand

**Rejected as universally reductive.** Some organized units can select, integrate, remember, respond, and contribute at a level not exhausted by one member, although every such attribution remains evidence-bearing.

### F — Level-qualified relational Participant with distinct supporting roles

**Retained as the Stream B working approach.** It supports plural substrates, preserves member and system levels, prevents role transfer, exposes dependencies, and does not invent a collective Bearer.

The working model classifies these cases without origin privilege, individual-only reduction, collective reification, or responsibility preemption.

## 15. Three-view preservation obligations

### 15.1 Human View

The Human View should explain:

- who is being called a participant and at which level;
- what each contribution was and why it mattered;
- how mutual orientation and relational connection were manifested;
- which roles, dependencies, asymmetries, exclusions, affected parties, and alternative descriptions remain;
- where the boundary changed and what the attribution does not imply.

### 15.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View should preserve, where material:

- operationalized candidate indicators for contribution, integration, responsiveness, and level;
- counterfactual, ablation, perturbation, process-tracing, observational, or comparative evidence;
- competing hypotheses such as aggregation, external control, role performance, leakage, or post hoc attribution;
- population, sampling, instrument, uncertainty, and defeat conditions.

Ablation or output gain alone cannot define participation or collective organization, but it can provide qualified evidence.

### 15.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View should eventually preserve:

- subject and participant identities separately from identifiers and roles;
- selected level, boundary, components, resources, dependencies, and versions;
- undertaking, contribution type, composition, derivation, and relational links;
- source, actor, contributor, participant, assessor, authority, beneficiary, affected party, Bearer, and responsible-party fields as distinct where material;
- attribution, assessment, status, challenge, entry, exit, replacement, expiry, and lineage;
- declared loss in simplified dashboards, logs, role tables, or participant graphs.

No schema, cardinality, formal relation, metric, workflow, or interface is selected here.

### 15.4 Alignment test

A Human account of “Human–AI cooperation,” a scientific output comparison, and an engineering trace of components may refer to different system boundaries. Until those boundaries and transformations align, no single participant, contribution, or hybrid-level conclusion may silently govern the others.

## 17. Consequences for governing artifacts

### Product Vision

“Professionals, AI, and other Intelligences cooperate” must be read as a claim requiring participant-, contribution-, and Intelligence-level evidence, not as a list of permanent classes or proof that every component is an Intelligence. The DIA, platform, and cooperative unit remain separate possible system boundaries.

### Candidate A2

The non-privilege direction is strengthened analytically: Human, artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, and hybrid origin cannot determine participation or role. A2's distinctions among capability, role, authority, authorization, responsibility, and affected-party standing remain necessary. Its exact normative force and rules are not accepted here.

### Candidate A0 and A3

- A0 should distinguish Participant from Intelligence, contributor, affected party, and beneficiary and avoid treating formal consent or membership as cooperation.
- A3 must identify the learner or formative Bearer separately from the Participant and cooperative unit. Individual formation cannot be copied to a team or hybrid, and system-level formation cannot be inferred from changed records alone.

These are revision-ledger findings, not axiom changes.

### Education Foundation Paper

The paper's individual, collective, and hybrid formation claims require level-specific Bearer and Participant evidence. “The cooperative system can become the learner” remains a hypothesis for Streams C and E; Stream B supplies the boundary rules but does not accept the learner attribution. The paper should eventually distinguish participant, learner, Bearer, contributor, actor, source, authority, beneficiary, affected party, and responsible party.

### Legacy Human/AI role axiom

The fixed Human Architect and AI Partner roles remain historical evidence only. Human/AI type does not decide participant level, contribution, authority, responsibility, or the correct hybrid boundary. Breadth, critique, purpose selection, and decision ownership may be contributions or role allocations when evidenced and governed, not origin-defined duties.

### DR-011 and DR-012

The model preserves their level, boundary, attribution, and Representation rules. Participant Cooperation does not establish a collective Intelligence. Cooperation records do not become the relation. Contribution provenance does not create Knowledge, authority, authorship, or responsibility.

### 2.1.6 through 2.1.8

- 2.1.6 receives explicit interfaces for actor, agency, authority, authorization, consent, contribution, responsibility, accountability, liability, and affected-party standing.
- 2.1.7 must distinguish individual participants, cooperative unit, DIA system boundary, hybrid or collective Intelligence Bearer, and Amplified Intelligence Bearer.
- 2.1.8 must distinguish contributor and beneficiary from answer owner, decision-maker, actor, affected party, and the Bearer of costs or consequences.

### Derived projects

Projects will eventually own Domain participant categories, professional roles, authorization rules, contribution practices, evidence methods, thresholds, and operational projections. They must preserve the universal distinctions and cannot grant participant or Intelligence status by interface label or organizational membership.

No derived-project artifact changes through this analysis.
