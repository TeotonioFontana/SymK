## 1. Question and analytical result

> What minimum meaning allows Cooperation to perform its role in the SymK value chain without collapsing into coordination, communication, aggregation, compliance, consensus, competition, coercion, collusion, or a favorable outcome?

The first 2.1.5 stream supports the following working answer:

> **Cooperation is an actual, scoped relational process in which two or more distinguishable participants make materially relevant contributions that are mutually oriented toward a bounded shared undertaking and sufficiently connected for the contributions to form part of that undertaking rather than merely coincide or aggregate.**

This candidate is deliberately thinner than *responsible cooperation* and deliberately stronger than compatible activity. It supplies the descriptive relational nucleus needed to identify what occurred. It does not by itself establish that the undertaking was legitimate, the participants were free and equally situated, the result was true or valuable, affected parties were protected, or SymK should authorize or commend the relation.

For SymK, three questions must therefore remain separate:

1. **Occurrence:** did an actual cooperative relation or episode satisfy the minimum relational conditions?
2. **Governed admissibility:** is the relation sufficiently scoped, accountable, challengeable, consequence-aware, and revisable to be treated as responsible or authorized cooperation for a stated SymK use?
3. **Value and outcome:** did the process actually contribute to better reasoning, an acceptable answer, responsible action, and acceptable consequences?

A collusive relation may pass the first question and fail the second and third. A productive disagreement may pass all three without consensus. A correct answer may pass the third output test while the process fails the first or second. This separation prevents both moral sanitization and outcome substitution.

The candidate makes Cooperation a relational process or achievement, not a final objective, participant trait, capability dimension, role, status, artifact, or permanent property. It is an analytical candidate only. Final classification, wording, and disposition remain for Streams B–F and the DR-013 checkpoint.

## 2. Accepted predecessor constraints

This analysis inherits and does not reopen the following:

1. Cooperation is the process in the Product Vision value chain, not SymK's final objective or an unconstrained good.
2. The principal value is better answers to complex questions, faster and at lower total cost only under epistemic accountability, responsibility, learning, and consequence constraints.
3. Knowledge is the governed epistemic medium, not a shared substance, common belief, consensus, or stored artifact.
4. Intelligence is a multidimensional capacity and does not entail cooperative disposition, wisdom, authority, responsibility, moral standing, or correct action.
5. Actual bearing or occurrence, attribution claim, assessment, authority, status, reliance authorization, and record are distinct.
6. Cooperation among participants does not automatically establish a collective Intelligence Bearer.
7. Representation, workflow state, organizational membership, compliance, output, score, credential, or self-description does not establish actual cooperation.
8. Disagreement, challenge, application, consequence, correction, and revision belong to the recursive epistemic lifecycle.
9. Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views remain coordinated where material; their formats and capabilities do not define Cooperation.
10. Detailed agency, authority, responsibility, accountability, and liability remain assigned principally to 2.1.6.

## 3. Why SymK needs a Cooperation concept

The Product Vision value chain places Cooperation between governed domain Knowledge and Amplified Intelligence. Without a minimum concept, that link can silently mean any of the following:

- information exchange;
- participants using the same platform;
- compatible interfaces;
- aggregation of independent outputs;
- compliance with a workflow;
- majority agreement;
- task allocation by authority;
- successful performance;
- or the mere presence of Humans and AI in one system.

Each substitution defeats the value chain. A system could then claim cooperation while concealing domination, dependency, collusion, passive tool use, suppressed dissent, accidental complementarity, or responsibility loss.

The concept performs five minimum jobs:

1. **Relational identification:** distinguish an actual relation among participants from an individual capability or isolated act.
2. **Composition control:** explain when plural contributions form one undertaking without automatically creating one collective Bearer.
3. **Difference preservation:** allow different goals, capabilities, roles, representations, and judgments to contribute without assimilation.
4. **Failure visibility:** distinguish relational occurrence from responsible admissibility and from outcome quality.
5. **Downstream connection:** give 2.1.7 a non-circular account of the process that may contribute to Amplified Intelligence and give 2.1.8 a process distinct from reasoning, answer, and value.

SymK does not need a complete sociology, ethics, political theory, economics of cooperation, or universal theory of collective action. It needs a bounded relation concept that survives its value chain, epistemic constraints, derived-project diversity, and responsibility boundary.

## 4. The three-level boundary

### 4.1 Actual cooperative occurrence

An actual cooperative occurrence is the relation or process that obtains when the relational minimum is met. It is not created by a label, agreement, contract, role assignment, system design, policy, or stored record.

### 4.2 Governed cooperation attribution and assessment

A Cooperation attribution is a fallible claim that identified participants cooperated in a stated undertaking, Context, Scope, and period. Its assessment tests the relevant conditions and evidence. A governance status may then accept, challenge, restrict, or reject that attribution for a purpose. None of these creates the underlying occurrence.

### 4.3 Responsible or authorized SymK use

An actual cooperative relation may still be irresponsible, exploitative, deceptive, harmful, or inappropriate for reliance. Treating it as responsible or authorized cooperation requires additional governance concerning participation constraints, affected parties, foreseeable consequences, challenge, correction, and revision. The complete responsibility and authority semantics remain for 2.1.6.

This is one governed concept with distinct occurrence, attribution, assessment, and qualification levels. The analysis does not create two unrelated concepts named “cooperation.”

## 5. Candidate minimum relational nucleus

### 5.1 Plural distinguishable participation

At least two participant loci must remain distinguishable in the relation. The participants need not be equal, human, individually intelligent in the same way, or independent outside the undertaking. Their identities and boundaries must nevertheless be sufficiently stable to identify contributions and relations.

Whether an artificial component, institution, collective, distributed arrangement, or hybrid qualifies as a participant rather than a resource, actor, interface, or Bearer remains for Stream B.

### 5.2 Bounded shared undertaking

The contributions must be directed toward a common bounded undertaking: a question, inquiry, product, maintenance activity, decision preparation, practice, protective action, or other joint object of work.

“Shared” does not require identical ultimate objectives, beliefs, interests, motives, interpretations, or expected benefits. Participants may contribute for different reasons. The necessary minimum is enough common practical orientation to identify what their contributions jointly concern.

### 5.3 Material contributions

At least two participants must contribute something materially relevant to the undertaking. Contribution may include inquiry, evidence, interpretation, critique, design, execution, restraint, access, care, monitoring, correction, authorization where legitimate, or another undertaking-relevant act or capacity.

Mere presence, membership, observation, exposure, or being affected is not contribution. Unequal contribution is compatible with cooperation. Whether a supposed contribution is material depends on the undertaking and evidence, not prestige, volume, or visibility.

### 5.4 Mutual orientation

The contributions must be made, selected, accepted, or governed with reference to the shared undertaking and at least one other contribution or participant relation. Direct dialogue is not required; orientation may be mediated, asynchronous, institutional, or technically coordinated. Pure coincidence is insufficient.

This condition is stronger than post hoc aggregation but does not require identical awareness or one theory of intention for all possible participant kinds. Stream B must test how orientation can be attributed to artificial, collective, institutional, and hybrid participants without anthropomorphic transfer.

### 5.5 Relational connection without fusion

The contributions must be sufficiently connected that each enters the organization of the shared undertaking rather than remaining an unrelated input placed in the same container. The connection may involve response, division of work, complementarity, mutual constraint, handoff, integration, correction, or governed composition.

Strong two-way dependence is not always necessary. A contribution can remain valuable and independently intelligible. What is necessary is a typed relational connection that explains why the plurality forms a cooperative process rather than an accidental collection.

### 5.6 Difference remains visible

Cooperation composes contributions; it does not erase their sources, roles, disagreements, limitations, or dependencies. Fusion, assimilation, and unattributed aggregation destroy information needed to assess the relation and do not supply a stronger form of cooperation merely by appearing unified.

## 6. Core conditions versus governed qualification

The candidate nucleus identifies the relation. SymK-governed qualification adds consequence-proportional obligations. A material attribution should preserve:

- participant identities and the boundary of the proposed cooperative unit;
- the undertaking, Domain, Context, Scope, time, and continuation conditions;
- contributions, dependencies, constraints, and relevant asymmetries;
- the basis on which participation occurs, including material inability to refuse or withdraw where relevant;
- disagreement, challenge paths, correction, repair, and unresolved dissent;
- affected parties, benefits, burdens, risks, and observed consequences;
- assessment criteria, evidence, uncertainty, alternatives, assessor, and authority;
- outcome and value evaluation separately from cooperation status;
- revision, restriction, breakdown, termination, and historical lineage.

These obligations do not require every low-consequence episode to materialize the same bureaucracy. The depth of representation and review should be proportionate to persistence, dependency, uncertainty, power, reversibility, and consequence. Exact profiles remain for later package and formalization work.

| Bounded shared undertaking | Necessary | Without an identifiable common object of work, coexistence or coincident activity is a better description |
| Material contribution by more than one participant | Necessary | One active party plus passive presence does not establish joint participation |
| Mutual orientation of contributions | Necessary, with bearer-specific evidence unresolved | Prevents accidental complementarity and post hoc aggregation from becoming cooperation |
| Relational connection or integration | Necessary in a weak typed sense | Contributions must form part of one undertaking; strong mutual dependence is not required |
| Identical or fully shared objectives | Not necessary | Participants may have different reasons and interests while sharing a bounded undertaking |
| Mutual benefit | Neither necessary nor sufficient | Benefits may be unequal, deferred, uncertain, or partly external; colluders may benefit mutually while harming others |

| Meaningful ability to refuse or challenge | Not universal to every occurrence; normally required for strong responsible qualification when materially possible | Complete elimination of agency or challenge makes a cooperation claim suspect and may reveal compelled coordination instead; 2.1.6 must govern justified authority and duty |
| Equality of power, role, contribution, or benefit | Not necessary | Asymmetry may be justified and productive; concealed domination, exploitation, and unchallengeable dependency remain failure risks |

## 8. Adjacent-relation boundary

| Adjacent relation | Minimum meaning for this analysis | Relation to Cooperation | Boundary failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coordination | Alignment, ordering, or compatibility of activities | Often enables or realizes part of cooperation; may also organize coercion, competition, attack, or independent machinery | Synchronized activity is called cooperation without shared participant contribution or responsible qualification |
| Communication | Transmission or exchange of signs, content, signals, or states | May support mutual orientation; need not involve a shared undertaking, understanding, truth, or contribution | Message exchange or common vocabulary is treated as cooperation |
| Aggregation | Combination or collection of outputs, data, votes, scores, or resources | May be one composition mechanism; is cooperative only when contributing relations satisfy the nucleus | Independent outputs are combined after the fact and the aggregate is called cooperative intelligence |
| Compliance | Conformity with a rule, request, instruction, interface, or criterion | May be a contribution under legitimate governance; can also be compelled, strategic, mechanical, or unreflective | Obedience, validation success, policy conformance, or model alignment is treated as cooperation |
| Consensus | Agreement or convergence in judgment, belief, preference, or decision | Possible outcome or governance rule; neither necessary nor sufficient for cooperation or truth | Majority, confidence, or silence erases dissent and acquires cooperation or epistemic status |
| Competition | Structured pursuit of scarce advantage or comparative performance | Can coexist with cooperation when bounded and subordinated to a wider shared undertaking | Rivalry is assumed to destroy cooperation, or a contest is called cooperative solely because rules exist |
| Coercion | Conduct shaped by threat, force, severe dependency, or removal of meaningful alternatives | Material coercion may degrade or defeat participant status and responsible qualification; not every obligation, constraint, or exercise of authority is thereby coercion | Credibly threatened compliance is sanitized as cooperation, or every legitimate constraint is mislabeled coercive without analysis |
| Collusion | Concealed or improper coordination among participants for advantage, often against affected parties or governing rules | May satisfy the thin cooperative occurrence nucleus; fails responsible SymK qualification where undertaking, means, concealment, or consequences are illegitimate | Defining collusion away hides real cooperative capacity used harmfully; accepting mutual benefit sanitizes it |
| Exchange | Reciprocal transfer of resources, services, rights, or information | May be cooperative, competitive, transactional, or coerced depending on the relation | Any completed transaction is treated as a cooperative relationship |
| Alliance | Declared alignment among parties for a purpose or against a threat | Supplies a possible governance arrangement, not proof of actual cooperation | Formal membership or treaty substitutes for contributions and occurrence |
| Coexistence | Presence within the same environment, organization, market, or system | Necessary background in many cases but no shared undertaking is required | Proximity, platform use, or organizational membership becomes cooperation |

### 8.1 Coordination is the closest but not the governing synonym

Coordination concerns how activities fit. Cooperation additionally concerns who participates, what undertaking the contributions jointly serve, and how the plurality forms a relation. A traffic-control system coordinates drivers who do not share an undertaking beyond local rule compliance. Attackers can coordinate effectively. Software components can coordinate by protocol. None of these facts alone settles cooperation.

### 8.2 Consensus is neither the method nor the result required

Cooperation can use dissent, adversarial review, minority reports, competing models, and role-separated judgment. Consensus may emerge, but it cannot replace grounds, attribution, authority, or outcome evaluation. A dissent-suppressing process may be coordinated and consensual while failing responsible cooperation.

### 8.3 Competition and conflict can be internal to cooperation

Participants can contest hypotheses, designs, interpretations, or performance while continuing to contribute to a wider undertaking. Compatibility depends on whether the contest remains bounded, whether contributions still serve the shared undertaking, whether affected parties and rules are governed, and whether the relation remains open to challenge and repair.

### 8.4 Collusion must remain visible as a hard case

If Cooperation were defined as responsible or socially beneficial by definition, collusion would disappear from the category before SymK could explain how cooperative capacity can be directed toward harm. Stream A therefore treats collusion as potentially satisfying the thin occurrence nucleus while failing responsible admissibility. This is not approval; it is the condition for accurate diagnosis.

## 9. Attribution, assessment, status, and record

The DR-011/DR-012 separation applies to Cooperation:

| Level | Question | Prohibited substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Actual cooperation | Did the relational process obtain? | A contract, role, policy, interface, label, or record creates the relation |
| Cooperation attribution | Who claims which participants cooperated in what undertaking and Scope? | The claim proves its own content |
| Cooperation assessment | What evidence and criteria support or defeat the attribution? | A score or reviewer creates occurrence |
| Governance qualification/status | Is the relation accepted, restricted, challenged, prohibited, or authorized for a stated use? | Accepted status makes the process responsible or successful in every respect |
| Outcome/value assessment | What answer, action, or consequence resulted, with what quality, cost, harm, and uncertainty? | Good outcome proves good process; bad outcome proves no cooperation |
| Cooperation record | What Representation preserves the claim, evidence, assessment, status, and history? | The stored record is the relation or a collective Bearer |

A future representation must not use one boolean such as `is_cooperative` to collapse these levels. No schema is selected here.

## 10. Candidate approaches considered

### A — Cooperation as SymK's objective or highest good

**Rejected as inconsistent with Stage-Accepted authority.** It reverses the Product Vision value chain, cannot explain harmful cooperation, and makes “more cooperation” appear automatically better.

### B — Cooperation as coordinated activity

**Rejected as insufficient.** It includes attack, coercive control, technical synchronization, and accidental alignment without identifying participant contribution or a shared undertaking.

### C — Cooperation as common belief, agreement, or consensus

**Rejected.** It excludes productive disagreement and admits manufactured consensus. It also conflicts with the accepted epistemic distinctions.

### D — Cooperation as voluntary mutual benefit

**Rejected as the universal nucleus.** It excludes some emergency, professional, care, public-duty, and asymmetrical relations; it admits collusion; and it leaves affected parties outside the frame. Consent and benefit remain material evidence and governance conditions.

### E — Cooperation as successful joint output

**Rejected.** It makes outcome create process, cannot recognize failed cooperation, and hides successful outputs produced through deception, coercion, or isolated work.

### F — Thin relational occurrence plus governed qualification and separate value assessment

**Retained as the Stream A working approach.** It identifies the process without moralizing occurrence, prevents collusion sanitization, preserves responsibility and consequence, and keeps the Product Vision chain intact.

## 11. Counterexample and compatibility tests

| Case | Result under the working approach |
|---|---|
| Efficient coordination used to dominate or exclude a third party | Coordination is present. Cooperation may occur among the immediate participants, but responsible qualification fails when affected-party harm, concealment, or illegitimate undertaking is established |
| Colluders share an objective and benefit mutually | The thin nucleus may be satisfied; mutual benefit is shown insufficient. The relation is disqualified from responsible SymK status without redefining the conduct away |
| Policy compliance follows credible threat with no practical refusal | Compliance and coordination are present. Cooperation is not inferred. Evidence of contribution, participation basis, agency, and challenge is required; complete compulsion may defeat participant status |
| Productive disagreement improves a shared undertaking | Cooperation remains possible and may be strengthened. Consensus is not necessary; the disagreement itself can be a material contribution |
| Material conflict occurs while work continues | Conflict does not automatically terminate cooperation. The test is whether mutual orientation and contribution to the undertaking persist and whether repair remains possible |
| Consensus is produced by suppressed dissent or agenda control | Consensus does not establish cooperation. Responsible qualification fails where challenge capacity and material alternatives were suppressed |
| A one-time exchange succeeds | An episode may qualify if the relational nucleus is met. No persistent cooperative relationship, Education, or habit follows automatically |
| Repeated interaction creates dependency but no shared undertaking | Persistence and dependency are insufficient. The relation may be service use, control, extraction, or coexistence rather than cooperation |
| Competition is structured to serve a wider inquiry | Local competition is compatible when bounded by, and materially contributing to, the wider cooperative undertaking |
| Contributions or benefits are unequal | Inequality alone does not defeat cooperation. Materiality, visibility, participation basis, domination risk, and consequences must be assessed |
| A successful answer arises from a non-cooperative process | The answer may be correct and valuable; cooperation is absent or unestablished. Output quality does not backfill the process |
| A cooperative process produces a false or harmful answer | Cooperation may have occurred; epistemic and value assessments fail. Cooperation creates no truth or responsible reliance |
| A highly Intelligent participant is deceptive and uncorrectable | Intelligence does not entail cooperative fitness. Deception and inability to challenge or correct may defeat responsible qualification and perhaps mutual orientation evidence |
| Independent analyses are aggregated without awareness of one another | Aggregation and possible division of work exist; cooperation is not established until participant orientation and relational organization are evidenced |
| Two software components exchange messages by protocol | Communication and coordination are established. Whether either is a participant, or both are resources within another cooperative system, remains for Stream B |
| A clear narrative says participants cooperate but the implementation erases dissent and source identity | Human–Engineering view mismatch defeats the governed attribution until the loss is exposed and corrected |
| A scientifically measured output gain is present but participants report coercion and hidden burden | Output evidence cannot settle relational admissibility; Human, Scientific, and Engineering evidence must be reconciled and affected-party consequences retained |

The candidate survives these Stream A cases better than the alternatives. The result is analytical compatibility, not empirical validation or acceptance.

## 12. Three-view preservation obligations

### 12.1 Human View

The Human View should preserve:

- what the undertaking means to participants and affected parties;
- how participants, contributions, differences, conflicts, and dependencies are understood;
- participation basis, power, challenge, repair, and alternative interpretations;
- why the process is or is not being called cooperative;
- consequences, uncertainty, dissent, and known limits.

### 12.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View should preserve, where material:

- observable indicators and competing explanations for contribution, orientation, dependency, conflict, and outcome;
- methods for distinguishing cooperation from coordination, aggregation, compliance, or coincidence;
- studied population or system boundary, instruments, measurements, uncertainty, and counterevidence;
- conditions under which the attribution would be defeated.

No single behavioral metric or output gain can define Cooperation universally.

### 12.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View should eventually preserve:

- identities and boundaries of represented participants, undertakings, contributions, relations, and affected parties;
- Context, Scope, time, dependencies, constraints, challenges, assessments, status, and lineage;
- separation of occurrence claim, qualification, outcome, and record;
- declared Projection loss when operational systems omit dissent, asymmetry, participation constraints, or consequence evidence.

This stream selects no vocabulary, schema, class hierarchy, relation cardinality, assessment calculus, workflow, or runtime form.

### 12.4 Alignment rule

A clear Human narrative, empirical indicator, or valid implementation cannot compensate automatically for material failure in another view. If the Human View reports coercion, the Scientific View measures only output, and the Engineering View stores one `cooperative=true` field, the views are not aligned and the status must remain challenged or qualified.

## 14. Consequences for governing artifacts

### Product Vision

The accepted value chain remains coherent if Cooperation is treated as a relational process distinct from medium, capability, reasoning, answer, value, action, and consequence. The vision's affected-party and failure clauses supply qualification constraints; they do not make every cooperative occurrence good by definition.

### Candidate A0

A0 cannot retain “cooperation is the objective” as governing language. Its working definition—“coordinated participation in which difference can contribute to a shared undertaking under governed relations”—contains useful elements but does not sufficiently distinguish occurrence, responsible qualification, and outcome. Its categorical statement that domination, deception, or collusion “fails cooperation” should be reconsidered: such conduct may demonstrate real cooperative coordination among immediate participants while failing SymK responsibility and admissibility. Defining the conduct away weakens diagnosis.

This is a revision-ledger finding for 2.2, not a revision or disposition of A0.

### Candidate A3 and Education Foundation Paper

The claim that cooperation requires formation cannot be evaluated until persistent relations, participants, learning, Education, Training, and formative effects are tested in Streams B–E. Stream A establishes only that Intelligence and Knowledge do not entail cooperation and that one episode does not imply stable formation.

The paper's “cooperation is the objective” wording conflicts with Product Vision v0.3 and must be corrected in the future v0.2 revision. Its strong claims that every architecture educates and stable cooperation depends on Education remain hypotheses, not Stream A conclusions.

### Legacy Human/AI role axiom

The historical artifact supplies examples of differentiated contributions, dialogue, critique, and feedback. It cannot govern participant kinds, fixed roles, mutual accountability, or the cooperation definition. Stream B must test Human/AI and broader participant arrangements under DR-011 without origin privilege.

### DR-011 and DR-012

The candidate preserves their non-entailments. Cooperation does not create a collective Bearer, Knowledge, Intelligence, authority, or status. Cooperative records are Representations. Consequence, challenge, disagreement, and revision remain lifecycle inputs rather than proof of successful cooperation.

### 2.1.6, 2.1.7, and 2.1.8

- 2.1.6 receives the exposed interfaces for participation basis, coercion, justified authority, duty, agency, responsibility, accountability, liability, and affected-party standing.
- 2.1.7 must not define Amplified Intelligence as cooperation, more participants, more content, or one successful output. It must test changes in the integrated capability configuration.

### Derived projects

A future compatibility claim may need to state the relevant undertaking, participant and contribution boundaries, adjacent relations, participation constraints, affected parties, challenge path, consequences, and projection loss. ## 17. Confidence and reopening conditions

**Confidence: High** that Cooperation must remain distinct from the final objective, coordination, consensus, compliance, outcome, and participant trait; that a shared undertaking is weaker than identical objectives; that mutual benefit is insufficient; and that occurrence, qualification, and value assessment must remain separate.

**Confidence: Medium** in the exact thin nucleus, especially the formulation of mutual orientation, the threshold above aggregation, and the treatment of collusion as an actual but irresponsibly directed cooperative relation.

**Confidence: Low to Medium** on bearer-specific awareness, internal cooperation, compelled participation, and one-shot terminology. These require Streams B and D.

The working approach must be reopened if later analysis shows that:

- the occurrence/qualification separation systematically legitimizes harmful relations rather than exposing them;
- the nucleus cannot distinguish aggregation or coordination without anthropomorphic intention;
- participant and bearer analysis makes plural participation incoherent;
- responsibility analysis shows that agency or consent is constitutive rather than qualificatory in the relevant SymK sense;
- formation analysis requires a different temporal unit;
- three-view representation cannot preserve the distinctions without disproportionate burden;
- derived-project grounding reveals systematic false positives or exclusions;
- or the candidate breaks the Product Vision value chain or accepted DR-009 through DR-012 boundaries.
