## 1. Question and analytical result

> What minimum boundary must SymK establish among response, output, answer, interpretation, explanation, recommendation, proposal, decision support, plan, instruction, decision, adoption, approval, execution, action, record, outcome, and consequence before it evaluates answer quality?

Stream C supports the following working answer:

> **An answer is a scoped and challengeable content-to-question relation in which identified semantic or practical content is presented as satisfying all or a declared part of the answer obligation of an identified governed question version. A material answer claim preserves question correspondence, content identity and version, Domain, Scope, Context, time, audience, answer form and kind, conditions, assumptions, uncertainty, grounds, limitations, residual obligations, source and contributors, assessment, status, adoption, use, revision, and challenge. An answer may be wrong, incomplete, conditional, plural, contested, superseded, unactionable, or unused; it is not established merely by an output, response, true statement, reasoning product, explanation, recommendation, authorized decision, action, favorable outcome, or record.**

> **A response is a communicative or operational occurrence made in relation to a request, prompt, event, or question. An output is an artifact, state, signal, or event produced by a process or system. Either may carry answer content, but neither is an answer unless the governed question relation is identified. The same content may answer one question, fail to answer another, and support several separately governed answer occurrences.**

> **A recommendation is advice favoring a course, option, or judgment under stated criteria, grounds, uncertainty, and conditions. Decision support is a contribution intended to inform a decision; a plan organizes proposed future actions or states; a decision is a selection or commitment that governs a subsequent state under an actual authority relation; execution or action is conduct that occurs; and outcome or consequence is a resulting state or effect. These relations may form a lineage, but none creates, transfers, validates, or substitutes for another automatically.**

This working result keeps at least seven states separate:

1. **answer content:** the semantic or practical material presented;
2. **answer occurrence:** a bounded event or state in which that content is offered in relation to a governed question;
3. **answer Representation:** text, speech, diagram, data, code, demonstration, model, form, or other expression of the content;
4. **answer claim:** an assertion that identified content answers all or part of an identified question;
5. **answer assessment:** evidence-bearing evaluation of correspondence, correctness, completeness, support, applicability, usefulness, or another declared dimension;
6. **answer status, adoption, authorization, reliance, and use:** governed relations that do not create content truth or quality; and
7. **answer record and lineage:** durable Representation of versions, sources, assessments, disputes, uses, consequences, revisions, and supersession.

The answer is relational rather than a self-sufficient text or object. Identical wording can be a correct answer to one question, a partial answer to another, an irrelevant true statement to a third, and a prohibited disclosure in a fourth Context. Different Representations can preserve one answer content, while a small change in assumptions, modality, population, time, or requested form can create a materially different answer.

No concept disposition is accepted here. Stream D must analyze answer quality, epistemic adequacy, practical usefulness, and responsible-answer profiles without treating answer occurrence, acceptance, adoption, Authority, or outcome as quality.

## 3. Why SymK needs this boundary

Without the boundary, the following incompatible states can all be described as an “answer”:

- a system emits a completion unrelated to the governed question;
- a retrieval component returns a relevant source without identifying which passage or claim addresses the question;
- a professional gives a true but nonresponsive statement;
- a report answers one subquestion while omitting the interaction that defines the parent question;
- a model presents a recommendation when an explanation was requested;
- a generated plan is treated as an authorized decision;
- a score automatically triggers a workflow and is called the decision-maker;
- a Human clicks approval without reading or understanding the output and is called the author of the answer;
- a committee adopts a position, making it operative but not true;
- an executor performs an instruction without owning the earlier recommendation or decision;
- a favorable outcome makes a poor answer appear retrospectively correct; or
- an earlier answer is overwritten after correction, erasing who relied on it and what consequences followed.

The opposite error is also dangerous: defining answer so strongly that only true, complete, justified, authorized, useful, or successful content counts. That would make wrong, partial, contested, superseded, and harmful answers impossible to govern precisely. SymK needs to record that an answer was given before it determines whether the answer was good.

The minimum must also accommodate non-propositional answer content. A demonstration, design, procedure, map, dataset, code artifact, model, proof, diagnosis, plan, or enacted performance may respond to a question. Its answer relation still differs from its Representation, execution, outcome, and Evidence.

## 5. Working answer anchor

### 5.1 Minimum positive conditions

A material answer claim should identify, as applicable:

- the governed **question Identity and version**;
- the **answer obligation** or declared part addressed;
- the **answer content identity and version**;
- the **answer occurrence**, including source, time, and Context;
- the **Domain, Scope, audience, purpose, intended use, and affected horizon**;
- the **answer kind and form**, without treating a taxonomy as closed;
- the **correspondence claim** connecting content to question;
- the **coverage**, including parts answered, omitted, deferred, reframed, or left unresolved;
- the **conditions, assumptions, definitions, constraints, exceptions, and dependencies**;
- the **grounds, Evidence, reasoning products, methods, sources, and their versions**;
- the **uncertainty, alternatives, disagreement, defeaters, and confidence qualifications**;
- the **authors, Contributors, responders, tools, providers, and organized arrangement boundaries**;
- the **Representation or Projection** and declared transformation or loss;
- the **assessment dimensions, assessor, authority, result, and limitations**;
- the **governance status, adoption, Authorization, reliance, and use** as separate relations;
- the **privacy, confidentiality, privilege, security, access, and disclosure conditions**;
- the **challenge, correction, revision, supersession, expiry, and reopening paths**; and
- the **decision, action, outcome, consequence, feedback, and Learning links** without property transfer.

These are semantic preservation questions, not a mandatory maximal record for every answer. Detail and assurance remain consequence-proportional.

### 5.2 Minimal correspondence without quality inflation

An answer occurrence requires identifiable content presented in relation to an identifiable question obligation. It need not be true, correct, complete, supported, useful, authorized, adopted, or successful. However, arbitrary unrelated output does not become an answer merely because a source labels it one.

The minimal correspondence threshold is intentionally thin: the content must purport in a semantically inspectable way to address the question or a declared part. Stream D owns the stronger relevance, correctness, completeness, evidential, practical, and responsible-quality tests.

### 5.3 Answer as relation, not intrinsic object type

The same content can answer multiple questions under separate relation claims. A stored statement can cease to be current without losing its historical answer role. A Representation can change while content is preserved. An answer can be offered by a source that did not author its underlying content or perform its supporting reasoning.

“Answer” therefore behaves provisionally as a governed relational concept with occurrence, content, Representation, claim, assessment, status, and lineage layers rather than as a permanent file or message type.

## 7. Response, reply, acknowledgment, and output

### 7.1 Response

A response is an occurrence related to a stimulus, request, prompt, question, event, or prior communication. It may be:

- an acknowledgment;
- clarification request;
- refusal or decline;
- error, timeout, or status message;
- retrieved content;
- direct answer;
- partial or conditional answer;
- recommendation or proposal;
- question, counterquestion, or challenge;
- instruction or action; or
- silence represented by a governed timeout or non-response state.

The fact that a response occurred says nothing yet about answer correspondence or quality.

### 7.2 Reply

A reply is a communicative response linked to an earlier communication. It may answer, acknowledge, contest, defer, redirect, or ignore the question. Thread or turn adjacency does not create semantic answer correspondence.

### 7.3 Acknowledgment and receipt

Acknowledgment establishes, at most, that a message, request, or artifact was received or recognized under declared conditions. It does not establish understanding, agreement, answer, acceptance, adoption, commitment, or action.

### 7.4 Output

An output is whatever a declared process or system produces at its boundary: text, score, file, signal, state change, alert, command, action request, model, or other artifact/event. Output Identity depends on the process boundary and version. A component output may become another component's input; neither role establishes an answer.

## 8. Answer content, Representation, and occurrence

### 8.1 Content

Answer content may include propositions, classifications, estimates, predictions, interpretations, diagnoses, explanations, designs, procedures, demonstrations, models, plans, code, or combinations. Not all content is truth-apt in the same way. A plan may be feasible or responsible rather than simply true; a design may satisfy constraints; a demonstration may manifest competence.

### 8.2 Representation

Answer content may be represented in language, mathematics, logic, data, diagrams, audiovisual media, code, physical demonstrations, simulations, or executable artifacts. Representation form affects accessibility, inspectability, precision, ambiguity, security, and use, but does not create content quality.

### 8.3 Occurrence

An answer occurrence binds content to a question version, source, time, Context, and presentation event or governed state. The content can preexist the occurrence. A retrieved passage may become an answer occurrence when presented against the question with a correspondence claim; the original author need not be the current responder.

### 8.4 Non-linguistic and performative cases

An action or demonstration may be answer content when the question asks “Can this be done?”, “How is this performed?”, or requests a demonstrative response. The same occurrence may therefore be both action and answer Representation under separate claims. The answer relation does not absorb the action's agency, authorization, risk, or consequence.

## 9. Question correspondence and answer obligation

### 9.1 Correspondence is version-specific

Answer correspondence is evaluated against the governed question version, not merely the latest wording, a hidden expected answer, a benchmark label, or the source's intention. Material changes in assumption, Scope, population, time, modality, affected horizon, or requested form may change what counts as addressing the question.

### 9.2 Topic overlap is insufficient

Content can be about the same topic and still fail to answer. A statement may be true, well supported, and useful while addressing a different descriptive, causal, normative, design, or decision obligation.

### 9.3 Presupposition and malformed-question handling

When a question contains a false, contested, harmful, incoherent, unauthorized, or underspecified presupposition, a direct answer may reinforce the defect. A governed response may:

- challenge or correct the presupposition;
- distinguish conditional answers;
- request clarification;
- reframe with lineage;
- provide a qualified no-answer; or
- answer a higher-order question while explicitly leaving the source question unanswered.

Reframing is not silent answering. The source and successor question relations remain explicit.

### 9.4 Rubrics and expected answers

A rubric, test key, benchmark target, official position, or expected response is a Representation of assessment or governance expectations. Matching it does not establish Domain truth, question adequacy, reasoning quality, or responsible use. Divergence does not establish non-answer without a semantic correspondence assessment.

## 10. Answer Identity, version, and lineage

### 10.1 Identity conditions

Material answer Identity depends on a combination of:

- governed question version;
- content and answering commitment;
- Domain, Scope, Context, audience, and time;
- conditions, assumptions, modality, and uncertainty;
- coverage and residual obligation;
- source or answer occurrence where provenance matters; and
- relation to prior answer versions.

Identical content applied to distinct questions creates distinct answer relations. Distinct wording or Representation may preserve one answer when semantic content and material qualifications remain stable.

### 10.2 Revision and successor

A change may be:

- presentational re-expression;
- clarification;
- correction of error;
- addition of Evidence or explanation;
- restriction or expansion of Scope;
- changed condition or assumption;
- increased or decreased uncertainty;
- changed recommendation, plan, or conclusion;
- partial replacement;
- full supersession; or
- withdrawal.

Whether Identity is preserved is evidence-bearing and Domain-sensitive. Material semantic change should not be hidden as formatting or routine update.

### 10.3 No overwrite

Correction and supersession do not erase the historical answer occurrence. The record should preserve what was offered, its status at the time, who relied on it, decisions or actions linked to it, consequences observed, challenges raised, and why it changed.

### 10.4 Fork, merge, and plural lineage

Answers may fork under competing assumptions or Views, merge through synthesis, and remain partially incompatible. A later synthesis does not retroactively make every earlier branch wrong or adopted. Lineage should support multiple parents, disputes, and unresolved residuals where material.

## 11. Answer source, authorship, contribution, and attribution

### 11.1 Source is not one universal author

The visible responder may retrieve, translate, summarize, generate, synthesize, sign, or merely transmit content. Material attribution should distinguish:

- question asker or commissioning source;
- answer author or originator;
- source of underlying claims or data;
- reasoning Contributor;
- retriever, translator, editor, summarizer, or generator;
- reviewer, challenger, and assessor;
- presenter or responder;
- system, provider, owner, deployer, and operator; and
- adopting, relying, deciding, executing, and affected subjects.

### 11.2 No source-origin privilege

Human, artificial, institutional, collective, or hybrid origin does not grant or deny answer status or quality. Origin affects evidence, capability, responsibility, provenance, legal, and governance questions that must be assessed explicitly.

### 11.3 Lowest adequate attribution

Attribute answer authorship or provision at the lowest organized level that explains the claimed content production or presentation without excluding constitutive integration. Multiple levels may receive separate attribution claims, but authorship, reasoning, approval, Authority, Responsibility, and ownership do not transfer among them automatically.

### 11.4 Imported and reused answers

Reusing prior content creates a new answer occurrence when it is presented against a current question. The current source should preserve prior provenance and evaluate current applicability. Reuse does not establish authorship, understanding, current review, or permission by itself.

## 12. Answer kinds and forms

Possible answer kinds include:

- factual or descriptive;
- identificatory or classificatory;
- explanatory or causal;
- predictive, probabilistic, or forecasting;
- interpretive or meaning-oriented;
- diagnostic;
- evaluative or comparative;
- normative, legal, ethical, or policy-oriented;
- design or specification;
- procedural or demonstrative;
- planning or strategic;
- recommendation or decision support;
- counterfactual or scenario-based;
- conditional or assumption-indexed;
- plural, contested, or alternative-set;
- negative answer;
- qualified no-answer, suspension, or abstention; and
- reframing or clarification response.

This is an open map, not a universal taxonomy. A single answer can occupy several kinds, and different Domains impose different truth conditions, evidence standards, forms, and authority rules.

An answer kind must not be inferred from surface grammar. “You should deploy X” may be a recommendation, quoted policy, prediction of advice, instruction, or description of another source's judgment depending on Context.

## 13. Partial, composite, plural, and conditional answers

### 13.1 Partial answer

A partial answer addresses a declared portion of the answer obligation while leaving a residual. It should identify:

- the covered subquestion or dimension;
- the omitted or unresolved remainder;
- dependencies and interactions with the remainder;
- whether the partial result is usable independently;
- uncertainty and risk of premature use; and
- what would complete, restrict, or supersede it.

Partial does not mean poor by definition. Concealed partiality is the failure.

### 13.2 Composite answer

A composite answer integrates multiple contents, sources, Views, subanswers, or modalities. Its adequacy depends not only on each component but also on dependency, consistency, interaction, recomposition, and residual whole-question obligations.

### 13.3 Plural and incompatible answers

Several answers may remain live under different assumptions, interpretations, models, authorities, value premises, or unresolved Evidence. SymK should preserve their conditions and conflict rather than force one status through formatting or majority. Plurality does not make all answers equally supported.

### 13.4 Conditional answer

A conditional answer explicitly binds content to conditions or assumptions. Removing the condition creates a stronger and potentially unsupported answer. The condition must remain visible in summaries, dashboards, decisions, and reused representations.

### 13.5 Interval, range, distribution, and scenario answers

For uncertain questions, an interval, distribution, scenario set, sensitivity map, or ranked alternatives may answer more responsibly than a point estimate. These forms do not eliminate uncertainty; they represent it under declared models and assumptions.

## 14. Negative answer, no-answer, abstention, refusal, and silence

### 14.1 Negative answer

“No,” “none,” “the event did not occur,” or another negative proposition can be a substantive answer when it addresses the question and is supported under the relevant conditions. It is not the same as providing no answer.

### 14.2 Qualified no-answer

A qualified no-answer states that the source cannot responsibly supply the requested answer under current conditions and identifies why, such as:

- insufficient or conflicting Evidence;
- underdetermination;
- malformed or ambiguous question;
- unavailable source, capability, time, or resource;
- absent Authority or Authorization;
- privacy, privilege, security, confidentiality, or legal restriction;
- unacceptable risk or affected-subject harm;
- unresolved dependency or model failure; or
- answerability limits in principle or current practice.

It may satisfy a governed answer obligation that permits explicit epistemic or governance limitation. It does not become a substantive answer to the original content question unless the question asks whether an answer is available.

### 14.3 Abstention

Abstention is a decision or response not to provide, select, or act on a candidate answer under stated conditions. It can be reasoned and responsible without being a substantive answer. Its source, reason, Authority, Scope, duration, and challenge remain explicit.

### 14.4 Refusal and decline

A refusal declines a request or question. It may be authorized, unauthorized, justified, evasive, protective, or harmful. It is a response and governance occurrence, not automatically an answer or evidence of inability.

### 14.5 Silence and non-response

Silence may result from absence, failure, timeout, strategy, refusal, suppression, incapacity, or no detected event. No meaning should be inferred without a governed convention and Evidence. A default rule may assign operational effect to silence, but it does not create agreement, understanding, truth, or answer content.

## 15. Interpretation and explanation as answer relations

### 15.1 Interpretation

An interpretation assigns, clarifies, or argues for meaning under a context, method, tradition, or authority. It can answer “What does this mean?” but remains distinct from the source text, actual meaning, authoritative legal effect, explanation, and application.

Multiple interpretations may remain reasonable or jurisdictionally distinct. An official interpretation may govern a use without becoming universally true.

### 15.2 Explanation

An explanation can answer a why/how question when its object, explanandum, explanatory relation, audience, Scope, and purpose correspond. Stream B still governs the difference among causal explanation, justification, process account, decision rationale, and explanation-shaped output.

An explanation that answers a question does not become Evidence that its described reasoning caused the result. An answer may be well supported but poorly explained; a clear explanation may support a false answer.

### 15.3 Summary and translation

A summary or translation may carry answer content if it preserves material correspondence. Compression, audience adaptation, language change, and format transformation can omit qualifications or alter commitments. The source content, transformation, loss, and responsibility for review remain visible.

## 16. Recommendation, proposal, and decision support

### 16.1 Recommendation

A recommendation is advice favoring a course, option, interpretation, or judgment under stated grounds, criteria, objectives, uncertainty, constraints, and affected consequences. A material recommendation should identify:

- recommending source and competence;
- recipient and decision context;
- options considered and excluded;
- criteria, values, Evidence, assumptions, and weights;
- expected benefits, costs, risks, harms, and uncertainty;
- conditions, dependencies, alternatives, reversibility, and expiry;
- Authority and Responsibility boundaries; and
- challenge, revision, and monitoring needs.

A recommendation can answer a normative or decision question, but recommendation and answer remain separate predicates. It does not create adoption, Authorization, decision, action, correctness, value, or Responsibility.

### 16.2 Proposal

A proposal presents a candidate for adoption, selection, action, agreement, or decision. It need not favor the candidate as advice and may be offered for testing or challenge. Proposal status does not establish recommendation, acceptance, feasibility, legality, or authority.

### 16.3 Decision support

Decision support includes information, analysis, Evidence, models, scenarios, forecasts, explanations, recommendations, comparisons, tools, or procedures intended to inform a decision. It may improve or impair decision conditions. Intended support does not establish actual use, causal influence, adequacy, or decision authority.

### 16.4 Score, ranking, alert, and classification

A score, ranking, alert, or classification may be an output and decision-support input. It is not automatically an answer, recommendation, or decision. Thresholds and workflow effects can make it causally decisive while leaving the formal decision role elsewhere; both the technical control and governance relation must be represented.

## 17. Plan, strategy, instruction, and commitment

### 17.1 Plan

A plan organizes proposed future actions or states through objectives, steps, dependencies, resources, roles, sequence, checkpoints, contingencies, uncertainty, and stopping or revision conditions. It may answer “How should we proceed?” without being adopted, authorized, feasible, executed, or successful.

### 17.2 Strategy

A strategy is a higher-order approach for pursuing objectives under uncertainty and interaction. It may generate or constrain plans. Strategy rhetoric does not establish coherent objectives, legitimate means, capability, authority, or benefit.

### 17.3 Instruction and directive

An instruction or directive communicates requested or purportedly required conduct. Its validity depends on source Authority, addressee, object, Scope, Context, time, conditions, communication, and applicable external constraints. A technically enforceable command may be unauthorized; an authorized instruction may remain unexecuted.

### 17.4 Commitment

A commitment is a relation through which a subject or governance arrangement binds itself, expresses intention, or creates an obligation under a governing source. It may be part of a decision but does not establish action, capability, success, truth, or complete Responsibility.

## 18. Decision, selection, adoption, approval, and Ratification

### 18.1 Decision occurrence

Under Stage-Accepted DR-014, a decision is a selection or commitment that governs a subsequent state. A material decision claim identifies:

- decision subject or constituted arrangement;
- authority source and kind;
- decision object, alternatives, and commitment;
- Domain, jurisdiction, Scope, Context, time, and affected horizon;
- procedure, participation, quorum, voting, review, or other conditions;
- Evidence, recommendations, answers, dissent, and uncertainty considered;
- retained, delegated, constrained, or absent Authority;
- communication, adoption, effective date, expiry, and challenge;
- execution and control pathways;
- Responsibility relations preserved separately; and
- record, status, correction, revocation, and lineage.

An actual selection can occur without valid Authority. A validly constituted body can abstain, defer, fail quorum, or issue a nonbinding output. “Decision” cannot be inferred from a label, final screen, signature, threshold crossing, model score, vote, or Human click alone.

### 18.2 Selection

Selection is choice among alternatives. It may be a computational operation, user preference, recommendation step, or decision component. It does not establish commitment, governance effect, Authority, or implementation.

### 18.3 Adoption

Adoption is a governance act making a claim, policy, artifact, standard, answer, plan, or position operative within a stated Scope. Adoption can govern organizational reliance while the adopted content remains uncertain, contested, or false. It does not establish universal force or actual conformance.

### 18.4 Approval

Approval is an Authority exercise expressing acceptance for a purpose or gate. It must identify what is approved, for which use, under which conditions, and by which authority kind. Approval does not establish independent review, truth, answer quality, implementation, or Responsibility transfer.

### 18.5 Ratification

Ratification is a constituted higher-order status act. It gives the specified normative or governance effect within its jurisdiction. It does not create truth, empirical success, external legal force, or implementation fact.

## 19. Execution, action, omission, outcome, and consequence

### 19.1 Execution

Execution is performance of an instruction, plan step, decision, operation, or committed course. It may be exact or deviate; authorized or unauthorized; successful or failed. Execution does not establish that the underlying answer, recommendation, plan, or decision was adequate.

### 19.2 Action

Action retains the DR-014 operational boundary: conduct attributable to an identified subject or organized arrangement under relevant agency and control conditions. An answer can guide action; an action can serve as answer Representation; neither relation absorbs the other.

### 19.3 Omission and non-action

Omission or non-action may result from decision, abstention, failure, incapacity, absence, delay, conflict, or no detected opportunity. It requires its own source, expectation, capacity, Authority, and causal analysis. “No action” does not establish “no answer” or “decision to do nothing.”

### 19.4 Outcome and consequence

An outcome is a resulting Domain state relative to an objective or process. A consequence is a broader resulting effect, including unintended, distributed, delayed, residual, or affected-subject effects. Their causal links to answers, decisions, or actions require separate Evidence.

Favorable outcome does not prove answer correctness because luck, external intervention, resilience, or an unmodeled cause may dominate. Harmful consequence does not prove the answer content false because application, timing, execution, or changed Context may explain the effect.

### 19.5 Record

An action, decision, or outcome record is a Representation of selected events and relations. Technical logs, minutes, approvals, signatures, plans, and dashboards may support Evidence but do not create the occurrence or Authority they record.

## 20. Authority, Authorization, Responsibility, and affected subjects

### 20.1 Epistemic and governance roles

The following may belong to different subjects:

- question framing authority;
- answer authoring or provision;
- epistemic assessment;
- professional recommendation;
- Reliance Authorization;
- decision Authority;
- adoption or approval Authority;
- execution Authorization;
- actual agency and control;
- prospective and retrospective Responsibility;
- answerability and accountability;
- challenge, correction, repair, and remedy; and
- representation of affected subjects.

No final-answer, final-approval, or Human-in-the-loop shortcut may collapse them.

### 20.2 Reliance Authorization

Reliance Authorization permits specified use of content under declared conditions. It can be narrower or broader than adoption and may depend on answer assessment. It does not make content true, complete, or safe outside Scope.

### 20.3 Recommendation Authority and decision Authority

Competence or standing to recommend does not create authority to decide. Decision Authority does not establish epistemic competence. An authorized decision-maker may legitimately consider resources, rights, law, values, timing, or policy beyond the answer, while remaining accountable for how those constraints are represented.

### 20.4 Responsibility preservation

Answer authorship, recommendation, adoption, approval, final decision, execution, and outcome do not allocate Responsibility automatically. The DR-014 object-first, sourced, scoped, phased, typed, multi-level, non-additive model applies to the actual relations.

### 20.5 Affected subjects

Affected subjects may not ask the question, receive the answer, participate in the decision, use the system, or benefit from the result. They remain inside the consequence, challenge, correction, and response horizon where material. An answer cannot be called complete or responsibly usable merely because immediate users are satisfied; Stream D owns the quality effect of this condition.

## 21. Answer grounds, reasoning, Evidence, and explanation

### 21.1 Answer occurrence and support

Evidence that an answer occurred differs from Evidence supporting its content. A timestamped response proves presentation under some conditions; it does not establish truth. A strong derivation can support content without proving that the responder performed the reasoning. A true answer may have weak documented support.

### 21.2 Reasoning linkage

A material answer may link to:

- one or more reasoning episodes;
- imported reasoning products;
- direct observation or perception;
- testimony or authoritative record;
- retrieval of governed content;
- calculation, simulation, or experiment;
- professional judgment or demonstrated competence;
- decision or policy constraints; and
- unknown or unavailable process.

No universal rule requires every answer to arise from a new reasoning episode. The support relation and its Evidence must be stated.

### 21.3 Grounds and citations

Citations identify sources; they do not establish that sources support the claim, remain current, apply to the Scope, or were actually used. Grounds should preserve content, role, provenance, version, interpretation, uncertainty, contradiction, and applicability.

### 21.4 Explanation relation

An answer may include an explanation of its content, support, process, or implications. Each explanation has its own object and fidelity claim. Explanation quality does not establish answer correctness, and answer correctness does not guarantee explanatory adequacy.

### 21.5 Answer without recoverable reasoning

Some answers may be supported by direct competence, perception, protected expertise, or systems whose internal route is not faithfully recoverable. Consequence-proportional assurance may use independent Evidence, tests, review, comparison, and restriction. Missing verbatim process does not eliminate the answer occurrence; it affects support and authorized reliance.

## 22. Uncertainty, alternatives, challenge, and disagreement

### 22.1 Typed uncertainty

Material uncertainty may concern:

- question Identity or answer obligation;
- content truth, precision, completeness, or interpretation;
- source integrity, provenance, competence, or currency;
- Evidence, model, method, reasoning, or process attribution;
- applicability, transfer, population, time, or jurisdiction;
- recommendation criteria and weights;
- decision constraints and authority;
- execution, causal Contribution, outcome, or consequence;
- answer status, adoption, use, and reliance; or
- disagreement among assessors and Views.

One confidence number cannot represent all of these.

### 22.2 Alternatives

Alternatives may include different answers, explanations, interpretations, models, recommendations, plans, decisions, no-answer states, and question framings. Their existence should be declared when material; it does not require equal support or infinite search.

### 22.3 Contest and dissent

An answer can remain an answer while contested. Dissent may challenge correspondence, content, grounds, method, assumptions, authority, use, distribution, or consequence. Status should preserve the object and basis of disagreement rather than collapse it into one “disputed” flag.

### 22.4 Defeat and restriction

Counterevidence may rebut content; new information may undercut support; changed Context may defeat applicability; governance conflict may restrict reliance; harmful consequence may reopen quality or use. Defeat of one relation does not erase every other relation.

### 22.5 Challenge and response

Effective challenge requires an identifiable object, standing or access path, comprehensible reasons, Evidence access proportionate to the claim, protection against retaliation where relevant, competent response, correction route, and preserved lineage. Challenge does not guarantee reversal or establish the challenger's claim.

## 23. Answer lifecycle, status, adoption, use, and supersession

### 23.1 Lifecycle events

Potential events include:

- drafting and candidate formation;
- presentation or submission;
- clarification and supplementation;
- assessment and review;
- qualification or restriction;
- challenge and counteranswer;
- adoption, approval, Authorization, or rejection;
- communication, reliance, and use;
- decision and action linkage;
- monitoring and consequence observation;
- correction, revision, reaffirmation, or withdrawal;
- expiry and revalidation;
- supersession, fork, merge, or succession; and
- historical retention.

This is not a universal status vocabulary. An event may occur without an authorized status transition, and a recorded status may be wrong.

### 23.2 Adoption and use are distinct

An answer can be adopted but unused, used without formal adoption, relied on beyond Authorization, or rejected while independently acted upon. These relations require separate subjects, objects, Scope, time, and Evidence.

### 23.3 Supersession

Supersession makes another answer operative or preferred for a stated Scope. It does not prove the earlier answer was false in every Context. The superseding answer inherits no support, adoption, Responsibility, or outcome history automatically.

### 23.4 Expiry and revalidation

Time-sensitive answers may expire through changed Evidence, models, law, environment, population, dependency, or question. Expiry is not automatic falsity; it restricts current reliance. Revalidation must address the current question version and material conditions rather than only rerun an old computation.

## 24. Proportionality and answer assurance

The required answer detail, assessment, review, challenge, and lineage should increase with material combinations of:

- consequence, irreversibility, and affected-subject exposure;
- uncertainty, novelty, and Domain or model shift;
- disputed facts, interpretations, values, law, or authority;
- incomplete, conditional, plural, or non-propositional content;
- dependence on opaque, stochastic, distributed, or changing systems;
- difficulty of correction, repair, remedy, or recurrence prevention;
- power asymmetry, exclusion, and constrained challenge;
- use in recommendation, decision, action, or automated control;
- reuse across Contexts, populations, jurisdictions, or time; and
- disagreement among Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views.

Proportionality does not make low-consequence answers intrinsically less true. It changes the burden for governed reliance. A simple authoritative lookup may need compact provenance and currency checks. A high-consequence recommendation may need explicit alternatives, assumptions, uncertainty, affected consequences, independent review, Authority boundaries, and lifecycle monitoring.

More content is not automatically more assurance. Length can hide nonresponse; citations can conceal weak support; a polished explanation can create false confidence; a complete log can omit the semantic question relation. Assurance targets the material claim and failure mode.

| Approach | Strength | Failure |
|---|---|---|

## 29. Human, Scientific, and Engineering View obligations

### 29.1 Human View

The Human View should expose, where material:

- the question being answered in comprehensible form;
- direct answer content before supporting detail where appropriate;
- whether the answer is partial, conditional, plural, contested, or unavailable;
- assumptions, uncertainty, alternatives, exceptions, and limitations;
- source, contributors, assessor, Authority, and intended use;
- distinction among advice, decision, plan, instruction, and action;
- affected subjects and consequence horizon;
- current status, revision, expiry, and supersession; and
- accessible challenge, clarification, correction, and no-answer paths.

It must not let fluency, confidence, professional title, interface prominence, “final answer” wording, or approval status manufacture quality.

### 29.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View should expose, where material:

- question construct, population, variables, Scope, Context, and time;
- answer claim and truth or adequacy conditions;
- Evidence, methods, models, assumptions, uncertainty, alternatives, and defeaters;
- correspondence, coverage, precision, calibration, robustness, and transfer limits;
- competing answers and negative results;
- assessment design, assessor disagreement, and replication status;
- causal distinction among answer, decision, action, outcome, and consequence; and
- what would restrict, defeat, or reopen the answer.

It must not convert statistical significance, model fit, benchmark score, publication, expert consensus, or reproducibility into complete answer quality or responsible use.

### 29.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View should expose, where material:

- question, answer content, response, output, Representation, and version identifiers;
- source, component, tool, model, data, configuration, and code versions;
- correspondence, coverage, condition, uncertainty, and status fields without reification;
- traceable transformations, summaries, translations, redactions, and losses;
- recommendation, decision-support, decision, authorization, execution, and outcome events as separate objects;
- access, privacy, security, retention, expiry, correction, rollback, and supersession behavior;
- actual use, reliance, workflow effect, and causal limitations; and
- View mapping and unresolved mismatches.

It must not treat endpoint completion, successful serialization, schema validity, workflow status, UI labels, threshold crossing, audit log, or database adoption as answer correspondence or quality.

### 29.4 Cross-view alignment

The Views may use different forms and granularity but must preserve the same material question version, answer content, correspondence, coverage, conditions, uncertainty, source, assessment, status, use, decision/action links, and lineage. Every material transformation or omission must be declared.

A View disagreement is governable Evidence. It may reveal ambiguous content, invalid measurement, implementation drift, audience failure, hidden Authority, or legitimate Domain pluralism. It is not resolved automatically in favor of the most readable, formal, technical, authoritative, or operational View.

## 30. Working concept consequences

Stream C provisionally supports these consequences:

1. **Answer** remains an independently anchored relational candidate.
2. **answer content** is semantic or practical material, not necessarily one proposition.
3. **answer occurrence** binds content to a question version, source, time, and Context.
4. **answer Representation** carries content with possible transformation and loss.
5. **answer claim, assessment, status, adoption, reliance, and use** remain distinct.
6. **response, reply, acknowledgment, and output** are occurrence or operation-boundary families that may carry no answer.
7. **answer correspondence** is a supporting relation requiring question-version and obligation identity.
8. **partial, composite, plural, conditional, and scenario answers** preserve coverage and residual obligations.
9. **negative answer, qualified no-answer, abstention, refusal, and silence** remain non-substitutable.
10. **interpretation and explanation** may answer relevant question kinds while retaining their own predicates.
11. **recommendation** is advice, not decision or Authorization.
12. **proposal** is a candidate for adoption or selection, not acceptance.
13. **decision support** is intended contribution, not decision occurrence.
14. **plan and strategy** organize possible future conduct without establishing adoption or execution.
15. **instruction and commitment** require separate Authority, communication, and occurrence claims.
16. **decision, adoption, approval, and Ratification** retain DR-014 governance meanings.
17. **execution, action, omission, outcome, and consequence** remain separate from answer content and quality.
18. **Answer** provisionally passes DR-009 admission while its final Foundational-versus-compound disposition remains open.
19. **answer quality** remains for Stream D and cannot be inferred from occurrence or status.
20. **speed, cost, value, outcome, feedback, and Learning** remain for Streams E–F.

## 32. Deferred-question treatment and derived-project probes

### 32.1 DQ-014

Stream C adds a third comparison prerequisite to “How should answer quality, speed, and total cost be measured and traded off?”:

> Measurement must identify the governed question version and distinguish output, response, answer content, answer occurrence, Representation, correspondence, coverage, uncertainty, assessment, status, adoption, use, recommendation, decision, action, outcome, correction, and supersession. Comparisons require materially stable answer obligations or an explicit relation among changed obligations.

Therefore:

- output rate is not answer rate;
- response latency is not time to an answer with required correspondence and review;
- expected-answer or benchmark match is not Domain answer quality;
- partial or conditional answers cannot be compared as complete without adjustment;
- adoption and use do not establish quality;
- decision or action success does not validate the answer;
- corrected answers retain prior reliance and consequence cost; and
- no-answer, abstention, and clarification may be responsible results rather than zero-value outputs.

This is substantial non-final progress. No quality measure, speed definition, cost model, threshold, weighting, optimization, or trade-off is accepted.

### 32.2 LexBrain translation probe

A legal workflow may retrieve authority, interpret text, answer a doctrinal question, explain grounds, recommend a strategy, draft a filing, support a professional decision, and execute an authorized submission. Stream C requires:

- source retrieval not be confused with a case-specific answer;
- legal interpretation, factual answer, recommendation, and decision remain distinct;
- an authoritative source's legal effect not manufacture factual truth;
- a draft or proposed argument not become adopted client strategy;
- professional advice not become client or tribunal decision;
- filing execution not validate the answer; and
- later ruling or settlement not retroactively prove reasoning or answer quality.

No LexBrain artifact, legal conclusion, practice rule, or Authority allocation is changed.

### 32.3 IaaC translation probe

An infrastructure-as-code workflow may output a plan, answer a configuration question, recommend remediation, receive approval, apply changes, and observe runtime effects. Stream C requires:

- plan output remain distinct from answer content and actual state;
- validator success not become complete configuration answer;
- recommendation not become approval or decision;
- approval not establish effective review;
- apply execution not establish Authorization or intended effect;
- drift and external changes remain consequence and revalidation inputs; and
- rollback or successful deployment not erase the prior answer lineage.

### 32.4 sshConnectivity translation probe

A connectivity workflow may emit command output, answer a diagnostic question, recommend a change, receive permission, execute a command, and observe a successful connection. Stream C requires:

- command output remain observation rather than diagnosis by itself;
- likely-cause answer preserve alternatives and conditions;
- remediation recommendation remain separate from execution Authorization;
- a copied command not become an adopted plan automatically;
- successful connection not prove the diagnosis or eliminate intermittent causes; and
- harmful or security-relevant side effects remain in the consequence horizon.

No sshConnectivity schema, runtime, diagnosis, command, or deployment is changed.

## 35. Confidence and reopening conditions

**Confidence: High** that response, output, answer content, answer occurrence, Representation, claim, assessment, status, recommendation, decision, action, outcome, and record must not substitute for one another; and that answer occurrence must be governable before quality is known.

**Confidence: High** that truth, correct reasoning, fluency, Authority, adoption, approval, final decision, execution, or favorable outcome cannot individually establish answer correspondence or quality.

**Confidence: Medium–High** in the scoped content-to-question anchor, seven-state separation, twenty-five-part claim envelope, partial/plural/conditional/no-answer model, answer Identity/version/lineage, and DR-014 application.

**Confidence: Medium** that Answer will pass final Foundational admission; that minimal correspondence can remain thin without making every labeled output an answer; and that adjacent-family routing will survive quality, value, outcome, and feedback analysis.

**Confidence: Low to Medium** in universal answer-obligation structure, non-propositional and performative answer boundaries, exact answer-continuity criteria, institutional authorship, and the status of qualified no-answer as answer versus response.

The model must be reopened if later analysis shows that:

- minimal correspondence cannot distinguish a bad answer from arbitrary claimed output;
- Answer cannot be independently anchored without importing quality;
- answer obligation fails across major factual, interpretive, normative, design, and performative Domains;
- content-to-question relation excludes tacit, embodied, demonstrative, or non-propositional answers;
- answer Identity cannot survive ordinary translation, summarization, or audience adaptation;
- question-version dependence prevents legitimate reuse;
- partial, plural, conditional, or no-answer forms cannot share one universal minimum;
- qualified no-answer cannot be represented without conflating answerability and source question;
- source, authorship, contribution, response, and organized-arrangement attribution cannot be separated in practice;
- recommendation, proposal, decision support, plan, instruction, decision, or action proves independently Foundational;
- DR-014's accepted decision/action boundaries conflict with the answer relation;
- adoption or Reliance Authorization is constitutive of Answer rather than a separate governance relation;
- Stream D shows relevance or correctness must be part of answer Identity rather than quality;
- Stream E shows value, actionability, or outcome is constitutive of Answer;
- Stream F shows feedback or correction creates a different lifecycle structure;
- Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views cannot preserve one material answer across representations;
- protected, privileged, or restricted answers cannot remain challengeable;
- derived-project grounding yields systematic false answer/non-answer classifications;
- formalization cannot preserve correspondence, conditions, plurality, partiality, lineage, and loss; or
- the model conflicts with the Product Vision or Stage-Accepted DR-009 through DR-015.
