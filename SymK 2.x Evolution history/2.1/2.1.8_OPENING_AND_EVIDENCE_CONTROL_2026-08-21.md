## 7. Principal analytical hazards

- **Question–prompt collapse:** treating the submitted wording, prompt, ticket, or test item as the complete complex question.
- **Question drift:** changing Scope, assumptions, objective, or answer form while reporting continuity.
- **Reasoning–output collapse:** inferring a reasoning process from a fluent or correct result.
- **Trace reification:** treating a generated explanation, derivation, log, or chain as the actual causal or epistemic process.
- **Validity inflation:** extending formal validity into premise truth, real-world truth, relevance, warrant, or responsible action.
- **Retrieval or computation inflation:** calling access, search, calculation, or pattern completion reasoning without the claimed relations and Evidence.
- **Answer–response collapse:** treating any output as an answer to the governed question.
- **Correctness compression:** reducing heterogeneous answer-quality dimensions to one label or score.
- **Fluency and confidence substitution:** treating clarity, style, certainty, or assertiveness as epistemic quality.
- **Authority substitution:** treating expert, institutional, model, legal, or approval status as truth or quality.
- **Decision sink:** collapsing answer, recommendation, adoption, decision, execution, and outcome into the final authorized act.
- **Outcome validation:** treating a favorable result as proof of sound reasoning, good answer, or responsible decision.
- **Speed fetishism:** optimizing response latency while hiding verification, coordination, correction, harm, or lifecycle time.
- **Price–cost collapse:** treating purchase price, compute cost, or labor time as total cost.
- **Cost transfer:** reporting savings while shifting burden, risk, maintenance, verification, harm, or capability loss to others.
- **Universal utility:** imposing one value function, weighting, stakeholder set, time horizon, or commensurability assumption.
- **Aggregation erasure:** allowing average or net benefit to hide material losses, rights, duties, vetoes, or affected distributions.
- **Value laundering:** inferring benefit, desirability, legitimacy, Authorization, or compatibility from capability, quality, adoption, profit, or compliance.
- **Feedback–Evidence collapse:** treating any signal, rating, reward, complaint, or outcome as valid Evidence.
- **Feedback–Learning collapse:** treating update, adaptation, changed behavior, or improved score as Learning.
- **Correction erasure:** overwriting the prior answer or decision without lineage, consequence, challenge, and repair.
- **Goodhart pressure:** allowing a selected metric to replace the quality, cost, value, or outcome construct it projects.
- **View mismatch:** accepting an Engineering metric while Human or Scientific Views expose unclear meaning, invalid construct, hidden cost, or affected consequence.
- **Axiom or paper pre-acceptance:** importing candidate constitutional or Foundation Paper conclusions as settled semantics.
- **Implementation authority:** allowing product behavior, analytics, benchmark design, or data availability to define the universal predicate.

- analyze inquiry, search, retrieval, experiment, investigation, dialogue, and workflow without importing a reasoning theory;

### B. Reasoning, inference, computation, retrieval, judgment, and explanation boundary

- distinguish actual reasoning from inference, derivation, calculation, retrieval, search, simulation, generation, deliberation, judgment, explanation, and presentation;

- separate formal validity, soundness, epistemic warrant, truth, relevance, robustness, and responsible use;

## 10. Mandatory counterexample set

At minimum, the analysis must explain:

1. a prompt is syntactically clear but misstates the underlying Domain question;
2. two differently worded questions preserve Identity, while a small assumption change creates a different question;
3. a complex question is decomposed into tractable tasks that omit a material interaction;
4. retrieval returns the relevant source without performing the claimed reasoning;
5. a formally valid inference begins from a false or inapplicable premise;
6. a correct conclusion is reached through luck, leakage, memorization, or an invalid path;
7. a sound reasoning process yields a wrong answer because material Evidence was unavailable;
8. a fluent explanation is a post hoc Representation that did not cause the answer;
9. an output is true but does not answer the governed question;
10. an answer is partially correct yet omits a material uncertainty, exception, or affected consequence;
11. several incompatible answers remain reasonable under unresolved Evidence;
12. abstention or a qualified no-answer is better than a confident response;
13. a recommendation is epistemically sound but outside the recommender's Authority;
14. an authorized decision rejects the best-supported answer for a legitimate non-epistemic constraint;
15. a successful action follows a poor answer through luck or an unmodeled intervention;
16. a high-quality answer produces a harmful outcome because application, timing, or Context differs;
17. immediate response latency falls while verification and correction make time to responsible answer longer;
18. a slower answer is preferable because uncertainty, challenge, or affected-party review is material;
19. purchase price falls while maintenance, supervision, lock-in, outage, error, or transition raises total cost;
20. reported savings are transferred to workers, providers, future experts, clients, the public, or another affected group;
21. aggregate benefit rises while one material group bears non-consensual or non-remediable loss;
22. a favorable outcome signal is biased, delayed, strategically manipulated, or irrelevant to the answer claim;
23. feedback changes behavior or raises a score without correction, capability change, Learning, Formation, or improvement; and
24. Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views disagree over question Identity, reasoning Evidence, answer quality, time, cost, value, outcome, or feedback.
