# SymK 2.1.4D — Three-View Representation and Alignment Reconciliation

**Status:** Analytical reconciliation — no decision authority  
**Date:** 20 August 2026  
**Working baseline:** `2.1-dev.22`  
**Authority:** Analysis authorized by `SYMK-2X-EV-098`–`099`; project-steward challenge incorporated  
**Evidence identifier:** `SYMK-2X-EV-103`

## 1. Reconciliation question

> Does the DR-009 human/machine minimum imply exactly two exhaustive semantic lanes, or does Knowledge Engineering require a richer view model?

The initial Stream D formulation treated the human-semantic and machine-semantic lanes too nearly as an exhaustive architecture. The project steward recalled the earlier conclusion that additional lanes were necessary and challenged that reduction. The challenge passes.

The reconciled result establishes **three coordinated semantic views**—Human, Scientific, and Engineering—while retaining human readability, formal expressibility, and machine inspectability as cross-view capabilities. It also preserves separate program-work lanes such as constitutional semantics, corpus integrity, and grounding/verification. Semantic views, representational capabilities, and program-work lanes are different classification axes and must not be counted as one list.

## 2. Why two exhaustive lanes fail

The two-lane minimum prevents human explanation from drifting away from machine-inspectable meaning. It is insufficient as an exhaustive view model because:

- scientific claims, methods, evidence, models, uncertainty, replication, and defeat cannot be reduced either to prose or executable structure;
- formal expression can occur in scientific or engineering work and is not identical to machine operation;
- human interpretation and challenge occur within scientific and engineering practice;
- engineering artifacts can be readable while serving runtime, validation, or interoperability purposes; and
- the project already distinguishes semantic governance, corpus integrity, and empirical grounding as separate work programs.

DR-012 therefore refines the interpretation of DR-009: its human/machine rule is a minimum alignment obligation, not a declaration that only two semantic views exist.

## 3. Three coordinated semantic views

| View | Primary purpose | Must preserve | Characteristic failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human | Comprehension, interpretation, purpose, judgment, explanation, dissent, and responsible challenge | meaning, Context, Scope, examples, assumptions, uncertainty, stakes, dissent, reasons | readability without precision; rhetorical authority; hidden ambiguity |
| Scientific | Inquiry, grounding, explanation, testing, comparison, uncertainty, replication, defeat, and revision | questions, methods, Grounds, Evidence, models, inferential bridges, limitations, disagreement | false objectivity; method or consensus substituted for truth |
| Engineering | Construction, operation, validation, interoperability, traceability, automation, and controlled transformation | identities, typed relationships, constraints, lifecycle, provenance, status, version, validation, loss | schema or implementation defining meaning; false completeness; silent loss |

The views are not organizations, formats, audiences, maturity levels, or authorities. One artifact may participate in more than one view, but its role and transformations must be explicit.

## 4. Cross-view capabilities

Human readability, formal expressibility, and machine inspectability cross all three views:

- a scientific model may be readable, formal, and machine-inspectable;
- an engineering specification may include prose, mathematics, constraints, examples, and executable tests;
- a human explanation may contain a formal argument without becoming an implementation; and
- a machine-inspectable record may support reproducibility without owning the scientific meaning it represents.

No capability is sufficient by itself. Formalization may expose ambiguity or introduce suppressed assumptions. Machine inspectability may improve validation while omitting tacit or contested meaning. Readability may reveal purpose and dissent while leaving identity or constraints underspecified.

## 5. Triangular alignment

Alignment must be tested pairwise and collectively:

- **Human ↔ Scientific:** Are claims, methods, uncertainty, and dissent intelligible without rhetorical distortion? Do scientific refinements preserve the human question, stakes, and counterexamples?
- **Scientific ↔ Engineering:** Can evidence, method, model, uncertainty, defeat, and revision be represented without a schema inventing scientific authority? Do transformations preserve assumptions and inferential roles?
- **Human ↔ Engineering:** Can a person inspect what a technical artifact means, omits, constrains, and authorizes? Can validation expose failures meaningful to users and affected parties?
- **Collective closure:** Complete transformation paths must preserve subject identity, Context, Scope, authority, status, lineage, dissent, and declared semantic loss.

## 6. Representation, Projection, Ontology, and Logic

DR-010 remains intact:

- **Representation** is a situated form making selected aspects of a subject available without becoming identical to it.
- **Projection** is a governed mapping of selected source dimensions into a Representation with additions, omissions, transformations, and loss declared.

A Representation may serve any combination of views. A Projection may transform within or between views. Alignment requires source and target identities, purpose, authority, mapping, assumptions, uncertainty, validation, version, and loss declarations.

SymK must govern the minimum meaning needed to identify and relate its foundational concepts, supporting distinctions, authorities, Contexts, Scopes, statuses, dependencies, Representations, Projections, claims, Grounds, Evidence roles, assessments, uncertainty, disagreement, reliance decisions, and lineage. It must support constraints sufficient to detect prohibited conflations, invalid dependency, scope leakage, authority substitution, and undisclosed loss.

This does not select a universal Domain Ontology, one Logic, OWL, FOL, SHACL, a rule language, probability calculus, schema, or execution architecture. Domain Ontology and Logic remain project-owned under SymK constraints; formal-language selection remains routed to 2.5 and 2.9.

## 7. Program-work lanes are a separate axis

The roadmap may organize work into additional lanes, including:

1. semantic and constitutional development;
2. corpus integrity, evidence preservation, and migration control; and
3. grounding, verification, and return of counterevidence from derived projects.

These describe how the program works. They do not replace the three semantic views or the cross-view capabilities. A corpus-integrity task may preserve artifacts from all views; a grounding task may test scientific and engineering claims; constitutional work may need all three views.

## 8. Reconciled disposition and dissent

- Human, Scientific, and Engineering are three coordinated semantic views.
- Human readability, formal expressibility, and machine inspectability are cross-view capabilities.
- Program-work lanes remain a distinct classification.
- DR-009’s human/machine rule remains a minimum preservation obligation but is superseded as an exhaustive two-view interpretation.
- Representation remains foundational; Projection remains a governed supporting relationship.
- Triangular alignment and declared-loss control are required in later package design.

Open questions include whether “Scientific” should later be named epistemic-scientific; whether three views are universally sufficient; whether one artifact can serve all views without hiding incompatible purposes; which alignment claims can be machine-validated; and whether formal expression later deserves its own view.

Proceed to Stream E and test the complete A–D system before preparing revised Proposed DR-012.
