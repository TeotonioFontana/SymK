## 1. Question and analytical result

> When does a cooperative episode become a continuing relation or persistent organized system, how can participants and systems form one another over time, and what evidence supports Learning at an artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, hybrid, or cooperative-unit level without copying change from components or reifying records?

Stream E supports a temporal, level-qualified model rather than treating duration, repetition, memory, adaptation, or improved output as system Learning.

The principal working anchors are:

> **Persistence is the continued identity of an identified relation or organized subject across a relevant temporal interval and material change under declared continuity conditions. Persistence may involve component replacement, interruption, revision, and loss. Duration, repetition, unchanged name, legal status, identifier, stored memory, or similar output is insufficient by itself.**

> **A continuing cooperative relation is a temporally extended relational organization whose bounded shared undertaking, participant/contribution organization, mutual orientation, connection, and lineage remain sufficiently continuous across episodes, pauses, conflicts, and qualified membership change. It does not require uninterrupted cooperation, permanent participants, consensus, success, or Education, and it can be suspended, degraded, repaired, transformed, succeeded, or terminated.**

> **Reciprocal formation is a temporally connected pattern in which two or more identified subjects or levels exert formative influence or produce separately evidenced formative effects on one another through interaction, participation, environment, design, power, challenge, feedback, or consequence. Reciprocity does not entail symmetry, equality, intent, benefit, equivalent change, Learning by every side, or one shared learner.**

> **System Learning is a comparatively durable change in an identified system-level capacity, disposition, representation-use pattern, practice, relationship, or organization that is attributable partly to experience, inquiry, practice, feedback, interaction, consequence, or adaptation at that same stable system level, is integrated through system organization rather than exhausted by one member's temporary state or component replacement, and manifests under relevant variation within Scope.**

The Stream E result is:

1. a cooperative episode, repeated interaction, recurring pattern, continuing relation, persistent organization, and persistent cooperative unit are distinct;
2. repetition can create dependency, habituation, coordination, or transaction history without creating Cooperation or a persistent cooperative relation;
3. a continuing relation can survive pauses and participant change when declared identity, undertaking, organization, lineage, and continuity conditions remain supportable;
4. formative influence can be reciprocal while actual formative effect or Learning occurs at only one level or on only one side;
5. system Learning requires stable learner identity, system-level experience and mechanism, integrated change, persistence, manifestation, and competing-explanation control;
6. stored records, changed policies, new members, model replacement, fine-tuning labels, one output gain, and current context do not establish system Learning;
7. a Human–AI cooperative system may learn through changed roles, practices, memory-use, review, and feedback while a fixed model does not learn;
8. a component may learn while the whole system does not, and a system may learn while not every member or component changes internally;
9. gain, drift, decay, dependency, deskilling, repair, and loss may occur simultaneously in different dimensions and at different levels; and
10. persistence and formative influence do not establish Training or Education, so the inherited universal persistent-DIA Education claim remains unproven.

These are analytical working candidates only. Final classification, system-Bearer criteria, educational necessity, responsibility, and DR-013 remain unresolved.

## 2. Inherited constraints

Stream E inherits without reopening:

1. Cooperation is an actual relational process, not SymK's final objective, a permanent participant trait, a favorable output, or a status created by repetition.
2. Actual occurrence, continuing relation, capability/disposition, attribution, assessment, status, authorization, record, outcome, and value remain distinct.
3. Participant, learner, contributor, actor, Bearer, authority, affected party, assessor, beneficiary, and responsible party remain distinct roles.
4. A cooperative unit is a scoped analytical configuration, not automatically a participant, Intelligence, learner, legal person, responsible party, or Bearer of every system-level predicate.
5. Predicates do not copy automatically among members, components, collectives, institutions, distributed arrangements, hybrids, or cooperative units.
6. The boundary used to claim success must remain visible when analyzing failure, cost, dependence, burden, power, correction, and consequence.
7. Formation is value-neutral; formative purpose, activity, environment, exposure, mechanism, effect, achievement, attribution, status, and record remain distinct.
8. Learning is comparatively durable experience-linked change at an identified learner level; external substitution, temporary context, record change, and relabeling are insufficient.
9. Training and Education are distinct; neither universally entails the other, and persistent architecture does not automatically train or educate.
10. Power capacity, exercise, effect, qualification, response, and record remain distinct; formal challenge permission is not practical capacity or an effective path.
11. Knowledge, Intelligence, Learning, Education, authority, responsibility, and cooperative fitness do not entail one another.
12. The epistemic lifecycle is recursive; application, consequence, feedback, challenge, correction, revision, preservation, drift, decay, and loss can reopen earlier assessments.
13. Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views must align on material boundaries, changes, dependencies, histories, and losses.
14. Detailed agency, authority, duty, responsibility, accountability, remedy, liability, legal standing, and moral status remain principally for 2.1.6.

## 3. The temporal-level separation

Ordinary statements such as “the team keeps cooperating,” “the institution remembers,” “the AI system learned,” or “the architecture educates participants” can refer to different temporal objects.

| Temporal level | Working question | Prohibited substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Encounter or exposure | Which subjects or systems were placed in contact with what environment, input, constraint, or event? | Contact proves interaction, formation, or relation |
| Interaction | Which actions or states were mutually or sequentially responsive? | Interaction proves Cooperation or Learning |
| Cooperative episode | Did plural material contributions, shared undertaking, mutual orientation, and connection obtain within a bounded period? | One episode proves a continuing relation or cooperative disposition |
| Repeated interaction | Did sufficiently similar or related interactions recur? | Frequency or duration proves one relation, persistence, habit, Learning, or Education |
| Recurring pattern | Is a pattern supportably reproduced across episodes? | Pattern proves the same Bearer, cause, value, or governance status |
| Continuing relation | Does one relational organization retain identity and lineage across episodes, pauses, conflict, and change? | Same participants, name, contract, or platform proves continuity |
| Persistent organized subject/system | Does an organized Bearer remain sufficiently continuous while components, state, practices, or versions change? | Legal identity, deployment name, identifier, archive, or output similarity proves Bearer continuity |
| Developmental trajectory | Which capacities, dispositions, practices, relationships, dependencies, gains, losses, drift, or repair changed across time and levels? | Later state rewrites earlier state or collapses all dimensions into improvement |
| Current attribution/status | What can be supportably claimed now within Scope? | Historical achievement or credential remains permanently true |
| Temporal record | Which Representation preserves events, identities, changes, evidence, uncertainty, challenge, succession, and loss? | History log, transcript, repository, memory store, model lineage, or institutional archive is the continuing relation or Learning itself |

No temporal level is created by a timestamp or duration field. Each requires an identity, boundary, relation, evidence, and declared Scope appropriate to the claim.

## 4. Persistence

### 4.1 Persistence is qualified continuity through change

Persistence does not mean immobility. A persistent relation, participant, institution, or hybrid may change members, models, tools, roles, procedures, memory, objectives, and representations while remaining continuous under declared identity conditions. Conversely, an unchanged name or legal shell may conceal replacement of the relevant system.

A material persistence attribution should identify:

1. the proposed persistent subject or relation;
2. the start, interval, interruptions, and current temporal Scope;
3. identity and continuity conditions relevant to the predicate;
4. undertaking, organization, participant/component boundaries, and dependencies;
5. entries, exits, exclusions, replacements, forks, merges, versions, and transfers;
6. preserved and changed memory, practice, capability, authority, interface, and environment;
7. causal and governance lineage connecting earlier and later states;
8. discontinuity, breakdown, dormancy, suspension, succession, or termination candidates;
9. different Human, Scientific, and Engineering boundary descriptions; and
10. uncertainty and conditions under which the identity attribution must be reopened.

### 4.2 Duration and repetition are insufficient

Long duration can characterize coexistence, lock-in, surveillance, a market transaction series, platform dependence, mechanical protocol execution, or repeated coercion. Repetition can reproduce coordination or harm without a shared undertaking or mutual orientation. Persistence must identify what continues and why the same relation or subject remains the proper explanatory unit.

### 4.3 Persistence is predicate-relative

The same organization may persist legally while its cooperative relation terminates, persist operationally while its learner identity is replaced, or persist as a project while its original undertaking is superseded. Identity conditions appropriate to institutional continuity may be insufficient for model continuity, hybrid Learning, Education, or responsible-Cooperation status.

## 5. Identity, continuity, versioning, and succession

### 5.1 Continuity dimensions

| Dimension | Evidence that may support continuity | Why it is insufficient alone |
|---|---|---|
| Identifier or name | Stable reference and traceability | Names and identifiers can be retained through total replacement or reassigned |
| Legal or governance identity | Authorized continuity, rights, obligations, and institutional recognition | Legal persistence does not prove same capability, practice, learner, or relation |
| Shared undertaking | Continued orientation to a bounded object of work | Undertaking wording may stay while operative purpose changes |
| Organization and relations | Persistent roles, coupling, procedures, feedback, integration, and review | Procedures can become ceremonial or be enacted by a different system |
| Components or membership | Material continuity of people, models, services, infrastructure, or nodes | Complete component identity is unnecessary; one surviving component is insufficient |
| Memory and lineage | Preserved trace, history, provenance, and ability to connect prior consequence to present action | Stored history may be inaccessible, uninterpretable, or unused |
| Capability and disposition profile | Comparable organization of capacities and recurring tendencies | Similar outputs can arise from a successor or external substitution |
| Causal/operational continuity | Later state develops through transformations of the earlier organization | Continuous operation can mask a boundary-changing replacement |
| External recognition | Participants and authorities treat the relation/system as continuing | Recognition is an attribution, not the underlying continuity |
| Self-maintenance and revision | The system preserves and changes its own organization through governed processes | Self-change is not required of every persistent system and does not by itself prove identity or Learning |

No one dimension governs every case. The relevant identity criteria must be stated before using the same boundary to attribute persistence, Learning, improvement, failure, or responsibility.

### 5.2 Change classifications

Stream E distinguishes:

- **continuous modification:** the same identified relation or system changes without crossing declared continuity limits;
- **versioned continuation:** material change requires a new version while governed identity and lineage remain continuous;
- **partial succession:** one relation or system inherits selected undertaking, memory, role, assets, or status while other predicates terminate;
- **successor:** a new subject or relation replaces the prior one and inherits declared lineage without inheriting all predicates;
- **fork:** multiple successors claim parts of one lineage;
- **merge:** multiple lineages form a new or continued organized subject under declared composition;
- **reconstitution:** the prior organization ends and a materially new organization is created, possibly under the same name;
- **termination:** identity conditions fail and no successor is asserted for the predicate; and
- **unresolved continuity:** evidence is insufficient or views disagree materially.

Historical contribution, harm, Learning, status, and consequence do not disappear through succession, but they do not transfer automatically to every successor.

## 6. From cooperative episode to continuing relation

### 6.1 Episode without persistence

A one-time exchange can satisfy the Cooperation nucleus. It need not create a relationship, shared memory, cooperative disposition, obligation, learner, or future participation. Successful completion does not retroactively create persistence.

### 6.2 Repetition without continuing Cooperation

Repeated transactions, API calls, assignments, approvals, or encounters may be independently bounded, centrally compelled, purely transactional, or lacking mutual orientation. Recurrence supports a pattern hypothesis but does not prove that one cooperative relation persists.

### 6.3 Continuing relation

A continuing cooperative relation is supportable when:

1. the bounded shared undertaking or an explicitly governed successor remains identifiable;
2. plural contribution and mutual-orientation organization recurs or remains available across material episodes;
3. the relation preserves sufficient identity and lineage across pauses and changes;
4. entry, exit, role, boundary, conflict, breakdown, repair, and termination can be represented;
5. the relation is not inferred solely from membership, contract, platform, shared archive, reputation, or favorable result; and
6. current responsible qualification remains separately assessed rather than inherited permanently.

A relation may remain dormant or suspended when its identity and resumption conditions persist even though no current cooperative episode occurs. Dormancy, suspension, and current occurrence must not be conflated.

### 6.4 Persistence through conflict and breakdown

Conflict can occur inside a continuing relation. A local breakdown can suspend one episode without terminating the relation. Persistent inability to contribute, loss of shared undertaking, destruction of mutual orientation, constitutive participant loss, unrepairable conflict, or governed termination may instead end or transform it. Stream E supplies the temporal distinctions; exact responsibility and remedy remain later.

## 7. Reciprocal formation

### 7.1 Influence is not effect

Continuing interaction creates opportunities for formative influence in multiple directions. Actual reciprocal formation requires separately evidenced effects:

```text
subject or system A condition/action
    may influence B
        may produce an effect in B
            may alter B's later relation to A
                may influence A or the shared system
```

Every arrow is contingent. A may shape B while remaining unchanged. B may resist. The shared system may change while neither component changes internally. Effects may be delayed, harmful, indirect, or mediated by institutions and artifacts.

### 7.2 Reciprocity is not symmetry

Reciprocity does not imply:

- equal power, contribution, benefit, burden, or vulnerability;
- the same mechanism or kind of change on each side;
- simultaneous or intentional change;
- Education, Training, Learning, understanding, consent, or improvement;
- one collective learner;
- or justified influence.

One participant may learn a Domain practice while an institution changes its interface, a model remains fixed, affected parties develop avoidance, and the hybrid system becomes more dependent. These are different trajectories.

### 7.3 Reciprocal formation across levels

Participants may shape a cooperative unit through contributions, dissent, errors, workarounds, repair, withdrawal, and changed practice. The organized unit may shape participants through interfaces, defaults, examples, incentives, memory, roles, metrics, permissions, sanctions, response timing, and challenge paths. External authorities, beneficiaries, and affected parties may shape the relation without becoming current participants.

Every formative effect must retain its learner/Bearer level. “The participants and system co-evolved” is insufficient unless the separate changes, mechanisms, dependencies, boundaries, and consequences are stated.

## 8. Persistent formative conditions

Interfaces, incentives, defaults, metrics, memory, examples, sanctions, permissions, role allocations, workflows, models, and operational feedback can become persistent formative conditions. Persistence changes exposure and cumulative possibility; it does not prove effect.

Material analysis should distinguish:

| Condition | Possible formative influence | Required caution |
|---|---|---|
| Interface and default | Makes some action easy, visible, expected, delayed, costly, or impossible | Use frequency does not prove habit or Education |
| Incentive and metric | Directs attention, effort, concealment, local optimization, or comparison | Correlated conduct does not establish internal change or legitimate purpose |
| Example and precedent | Supplies patterns for imitation, interpretation, judgment, or refusal | Availability does not prove uptake, transfer, or correctness |
| Sanction and permission | Shapes practical alternatives, compliance, challenge, and avoidance | Compliance may be coercive, strategic, temporary, or learned |
| Memory and record | Makes prior claims, consequences, dissent, and repair available | Storage does not prove retrieval, interpretation, use, or institutional memory |
| Role and authority | Structures who proposes, reviews, decides, corrects, and bears exposure | Formal allocation does not prove meaningful participation or responsible exercise |
| Operational feedback | Returns outcome, error, burden, or affected-party response | Signal does not prove reception, incorporation, Learning, or correction |
| Model or automation | Frames, ranks, predicts, generates, filters, or controls | Changed human routine does not imply model Learning; model change does not imply system improvement |

The hidden curriculum is a hypothesis about operative formative influence. An actual habit, Learning, Training effect, socialization, conditioning, deskilling, or Education attribution still requires Stream C evidence.

## 9. Memory, retention, and use

### 9.1 Memory layers

| Layer | Working meaning | Non-entailment |
|---|---|---|
| Trace or record | Preserved Representation of an event, state, claim, decision, or consequence | Accessibility, interpretation, Knowledge, memory capacity, or Learning |
| Storage retention | Continued technical or institutional availability of traces | Retrieval, relevance, integrity, use, or continuity of the learner |
| Retrieval capacity | Ability to locate or reactivate selected traces within Scope | Correct interpretation, integration, or changed practice |
| Working/current context | Temporarily active information shaping current performance | Durable change in the model, person, institution, or hybrid |
| Procedural retention | Recurring ability to enact a practice, sequence, constraint, or response | Understanding, justified authority, Education, or transfer |
| Institutional memory | Organized capacity to retain, retrieve, interpret, apply, challenge, revise, and transmit relevant traces or practices across role and membership change | One archive, policy, employee memory, or legal continuity |
| Distributed memory | Retention and reactivation realized across multiple nodes, artifacts, roles, and processes | One collective Bearer, equal access, or immunity from fragmentation |
| Developmental history | Traceable sequence of conditions, experiences, changes, assessments, consequences, and revisions | The history itself being Formation or Learning |

### 9.2 Memory can persist while capability decays

An institution may retain documents that nobody can find, interpret, trust, or enact. A model may have parameters encoding statistical regularities while a deployment loses the retrieval service needed for performance. A team may remember a lesson informally until member exit. Memory availability, memory use, and system capability require separate assessment.

### 9.3 Forgetting and selective loss

Forgetting can be accidental, imposed, protective, legally required, epistemically corrective, or harmful. Removing false or dangerous content may improve one capability while losing provenance or minority testimony. Stream E does not select retention policy; it requires the object, level, authority, reason, effect, affected parties, and irreversible loss to remain visible.

## 10. Feedback, uptake, adaptation, and Learning

Feedback must be separated into a chain:

```text
event or consequence
    -> feedback signal or Representation
        -> availability and reception
            -> interpretation and assessment
                -> selection or decision
                    -> incorporation into organization/practice/state
                        -> manifested change
                            -> later consequence and reassessment
```

A system can receive feedback and ignore it, misinterpret it, suppress it, or change only a dashboard. A component can adapt transiently without durable system Learning. A policy can be edited without enactment. A later outcome can improve because the environment changed rather than because the proposed learner changed.

Learning attribution therefore requires evidence across the chain at the same selected learner level. Feedback is neither correction nor Learning by itself.

## 11. System Learning

### 11.1 Working evidence profile

A material system-Learning attribution should identify:

1. the system learner's Identity, level, boundary, version, components, dependencies, and continuity conditions before and after change;
2. baseline system-level capacity, disposition, representation-use pattern, practice, relationship, or organization;
3. relevant experience, inquiry, practice, interaction, feedback, consequence, or adaptation at that system level;
4. a plausible mechanism connecting experience to integrated change in the system's organization;
5. the changed capability, practice, relationship, or organization and where it is realized;
6. comparative durability, manifestation, relevant variation, transfer, and dependence on temporary support;
7. composition across changed and unchanged members, components, artifacts, roles, and interfaces;
8. competing explanations including member Learning alone, selection, replacement, redesign, external control, changed environment, prompt/context, retrieval, leakage, maturation, damage, random drift, and measurement change;
9. gains, losses, dependency, power, challenge, affected parties, and downstream consequences; and
10. uncertainty, current status, decay, supersession, and reopening conditions.

### 11.2 Integration rather than summation

System Learning is not the sum or average of member Learning. It requires change in organization—how the system selects, relates, composes, remembers, responds, revises, or enacts—at the claimed level. Member Learning may be a mechanism or dependency without becoming system Learning automatically.

### 11.3 No requirement that every member learn

An institution may learn through changed procedures, role relations, review, memory-use, and feedback even when some members remain unaware or later leave. A distributed community may learn through revised integration practices. A hybrid may learn through changed coupling. The attribution is defeated when the claimed change is exhausted by one individual's private state and the system cannot manifest it without that temporary presence or role.

### 11.4 External redesign

External redesign can produce developmental change or Formation in a system without constituting that system's Learning. A system-Learning claim becomes more supportable when prior experience or consequence is received, assessed, and incorporated through the system's own organized processes or stable coupling. The exact degree of internality is substrate- and Domain-sensitive, but the source and mechanism must not be hidden.

### 11.5 Learning is not improvement

A system can learn a harmful pattern, concealment strategy, exploitative practice, or brittle shortcut. It can improve one measure while losing challenge, professional capability, resilience, fairness, or repair. Learning and value require separate attribution.

## 12. Learner attribution across levels

| Proposed learner | Possible evidence | Insufficient by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Individual | Durable change connected to experience, inquiry, practice, feedback, interaction, or consequence | Presence, instruction, score, compliance, credential, or changed environment |
| Model | Parameter/state change and evaluated capability/disposition change attributable to a model-level learning process under stable version/boundary criteria | Prompt, retrieval, wrapper, fine-tuning label, benchmark gain, or new model replacing the prior one |
| Configured artificial system | Durable integrated change across model, memory, tools, policies, controllers, feedback, or orchestration attributable at that configured-system level | One component update, current context, operator workaround, or deployment label |
| Collective/team | Changed division of work, selection, integration, critique, memory-use, coordination, correction, or practice that persists beyond one member's temporary state | Several members learn independently, membership changes, combined files, or improved output |
| Institution | Changed persistent roles, procedures, memory-use, authority paths, review, response, and enactment connected to institutional experience/consequence | New policy document, consultant recommendation, legal continuity, new staff, or official declaration |
| Distributed arrangement | Changed protocols, standards, integration, memory, governance, feedback, or node relations preserved across distributed continuity | Repository commit, shared format, network growth, or one maintainer's Learning |
| Hybrid | Durable change in human–artificial coupling, role allocation, memory-use, review, feedback, or combined practice attributable to the stable hybrid boundary | Human Learning alone, model update alone, tool replacement, occasional use, or a boundary drawn after success |
| Cooperative unit | Changed relational organization among participants, undertaking, contributions, interfaces, dependencies, affected parties, feedback, challenge, or repair | The analytical boundary itself, any participant change, cooperation history, or aggregate performance |

Different levels may receive separate Learning attributions in the same history. Their evidence and causal relations must remain explicit; they cannot be counted as independent gains when one is only a summary of another.

## 13. Fixed components and changing systems

### 13.1 Fixed model inside an improving Human–AI system

A fixed model can remain unchanged while humans revise prompts, verification, role allocation, memory, escalation, challenge, and integration. If those changes are attributable to system-level experience and persist through the hybrid organization, the configured or hybrid system may support a Learning attribution. The model does not inherit it.

### 13.2 Changing model inside a non-learning system

A model can be retrained or replaced while the institution continues the same unexamined practice. The model may have undergone a technical learning process; the deployment or institution does not thereby learn, and the whole system may regress.

### 13.3 Component dependence

A system-level capability may depend constitutively on a component without belonging to that component alone. Ablation, perturbation, replacement, and transfer evidence can clarify dependency. Removing a component and losing performance supports a dependency claim, not automatically component Learning, system Intelligence, or responsible design.

### 13.4 Boundary stability through evaluation

If the Human is included when claiming success but excluded when measuring cost or deskilling, or the retrieval layer is included for performance but excluded for failure, the system claim is invalidly boundary-shifted. Success, Learning, decay, dependency, and consequence must use compatible boundaries or declare the transformation.

## 14. Entry, exit, replacement, and role change

### 14.1 Entry

A newcomer may be socialized into an existing pattern, challenge it, or change the system through participation. The relation's persistence and the newcomer's formation are separate. Admission, membership, onboarding, or access does not establish Participation, Learning, or Education.

### 14.2 Exit

Exit can remove capability, tacit Knowledge, dissent, memory, burden, or a source of coercion. A relation may persist, degrade, fork, or terminate. The exiting subject's prior contributions and consequences remain historical; the remaining system does not automatically retain their capacity.

### 14.3 Replacement

Replacing a person, model, service, dataset, institution, or procedure may:

- preserve system identity and capability;
- preserve identity while changing capability or disposition;
- create a versioned continuation;
- produce a successor or reconstitution;
- or terminate the relevant learner or relation.

Replacement is not Learning by the replaced subject. System Learning requires evidence that experience-linked change is integrated at the continuing system level rather than explained solely by selection of a more capable component.

### 14.4 Role change

Changing who proposes, reviews, decides, acts, remembers, challenges, or repairs can alter the cooperative system even if components remain. Role change may be an intervention, formative effect, power redistribution, correction, or system-Learning mechanism. Authority and responsibility do not follow automatically.

### 14.5 Succession and inherited records

A successor may inherit archives, policies, models, obligations, reputation, or status without inheriting actual Knowledge, Intelligence, Education, Learning, cooperative disposition, or responsible qualification. Each inherited predicate needs its own continuity and evidence rule.

## 15. Developmental profiles rather than one improvement score

Persistent systems can develop across multiple dimensions:

- epistemic quality, provenance, uncertainty, and challenge;
- participant capability, contribution, and role integration;
- cooperative orientation, conflict handling, and repair;
- speed, cost, scale, coverage, and operational reliability;
- memory retention, retrieval, interpretation, and revision;
- adaptability, robustness, transfer, and graceful degradation;
- dependency, lock-in, substitutability, and recovery capacity;
- distribution of benefit, burden, risk, power, and affected-party standing;
- professional formation, deskilling, displacement, and future-expert capacity;
- privacy, dignity, safety, and other Domain consequences.

Gain in one dimension does not offset loss in another automatically. An overall “system improved” or “amplified” claim requires declared trade-offs and belongs principally to 2.1.7–2.1.8. Stream E requires profile preservation so Learning cannot become a synonym for favorable aggregate performance.

## 16. Drift, decay, deskilling, and unlearning

### 16.1 Drift

Drift is gradual or accumulated divergence in practice, representation, relationship, capability, objective, environment fit, or behavior from an earlier state, reference, or justified condition. Drift may be adaptive, harmful, neutral, or contested. It does not require Learning or decline.

### 16.2 Decay and loss

Decay is a reduction in availability, reliability, integration, transfer, challengeability, or enactment of a prior capability, disposition, practice, relationship, or memory. Loss may be partial, contextual, reversible, irreversible, component-specific, or system-level. A prior achievement can have been real even when it no longer obtains.

### 16.3 Deskilling

Deskilling is a reduction or nondevelopment of relevant capability, judgment, practice, or transfer at an identified participant or system level, often connected to substitution, automation, dependency, changed role, or reduced practice. It may coexist with higher whole-system performance. It is not established solely by less manual activity and does not decide whether the change is unacceptable.

### 16.4 Unlearning

Unlearning may mean loss of a learned pattern, active revision of an entrenched practice, suppression without genuine change, or replacement of one disposition by another. These meanings must be typed. Removing a harmful habit may be improvement; forgetting grounds or challenge history may be decay.

### 16.5 Context change

A previously effective habit can become irrelevant or harmful when Context, Scope, participants, power, or consequences change. Current assessment must not rewrite historical evidence, and historical success cannot authorize current reliance without reassessment.

## 17. Correction, repair, recovery, and resilience

| Distinction | Working meaning | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Correction | Intervention or process directed at an identified error, deviation, false claim, defective output, rule, or practice | Can improve future operation without restoring relationships, capability, standing, or prior harm |
| Repair | Response to damaged relationship, system condition, participation, trust, capability, or consequence | May require acknowledgement, restoration, redesign, restitution, renewed challenge, or other Domain action; not guaranteed by correction |
| Recovery | Return of selected capability, function, participation, or relation after disruption or loss | May restore an earlier state, create a new state, or depend on external substitution; not necessarily Learning |
| Resilience | Capacity to absorb, adapt to, recover from, or transform under disruption while preserving declared critical identity or function | Can preserve harmful systems and does not entail responsibility, Education, or improvement |
| Adaptability | Capacity to change relative to environment or objective | Does not establish that change occurred, was learned, was responsible, or preserved identity |

A system that corrects output after feedback may still fail to repair excluded participants, restore lost expertise, reduce dependency, or change the mechanism that produced harm. A repair process may itself support system Learning if its experience-linked changes persist at the same system boundary.

## 18. Persistence, power, dependency, and challenge over time

Persistence can accumulate both capability and power. Repeated use may create network effects, reliance, skill atrophy, agenda control, switching cost, normalized surveillance, or practical inability to refuse. A formally unchanged authority relationship may become more coercive as alternatives disappear.

Material persistent-system assessment should preserve:

- how dependencies originated and changed;
- which components or participants became difficult to replace;
- who controls memory, interfaces, standards, metrics, access, updates, and succession;
- whether challenge capacity remains practical as expertise and alternatives change;
- whether affected parties can introduce consequence evidence;
- whether successful correction reduces or merely redistributes burden;
- and whether exit, fork, rollback, appeal, representation, or recovery remain possible.

Challenge capacity can decay. A procedure may remain formally available while participants lose the Knowledge, time, confidence, language, protection, or alternative infrastructure needed to use it. Persistent responsible qualification therefore requires current evidence, not inherited status.

## 19. Persistence, Education, and the persistent-DIA question

### 19.1 What persistence establishes

A persistent architecture or DIA can establish continuing exposure, recurring conditions, cumulative dependency, and opportunities for formative influence. It should therefore be assessed for formative effects and reciprocal consequences.

### 19.2 What persistence does not establish

Persistence does not establish:

- an identified learner;
- actual formative effect;
- Learning, Training, Education, socialization, conditioning, or habit;
- reciprocal change in every participant or component;
- system Learning;
- responsible cooperation;
- effective challenge;
- or improvement.

### 19.3 Stability does not prove Education is necessary

Stable Cooperation can be produced or preserved through protocol, routine, role design, external constraint, dependence, narrow Training, or favorable environment without satisfying the Stream C Education profile. Some such stability may be brittle, coercive, unjustified, or unable to adapt; others may be appropriately bounded. This defeats a conceptual inference from stability alone to Education.

Responsible, evolvable, challengeable persistent cooperation may often depend practically on developed capabilities, dispositions, practice, memory, correction, and repair. Whether these amount to Education, whether Education is universally necessary, and at which learner level remain empirical and constitutional questions for Stream F, 2.1.7, 2.2, and project grounding.

### 19.4 Persistent systems should preserve formative accountability without automatic educational attribution

SymK may ultimately require persistent systems to identify formative conditions, assess material effects, preserve challenge and consequence feedback, and respond to dependency or decay. Those governance duties would not mean that every system educates, every participant is a learner, or Education has occurred.

## 20. Alternatives considered

### A — Persistence is sufficient duration or repetition

**Rejected.** It conflates recurring transactions, dependency, coexistence, protocol execution, repeated coercion, and one continuing relation.

### B — Stable name, legal identity, deployment ID, or repository proves continuity

**Rejected.** Identifiers and statuses support traceability but can survive complete replacement or hide a materially different learner and relation.

### C — A persistent relation requires uninterrupted cooperative episodes and fixed participants

**Rejected.** Relations can survive pauses, conflict, role change, entry, exit, and component replacement under declared continuity conditions.

### D — Stored records constitute institutional memory and system Learning

**Rejected.** Retention must be separated from retrieval, interpretation, integration, enactment, challenge, revision, and experience-linked change.

### E — Any improved system output proves system Learning

**Rejected.** Changed environment, temporary context, retrieval, external redesign, selection, component replacement, leakage, or measurement change may explain the gain.

### F — A system learns whenever one member or component learns

**Rejected.** Member change must become integrated into the continuing system organization and remain manifest at the claimed level.

### G — A collective or institution learns only if every member learns

**Rejected.** System organization can change across membership, roles, memory, procedures, and feedback without equivalent change in every member.

### H — External redesign is always system Learning

**Rejected.** Redesign may form or change a system without an experience-linked mechanism attributable at that system level.

### I — Reciprocal influence proves reciprocal Learning, symmetry, or mutual benefit

**Rejected.** Influence and effect require separate evidence in each direction and at each level.

### J — A persistent architecture inevitably trains or educates

**Rejected.** Persistent conditions may exert formative influence; Training and Education require identified activities/effects/achievements and learner evidence.

### K — Once learned or educated, the status remains unless formally revoked

**Rejected.** Capability, habit, integration, transfer, challenge, and context fit may decay; formal status neither creates nor preserves the underlying achievement.

### L — System Learning is impossible and all collective claims are shorthand

**Rejected as universally reductive.** Stable organized systems can integrate experience-linked change through roles, memory-use, procedures, coupling, feedback, and practice at a level not exhausted by one member, subject to strong evidence.

## 22. Three-view preservation obligations

### 22.1 Human View

The Human View should explain:

- what participants and affected parties understand to be continuing, changing, remembered, learned, lost, or succeeded;
- how entry, exit, replacement, dependence, habit, deskilling, challenge, correction, repair, and Context change affect the relation;
- which histories, tacit practices, identities, burdens, and dissent disappear from official continuity claims;
- why a person, institution, artificial system, distributed arrangement, hybrid, or cooperative unit is called the learner;
- and what the narrative cannot establish without longitudinal and system evidence.

### 22.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View should preserve, where material:

- longitudinal baselines, intervention and consequence sequences, learner boundaries, continuity hypotheses, comparison conditions, and time scales;
- process tracing, counterfactuals, ablation, perturbation, transfer, retention, decay, recurrence, and recovery evidence;
- competing explanations such as selection, replacement, external redesign, changed environment, current context, retrieval, leakage, measurement drift, or survivor bias;
- multi-level dependencies, gains, losses, affected populations, uncertainty, and defeat conditions;
- and evidence distinguishing record retention from memory use and component change from system Learning.

No duration threshold, interaction count, benchmark gain, policy change, member survey, archive size, or version history defines persistence or system Learning universally.

### 22.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View should eventually preserve:

- identities, versions, boundaries, components, dependencies, undertakings, roles, relations, and continuity criteria across time;
- episodes, repeated interactions, continuing relations, persistent systems, suspension, dormancy, breakdown, entry, exit, replacement, fork, merge, succession, and termination distinctly;
- formative conditions, exposure, feedback, uptake, incorporation, change, manifestation, transfer, decay, correction, repair, and Learning attribution separately;
- traces, storage, retrieval, current context, procedural retention, institutional/distributed memory, and developmental history distinctly;
- member-, model-, configured-system-, collective-, institutional-, distributed-, hybrid-, and cooperative-unit-level claims with composition and no duplicate gain;
- power, dependency, challenge capacity, affected parties, deskilling, burden, consequence, uncertainty, view mismatch, Projection loss, and lineage;
- and compatible success/failure boundaries across capability, cost, error, reliance, and harm.

No schema, event model, versioning standard, identity calculus, Learning metric, memory architecture, workflow, or implementation is selected here.

### 22.4 Alignment test

A system fails alignment when human materials say “we learned,” scientific evaluation compares different learner boundaries, and engineering history records only a new model version or policy file. The boundary mismatch, omitted components, continuity claim, current status, uncertainty, and correction path must remain explicit.
