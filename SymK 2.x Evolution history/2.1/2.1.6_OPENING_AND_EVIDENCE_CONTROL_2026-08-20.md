## 5. Inherited constraints — not questions reopened by default

2.1.6 inherits these Stage-Accepted constraints:

1. Capability, Intelligence, Knowledge, performance, origin, Participation, Contribution, Cooperation, Power, governance status, and successful outcome do not automatically confer agency, authority, authorization, responsibility, accountability, legal personality, or moral standing.
2. Bearer is an attribution role. Predicate bearing, attribution claim, assessment, authority for use, role allocation, responsible assignment, and record remain distinct.
3. Participant, Contributor, actor, source, resource, beneficiary, affected party, authority, and responsible party are non-substitutable roles.
4. A cooperative unit, collective, institution, distributed arrangement, hybrid, configured system, or DIA cannot become a responsible subject merely through naming, membership, output, Persistence, memory, Learning, or legal registration.
5. Cooperation occurrence remains distinct from responsible qualification and from answer, action, outcome, value, and consequence.
6. Power capacity, exercise, mechanism, effect, qualification, recognized authority, governance response, and record remain distinct.
7. Formal permission to challenge is not effective challengeability; affected nonparticipants remain in the consequence and challenge horizon.
8. Epistemic warrant, governance status, Reliance Authorization, organizational permission, legal authority, and moral justification do not collapse into one status.
9. Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views must remain aligned where material, and material projection loss must be declared.
10. Derived projects and applicable external authorities own Domain-specific laws, professional duties, institutional roles, liability rules, remedies, and operational allocations within the universal SymK minimum.
11. Constitutional selection and Ratification remain for 2.2 and 2.3; A0, A1, A2, A4, A6, and J1 are not current norms.
12. Detailed amplification, DIA identity, answer value, package anatomy, grounding, and formal realization remain assigned to their later stages.

## 7. Principal analytical hazards

- **Human-approval sink:** treating a click, signature, nominal review, or final approval as absorption of all responsibility.
- **Artificial-blame sink:** assigning responsibility to an artificial system to conceal Human, institutional, organizational, provider, or deployer obligations.
- **Capability–authority substitution:** inferring permission, standing, or decision authority from competence or Intelligence.
- **Authority–warrant substitution:** treating legal, organizational, or workflow authority as truth or Knowledge.
- **Causation–responsibility collapse:** treating every causal condition as equally responsible or one proximate act as exhaustive.
- **Final-action bias:** ignoring design, procurement, data, policy, incentives, supervision, maintenance, update, and deployment Contributions.
- **Responsibility-gap declaration:** treating distributed or hybrid complexity as proof that nobody can be answerable.
- **Collective reification:** copying member predicates to an institution or system, or system predicates to every member.
- **Type privilege:** assigning permanent Human or artificial roles solely by origin.
- **Legal–moral collapse:** equating legal personality, liability, permission, prohibition, moral standing, ethical justification, and practical capability.
- **Accountability theater:** treating a log, audit trail, committee, policy, explanation, or appeal label as effective accountability.
- **Consent theater:** treating terms acceptance, access, silence, continued use, or no formal objection as informed and practical consent.
- **Affected-party erasure:** excluding subjects who bear material consequences because they are not Participants, users, customers, or contracting parties.
- **Responsibility laundering:** outsourcing, delegation, automation, contracting, model procurement, or organizational layering used to hide retained obligations.
- **Retrospective moralization:** changing the responsible subject boundary after harm or success to fit a preferred conclusion.
- **Temporal erasure:** ignoring responsibility across update, replacement, succession, dissolution, merger, maintenance, or decommissioning.
- **Domain leakage:** universalizing one legal system, profession, moral theory, organizational form, or risk practice.
- **Artifact reification:** treating role tables, permission fields, contracts, model cards, audit logs, or responsibility matrices as the actual relations.
- **Axiom pre-acceptance:** treating A0 or A2 as governing merely because drafted.
- **Implementation authority:** allowing a workflow, access-control system, runtime identity, or deployed behavior to define agency or responsibility.

## 9. Governed analysis sequence

### A. Agency, action, causal Contribution, and control

- distinguish event, output, action, omission, recommendation, decision, authorization, execution, and consequence;
- compare capability, Intelligence, agency, autonomy, intention, foreseeability, causal Contribution, influence, control, intervention, and practical alternatives;
- test individual, artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, configured-system, and hybrid candidate agents under stable boundaries;
- establish an initial agency/action/control model without allocating responsibility or assigning final disposition.

### B. Role, authority, authorization, decision, delegation, and supervision

- distinguish role, title, status, capability, expertise, epistemic standing, Power, authority, authorization, mandate, permission, approval, reliance, delegation, supervision, and execution;
- test source, jurisdiction, Scope, Context, time, expiry, reassessment, challenge, revocation, and succession;
- expose ceremonial Human oversight, rubber-stamp approval, capability-based privilege, and delegation-based responsibility laundering;
- preserve external legal and professional authority without universalizing it.

### C. Responsibility, duty, accountability, answerability, blame, liability, and remedy

- compare prospective responsibility, duty, obligation, retrospective responsibility, causal responsibility, role responsibility, answerability, accountability arrangements, blame, sanction, legal liability, remedy, and repair;
- separate actual relations from assignment, attribution, assessment, acceptance, enforcement, and record;
- determine whether Responsibility or another minimum anchor passes DR-009 admission and how supporting distinctions should be routed;
- preserve normative pluralism without making the universal minimum empty.

### D. Distributed, collective, institutional, and hybrid allocation

- test provider, developer, data source, integrator, owner, deployer, operator, approver, user, maintainer, auditor, institution, affected party, external authority, and system roles;
- preserve multi-level causal and responsibility claims without boundary shopping or property transfer;
- analyze delegation, outsourcing, procurement, model update, maintenance, replacement, succession, merger, dissolution, and inherited obligations;
- test whether a responsibility gap is real, manufactured, or a failure of representation and governance.

### E. Minimum ethical boundary, consent, standing, challenge, and repair

- determine what ethical minimum SymK itself must own without selecting a complete moral, political, or legal theory;
- distinguish consent, compliance, notice, understanding, voluntariness, practical refusal, representation, affected-party standing, challenge, appeal, remedy, correction, and repair;
- test Power, vulnerability, foreseeable consequence, uncertainty, severe harm, emergency action, and conflicting duties;
- record DQ-005 and DQ-017 progress and consequences for A0, A1, A2, A4, A6, and J1.

### F. Consolidated stress test and consequences

- apply every mandatory counterexample and stable-boundary test;
- test Product Vision, DR-009 through DR-013, Human/Scientific/Engineering Views, jurisdiction, candidate artifacts, DQ-005, DQ-017, Foundation Papers, derived-project ownership, and 2.1.7–2.1.9 dependencies;

## 10. Mandatory counterexample set

At minimum, the analysis must explain:

1. a Human gives a ceremonial approval without time, competence, Evidence, or practical ability to review;
2. an artificial system produces a correct recommendation but has no authorization to decide or act;
3. an artificial component executes a delegated action while the nominal supervisor cannot understand, interrupt, or reverse it;
4. a subject materially causes an outcome without knowledge, intention, foreseeability, or control;
5. a foreseeable omission contributes more to harm than the final proximate action;
6. many small Contributions across data, model, integration, policy, deployment, and use produce harm with no single sufficient cause;
7. a highly capable expert has no lawful authority, while an authorized official lacks relevant competence;
8. organizational authority permits action that remains epistemically unwarranted or morally challenged;
9. a legal entity bears liability although no individual member satisfies the same agency or control predicate;
10. an artificial or hybrid system shows planning and control but has no accepted legal personality or moral standing;
11. a Human operator relies on automation after a nominal warning that design and incentives make ineffective;
12. a provider contract disclaims responsibility while the provider retains decisive design, update, or information control;
13. a deployer cannot inspect a model but selects the deployment Context and affected population;
14. a user intentionally misuses a system whose provider and deployer took proportionate preventive measures;
15. an affected nonparticipant bears severe consequences but has no contract, account, operational role, or formal challenge path;
16. consent is recorded through terms acceptance although refusal is impractical and consequences are opaque;
17. an emergency action is justified without prior authorization but creates later answerability and review obligations;
18. professional, organizational, legal, and epistemic duties conflict within overlapping Scope;
19. a whistleblower or artificial monitor raises valid Evidence against an authorized decision and faces suppression;
20. an audit log is complete but no subject must answer, correct, repair, or provide remedy;
21. a harmful outcome follows a compliant process, while a beneficial outcome follows an unauthorized or irresponsible process;
22. a system update, maintainer intervention, component replacement, merger, dissolution, or succession changes who can answer for earlier and later conduct;
23. collective or hybrid capability is real, but responsibility is copied either entirely to the collective or entirely to one member without level Evidence; and
24. one Human, Scientific, or Engineering View reports adequate oversight while another exposes missing control, invalid attribution, or implementation-induced responsibility laundering.
