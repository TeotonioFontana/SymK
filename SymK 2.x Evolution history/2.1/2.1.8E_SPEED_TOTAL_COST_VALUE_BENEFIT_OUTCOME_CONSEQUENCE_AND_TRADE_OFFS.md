## 1. Question and analytical result

> What minimum boundary must SymK establish among speed, time, price, cost, total cost, value, utility, usefulness, benefit, preference, goal attainment, outcome, consequence, and trade-off so that “better, faster, and lower total cost” remains a governed plural claim rather than a universal scalar or an optimization license?

Stream E supports the following working answer:

> **Speed is a scoped comparison among identified temporal intervals or event relations under materially stable start and end conditions, included work, subjects, Context, quality obligations, and uncertainty. Immediate response latency, inquiry duration, verification, coordination, decision, action, correction, recovery, and lifecycle time are distinct. “Faster” never establishes better quality, lower total cost, greater value, responsible use, or favorable outcome automatically.**

> **Cost is a scoped burden, resource use, sacrifice, exposure, or foregone alternative attributed under a declared method to identified cost objects and Bearers over a declared boundary, counterfactual, and time horizon. Total cost is not literally every conceivable cost; it is a completeness claim that all material cost families within the declared system and affected horizon have been included, with exclusions, uncertainty, distribution, transfer, externalization, residual effects, and correction obligations visible. Price, payment, compute, labor, and budget are cost observations or families, not total cost by themselves.**

> **Value is a scoped and challengeable evaluative relation connecting an identified object, state, change, or alternative to identified claimants, beneficiaries, affected subjects, purposes, commitments, criteria, authorities, counterfactuals, time horizons, uncertainty, and distributions. Utility, usefulness, benefit, preference satisfaction, goal attainment, epistemic, professional, economic, public, moral, and market value are non-substitutable value families. No one value function, stakeholder set, currency, weighting, aggregation, or outcome has universal SymK authority.**

> **An outcome is a realized Domain-relevant state or change assessed against an identified target or expectation. A consequence is an effect or changed condition associated causally or practically with an answer, decision, action, omission, system, or outcome across identified subjects, boundaries, and time. Target, actual outcome, causal Contribution, attribution, side effect, residual effect, distribution, and later revision remain separate. Favorable outcome does not prove answer quality, sound reasoning, responsible decision, value, or rightful Responsibility allocation.**

> **A trade-off is a scoped comparative claim that alternatives differ across two or more material dimensions, subjects, times, or constraints such that a gain, preservation, or reduction in one accompanies or requires a loss, burden, risk, or forgone gain in another under stated assumptions. Correlation, provider inconvenience, current design, budget convention, or transferred burden does not prove necessity. A legitimate trade-off claim preserves alternatives, avoidability, Authority, consent where relevant, non-compensable conditions, uncertainty, distribution, reversibility, remedy, and challenge.**

This working result keeps at least ten states separate:

1. **temporal event or interval:** the identified start, end, duration, ordering, waiting, concurrency, or deadline relation;
2. **speed comparison:** an assessment that one materially comparable interval is shorter or temporally preferable;
3. **cost occurrence or exposure:** an identified resource use, burden, sacrifice, risk, or foregone alternative;
4. **cost attribution and total-cost claim:** method-bound allocation and material-completeness assessment across Bearers and horizon;
5. **value claim:** an evaluative relation asserted by or for identified subjects under criteria and authority;
6. **benefit or harm claim:** favorable or adverse difference against a declared counterfactual for identified subjects;
7. **target or expected outcome:** a desired, required, predicted, or reference state;
8. **actual outcome and consequence:** realized Domain state and broader effects, with causal uncertainty;
9. **trade-off, priority, threshold, or decision rule:** governance relations over plural profiles, not intrinsic facts; and
10. **record and lineage:** durable Representation of objects, methods, subjects, assessments, decisions, effects, corrections, and supersession.

No definition or disposition is accepted here. Stream F must consolidate feedback, correction, Learning, consequences, all prior Streams, and proposal readiness before DR-016 can acquire any content or status.

## 3. Why SymK needs this boundary

Without the boundary, materially different claims become “more value”:

- a model emits text sooner while investigation and verification take longer;
- a dashboard reports lower latency after excluding queueing or Human review;
- a product price falls while integration, supervision, maintenance, lock-in, or correction rises;
- labor disappears from the provider's ledger because users now perform it;
- reported savings are borne as deskilling, risk, delay, or unpaid work by another group;
- a high-quality answer to a low-value question is reported as high value;
- user satisfaction is treated as public benefit;
- market price is treated as moral or epistemic value;
- goal attainment is treated as legitimate although the goal or distribution is contested;
- a favorable outcome caused by luck validates a poor answer;
- a harmful side effect is omitted because it lies outside the project boundary;
- average benefit conceals severe non-remediable loss to a minority;
- one short-term gain hides later correction, remedy, dependence, or capability loss;
- a correlation between quality and time is called an unavoidable trade-off; or
- a chosen metric is optimized until it replaces the underlying purpose.

The opposite error is requiring complete certainty and every possible cost or consequence before any comparison. SymK needs proportional, bounded claims whose limits are visible and challengeable, not impossible omniscience.

## 5. Working anchors

### 5.1 Temporal profile

A **temporal profile** is a scoped representation of material event times, intervals, ordering, waiting, concurrency, deadlines, currency, expiry, correction, and lifecycle horizons for identified subjects and processes.

It may include response latency, inquiry duration, verification, coordination, decision, action, recovery, correction, and total elapsed time without forcing them into one duration.

### 5.2 Responsible-answer time

**Time to responsible answer** is the elapsed and structured time required for an identified answer version to reach a declared reliance-ready condition under applicable quality, Evidence, review, Authority, affected-subject, and correction requirements for a declared use.

This is not a universal status or fixed workflow. In emergencies, provisional reliance may precede complete review under explicit uncertainty, authority, monitoring, and correction conditions. A later revalidated answer may have several responsible-answer times for different uses.

### 5.3 Cost and total cost

A **cost** is a scoped burden, resource use, sacrifice, exposure, or foregone alternative attributed under a declared method to an identified cost object and Bearer over a declared boundary and time.

A **total-cost profile** is a material-completeness claim covering the cost families relevant to a declared question, use, system boundary, counterfactual, affected horizon, and lifecycle. “Total” means no known material family within that declaration is silently omitted; it does not claim omniscience.

### 5.4 Value

**Value** is a scoped and challengeable evaluative relation connecting an identified object, state, change, or alternative to identified claimants, beneficiaries, affected subjects, purposes, commitments, criteria, authorities, counterfactuals, time horizons, uncertainty, and distributions.

Value may be positive, negative, mixed, conditional, disputed, incomparable, unknown, or not applicable under a stated framework. Value is not one substance stored in an answer or system.

### 5.5 Benefit and harm

A **benefit** is a favorable difference or preserved good for an identified beneficiary relative to a declared baseline or counterfactual under stated criteria and time. A **harm** is an adverse difference, impairment, burden, exposure, or loss for an identified affected subject under stated criteria.

Absence of observed harm is not benefit automatically. A benefit to one subject may coincide with cost or harm to another.

### 5.6 Outcome and consequence

An **outcome** is a realized Domain-relevant state or change assessed against an identified target, expectation, baseline, or counterfactual.

A **consequence** is an effect or changed condition associated causally or practically with an answer, decision, action, omission, system, or outcome across identified subjects, boundaries, and time.

### 5.7 Trade-off

A **trade-off** is a scoped comparative claim that alternatives differ across material dimensions, subjects, horizons, or constraints such that a gain, preservation, or reduction in one accompanies or requires a loss, burden, risk, or forgone gain in another under stated assumptions.

## 7. Time architecture

Material temporal relations may include:

- trigger or request time;
- queueing and wait time;
- question-framing and clarification time;
- search, retrieval, observation, experiment, modeling, computation, generation, and reasoning time;
- answer composition and presentation latency;
- validation, verification, review, replication, and challenge time;
- coordination, consultation, consent, and affected-party participation time;
- recommendation, deliberation, and decision time;
- authorization and handoff time;
- execution and action time;
- time to first observable outcome;
- consequence maturation and detection time;
- correction, rollback, recovery, repair, remedy, and recurrence-prevention time;
- learning, formation, capability renewal, and succession time;
- maintenance, update, expiry, and revalidation time; and
- whole-lifecycle and residual-effect horizon.

These intervals can overlap, recur, or belong to different subjects. Summing them without a process model can double-count parallel work; ignoring them can hide transferred delay.

## 8. Immediate latency, end-to-end time, and time to responsible answer

### 8.1 Immediate response latency

Immediate response latency is the interval from an identified trigger to a response or output event. Start and end events, queueing, batching, cache state, retries, network delay, Human preparation, and excluded work must be declared.

Low latency is useful for some purposes but does not establish answer correspondence, quality, currency, completion, or responsible reliance.

### 8.2 Inquiry duration

Inquiry duration begins and ends with governed inquiry events, not necessarily prompt submission and output. It may include framing, Evidence acquisition, analysis, dialogue, experimentation, and qualified no-answer determination.

### 8.3 End-to-end answer time

End-to-end answer time concerns the interval from a declared initiating event to delivery of an identified answer version. It may still exclude later verification, Authorization, use, correction, or consequence.

### 8.4 Time to responsible answer

Time to responsible answer ends only at the reliance-ready condition declared for the intended use. The condition can include material quality review, uncertainty, Authority, affected subjects, challenge, safety, legality, and correction readiness.

Several conditions may coexist: preliminary, provisional, emergency, routine, high-consequence, and revalidated reliance. No one universal timestamp follows.

### 8.5 Time to decision, action, outcome, and correction

Answer availability, decision, execution, outcome, and correction each have separate clocks. A faster answer can leave decision time unchanged; a slower decision can improve or degrade consequence depending on the Domain; rapid action can precede Authorization; observed outcomes can be delayed; correction may dominate lifecycle time.

## 9. Speed comparison and temporal improvement

A “faster” claim should preserve materially stable:

- start and end events;
- question, answer obligation, Scope, and use;
- included work, actors, queueing, waiting, retries, and concurrency;
- answer-quality and responsible-reliance conditions;
- load, case mix, environment, dependencies, and failure modes;
- recurrence, amortization, and lifecycle horizon;
- measurement method, resolution, uncertainty, and missingness;
- affected distributions and tail cases; and
- baseline, comparator, and version.

Median improvement can hide tail delay. Throughput can rise while individual latency worsens. Machine time can fall while Human coordination rises. First-response time can fall while correction time rises. Calendar duration can fall by shifting work before the measured start or after the measured end.

Temporal improvement is therefore phase-, subject-, and use-specific. “Faster overall” is a completeness claim requiring a declared temporal profile.

## 10. Deadlines, urgency, waiting, concurrency, and temporal distribution

### 10.1 Deadlines and temporal adequacy

A deadline is a sourced temporal constraint under an identified purpose and Authority. Meeting it does not establish quality; missing it does not establish uselessness if the result remains valuable or the deadline was arbitrary.

### 10.2 Urgency

Urgency reflects consequence-sensitive time pressure, not merely requester preference. It should identify what worsens with delay, for whom, under what Evidence, and whether provisional or reversible actions exist.

### 10.3 Waiting and queueing

Waiting borne by users, reviewers, providers, affected subjects, or external authorities remains time even when no provider resource is active. Queue exclusion can transfer delay invisibly.

### 10.4 Concurrency and parallelism

Parallel work can reduce calendar duration while increasing total labor, coordination, compute, or error risk. Speedup claims must state whether they concern elapsed time, work time, resource-time, or throughput.

### 10.5 Distribution

Mean or median time can hide high-percentile delay, subgroup disparity, accessibility burden, outages, rare failure recovery, or delayed harm. Temporal distribution and affected subjects remain visible where material.

## 12. Price, payment, budget, resource use, and cost

**Price** is an offered or agreed exchange amount for an identified good, service, right, or obligation under stated terms. It may include margin, subsidy, tax, bundling, or market power and need not equal resource cost or social cost.

**Payment** is an actual transfer. The payer need not be the cost Bearer or beneficiary.

**Budget** is an authorized or planned resource limit or allocation. Staying within budget does not establish low total cost or value; exceeding budget does not establish negative value.

**Resource use** is consumption, occupation, commitment, or degradation of an identified resource. Its cost depends on scarcity, alternatives, timing, affected subjects, and valuation method.

**Cost** can be monetary or non-monetary. Conversion to currency requires a declared method and loss. Not every harm, duty, right, capability, or irreversibility is meaningfully monetizable.

## 13. Direct, indirect, fixed, variable, contingent, and opportunity cost

**Direct costs** are attributed closely to an identified object under a declared method. **Indirect costs** require allocation across objects or activities. The distinction depends on accounting boundary and method, not metaphysical closeness.

**Fixed** and **variable** costs depend on relevant activity or scale range. A fixed cost in one horizon can vary in another.

**Contingent cost** depends on uncertain events, failures, or obligations. Expected value can hide tail severity and non-compensable loss.

**Opportunity cost** concerns the value of foregone alternatives under a declared feasible set and decision perspective. An imagined alternative is not automatically feasible; the selected baseline and Authority matter.

Sunk cost, marginal cost, average cost, incremental cost, and avoided cost may be useful Domain concepts. None substitutes for the total-cost profile universally.

## 14. Lifecycle, dependency, capability, correction, and residual cost

### 14.1 Lifecycle cost

Lifecycle cost includes material acquisition, development, operation, maintenance, change, correction, recovery, retirement, and residual phases over a declared horizon.

### 14.2 Dependency and lock-in cost

Dependency cost concerns vulnerability, switching constraints, provider concentration, interoperability loss, unavailable alternatives, update control, and exit burden. Dependence may be beneficial and still costly or risky.

### 14.3 Capability and formation cost

Automation or assistance can reduce execution labor while increasing supervision, evaluation, recovery, or formation burden. Deskilling, lost practice, reduced future-expert formation, and weakened fallback capability may be material costs or consequences under DR-015.

### 14.4 Error and correction cost

Error cost includes detection, diagnosis, rework, rollback, recovery, communication, affected-subject response, repair, remedy, recurrence prevention, and lost trust or capability where applicable. A low error frequency can coexist with catastrophic tail cost.

### 14.5 Residual cost

Residual cost or effect remains after correction, compensation, repair, or retirement. It can include irreversible harm, persistent dependency, lost data, environmental burden, institutional memory loss, or continuing liability.

## 15. Total cost, boundary, completeness, and uncertainty

“Total cost” is a governed completeness claim, not a promise that every possible effect has been monetized.

A defensible total-cost profile should state:

- cost object and decision question;
- included and excluded subjects, systems, phases, and interfaces;
- baseline, comparator, counterfactual, and feasible alternatives;
- payer, funder, cost Bearers, beneficiaries, providers, and affected subjects;
- material cost families and recognition methods;
- time horizon, recurrence, discounting, residual effects, and terminal assumptions;
- uncertainty, scenario, sensitivity, tail, and missingness treatment;
- transfer, externalization, subsidy, cross-boundary, and intergenerational effects;
- currency and non-monetary measures, conversion assumptions, and non-commensurability;
- allocation, double-counting, shared-cost, and causal-attribution rules;
- assessor, authority, conflicts, challenge, and update conditions; and
- conditions under which “total” must be withdrawn or qualified.

“Known material lifecycle cost within boundary B under method M and horizon H” is often more defensible than an unqualified total.

## 16. Cost Bearers, transfer, externalization, and distribution

### 16.1 Bearer separation

At least the following may differ:

- buyer or purchaser;
- payer or funder;
- provider or seller;
- resource owner;
- direct user;
- labor contributor;
- beneficiary;
- cost Bearer;
- risk Bearer;
- affected subject;
- responsible subject;
- decision Authority; and
- assessor.

A lower price to the purchaser can coincide with higher cost to users, workers, providers, future experts, clients, public institutions, communities, or ecosystems.

### 16.2 Cost transfer

Cost transfer occurs when a burden, resource use, risk, delay, correction duty, or sacrifice moves among subjects, boundaries, phases, or time horizons. Transfer may be authorized, compensated, reciprocal, exploitative, hidden, or disputed.

Transfer is not cost reduction for the whole affected horizon. It may be a legitimate allocation under competent authority, but the allocation and its grounds must remain visible.

### 16.3 Externalization

Externalization occurs when material costs or consequences fall outside the boundary used by the decision, price, budget, or performance claim. Externalized cost can be uncertain or hard to monetize without being zero.

### 16.4 Distribution

Total and average cost can hide who pays, when, and under what capacity to absorb loss. Distribution should preserve ordinary, minority, vulnerable, high-consequence, tail, and future cases where material.

## 17. Valuation, currency, discounting, and non-commensurability

### 17.1 Valuation

Valuation maps a cost, benefit, risk, capability, outcome, or consequence into a declared evaluative form. It can be monetary, ordinal, qualitative, categorical, distributional, capability-based, rights-based, or plural.

Valuation is not neutral observation. It depends on criteria, claimant, authority, information, method, and horizon.

### 17.2 Currency and conversion

Currency enables some comparisons but can conceal non-market effects, unequal purchasing power, uncertainty, distribution, and non-compensable conditions. Conversion requires source, rate, time, and sensitivity.

### 17.3 Discounting and time preference

Discounting represents temporal valuation under a selected rate and model. It can change the apparent importance of future cost, benefit, harm, or residual effect. No discount rate has universal SymK authority.

### 17.4 Non-commensurability

Some values, duties, rights, harms, capabilities, or uncertainties may lack a justified common scale. Non-commensurability does not prohibit every decision; it prohibits pretending that an unauthorized arithmetic conversion resolved the conflict.

Profiles may preserve partial order, thresholds, constraints, vetoes, narrative reasons, or explicit unresolved conflict.

## 18. Value architecture

A material value claim should distinguish:

- value object or bearer;
- claimant and assessment occurrence;
- beneficiary and affected subjects;
- purpose, preference, commitment, duty, right, or criterion;
- Domain and jurisdiction;
- baseline, comparator, counterfactual, and feasible alternatives;
- positive, negative, mixed, conditional, disputed, or unknown relation;
- time horizon, persistence, recurrence, and reversibility;
- uncertainty, robustness, and missingness;
- distribution and Power;
- commensurability and aggregation rule;
- assessor and value or decision Authority;
- consequences, residual effects, correction, and remedy; and
- record, challenge, and revision.

Value does not reside in a system independently of subjects and purposes. The same answer may have epistemic value for inquiry, practical value for a professional, market value for a provider, public value for a community, and negative moral or distributional value under another assessment.

## 19. Utility, usefulness, preference, goal attainment, and satisfaction

### 19.1 Utility

Utility is a representation of preference, welfare, desirability, payoff, or another evaluative relation under a declared theory or model. It may be ordinal or cardinal, individual or collective, expected or realized.

A utility function is a Domain or decision model, not universal SymK semantics. Its arguments, subjects, uncertainty, aggregation, and authority must be explicit.

### 19.2 Usefulness

Practical usefulness retains Stream D's subject-, purpose-, capability-, constraint-, Context-, and time-relative relation. Useful does not imply beneficial, safe, fair, lawful, or valuable overall.

### 19.3 Preference satisfaction

Preference satisfaction concerns whether an identified subject's preferences are met. Preferences may be uninformed, adaptive, manipulated, conflicting, unstable, unlawful, harmful, or outside legitimate authority. Satisfaction is Evidence for a scoped value claim, not universal benefit.

### 19.4 Goal attainment

Goal attainment concerns achievement of an identified target under a declared measure. It does not establish that the goal was legitimate, complete, well-framed, beneficial, or worth its cost.

### 19.5 Satisfaction and experience

User satisfaction, confidence, trust, adoption, retention, or delight can matter for Human use. They remain distinct from answer quality, welfare, benefit, public value, and responsible operation.

## 20. Benefit, avoided harm, preserved capability, and compatibility

### 20.1 Benefit

A benefit claim requires an identified beneficiary, favorable dimension, baseline or counterfactual, causal or comparative basis, time, uncertainty, distribution, and assessor.

Benefit may concern improved capability, access, understanding, answer quality, timeliness, safety, welfare, productivity, resilience, option value, learning, or another Domain state.

### 20.2 Avoided harm

Avoided harm is a counterfactual benefit claim. It requires Evidence that harm was plausible and that the intervention reduced it relative to alternatives. Absence of observed harm alone is insufficient.

### 20.3 Preserved capability and option value

Preserving fallback, recovery, Human evaluation, future-expert formation, interoperability, contestability, or future choice can have value even without immediate output gain. The capability, beneficiary, horizon, and counterfactual must be identified.

### 20.4 Compatibility

Compatibility means conformity with identified constraints, commitments, standards, or interfaces. SymK compatibility, legal compatibility, technical compatibility, and moral compatibility are distinct. Compatibility can be necessary for some value claims but is neither benefit nor sufficiency for value.

### 20.5 Net benefit

Net benefit is an aggregation claim under declared inclusions, conversions, weights, uncertainty, distribution, and authority. It cannot erase non-compensable loss or transfer by arithmetic alone.

## 21. Epistemic, professional, economic, public, moral, and market value

### 21.1 Epistemic value

Epistemic value may concern truth, understanding, warranted belief, error reduction, inquiry capacity, challenge, explanatory power, or preserved uncertainty under an identified epistemic framework. It does not automatically determine action or public value.

### 21.2 Professional value

Professional value may concern capability, quality of judgment, service, autonomy, responsibility, learning, trust, continuity, or standards within a profession. Provider revenue or productivity does not define it universally.

### 21.3 Economic value

Economic value may concern welfare, productivity, surplus, income, cost avoidance, option value, risk, or resource allocation under an economic model. Market price is one observation, not economic value as a whole.

### 21.4 Public value

Public value may concern collective capabilities, institutions, rights, equity, legitimacy, trust, resilience, sustainability, or shared outcomes under public authority and participation. Aggregated private utility does not define it automatically.

### 21.5 Moral value

Moral value concerns evaluation under an ethical framework. Stream E does not select a complete theory. DR-014's accepted minimum preserves affected-subject non-erasure, non-privilege, consequence sensitivity, consent-claim integrity, challenge, response, correction, repair, and visible gaps.

### 21.6 Market value

Market value concerns exchange conditions in a market at a time. It may reflect scarcity, expectations, bargaining power, regulation, subsidy, information asymmetry, or speculation. It does not establish truth, quality, moral value, public value, or total benefit.

## 22. Plural value, conflict, aggregation, and distribution

Value claims can conflict because subjects, purposes, criteria, horizons, counterfactuals, uncertainty, and authority differ.

A value profile should preserve:

- who values what and why;
- who benefits and who bears cost or harm;
- which values are compatible, competing, conditional, or incomparable;
- whether aggregation is descriptive, advisory, authorized, or contested;
- which rights, duties, prohibitions, safety limits, or other conditions are non-compensable;
- whether preferences reflect Power, manipulation, exclusion, or constrained alternatives;
- whether gains and losses occur to the same subjects and at the same time;
- whether compensation, consent, remedy, or alternative design is feasible; and
- how uncertainty and future subjects are treated.

Majority or average value cannot erase material minority loss. One subject's gain does not compensate another automatically. Compensation does not erase every harm or make an unauthorized act legitimate.

## 23. Target, objective, expected result, and intended outcome

### 23.1 Target

A target is a stated reference level or state. It can guide assessment without being legitimate, complete, feasible, or authorized.

### 23.2 Objective

An objective is a purpose or desired state selected for a subject, process, system, policy, or decision. Objective selection requires a source and authority; optimization does not justify the objective.

### 23.3 Expected or predicted result

An expected result is a probabilistic, modeled, conventional, or forecast state. It remains distinct from desired target and actual result.

### 23.4 Intended outcome

An intended outcome is a desired or aimed-at Domain-relevant state connected to an action, decision, or intervention. Intention does not establish causation, realization, legitimacy, or value.

### 23.5 Success criterion

A success criterion is a rule used to judge outcome or progress. Meeting it establishes only what the rule and Evidence support. A narrow criterion can be gamed or omit consequences.

## 24. Actual outcome, output, result, effect, and state

**Output** remains the process-relative artifact, state, signal, or event from Stream C.

**Result** is ordinary vocabulary requiring qualification: it may name output, calculation, finding, decision, outcome, score, or effect.

**Actual outcome** is a realized Domain-relevant state or change relative to a target, expectation, baseline, or counterfactual.

**Effect** is a changed condition attributed causally or practically to an event, action, omission, system, or process under a declared model.

**State** is a condition at a time. A state may be observed without being an outcome of the intervention under study.

A deployment output can exist without Domain outcome. A target can be met while public or moral value is negative. A favorable state can occur without causal Contribution from the system.

## 25. Consequence, side effect, residual effect, and affected horizon

Consequences may be:

- intended or unintended;
- anticipated or unanticipated;
- direct or indirect;
- immediate, delayed, cumulative, persistent, or intergenerational;
- beneficial, harmful, mixed, neutral, disputed, or unknown;
- reversible, correctable, repairable, compensable, or irreversible;
- local, distributed, transferred, externalized, or systemic;
- epistemic, capability, professional, economic, social, institutional, legal, moral, environmental, or technical; and
- observed, inferred, predicted, reported, suppressed, or unmeasured.

A side effect is not necessarily minor or unintended in every usage; the claim must specify its relation to the primary objective. A residual effect remains after action, correction, repair, compensation, or termination.

The affected horizon extends beyond users and Participants to subjects bearing material consequence, including those lacking participation, visibility, bargaining power, legal recognition, or current existence.

## 26. Causal Contribution, attribution, counterfactual, and Responsibility

### 26.1 Causal Contribution

Causal Contribution retains DR-014's model-relative, level-qualified relation. Outcome difference after an answer, decision, or action does not prove causation.

A material causal claim should identify event or state, cause candidate, mechanism, timing, alternatives, confounding, intervention or observational basis, level, uncertainty, and defeaters.

### 26.2 Counterfactual

A counterfactual compares what occurred with what would have occurred under an alternative condition. Counterfactuals are model- and Evidence-dependent; the no-action baseline is not always the relevant alternative.

### 26.3 Attribution

Attribution is a claim assigning content, contribution, effect, value, cost, or outcome to an identified subject or system. It requires the lowest adequate organized level and must not copy properties between components, participants, wholes, providers, users, or successors.

### 26.4 Responsibility

Causal Contribution, benefit, harm, control, Authority, duty, blame, liability, remedy, and Responsibility remain separate. A successful outcome does not allocate Responsibility; a harmful consequence does not identify one residual bearer automatically.

### 26.5 Outcome symmetry

The same attribution rules must apply to favorable and unfavorable outcomes. Expanding the system boundary to claim success and narrowing it to avoid harm is prohibited boundary shopping.

## 27. Evidence for outcomes and consequences

Outcome or consequence observations become Evidence only for identified claims under declared methods.

Material questions include:

- what was observed, measured, inferred, reported, or missing;
- whether the measure captures the Domain construct;
- which target, baseline, comparator, and counterfactual apply;
- temporal ordering, delay, maturation, and censoring;
- confounding, selection, reporting, survivorship, and measurement bias;
- manipulation, strategic behavior, Goodhart pressure, and gaming;
- subgroup, tail, cumulative, transferred, and residual effects;
- alternative explanations and causal model uncertainty;
- source competence, incentives, conflicts, and data access;
- affected-subject reports, complaints, dissent, and suppressed signals; and
- correction, reanalysis, replication, and reopening conditions.

One favorable signal cannot validate the answer, reasoning, decision, action, system, quality profile, cost model, or value claim as a whole.

## 28. Trade-off architecture

### 28.1 Trade-off object

A trade-off claim compares identified alternatives across material dimensions. It must state whether the claim concerns physical necessity, logical incompatibility, resource constraint, budget choice, design limitation, policy, preference, risk tolerance, current capability, or observed correlation.

### 28.2 Necessary versus contingent trade-off

A trade-off is necessary only relative to stated constraints and alternatives. Current architecture, provider policy, missing investment, institutional habit, or poor design can create contingent trade-offs.

### 28.3 Same-subject versus cross-subject trade-off

A subject choosing between its own gains and losses differs from one subject receiving gains while another bears loss. Cross-subject aggregation requires authority and cannot manufacture consent or legitimacy.

### 28.4 Same-time versus intertemporal trade-off

Immediate gain and later loss require horizon, persistence, discounting, uncertainty, reversibility, and future-subject treatment. Short evaluation windows can erase maintenance, correction, deskilling, environmental, or residual effects.

### 28.5 Risk and uncertainty

Expected values can hide catastrophic tails, model uncertainty, ambiguity, and non-compensable harm. Risk tolerance and precaution require competent authority; uncertainty is not a zero-valued cost.

### 28.6 Trade-off response

Possible responses include accept, reject, redesign, mitigate, insure, compensate, stage, monitor, restrict, reverse, defer, or preserve incomparability. SymK does not choose among them universally.

## 29. Constrained value and the Product Vision triad

The Product Vision's “better + faster + lower total cost” should be interpreted as three independently governed comparison families under responsibility and learning constraints:

1. **better:** a materially improved or preserved answer-quality and responsible-use profile for a stable governed question and use;
2. **faster:** a materially improved temporal profile for identified phases and subjects without hiding work, delay, correction, or lifecycle effects; and
3. **lower total cost:** a materially improved cost profile within a declared complete boundary, horizon, counterfactual, distribution, and uncertainty treatment.

The triad does not imply:

- one scalar objective;
- equal weights;
- that every case must improve all three simultaneously;
- that strength in one can compensate every failure in another;
- that a DIA is defined by achieving the triad;
- that project metrics own the concepts;
- that value belongs only to users or purchasers;
- that favorable outcome proves the triad; or
- that SymK authorizes the trade-off.

A **constrained-value profile** may represent quality, time, cost, plural value, affected distribution, Authority, non-compensable conditions, outcomes, consequences, uncertainty, and revision relations without aggregating them universally.

## 34. Human, Scientific, and Engineering View obligations

### 34.1 Human View

The Human View should expose, where material:

- what is claimed to be faster, lower-cost, valuable, beneficial, successful, or traded off;
- start/end events, waiting, review, correction, and lifecycle horizons in comprehensible terms;
- payer, beneficiary, cost Bearer, affected subject, provider, Authority, and responsible subject;
- included and excluded costs, transferred burdens, uncertainty, and residual effects;
- value claimant, purpose, commitments, alternatives, and conflicting values;
- target, actual outcome, causal uncertainty, side effects, and distribution;
- who gains, who loses, whether losses are avoidable or non-compensable, and who authorized the choice;
- what scores, currencies, averages, discounting, or summaries omit; and
- effective challenge, correction, appeal, repair, remedy, and revision paths.

It must not let “instant,” “free,” “efficient,” “valuable,” “successful,” “net positive,” “optimized,” or “approved” labels conceal work, transfer, uncertainty, loss, Power, or consequence.

### 34.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View should expose, where material:

- temporal, cost, value, outcome, consequence, and trade-off constructs;
- operationalizations, instruments, populations, Contexts, boundaries, and horizons;
- baselines, comparators, counterfactuals, alternatives, and causal models;
- data, Evidence, assumptions, uncertainty, missingness, censoring, and sensitivity;
- distributions, tails, subgroups, externalities, and residual effects;
- valuation, currency conversion, discounting, aggregation, and non-commensurability;
- attribution levels, confounding, bias, manipulation, and alternative explanations; and
- replication, monitoring, correction, defeat, and reopening conditions.

It must not convert statistical significance, average effect, expected value, model fit, cost estimate, forecast, or correlation into causality, value, legitimacy, totality, or necessary trade-off.

### 34.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View should expose, where material:

- identifiers and versions for questions, answers, profiles, processes, systems, decisions, actions, and outcomes;
- event timestamps, clocks, queues, retries, concurrency, phases, horizons, and measurement loss;
- cost objects, families, units, Bearers, allocations, boundaries, recurrence, and uncertainty;
- value kinds, claimants, beneficiaries, affected subjects, criteria, weights, constraints, and authority metadata;
- targets, actual outcomes, observations, causal claims, side effects, residuals, and distributions;
- metrics, models, currencies, discount rates, thresholds, objectives, and decision-rule lineage;
- transfer, externalization, dependency, failure, correction, rollback, recovery, and decommissioning; and
- mappings to Human and Scientific meanings and unresolved mismatches.

It must not treat telemetry availability, timer precision, invoice data, budget field, objective function, optimization result, KPI, dashboard, deployment, or status as semantic completeness, total cost, value, causality, legitimacy, or consequence.

### 34.4 Cross-view alignment

The Views may use different forms and granularity but must preserve the same material objects, boundaries, clocks, cost Bearers, value claimants, affected subjects, baselines, counterfactuals, horizons, uncertainty, causal claims, Authority, trade-offs, and lineage. Transformations and omissions must be declared.

View disagreement is governable Evidence. It may reveal hidden waiting, invalid cost allocation, inaccessible burden, missing affected subjects, proxy drift, causal overclaim, valuation conflict, or legitimate Domain pluralism. No View prevails automatically because it is more intuitive, measurable, monetizable, authoritative, or operational.
