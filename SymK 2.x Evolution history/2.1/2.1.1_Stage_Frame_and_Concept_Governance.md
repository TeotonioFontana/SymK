# SymK 2.1.1 — Stage Frame and Concept Governance

**Status:** Stage-Accepted for continuation of SymK 2.1  
**Decision target:** `SYMK-2X-DR-009`  
**Accepted by/date:** Project steward, 18 August 2026  
**Normative effect:** Provisional program authority governing subsequent 2.1 analysis; admits no individual concept, Ratifies no norm, and changes no canonical artifact

## 1. Decision question

> By what method will 2.1 decide that a concept or distinction belongs to SymK, and how will its meaning remain stable across human explanation, machine-semantic expression, project specialization, and implementation?

## 2. Why 2.1 requires a new governing method

The inherited concept corpus contains important insights:

- the concept is primary and representations are projections;
- definitions must survive counterexamples and elimination attacks;
- identity, scope, dependencies, evolution, and engineering consequences matter;
- implementation structures must not be confused with semantic concepts;
- concept history and unresolved questions should remain visible.

The corpus nevertheless cannot be continued unchanged after SymK 2.0:

1. the historical method asks mainly whether a candidate is an irreducible engineering primitive, while 2.1 asks whether it is necessary to the accepted SymK identity and constitutional vocabulary;
2. several concepts essential to SymK may be composite, relational, reciprocal, or dependent without being eliminable from the foundation;
3. the Primitive Evaluation Standard, backlog, dialogue protocol, confidence assessment, and review template are currently only headings, so their asserted governance is not operationally specified;
4. the surviving Primitive Review material is uneven and does not establish authority for the catalog’s current statuses;
5. the old catalog begins from Entity and Relationship, while the accepted 2.0 identity makes Knowledge, Intelligence, cooperation, formation, responsibility, amplification, reasoning, and answers semantically central;
6. the former three-layer specification mixes semantic communication with reference engineering models and risks making JSON, YAML, or another current representation appear constitutive;
7. the existing `FC-*` artifacts predate the two-axis authority model and do not consistently distinguish S2 meaning from E2 projection.

Therefore, Primitive Review attacks remain useful evidence techniques, but they cannot be the sole governing method of 2.1.

## 3. Foundational does not mean metaphysically primitive

For SymK 2.1, **foundational** means necessary to the coherent expression and governance of SymK across its declared jurisdiction. It does not automatically mean:

- metaphysically irreducible;
- ontologically atomic;
- independent of every other concept;
- a complete theory of the phenomenon;
- universal outside SymK;
- implemented as one class or schema;
- or entitled to its own `FC-*` file before the 2.5 reconstruction.

A concept may be foundational because removing it makes the SymK identity, value chain, or constitutional inquiry incoherent. Knowledge and Intelligence may therefore be reciprocally dependent and still be foundational, provided each retains independent semantic anchors and counterexamples.

Conversely, a highly reusable implementation abstraction may fail foundational admission when it is not necessary to SymK’s universal meaning.

## 4. Methodological vocabulary

These are role distinctions for conducting 2.1, not final dispositions of similarly named candidates such as Relationship or Property.

| Role | Meaning in the 2.1 method | Must not be confused with |
|---|---|---|
| Term | A word, phrase, symbol, or identifier used to refer | The concept itself; one term may be ambiguous and one concept may have several terms |
| Concept | A governed unit of meaning used to recognize, distinguish, relate, or reason about a subject within declared scope | Its definition, document, class, schema, or implementation |
| Definition | A governed expression of the criteria and boundaries through which a concept is interpreted | Exhaustive possession of the concept or proof that the definition is final |
| Constitutive condition | A condition affecting what counts as an instance or valid use of a concept | A universal duty to build or act in one way |
| Distinction | A boundary that prevents two meanings or roles from collapsing | Necessarily a standalone concept artifact |
| Property | A quality, state, or predicate attributed within scope | Necessarily an Entity or independent foundational concept |
| Relationship | A semantic association among subjects, roles, or concepts | Necessarily a pointer, foreign key, edge implementation, or independently admitted foundational concept |
| Principle | A general direction or rule for reasoning, design, or conduct, normally derived from deeper commitments | An irreducible constitutional axiom |
| Axiom | A candidate fundamental, universal, independent constitutional commitment tested in 2.2 | A definition or frequently useful principle |
| Model | A selected structure that emphasizes particular dimensions for explanation, reasoning, or engineering | The modeled subject in full |
| Representation | A form in which selected aspects are expressed, communicated, stored, or manipulated | The represented concept or truth |
| Projection | The governed relationship mapping selected dimensions from a source into another representation | Exhaustive equivalence or ownership transfer |
| Implementation | A concrete technical realization of selected specifications and designs | Semantic or constitutional authority merely because it operates |

### 4.1 Semantic criteria versus normative duties

Definitions state what a concept means or what counts under it. Axioms, principles, standards, and policies state what must, should, or may be done.

Some concepts—such as responsibility or authorization—contain normative dimensions. Even then, an artifact must distinguish:

- semantic criteria used to recognize the concept;
- constitutive consequences of applying the concept;
- and independent duties imposed on participants or systems.

This prevents a definition from smuggling an unreviewed axiom into the conceptual layer, and prevents 2.2 from treating every semantic relation as constitutional.

## 5. The two-stage admission and disposition process

Admission is not a single intuition that a term “feels fundamental.” It consists of an eligibility gate followed by an explicit disposition.

### 5.1 Stage A — Foundational eligibility gate

A candidate qualifies for foundational consideration only when all six tests survive review:

1. **Identity or constitutional necessity** — the concept is required to express or interpret the accepted SymK identity, jurisdiction, complete value chain, or candidate constitutional commitments.
2. **SymK-level reach** — a minimum meaning is applicable across the class of SymK-compatible projects, even though projects may add narrower specializations.
3. **Non-delegability** — delegating the entire meaning to derived projects, engineering, or implementation would permit materially incompatible interpretations of SymK.
4. **Material distinctiveness** — collapsing the candidate into a neighbor would create a demonstrable epistemic, constitutional, or engineering failure.
5. **Bounded definability** — SymK can govern the minimum necessary meaning without claiming the whole surrounding philosophical or scientific field.
6. **Challengeability** — its scope, relations, exclusions, examples, counterexamples, and failure conditions can be stated well enough to permit criticism and evidence.

Cross-domain translation is required evidence for Tests 2 and 3. It is not proof of philosophical universality.

### 5.2 Stage B — Disposition test

Passing the eligibility gate does not automatically require an independent `FC-*` artifact. The candidate receives one of six dispositions:

| Disposition | Test | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Foundational concept | Has a stable independently referable subject; is not exhausted by another admitted concept, relationship, or property; needs its own scope and change lineage | Enters the 2.1 conceptual baseline; final `FC-*` packaging remains for 2.5 |
| Governed supporting distinction | Is necessary to preserve meaning but is best governed inside or across other concepts | Recorded as a binding semantic distinction at the status granted to its owning record |
| Derived-project specialization | Becomes necessary only after domain, profession, product, organization, or deployment scope is introduced | Routed to P1/P2 or narrower ownership with parent obligations preserved |
| Engineering representation or projection | Selects a form, constraint, model, or realization of a broader meaning | Routed to S2, P2, E2, or I1 by declared authority and use; format alone does not decide |
| Excluded from SymK | Lies outside SymK jurisdiction or adds no necessary distinction | Preserved with reasons; may remain legitimate elsewhere |
| Deferred pending evidence | Cannot yet receive a responsible disposition | Remains explicit with consequence, destination, and reopening evidence |

### 5.3 Three independent axes

Every record must keep three questions separate:

1. **Disposition:** What role does this candidate have?
2. **Maturity and authority:** Is the analysis Questioned, Proposed, Contested, Stage-Accepted, Ratified, Superseded, or Retired?
3. **Artifact form:** In which human, formal, graphical, or engineering representations is it expressed?

A candidate may be a **Proposed foundational concept** without a final `FC-*` package. A JSON schema may be a mature projection without making its subject foundational. A file called `FC-001` does not establish either foundationality or acceptance by name.

## 6. Concept evaluation protocol

Every material candidate or concept cluster in 2.1 must pass through the following sequence:

1. **Problem statement** — identify the ambiguity or failure that makes the concept necessary.
2. **Inherited uses** — collect Product Vision, Foundation Paper, constitutional, legacy, project, external, and implementation formulations with their authority statuses.
3. **Term normalization** — identify synonyms, homonyms, translations, capitalized SymK uses, and ordinary-language uses.
4. **Scope and jurisdiction** — state domain, population, task, time, stakes, bearer, and authority boundaries where relevant.
5. **Candidate definitions** — preserve credible alternatives before selecting a working definition.
6. **Distinction analysis** — identify neighbors, overlaps, exclusions, and prohibited equivalences.
7. **Dependency analysis** — classify what the candidate presupposes, constitutes, constrains, specializes, or represents.
8. **Admission and disposition tests** — apply Section 5 explicitly rather than assuming the result.
9. **Adversarial tests** — attempt elimination, substitution, boundary cases, category-error cases, harmful cases, and translation across domains and bearer types.
10. **Two-lane analysis** — identify what humans must understand, what machines must be able to distinguish, and what cannot currently be projected without loss.
11. **Authority and impact analysis** — record constitutional, conceptual, Foundation Paper, project, engineering, and operational consequences.
12. **Decision and preservation** — record the proposed conclusion, dissent, confidence, unresolved questions, destination, and reopening conditions.

The sequence may iterate. Iteration must update the evidence and lineage rather than silently replacing the prior formulation.

## 7. Required 2.1 conceptual-analysis anatomy

Each work package may group related concepts, but every material disposition must remain individually recoverable through:

1. concept or distinction identifier and working term;
2. problem and constitutional relevance;
3. primary owner, reach, authority kind, status, and version context;
4. source formulations and alternatives;
5. minimal working definition;
6. constitutive conditions;
7. scope, bearer, Context, and applicability;
8. related concepts and typed dependencies;
9. exclusions and prohibited interpretations;
10. examples, counterexamples, and boundary cases;
11. human-semantic explanation;
12. machine-semantic preservation obligations;
13. known unrepresented or unrepresentable dimensions;
14. downstream normative and engineering consequences;
15. dissent, confidence, open questions, and challenge evidence;
16. decision and revision lineage.

This is the required anatomy of 2.1 analysis, not the final Canonical Concept Package structure deferred to 2.5.

## 8. Dependency and reciprocal-definition rules

### 8.1 Dependency is a graph

Concepts must not be forced into a false linear hierarchy. Each dependency must be typed at least as one of:

- **presupposes** — one concept cannot be applied without another;
- **constitutes** — one concept contributes to what another is;
- **constrains** — one limits valid interpretation or application of another;
- **contrasts with** — the boundary between concepts carries material meaning;
- **specializes** — a narrower concept preserves and enriches a parent meaning;
- **represents or projects** — an expression selects dimensions from a source;
- **reciprocally develops** — each may change or enable the other over time without becoming identical.

The existence of dependency does not make the dependent concept non-foundational.

### 8.2 Acceptable reciprocity

A reciprocal pair or cluster is acceptable only when:

1. each concept has at least one independent semantic anchor not stated solely through the other;
2. instances and counterexamples can distinguish the concepts;
3. the direction and type of each dependency are explicit;
4. removing either concept creates a stated loss rather than merely changing vocabulary;
5. the reciprocal relationship describes interaction or development, not an empty definitional loop.

“Knowledge is what Intelligence has, and Intelligence is what has Knowledge” fails this test. An account in which Intelligence includes scoped adaptive capacity while Knowledge concerns a scoped epistemic achievement may support a meaningful reciprocal relationship, subject to the substantive review in 2.1.3.

## 9. Two lanes and semantic authority

### 9.1 The concept is not either lane

Semantic authority belongs to the governed concept record and its decision lineage, not inherently to natural language, logic, OWL, JSON, diagrams, code, or any other notation.

The two lanes are coordinated expressions of the same governed subject:

- the **human-semantic lane** must communicate purpose, meaning, scope, rationale, distinctions, examples, counterexamples, uncertainty, dissent, and interpretation;
- the **machine-semantic lane** must make selected identities, types, relations, scope, applicability, constraints, dependencies, lineage, and declared loss computable or mechanically inspectable.

### 9.2 Human-semantic nucleus

Every 2.1 concept requires a disciplined natural-language semantic nucleus because purpose, scope, interpretation, dissent, and known limits cannot be presumed fully expressible in one formalism. Natural language is not treated as perfectly precise or infallible; ambiguity is exposed through definitions, distinctions, examples, counterexamples, and challenge.

### 9.3 Machine-semantic projection

A machine-semantic expression may be exact and authoritative for the formal claims explicitly incorporated within its declared expressive scope. It does not thereby become an exhaustive definition or acquire authority over omitted dimensions.

Every material machine-semantic projection must eventually declare:

- source concept and version;
- purpose, audience, and owning layer;
- source statements it claims to express;
- preserved identities, distinctions, and constraints;
- transformations and added restrictions;
- omitted or inexpressible dimensions;
- known semantic loss;
- verification or alignment evidence;
- and its authority status.

2.1 establishes these obligations. The final alignment-contract format belongs to 2.5, formal-language selection to 2.5/2.9, and lowering to 2.9.

### 9.4 Mismatch rule

When the human and machine expressions materially disagree:

1. neither silently rewrites the other;
2. the affected compatibility or completeness claim becomes qualified;
3. the mismatch is classified as ambiguity, omission, strengthening, distortion, version conflict, or source defect;
4. the nearest adequate owner corrects the artifact it owns;
5. evidence may challenge the source concept without allowing a projection or implementation to amend it automatically.

Where an expressly governed formal clause covers the exact disputed question, it controls that formal claim within its declared scope. It does not control the concept’s broader meaning by implication.

### 9.5 Disposition of the historical three-layer note

`SYMK-NOTE-001 — Multi-Layer Concept Specification` correctly states that the concept is primary and representations are secondary. Its three sections should be reinterpreted provisionally as:

- Human Definition → human-semantic lane;
- Semantic Model → possible bridge or machine-semantic projection, depending on authority and notation;
- Reference Engineering Model → one or more E2 projections by default, not a mandatory coequal semantic layer.

Its requirement that every `FC-*` contain JSON, YAML, graph, or reference contracts is not adopted by 2.1. Whether and when reference models belong inside a Canonical Concept Package remains for 2.5.

## 10. Status and authority model

2.1 uses the program Evolution Status Model rather than silently preserving the older concept-catalog status vocabulary.

| 2.1 status | Effect |
|---|---|
| Questioned | Candidate or inherited formulation is under explicit examination; no governing effect |
| Proposed | A reasoned definition, disposition, or relationship is available for review; no governing effect |
| Contested | Material alternatives or objections remain active; no governing effect unless a narrower accepted constraint is identified separately |
| Deferred | Resolution has an explicit destination and no present governing effect beyond preserving the question |
| Stage-Accepted | Provisional program authority for subsequent 2.x analysis within declared scope; challengeable and not constitutionally Ratified |
| Ratified | Available only through constituted authority; not granted by 2.1 stage work |
| Superseded or Retired | Historical status with lineage preserved |

The historical labels “Under Evaluation,” “Provisionally Accepted,” and “Accepted” remain evidence about prior intent. They are not automatically mapped to Stage-Accepted or Ratified status. Their disposition belongs to the concept reconstruction and legacy work in 2.4–2.5.

A Stage-Accepted 2.1 definition governs later 2.x analysis as a program baseline. It does not automatically promote an existing `FC-*` file into a final governed S2 package.

## 11. Change, conflict, and evidence

### 11.1 Ownership

The S2 owner governs the universal SymK meaning, status, lineage, and semantic obligations of an admitted concept. P2 owners govern domain specializations. E2 and I1 owners govern projections and realizations within their declared authority.

### 11.2 Change

A material semantic change must state whether it:

- clarifies the same concept;
- narrows or broadens scope;
- changes constitutive conditions;
- replaces the concept;
- splits or merges concepts;
- or revises only a representation.

Exact identity and versioning mechanics remain for 2.5, but no change may reuse an identifier in a way that hides replacement or incompatibility.

### 11.3 Evidence return

Project, engineering, implementation, operational, scientific, professional, legal, and affected-party evidence may challenge any concept. Evidence supplies epistemic force, not automatic amendment authority.

An isolated implementation inconvenience normally challenges the implementation or projection first. Recurrent cross-realization or cross-domain evidence may challenge an S2 concept directly. A single severe consequence may justify immediate containment and direct review without waiting for recurrence.

## 12. Foundation Paper relationship

Foundation Papers preserve inquiry, arguments, sources, alternatives, interpretations, candidate commitments, and dissent. They may discuss more than the accepted conceptual baseline when the additional material is marked clearly as hypothesis, alternative, open inquiry, or authorial interpretation.

They must not:

- present a Proposed or contested definition as Stage-Accepted;
- use publication or rhetorical fluency to acquire semantic or constitutional authority;
- hide divergence from the conceptual baseline;
- or overwrite v0.1 history during the v0.2 revision.

Every 2.1 work package records paper impacts in the revision ledger. The integrated 2.1.9 baseline controls the bounded v0.2 revision plan; 2.1.10 performs the human-communication revision and PDF publication.

## 13. Axiom-readiness gate

A concept need not be philosophically final or fully formalized before 2.2. It is ready for constitutional analysis when:

1. its minimum definition and SymK scope are explicit;
2. neighboring concepts and prohibited conflations are explicit;
3. reciprocal and downstream dependencies are visible;
4. material counterexamples do not make the working meaning incoherent;
5. open questions are bounded and do not make candidate axioms uninterpretable;
6. semantic criteria are distinguishable from normative duties;
7. a candidate axiom can cite the concept version or decision lineage it uses;
8. a changed concept would trigger an identifiable axiom-impact review.

2.2 may challenge a 2.1 definition when axiom analysis exposes a defect. Sequential control does not prohibit governed feedback.

## 14. Method compatibility tests

### 14.1 Implementation abstraction presented as a foundation

A JSON object used by several projects is convenient and reusable. It fails foundational admission unless the meaning it carries is necessary, nondelegable, and independently governed. Reuse alone is insufficient.

### 14.2 Domain concept presented as universal

LexBrain’s `LegalAuthority` may be indispensable to legal reasoning but remains P2 unless a distinct universal SymK concept is demonstrated. Domain importance does not create universal reach.

### 14.3 Dependent concept rejected as non-primitive

A candidate depends on Context and Relationship. Dependency alone does not defeat foundationality. The test is whether the candidate remains necessary and independently meaningful within SymK.

### 14.4 Formal projection adds cardinality

An OWL or schema projection requires exactly one value where the human-semantic record permits several. This is a declared strengthening or a projection conflict, not a silent clarification of the concept.

### 14.5 Natural-language ambiguity

Two reasonable readers interpret “responsible reliance” differently. The response is to refine scope, distinctions, examples, and counterexamples and, where possible, add aligned formal constraints. Ambiguity is not proof that a formalism should own all meaning.

### 14.6 Running implementation contradicts the concept

The implementation creates valuable evidence. It does not become the concept. The nearest adequate owner determines whether the implementation, projection, project specialization, or S2 concept is defective.

### 14.7 Foundation Paper develops a new possibility

A v0.2 paper may discuss an alternative bearer theory beyond the accepted baseline if it marks that theory as open inquiry. The explanation does not amend the concept by publication.

### 14.8 Human and machine lanes disagree

The package cannot claim unqualified alignment. The exact mismatch and affected scope remain visible until the owning layers correct their respective artifacts or record a governed challenge.

## 15. Alternatives considered

### Alternative A — Continue the ontology-first Primitive Evaluation sequence

**Strength:** preserves existing work and adversarial elimination tests.  
**Failure:** foundationality is reduced too closely to primitiveness; current standards are incomplete; constitutional and jurisdictional necessity are underrepresented.

### Alternative B — Create a natural-language glossary only

**Strength:** accessible and fast.  
**Failure:** does not preserve dependencies, authority, projection obligations, counterexamples, or machine-semantic needs.

### Alternative C — Select a formal language first

**Strength:** early precision and automated consistency.  
**Failure:** the formalism would preselect what can be expressed and risk defining the concept by representational convenience.

### Alternative D — Let each project define the concepts it needs

**Strength:** maximum local freedom.  
**Failure:** destroys the stable common meaning required for SymK inheritance and cross-project identity.

### Alternative E — Jurisdiction-first, purpose-led, evidence-tested conceptual governance

**Strength:** preserves the accepted SymK identity, bounded philosophical scope, semantic ownership, counterevidence, derived-project autonomy, and two-lane obligations.  
**Risk:** requires more explicit records and may become bureaucratic if every minor term is over-governed.  
**Proposed:** adopt with a materiality threshold and the six dispositions.

## 16. Scope boundary

This decision governs how 2.1 conducts conceptual inquiry. It does not:

- admit Entity, Relationship, Identity, Context, Knowledge, Intelligence, or any other candidate;
- finalize any definition;
- select or Ratify an axiom;
- reconstruct `FC-*`, `FP-*`, or `SYMK-P-*` artifacts;
- choose a formal language;
- define final concept-package, specialization, compatibility, or versioning procedures;
- revise Foundation Papers;
- or migrate canonical artifacts.

## 17. Stage-Accepted decision — SYMK-2X-DR-009

> **SymK 2.1 shall govern foundational concepts through a jurisdiction-first, purpose-led, evidence- and counterexample-tested method rather than treating ontological primitiveness, historical file status, or implementation reuse as sufficient. A candidate must survive tests of identity or constitutional necessity, SymK-level reach, non-delegability, material distinctiveness, bounded definability, and challengeability, then receive an explicit disposition as foundational concept, governed supporting distinction, derived-project specialization, engineering projection, exclusion, or deferral. Foundationality, maturity status, and artifact form remain separate. Dependencies may be reciprocal when each concept retains independent semantic anchors and distinguishable counterexamples. Semantic authority belongs to the governed concept record and lineage: the human-semantic lane carries the necessary interpretive nucleus, while machine-semantic projections may govern declared formal claims within their scope but may not silently redefine omitted meaning. Material lane mismatches qualify compatibility and return to the appropriate owners. Existing concept catalogs, Primitive Reviews, and the three-layer note remain evidence; their prior statuses and package requirements are not adopted automatically. Stage-Accepted 2.1 concepts provide provisional program meaning for later 2.x work, while final concept packages remain for 2.5, formal-language and lowering choices for 2.5/2.9, and constitutional selection for 2.2.**

## 18. Consequences of acceptance

### 18.1 For 2.1

- 2.1.2 begins with a complete inventory rather than continuing the inherited primitive order.
- Every candidate receives an explicit disposition and authority status.
- Concept clusters may be reviewed together when dependencies are reciprocal.
- The Concept Register gains typed dependencies and decision lineage progressively.

### 18.2 For the existing concept corpus

- Current `FC-*`, property, relationship, methodology, PRR, and multi-layer artifacts remain unchanged and become review inputs.
- “Accepted” and “Provisionally Accepted” historical labels do not acquire 2.x authority automatically.
- JSON/YAML/reference models remain projections whose placement and necessity will be reconsidered in 2.5.

### 18.3 For Foundation Papers

- Every conceptual package records revision impacts.
- Papers may preserve wider inquiry but must distinguish it from the Stage-Accepted baseline.
- v0.2 publication remains blocked until the 2.1.9 integrated baseline authorizes it.

### 18.4 For 2.2 and later stages

- 2.2 receives an explicit axiom-readiness gate and versioned semantic lineage.
- 2.5 receives dispositions and preservation obligations rather than a predetermined file anatomy.
- 2.6–2.7 receive stable parent meanings plus visible areas requiring project evidence.
- 2.9 receives declared formalization and lowering obligations without a premature language decision.

## 19. Preserved dissent and open questions

1. The six admission tests may be too strict for concepts whose universal relevance is plausible but not yet demonstrated across mature derived projects.
2. A mandatory human-semantic nucleus may retain ambiguity or privilege human interpretive habits; formal and non-textual Intelligences may expose limitations.
3. Allowing expressly governed formal claims to control questions within their scope may create difficult boundary disputes between “formal claim” and “broader meaning.”
4. The boundary between a foundational concept and a governed supporting distinction may remain contestable until 2.5 tests artifact packaging.
5. Materiality thresholds are necessary to prevent bureaucracy but cannot yet be reduced to a universal quantitative rule.
6. Final concept identity, splitting, merging, version pinning, and compatibility mechanics remain deferred to 2.5–2.6 under DQ-022 and DQ-024.

These questions do not prevent use of the proposed method, but they must remain visible during 2.1.2 and may reopen DR-009 if the method systematically misclassifies candidates.
