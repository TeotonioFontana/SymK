# SymK 2.1.3C — Joint Bearer and Attribution Model

**Status:** Analytical working proposal — no DR-011 authority
**Date:** 20 August 2026
**Working baseline:** `2.1-dev.11`
**Evidence record:** `SYMK-2X-EV-090`
**Depends on:** `2.1.3A_KNOWLEDGE_INDEPENDENT_ANCHOR_ANALYSIS.md` and `2.1.3B_INTELLIGENCE_INDEPENDENT_ANCHOR_ANALYSIS.md`
**Structural authority:** Stage-Accepted DR-010

## 1. Analytical question

> What common attribution structure is required to say that a subject bears Knowledge, Intelligence, capability, competence, or another predicate without confusing the subject, the actual relation, the claim, its evidence, its assessor, its authority, or its Representation?

The independent anchors both require Bearer, Scope, Context, evidence, and challenge. The common structure must be strong enough to prevent opportunistic system boundaries and weak enough not to make one predicate's conditions define every other attribution.

## 2. Core distinction

SymK must keep four layers separate:

1. **Bearing relation:** the subject actually satisfies a predicate or status within the relevant conditions.
2. **Attribution claim:** a source asserts that the bearing relation holds.
3. **Attribution assessment:** an assessor evaluates the claim under criteria and evidence and assigns a governed status.
4. **Attribution record:** one or more Representations preserve the claim, assessment, evidence, Scope, Context, lineage, and challenge state.

These layers can diverge. A Bearer may possess a capability before anyone attributes it. An attribution may be false. A correct attribution can be supported by weak reasons. An authorized assessment can govern use without creating the underlying Knowledge or Intelligence.

## 3. Working definitions

> **Bearer is the role occupied by the subject to which a predicate, capability, status, relation, or responsibility is attributed within Scope.**

> **A bearing relation is the scoped semantic relation in which a subject actually satisfies the conditions of an attributed predicate.**

> **An attribution claim is a fallible assertion that an identified subject occupies the Bearer role for a specified predicate within declared Scope and relevant Context.**

> **An attribution assessment is a governed evaluation of an attribution claim against declared criteria, evidence, alternatives, and authority.**

These definitions preserve DR-010's Bearer role while adding the distinctions needed by Knowledge and Intelligence. They do not turn Bearer into an Entity class or make every attribution a first-class engineering object.

## 4. Qualified attribution structure

The human-semantic model is:

```text
Subject identity
    │
    │ candidate Bearer of
    ▼
Attributed predicate or profile
    │
    ├── actual Scope and time
    ├── relevant Context
    ├── predicate-specific criteria
    ├── mode or capability dimensions
    └── dependencies and resources

Attribution claim about that relation
    │
    ├── source/proposer
    ├── represented subject and predicate versions
    ├── declared Scope and Context
    ├── evidence and grounds
    ├── tests, failures, and alternatives
    ├── assessor and method
    ├── authority and intended use
    ├── status, confidence, and uncertainty
    └── challenge, revision, expiry, and lineage
```

This is a semantic inventory, not a schema. Particular technical records are Projections whose additions, omissions, cardinalities, and loss must be declared.

## 5. Common validity conditions

Every material Knowledge or Intelligence attribution must make the following recoverable:

| Condition | Required question | Failure prevented |
|---|---|---|
| Predicate identity | Exactly which concept, mode, capability, or profile is attributed, under which version? | Bare labels and moving definitions |
| Bearer identity | Which subject is claimed to bear it, and what preserves that subject's continuity? | Identifier equality treated as Identity |
| Boundary | Which components, people, artifacts, tools, resources, and environment are inside or outside the Bearer? | Opportunistic inclusion and exclusion |
| Scope | Where, when, for which domains, tasks, populations, and variation does the attribution reach? | Universalization from one case |
| Context | Which conditions affect interpretation, evidence, manifestation, or application? | Hidden material conditions |
| Criteria | What predicate-specific conditions must hold? | One generic attribution test for every concept |
| Evidence | Which observations, grounds, processes, competences, encounters, tests, and failures support the claim? | Status or confidence replacing grounds |
| Dependencies/resources | What priors, representations, tools, collaborators, access, time, computation, and institutional support were required? | Attributing the whole result to the visible component |
| Source | Who or what makes the claim, and from which position? | Claim origin disappearing |
| Assessment | Who or what evaluates it, through which method and comparison class? | Self-assertion becoming validation |
| Authority/use | What decision or reliance may the assessment govern? | Epistemic judgment becoming unlimited authorization |
| Alternatives | Which competing Bearers, causal explanations, errors, luck, leakage, or aggregation hypotheses remain? | Premature closure |
| Status/uncertainty | Is it observed, proposed, accepted for Scope, contested, restricted, expired, or superseded? | A boolean presenting false certainty |
| Temporal validity | Which changes in version, members, tools, environment, or evidence require re-evaluation? | Timeless capability and Knowledge claims |
| Challenge/lineage | How can the claim be challenged, revised, or traced to earlier states? | Canonical status becoming infallible |

Common structure does not imply common criteria. Knowledge and Intelligence must preserve their own constitutive conditions.

## 6. Predicate-specific criteria

### 6.1 Knowledge attribution

In addition to the common conditions, a Knowledge attribution must identify:

- Knowledge mode: propositional, practical, familiarity, or an explicit combination;
- proposition, task class, or subject of familiarity;
- truth or the appropriate mode-specific success condition;
- the Bearer's epistemically significant state, competence, process, or encounter;
- the non-accidental connection among Bearer, grounds, and success;
- defeaters, counterevidence, relevant alternatives, and uncertainty;
- and the distinction between the Knowledge attribution and any later reliance authorization.

A claim, asset, Representation, or accepted status does not satisfy these criteria merely by carrying detailed metadata.

### 6.2 Intelligence attribution

In addition to the common conditions, an Intelligence attribution must identify:

- substrate and organized System supporting the Bearer;
- claimed capability dimensions and profile;
- integration mechanisms at the attributed level;
- relevant variation, novelty, transfer, and comparison class;
- performance and failure evidence under controlled changes;
- priors, training, experience, tools, collaborators, and resource limits;
- alternative explanations such as memorization, leakage, lookup, luck, or external control;
- and separate assessments for learning, adaptation, agency, autonomy, rationality, understanding, consciousness, moral standing, authority, and responsibility where any is claimed.

An Intelligence label, participant type, benchmark, or successful output does not satisfy these criteria by itself.

## 7. Candidate Bearer classes

Bearer classes are not ontological kinds defined here. They are recurring attribution patterns whose boundaries require different evidence.

| Candidate pattern | Minimum boundary question | Knowledge-specific risk | Intelligence-specific risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual biological subject | What preserves the same subject across time and changing state? | Mistaking testimony, credential, or memory report for Knowledge | Treating Human type, IQ, or one performance as Intelligence |
| Artificial executing system | Is the Bearer the model, runtime, configured agent, toolchain, service, or deployment? | Treating stored weights or generated text as epistemic uptake | Crediting hidden retrieval, prompts, tools, or humans to the model alone |
| Collective/team | What coordination and integration produce a group-level state or capacity? | Calling pooled information collective Knowledge without integrated access or use | Calling averaged or aggregated outputs emergent Intelligence |
| Institution | Which roles, records, procedures, memory, authority, and revision mechanisms persist beyond members? | Confusing institutional acceptance or legal authority with Knowledge | Reifying organizational outcomes without system-level causal evidence |
| Distributed system | Which coupling, communication, timing, memory, and control relations constitute one system? | Claiming a distributed epistemic state when evidence remains disconnected | Selecting a convenient network boundary after success |
| Hybrid human-artificial system | Which dependencies and feedback loops make the combined capacity irreducible to one participant? | Attributing joint grounds or competence to the most visible contributor | Counting human/tool assistance for success but excluding it from the Bearer profile |
| Static artifact or repository | Does any organized mode-specific state or capacity exist at the artifact level? | Treating represented content as a knowing subject | Treating storage, code, weights, or connectivity as Intelligence |

Static artifacts can be components, resources, evidence, or representations. They are not excluded metaphysically from every possible organized system, but passive containment alone does not qualify them as Knowledge or Intelligence Bearers.

## 8. Boundary and level rules

### 8.1 Explanatory-level rule

Attribute at the lowest organized level that explains the claimed capacity or epistemic relation without omitting constitutive integration. A component should receive the attribution when its own organization and evidence suffice. A larger system should receive it when the capacity depends materially on coordination unavailable to the component alone.

### 8.2 Stable-boundary rule

The descriptive boundary used to claim success must remain visible when failures, dependencies, cost, authority, and consequences are analyzed. Responsibility may ultimately belong to different Bearers or roles, but changing the capability boundary opportunistically is prohibited.

### 8.3 Resource-versus-component rule

A resource becomes part of the candidate Bearer only when the claimed capacity depends on sufficiently stable, integrated, and identity-relevant coupling. Mere availability, occasional consultation, or external supply does not automatically make it a component; material dependence must nevertheless remain disclosed.

### 8.4 Temporal-boundary rule

Changing members, models, prompts, tools, datasets, procedures, or environments may preserve, revise, or replace the Bearer. Identifier continuity alone does not settle the question. Each attribution must state which changes preserve its validity.

### 8.5 Multiple-level rule

The same event may support distinct attributions at several levels without duplicating one predicate automatically. A person may know a fact; a team may possess a distributed procedure; and an institution may maintain an integrated epistemic capability. Each requires its own Bearer, criteria, and evidence.

## 9. Roles that must remain distinct

For any attribution, the following may be different subjects:

- Bearer of the predicate;
- subject represented by the claim;
- source or proposer of the attribution;
- producer of the evidence;
- assessor or validator;
- authority adopting the assessment for use;
- actor applying the result;
- owner or maintainer of a Representation;
- person or institution responsible for consequences;
- and affected party with challenge standing.

No role transfers automatically because one subject occupies another. In particular:

- source is not necessarily Bearer;
- assessor is not necessarily authority;
- authority is not necessarily epistemically correct;
- causal contributor is not necessarily author, decision-maker, or responsible party;
- represented subject is not necessarily the Representation's owner;
- and Bearer of Intelligence is not automatically Bearer of responsibility, autonomy, consciousness, or moral status.

## 10. Cross-attribution non-entailment

The joint model prohibits automatic implications:

```text
bears Knowledge        -/-> bears Intelligence
bears Intelligence     -/-> bears Knowledge
bears capability       -/-> bears Intelligence
bears competence       -/-> bears general Intelligence
performs successfully  -/-> bears capability
is represented as P    -/-> bears P
is authorized for role -/-> bears Knowledge or Intelligence
bears Intelligence     -/-> has agency, autonomy, authority,
                         responsibility, consciousness, or moral standing
```

`-/->` means “does not follow merely from this relation.” Later reciprocal analysis may establish narrower enabling, constitutive, or evidentiary dependencies. The prohibition prevents those dependencies from being assumed globally in advance.

## 11. Applied cases

### 11.1 Document classification

For `symk.document.classified_as_type`:

- the **Document** is Bearer of the classification property under a Domain scheme;
- the **classification assertion** is a claim/Representation that the bearing relation holds;
- the **file occurrence** and **repository binding** bear different technical properties;
- the **classifier** may bear capability or competence;
- a **human, institution, artificial, or hybrid system** may be the candidate Knowledge Bearer regarding the document's type;
- the **reviewer** may assess the attribution;
- and the **repository** stores a projection without becoming the Knowledge Bearer by storage alone.

This resolves the earlier property question without making one object bear every semantic and technical predicate.

### 11.2 SharePoint projection

A SharePoint column can represent a classification assertion. Its value neither creates the Document's semantic type nor establishes that a participant knows the classification. Search and filtering usefulness are engineering consequences of the projection, not evidence of epistemic authority.

### 11.3 Assisted artificial performance

If a model answers using retrieval, tools, prompts, and human correction, attribution must distinguish:

- component capability of the model;
- configured-system capability;
- hybrid workflow capability;
- Knowledge attribution to any participant or system;
- authority to rely or act;
- and responsibility for consequences.

The largest convenient system is not automatically the Bearer, but invisible assistance cannot be discarded.

### 11.4 Institutional Knowledge

An institution may preserve evidence, procedures, role-distributed access, challenge, and revision beyond any member. That supports a candidate institutional Knowledge attribution only if the organization integrates the epistemic relation at the institutional level. Legal authority or document retention alone is insufficient.

### 11.5 Collective Intelligence

A team producing better answers than its members may support a collective Intelligence attribution when integration, feedback, division of labor, memory, and counterfactual tests identify a system-level capacity. Simple voting or averaging is evidence of aggregation, not automatically of a new Bearer.

## 12. DR-009 disposition test

### Bearer

| Test | Result | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Necessary distinction | Pass | Every capability or status attribution requires an identified subject |
| Independent semantic subject | Not established | Bearer exists as a role relative to an attributed predicate; criteria remain predicate-specific |
| Material distinctiveness | Pass | Confusing Bearer with source, System, component, participant, represented subject, owner, or responsible party causes material failures |
| Best disposition | Governed supporting attribution role | Binding meaning is required, but an independent foundational kind would reify a relational position |

**Working recommendation:** retain DR-010's Bearer disposition. The 2.1.3 evidence strengthens its importance but does not demonstrate that Bearer should be reopened as a standalone foundational concept.

### Attribution

| Test | Result | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Necessary distinction | Pass | SymK must separate actual bearing, claims, assessments, records, authority, and status |
| Independent semantic subject | Partial | Particular attributions can possess identity and lineage, but the universal meaning is relational and predicate-dependent |
| Material distinctiveness | Pass | Without it, claims and assessments create the predicates they report |
| Best disposition | Governed supporting relationship family | `bears`, `attributes`, `assesses`, `adopts`, `challenges`, and related types require governed semantics; final artifact split remains for 2.5 |

Knowledge Attribution and Intelligence Attribution are specialized claim/relationship types governed by their owning concepts. An attribution record is a Representation, not the semantic relation itself.

## 13. Two-lane obligations

The human-semantic lane must explain:

- why this subject is the Bearer;
- what is attributed and what is not;
- boundary, Scope, Context, criteria, evidence, alternatives, authority, uncertainty, and consequences;
- why a collective or hybrid level is justified;
- and what would restrict or defeat the attribution.

The machine-semantic lane must be able to distinguish:

- Bearer identifier from Bearer Identity;
- predicate/concept identifier and version;
- actual bearing relation from attribution claim and assessment;
- source, assessor, authority, actor, responsible party, and affected party;
- Scope, Context, Domain, criteria profile, time, and intended use;
- evidence, tests, failures, resources, dependencies, and alternatives;
- status, confidence, uncertainty, challenge, expiry, and supersession;
- component, resource, environment, participant, and system boundaries;
- and human, artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, and hybrid attribution levels.

No required serialization, ontology, class hierarchy, or database design is selected here.

## 15. Consequences if retained

### Product Vision

Each “Intelligence,” “Knowledge,” and “Amplified Intelligence” statement must identify its Bearer and cannot assign the complete value chain to the most visible participant or artifact.

### Candidate Constitution

A1 must distinguish Knowledge attribution and assessment from Knowledge. A2 must separate Bearer, role, authority, authorization, and responsibility. A4 must preserve claims and records without pretending that engineering creates the bearing relation.

### Foundation Papers

The Knowledge and Intelligence v0.2 papers should share one attribution vocabulary and use predicate-specific criteria. The Intelligence paper must not make organized System synonymous with Bearer. The Knowledge paper must not make asset, community acceptance, or responsible reliance synonymous with actual Knowledge.

### Derived projects and engineering

Projects own concrete bearer boundaries, criteria profiles, evidence methods, and attribution decisions within their Domain. Runtime stores may preserve attribution assertions and assessments, while repository-native metadata remains a purpose-specific Projection. None owns the universal meaning by implementation.

## 16. Preserved dissent and open questions

1. Some bearing relations may be better modeled as predication rather than attribution; ordinary-language use of “attribution” can imply uncertainty or an assessor.
2. Particular attributions can have identity, provenance, and lifecycle; 2.5 must decide whether they become first-class semantic objects, qualified relations, or both in different projections.
3. “Lowest organized level that explains” may conflict with multi-level emergence and distributed causation.
4. Resource-versus-component boundaries may remain task-relative and change across time.
5. Institutional and collective Knowledge may require conditions beyond integration, memory, and revision.
6. Artificial Knowledge may require a stronger account of epistemic commitment, uptake, or self-revision.
7. Intelligence attribution thresholds remain contestable for narrow systems and simple adaptive controllers.
8. Stable success and failure boundaries do not themselves allocate legal or moral responsibility.
9. Actual Scope may not be fully knowable even when declared Scope is explicit.

## 17. Confidence and reopening conditions

**Confidence:** High in the separation of bearing relation, attribution claim, assessment, and record; High that Bearer remains a governed supporting role; Medium-High in the common validity conditions; Medium in collective, institutional, distributed, and hybrid boundary criteria.

Reopen or revise if:

- the reciprocal analysis requires a shared bearer condition not captured here;
- multi-level cases cannot be represented without contradictory Bearers;
- derived-project translation shows the boundary rules to be unusable;
- 2.1.6 demonstrates that capability and responsibility boundaries require a different common model;
- or 2.5 cannot preserve actual relation, claim, assessment, and record without semantic duplication or loss.

## 18. Working conclusion and next dependency

Bearer should remain the Stage-Accepted **governed supporting attribution role** established by DR-010. The new evidence does not justify reopening it as a foundational concept. Attribution should be treated as a **governed supporting relationship family**, with Knowledge Attribution and Intelligence Attribution as predicate-specific specializations and attribution records as Representations.

These are analytical recommendations without DR-011 authority. With the shared model established, the next analysis can classify the reciprocal Knowledge–Intelligence dependencies without relying on a hidden common subject or allowing one attribution to imply the other automatically.
