## Objective

SymK 2.1 establishes the meanings, distinctions, dependencies, boundaries, counterexamples, and preserved uncertainties required to interpret the Stage-Accepted SymK identity and to conduct the constitutional axiom review in 2.2.

The stage treats concepts as an interdependent system rather than a dictionary. It must determine which terms are foundational to SymK, which are governed supporting distinctions, which belong to derived projects, which are engineering projections, and which must remain excluded or deferred.

## Principal outcome

A Stage-Accepted conceptual baseline sufficiently precise to support 2.2 constitutional analysis, accompanied by revised Foundation Papers v0.2 that communicate the resulting understanding to humans without acquiring constitutional authority merely by publication.

## Concept disposition classes

Every examined concept must receive one explicit disposition:

1. **Foundational concept** — required across SymK to express its identity or constitutional commitments.
2. **Governed supporting distinction** — necessary to prevent a material conflation but not independently foundational.
3. **Derived-project specialization** — owned by a narrower domain or project under SymK constraints.
4. **Engineering representation or projection** — expresses a concept in a technical form without owning its meaning.
5. **Excluded from SymK** — outside SymK jurisdiction.
6. **Deferred pending evidence** — unresolved with an assigned destination and consequence.

No candidate receives a disposition merely from its historical name, file location, identifier, or implementation utility.

## Required anatomy of a conceptual analysis

Each material concept or concept cluster must record:

1. the problem requiring the concept;
2. inherited formulations and sources;
3. a minimal working definition;
4. scope, bearer, and applicability;
5. necessary distinctions and exclusions;
6. relationships and dependencies;
7. examples, counterexamples, and failure cases;
8. prohibited interpretations;
9. human-semantic meaning;
10. machine-semantic preservation obligations without premature language selection;
11. consequences for the Product Vision, candidate Constitution, axioms, and derived projects;
12. dissent, confidence, open questions, and reopening conditions.

## View, capability, and program-lane boundary

DR-009's human/machine rule remains a minimum preservation obligation: governed meaning must remain interpretable by people and materially inspectable in declared machine-semantic expressions. DR-012 establishes that this is not an exhaustive count of semantic views.

- The **Human View** preserves purpose, meaning, Context, Scope, explanation, alternatives, dissent, consequences, and limits.
- The **Scientific View** preserves questions, methods, Grounds, Evidence, counterevidence, models, uncertainty, competing accounts, robustness, and conditions of defeat.
- The **Engineering View** preserves specifications, constraints, interfaces, formal profiles, implementations, validation, runtime records, failure handling, migration, and operational Projections.

Human readability, formal expression, and machine inspectability are cross-view capabilities; format does not determine view. Semantic/constitutional work, corpus integrity, and grounding/verification are separate program-work lanes; they organize work and evidence flow, not semantic meaning.

2.1 may define semantic preservation obligations, but it does not select first-order logic, OWL, SHACL, rule languages, schemas, APIs, programming languages, persistence structures, or lowering mechanisms. Final `FC-*`, `FP-*`, and `SYMK-P-*` package design belongs to 2.5. Formal-language selection and operational lowering belong to 2.5 and 2.9 as routed by the 2.0 deferrals.
