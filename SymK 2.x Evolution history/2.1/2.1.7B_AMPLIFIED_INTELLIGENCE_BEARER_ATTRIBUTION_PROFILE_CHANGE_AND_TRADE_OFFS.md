## 1. Question and analytical result

> To whom or what may Amplified Intelligence be attributed when capability is enabled, composed, integrated, retained, lost, redistributed, or transformed across participants, tools, relations, teams, institutions, configured systems, distributed systems, hybrids, professions, and successors?

Stream B supports the following working answer:

> **An Amplified Intelligence attribution is a qualified claim that an identified, sufficiently continuous organized Bearer has an actual Intelligence configuration whose relevant capability profile increased, relative to a declared materially comparable baseline, through an evidenced amplification relation across relevant variation. The attribution must preserve the Bearer's level and boundary; the capability dimensions, specialization, integration, gains, losses, unchanged and unknown dimensions; resources, assistance, dependencies, alternatives, uncertainty, and time; and the distinction among the actual configuration, the amplification claim, its assessment, its governance status, and every Representation or Projection.**

> **The amplified Bearer is the subject at the lowest organized level that explains the claimed capability without omitting constitutive integration. It is not automatically the user, beneficiary, most visible participant, most capable component, legal owner, provider, organization, profession, or widest successful system. Multiple levels may receive separate, simultaneously supportable capability-change attributions, but no gain, loss, Intelligence, agency, Authority, Responsibility, value, or status transfers among levels without its own evidence.**

This produces a level-qualified model rather than one universal Bearer choice:

- an **individual** may bear a concurrent, retained, or developmental gain when the relevant capability remains attributable to that individual's organized activity under declared support conditions;
- a **component** may bear a gain when its own configuration changes and its capability is separately evidenced;
- a **team or institution** may bear a gain when organization-level integration, memory, coordination, substitution tolerance, and performance across variation support that level;
- a **configured, distributed, or hybrid system** may bear a gain when the capability depends on stable constitutive coupling and integrated organization not adequately explained by one component;
- a **relation** may enable capability but is not a Bearer merely by connecting subjects; it must itself constitute an organized subject before capability is attributed at that level;
- a **profession, role, service, product, project, or brand** is not automatically an Intelligence Bearer; its capability claims require an identified organized subject and continuity conditions; and
- a **successor** does not inherit its predecessor's capability or amplification status merely through name, function, ownership, contract, data, or output continuity.

Stream B also supports a provisional conceptual distinction:

1. **Amplification** is the comparative causal relationship retained in Stream A.
2. **Amplified Intelligence** is a governed compound capability-change attribution applying that relationship to Intelligence; it is not a new substance or independent kind of Intelligence.
3. **Amplified capability profile** is the declared multidimensional change description supporting the attribution, not the actual configuration itself.
4. **Amplified Bearer** is an attribution role occupied by the identified subject of that claim, not a permanent Entity class.
5. **Amplifier** is the role of an intervention, resource, relation, or organized arrangement in the amplification explanation; it need not be the amplified Bearer.

These are analytical directions only. Stream C must determine the Domain Intelligence Amplifier system-class boundary. No candidate receives a disposition and DQ-012 is not authoritatively answered here.

## 3. Why SymK needs a Bearer and profile-change model

Without an explicit model, the same successful episode can generate incompatible claims:

- a professional says “my intelligence was amplified” because a tool was useful;
- a provider says “the model amplified the professional” because the product was present;
- an institution says “our intelligence increased” because aggregate output rose;
- an Engineering View labels the full workflow an amplified system because its components are connected;
- a benchmark attributes the gain to the model while excluding prompts, retrieval, users, data, infrastructure, and selection;
- management attributes gain to the organization while member capability and future-expert formation decline;
- a successor system inherits a predecessor label while its boundary, components, dependencies, and failure modes change; or
- every useful resource is placed inside the system after success and outside it after harm.

These claims cannot be reconciled by choosing the Human, the AI, or the widest system in advance. They require a repeatable relation among subject identity, explanatory level, system boundary, actual organization, capability object, baseline, profile change, integration, dependency, evidence, and time.

The model must also avoid the opposite error. If only unaided retained Human capability counts, a stable hybrid or configured system can never bear Intelligence, continuously supported capability disappears conceptually, accessibility-related enablement is misdescribed, and tool-mediated professional competence is forced into a fictitious unaided subject. The correct boundary is neither widest-system inflation nor unaided-individual privilege.

## 4. Five semantic layers

Every material Amplified Intelligence statement must separate at least five layers:

1. **Actual Bearer and configuration:** the identified organized subject and the capability configuration it actually realizes under declared conditions.
2. **Comparative amplification relation:** the relationship between intervention, subject, baseline, changed configuration, and causal Evidence.
3. **Attribution claim:** a source asserts that the subject bears Amplified Intelligence for a Domain, Scope, Context, time, specialization, and capability object.
4. **Assessment and governance status:** an assessor evaluates the claim and a competent Authority may permit specified reliance without creating the actual capability.
5. **Record and Projection:** profiles, scores, benchmarks, reports, system cards, dashboards, certificates, labels, and registries represent selected aspects with declared loss.

The layers may diverge. A Bearer may be amplified before a claim is recorded. A claim may be correct but poorly evidenced. An assessment may be authoritative within one use while false as a universal capability attribution. A precise profile can still misrepresent the actual configuration. A legally accepted or commercially successful label does not create the predicate.

## 6. Bearer-selection rules

### 6.1 Lowest adequate organized level

Attribute a capability to the lowest organized subject level that explains it without excluding constitutive integration. “Lowest” does not mean smallest physical component; “adequate” requires that the proposed subject can realize the relevant organized capacity across variation.

If one component explains the capability with others acting only as replaceable resources, the component-level claim may be adequate. If no component explains the capability and stable cross-component organization is constitutive, a configured-system or higher-level claim may be needed.

### 6.2 No widest-system default

The ability to improve performance by adding resources does not make every resource part of the Bearer. Electricity, a network, a library, a model provider, an expert, organizational permission, and social infrastructure may all be necessary conditions without constituting one Intelligence Bearer.

The widest successful boundary is especially suspect when it disappears under analysis of failure, cost, Responsibility, harm, or remedy.

### 6.3 No unaided-subject privilege

A subject may bear a capability under explicit support conditions. Dependence does not by itself disqualify the attribution. Human capabilities routinely depend on language, instruments, institutions, medication, accessibility supports, training, records, and other people.

However, support-conditioned capability must not be restated as unaided retained capability. The support, access condition, integration, practical availability, fragility, and removal effect remain part of the attribution.

### 6.4 No visible-component privilege

The component that produces the visible output, receives the prompt, signs the report, or appears in the interface does not automatically bear the relevant capability. Hidden retrieval, model services, Human review, institutional routines, data selection, orchestration, and maintenance may explain the result.

### 6.5 No legal or organizational shortcut

Legal personality, ownership, employment, contract, role assignment, product identity, brand continuity, or institutional authority may determine governance relations. None establishes Intelligence, capability, amplification, or Bearer continuity.

### 6.6 Multiple compatible attributions

One configuration may support distinct claims such as:

- retained individual professional capability increased;
- the professional's concurrent support-conditioned capability increased further;
- one artificial component's narrow specialization improved;
- the configured Human–artificial system acquired an integrated capability not held by either alone;
- the institution's service capacity increased while some member capabilities declined; and
- affected nonparticipants experienced increased risk or cost.

These statements are not contradictory when their predicates, subjects, levels, boundaries, baselines, dimensions, time, and Evidence differ. None may be compressed into one scalar “net intelligence” result by default.

## 7. Candidate Bearer patterns

| Candidate pattern | What may support a Bearer attribution | Principal false-positive risk |
|---|---|---|
| Individual Human or other biological subject | Organized capability attributable to that individual across relevant variation, with support conditions and retained/concurrent status explicit | Crediting external reasoning or institutional output to the individual |
| Artificial component | Capability evidenced at the model, agent, runtime, or component's actual configured boundary | Crediting prompts, tools, retrieval, humans, and services to the model alone |
| Tool or artifact | Organized capability at the tool's level, not passive containment or output production alone | Treating storage, weights, interface, or generated content as Intelligence |
| Relation-enabled subject | Capability attributable to an identified subject under a declared continuing relation | Treating the relation itself as a Bearer or hiding dependence |
| Team | Recurrent organization, differentiated contributions, integration, correction, and team-level capacity robust to relevant member variation | Averaging member performance or naming a team |
| Institution | Persistent organization-level processes, roles, memory, authority, correction, substitution, and capability beyond current members | Confusing legal/institutional status or aggregate output with Intelligence |
| Configured system | Stable configuration and cross-component integration producing a capability not adequately assigned to one component | Drawing a post hoc success boundary around tools and users |
| Distributed system | Constitutive coupling, communication, timing, memory, coordination, and failure relations across sites | Treating network connection or shared platform as one subject |
| Hybrid Human–artificial system | Cross-substrate organized integration with level-specific contributions, feedback, correction, dependency, and boundary stability | Marketing “partnership” or Human presence manufacturing a hybrid Bearer |
| Role or service | Persistent organized subject realizing the role/service across occupant or component change | Treating an abstract function or contract as an Intelligence |
| Profession | Evidence of an organized profession-level subject and capacity, not merely member distribution or labor-market statistics | Copying selected professional gains to the whole profession |
| Successor | Supported Identity continuity or an explicitly different successor attribution with a new baseline | Name, data, function, owner, or output continuity copying capability |

These are recurring attribution patterns, not ontological kinds or accepted concept classes.

## 8. Individual and support-conditioned capability

### 8.1 Three individual claims

At least three statements must remain distinct:

1. **Unaided retained capability:** the individual can manifest the capability without the intervention under the declared conditions.
2. **Support-conditioned individual capability:** the individual's organized use, interpretation, judgment, correction, and action under a declared support relation enables the capability while support remains available.
3. **Individual performance with external production:** the individual is associated with an output produced substantially by another subject or mechanism, without evidence that the relevant capability belongs to the individual.

Only the first two can support an individual capability attribution, and they require different Evidence. The third may still establish use, access, orchestration, reliance, supervision, or role performance, but not the claimed Intelligence gain.

### 8.2 Integration at the individual level

Support-conditioned individual capability is stronger when Evidence shows that the individual can:

- identify when the support is relevant;
- formulate and reformulate the task or inquiry;
- select among tools, sources, or modes;
- interpret outputs in Domain Context;
- detect errors, limits, or uncertainty;
- compare alternatives and request correction;
- integrate results with prior capability and practical constraints;
- decline, override, or escalate when appropriate;
- adapt use across relevant variation; and
- preserve enough independent orientation to avoid becoming a ceremonial relay.

This list is evidence-oriented, not a universal threshold. Some accessibility or execution supports may enable genuine individual capacity through a different integration mechanism. Domain and affected-subject evidence must remain able to challenge the interpretation.

### 8.3 Tool operation is not automatically Intelligence amplification

Learning interface commands, copying outputs, following a fixed workflow, or achieving higher throughput may be skill, access, task performance, or operational adaptation. An Amplified Intelligence claim requires the relevant Intelligence capability object and integration across variation to be evidenced.

### 8.4 Retention is a qualification, not the universal criterion

Retained gain can strengthen claims about individual development, resilience, and alternatives. Its absence does not automatically defeat concurrent support-conditioned capability, but it defeats any unqualified claim that the individual now possesses the same unaided capability.

## 9. Relation, team, and institution boundaries

### 9.1 A relation is not automatically a subject

A support, cooperative, contractual, educational, supervisory, or technical relation can enable capability. The relation itself does not bear Intelligence unless it constitutes an organized subject with Identity, boundary, integration, continuity, and capability at that level.

“The partnership is intelligent” may therefore mean:

- participants have complementary capabilities;
- the relation improves their separate performance;
- a configured system constituted through the relation bears capability; or
- the phrase is only rhetorical.

These meanings require separate tests.

### 9.2 Team-level capability

A team-level Amplified Intelligence claim should identify evidence such as:

- differentiated and composable contributions;
- information or state made available at the team level;
- coordination responsive to changing cases;
- distributed error detection, challenge, and correction;
- task allocation or reallocation across variation;
- preserved capability despite ordinary member absence or substitution;
- team-level learning or memory only where separately evidenced; and
- failure patterns that cannot be explained by one member alone.

A meeting, roster, shared tool, message count, workflow completion, or aggregate average is insufficient.

### 9.3 Institution-level capability

An institution may support an Intelligence attribution when persistent roles, processes, memory, resources, challenge, correction, succession, and organization constitute a capacity beyond current member aggregation. Legal identity may help track the subject but does not establish its capability.

Institution-level amplification can coexist with:

- no gain for individual members;
- gain for some members and loss for others;
- increased dependence on external providers;
- reduced retained professional capability;
- improved service capacity but worse affected-subject consequences; or
- loss of future-expert formation pathways.

Each must remain separately represented.

## 10. Configured, distributed, and hybrid system Bearers

### 10.1 Configured-system candidate

> **A configured-system Bearer candidate is an identified, bounded, sufficiently continuous organized configuration whose relevant capability is supported by constitutive integration among components and is not adequately attributable to any one component under the same Scope and conditions.**

This candidate does not require consciousness, agency, autonomy, legal personality, moral standing, Responsibility, or artificial Intelligence. It requires actual organization and capability at the claimed level.

### 10.2 Minimum integration evidence families

No one family is universally sufficient. Relevant Evidence may include:

- reciprocal or staged information dependence;
- state transfer and preservation across components;
- complementary capability composition;
- dynamic coordination or task allocation;
- feedback, error detection, correction, and recovery across component boundaries;
- constraint propagation and joint adaptation to variation;
- counterfactual recombination or removal effects;
- whole-level failure modes and performance regularities;
- stable configuration management and boundary continuity; and
- inability of a component-only explanation to account for the capability.

Connection, co-location, orchestration, workflow order, message exchange, shared storage, or aggregate output does not establish integration alone.

### 10.3 Distributed system

Physical or organizational distribution does not prevent a system-level Bearer. The attribution must nevertheless identify constitutive coupling, timing, interfaces, state, coordination, dependencies, failure boundaries, and continuity. A platform ecosystem or supply chain is not one Intelligence merely because its outputs are interdependent.

### 10.4 Hybrid system

A hybrid Human–artificial Bearer requires cross-substrate integration at the configured-system level. It is not established by Human presence, AI presence, tool use, co-authorship, approval, supervision, or complementarity alone.

The Human and artificial participants retain their own capability, agency, Authority, Responsibility, affected-subject, and developmental attributions. The hybrid claim neither absorbs nor redistributes them automatically.

### 10.5 Component opacity

Opacity in one component may weaken Evidence about mechanism, boundary, dependency, and failure. It does not by itself prove or disprove whole-level capability. A strong output profile cannot repair missing evidence by merely widening the Bearer boundary.

## 11. Role, service, profession, and successor claims

### 11.1 Role and service

An abstract role or service definition is not an organized Bearer. An institution, team, configured system, or other subject realizing a persistent service may bear capability, including through occupant substitution, when Identity and organization remain supportable.

Claims such as “diagnostic capability was amplified” must specify whether the Bearer is a clinician, clinical team, institution, configured diagnostic system, or service organization.

### 11.2 Profession

A profession-level statement often compresses a distribution:

- which professionals gained concurrent capability;
- which gained retained capability;
- which tasks were automated or substituted;
- which institutions gained service capacity;
- which groups lost access, work, discretion, or alternatives;
- how entry, apprenticeship, and future-expert formation changed; and
- whether a profession-level organized capacity exists at all.

Aggregate adoption, productivity, employment, or outcome statistics do not establish one professional Intelligence Bearer.

### 11.3 Successor

A successor may receive a new capability attribution. It does not receive the predecessor's actual configuration, profile, amplification history, Responsibility, Authority, or status automatically.

Three cases must be separated:

1. **Same Bearer, changed version:** continuity is supported; compare versioned configurations while preserving the changed intervention and baseline.
2. **Successor Bearer:** continuity is insufficient for numerical Identity; create a new attribution and explain lineage.
3. **Persistent higher-level Bearer:** components or occupants change while an institution or configured system remains the same subject; attribute at that stable level if Evidence supports it.

Name, owner, contract, data, interface, purpose, role, or output continuity may be relevant evidence but is not sufficient alone.

## 12. Baseline and counterfactual discipline

### 12.1 Same-Bearer preference

When possible, compare the same identified Bearer across versioned conditions. The subject boundary, Domain, Scope, Context, time, resources, assistance, selection, and relevant variation must remain materially comparable or their changes must be modeled explicitly.

### 12.2 New configured systems

A newly composed system may lack a historical unamplified state. Legitimate alternatives include:

- a matched counterfactual configuration without the intervention;
- an ablation or reconfiguration preserving the proposed Bearer identity where possible;
- an alternative arrangement pursuing the same capability object;
- a predecessor configuration only when Identity continuity is supportable; or
- a restricted claim that the configuration has a capability, without yet calling it amplified.

The absence of a valid baseline may leave Intelligence supportable while Amplified Intelligence remains indeterminate.

### 12.3 Baseline kinds do not transfer conclusions

| Baseline | Legitimate use | Principal limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Prior same-Bearer state | Longitudinal change | Confounded by time, learning, selection, environment, and co-interventions |
| Unaided state | Support-conditioned difference | May be unrealistic, inaccessible, unsafe, or normatively privileged |
| Alternative tool or arrangement | Comparative intervention choice | Requires matched resources, maturity, users, and support |
| Matched subject or population | Group comparison | Does not by itself establish same-Bearer change or causation |
| Counterfactual model | Causal estimation | Depends on model assumptions and uncertainty |
| Target profile | Gap analysis | Target authority and value choices do not establish actual gain |
| Predecessor or incumbent system | Migration comparison | Requires Identity and functional-comparability discipline |

No result may silently move from one baseline meaning to another.

### 12.4 Baseline and Bearer co-stability

A claim fails when the target includes a tool, team, or institutional resource that the baseline excludes while still naming the “same” Bearer without qualification. It also fails when the baseline includes Human verification or expert support omitted from the target dependency account.

## 13. Actual configuration, attributed profile, and Projection

### 13.1 Actual configuration

The actual Intelligence configuration is the Bearer's realized organization of relevant capabilities. It may be only partially knowable and need not have one canonical vector representation.

### 13.2 Attributed profile

An attributed profile is a fallible claim describing selected dimensions, relations, specializations, limits, resources, and dependencies. Different assessors may produce different profiles under different authority, purpose, evidence, or uncertainty.

### 13.3 Projection

A score, category, benchmark, chart, matrix, dashboard, system card, capability vector, or maturity level is a Projection of the attributed profile or evidence. It cannot become the actual configuration through precision, formalism, certification, or adoption.

### 13.4 Change description

A material change description should distinguish:

- increased dimensions;
- decreased dimensions;
- newly integrated relations among dimensions;
- lost integration;
- unchanged dimensions;
- unknown or untested dimensions;
- added or removed specializations;
- altered resources and dependencies;
- changed practical alternatives;
- redistributed capability across subjects or levels; and
- changed uncertainty about the configuration.

The change may be qualitative, partial-order, set-valued, interval-based, probabilistic, narrative, or otherwise structured. SymK does not require one universal numeric delta.

## 14. Dimensional and specialization-qualified amplification

### 14.1 Claim ladder

Stream B distinguishes increasingly demanding claims:

1. **Improved manifestation:** performance improved in an observed case.
2. **Dimension-specific capability amplification:** one declared capability dimension increased across relevant variation for the identified Bearer.
3. **Specialization-level Amplified Intelligence:** an integrated capability profile within a named specialization increased, with gains, losses, and dependencies explicit.
4. **Configuration-level Amplified Intelligence:** a material portion of the Bearer's integrated multidimensional Intelligence configuration increased under a declared target profile and trade-off rule.
5. **Unqualified Amplified Intelligence:** a broad rhetorical form that is unsafe unless its Domain, Scope, capability object, target profile, material dimensions, and omissions are made explicit.

A higher rung cannot be inferred from a lower rung.

### 14.2 Narrow gain

A gain in one dimension may support a dimension-qualified amplification claim even when another dimension declines. It does not establish unqualified profile amplification. The loss must remain visible and may defeat the intended-use assessment.

### 14.3 Specialization

Specialization identity, inheritance, Domain boundary, and target capability profile must be explicit. A narrow specialization gain cannot be copied to Universal Intelligence or to unrelated task families.

### 14.4 New integration

New integration may itself be a material capability change when previously separate dimensions or component contributions become jointly available, coordinated, and robust across relevant variation. It is not established by co-presence, aggregation, interface compatibility, or workflow completion alone.

## 15. Gain, loss, trade-off, and target profile

### 15.1 No universal net Intelligence scalar

SymK has no accepted conserved quantity or universal weighting that permits every capability gain, loss, uncertainty, dependency, and distributional effect to be combined into one number.

The statement “net Intelligence increased” therefore requires an explicitly authorized target profile, weighting or partial-order rule, materiality treatment, uncertainty model, omitted-dimension account, and intended use. It remains a scoped assessment, not a universal fact.

### 15.2 Working profile-change statuses

The following human-semantic qualifications may aid later work:

- **supported dimension-specific amplification**;
- **supported specialization-level amplification**;
- **supported configuration-level amplification under a declared target profile**;
- **supported gain with material loss or dependency**;
- **mixed or non-comparable change**;
- **indeterminate because baseline, Bearer, integration, Evidence, or material dimensions are inadequate**;
- **contradicted for the claimed Scope**; and
- **expired or superseded after material change**.

These are analytical descriptions, not accepted status vocabulary or schema values.

### 15.3 Target-profile authority

Target profiles encode purpose and often value. The party selecting dimensions, weights, tolerances, thresholds, and acceptable losses must be identified with its authority, jurisdiction, affected-subject relations, challenge path, and intended use.

An authorized target can govern a project decision without defining Intelligence or proving that the change is beneficial.

### 15.4 Distribution matrix

Material claims should be able to preserve a matrix such as:

```text
subject or level × capability dimension × time × condition
    = gain / loss / unchanged / unknown
      plus dependency, uncertainty, alternatives, and Evidence
```

This is an explanatory device, not a required data model. It prevents organization-level productivity from erasing individual deskilling, configured-system gain from erasing affected-subject harm, or current gains from erasing future capability loss.

## 16. Dependency, removal, and counterfactual degradation

### 16.1 Dependency is part of the profile

Dependence may be constitutive, enabling, evidentiary, operational, governance-related, economic, formative, or environmental. Its presence does not decide the Bearer or invalidate amplification, but hidden dependence defeats an unqualified attribution.

### 16.2 Removal tests

Removal, disruption, substitution, degradation, or reconfiguration can help distinguish:

- replaceable resource from constitutive component;
- component capability from configured-system capability;
- concurrent from retained gain;
- robust integration from brittle orchestration;
- stable capability from favorable operating conditions;
- recoverable dependency from lock-in; and
- same Bearer from materially changed successor.

### 16.3 What counterfactual degradation does not prove

Performance decline after removal does not by itself prove:

- that the removed item was part of the Bearer;
- that the remaining subject previously bore the full capability;
- that the whole is an Intelligence Bearer;
- that the capability was amplified rather than merely enabled;
- that the dependency is unacceptable;
- that the arrangement is irresponsible; or
- that retained capability should have existed.

Water, electricity, data, language, institutional permission, and many external services may be indispensable without becoming components of one Bearer.

### 16.4 Substitution and robustness

If a component can be replaced without material loss because the system reorganizes around an equivalent, the component may be a resource rather than Identity-defining. If only one proprietary provider can sustain the capability, the dependency, practical alternatives, concentration, outage fragility, and lifecycle consequences remain material even when system-level capability is valid.

## 17. Temporal qualifications and revalidation

### 17.1 Temporal forms

| Form | Working meaning | Required caution |
|---|---|---|
| Episodic support-conditioned | Gain exists during one bounded supported episode | One episode may not evidence capability across variation |
| Continuing support-conditioned | Gain persists while a stable support relation remains available | Access, version, provider, maintenance, and alternatives remain conditions |
| Retained | Gain remains after intervention removal under declared conditions | Retention may decay or be narrower than concurrent capability |
| Developmental | Capability changes through practice, Formation, Learning, or institutional development over time | Mechanism and achievement must not be inferred from exposure |
| Configured-system | Gain belongs to a stable organized whole while configuration persists | Component change may trigger revalidation |
| Successor | A different subject receives a new capability attribution linked by lineage | No automatic inheritance |

### 17.2 Material revalidation triggers

Reassessment is required when material to the claim:

- Bearer boundary or level changes;
- participants, components, model, data, prompts, tools, provider, or infrastructure change;
- Domain, Scope, Context, target profile, or intended use changes;
- dependency, access, cost, or practical alternatives change;
- new failures or counterevidence appear;
- member turnover or organizational redesign changes integration;
- maintenance, retraining, update, or drift changes the configuration;
- relevant professional standards or external authority change;
- affected-subject consequences reveal omitted material dimensions; or
- challenge shows that the baseline or causal explanation was invalid.

A version number or audit date is evidence about change, not proof that the Bearer persisted or that revalidation succeeded.

## 18. Causal and evidentiary model

### 18.1 Two propositions are required

An Amplified Intelligence claim must support both:

1. **capability proposition:** the identified Bearer has the claimed changed capability configuration; and
2. **amplification proposition:** the identified intervention or arrangement contributed to the difference relative to the declared baseline.

Evidence for one does not automatically support the other.

### 18.2 Evidence families

Depending on Domain and consequence, relevant Evidence may include:

- repeated and varied task-family performance;
- novel-case transfer and perturbation tests;
- failure, adversarial, and boundary-condition evidence;
- controlled comparison, ablation, removal, substitution, and reconfiguration;
- process and integration evidence;
- longitudinal retention, decay, drift, and recovery evidence;
- component- and whole-level causal analysis;
- qualitative observation, expert assessment, participant testimony, and affected-subject challenge;
- dependency, outage, maintenance, verification, and alternative-path evidence;
- uncertainty, missingness, disagreement, and replication evidence; and
- lineage across versions, members, models, procedures, and environments.

No evidence family is universally sufficient. Operational test selection remains Domain- and project-owned within SymK's claim envelope.

### 18.3 Relevant variation

Variation must be relevant to the claimed capability object and Scope. More test cases do not cure narrow sampling, leakage, repeated templates, invalid labels, construct mismatch, or omitted failure conditions.

### 18.4 Counterevidence symmetry

The boundary, subject, level, comparator, and target profile used to claim gain must remain stable when counterevidence appears. A provider may not claim whole-system success and component-only failure; an institution may not claim member output as institutional gain and member deskilling as external.

## 19. Candidate approaches considered

### A — The original Human is always the amplified Bearer

**Rejected as universal.** It erases configured-system, institutional, artificial, distributed, and hybrid capability; it also copies external production to a Human relay and privileges origin.

### B — The tool or AI is always the amplified Bearer

**Rejected.** It erases prompts, Human interpretation, retrieval, data, providers, institutional organization, and support; tool presence does not establish Intelligence or profile change.

### C — The widest successful Human–tool–institution system is the Bearer

**Rejected.** It rewards boundary inflation, treats infrastructure and society as one subject, and obscures failures, Responsibility, cost, and affected subjects.

### D — Only retained unaided capability counts

**Rejected as universal.** It cannot represent support-conditioned and configured-system capabilities and may misdescribe accessibility, instruments, institutions, and stable relations.

### E — The beneficiary or user receives the capability

**Rejected.** Benefit, access, use, ownership, role, and capability are distinct.

### F — Aggregate score or productivity selects the Bearer

**Rejected.** Scores and outputs are Projections or performances; aggregation hides integration, level, dimensions, dependencies, distribution, and continuity.

### G — Every level that participated receives the gain

**Rejected.** Participation and Contribution do not transfer Intelligence or amplification.

### H — One stable level-qualified Bearer per claim, with multiple separately evidenced claims allowed

**Retained as the Stream B working direction.** It supports individual, component, team, institution, configured-system, distributed, hybrid, and successor analysis without defaulting to one substrate or copying properties.

## 23. Human, Scientific, and Engineering View obligations

### 23.1 Human View

The Human View must make intelligible:

- who or what is said to have become more capable;
- whether the capability is unaided, support-conditioned, retained, developmental, or configured-system-level;
- which abilities increased, decreased, remained unchanged, or are unknown;
- what support and dependence remain;
- whose capability is not being described;
- which comparison and time frame govern the claim;
- what practical alternatives, deskilling, displacement, and future formation are affected; and
- who may challenge the claim and its use.

Phrases such as “AI-augmented professional,” “intelligent organization,” “supercharged team,” or “human–AI partnership” require unpacking rather than acceptance as definitions.

### 23.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View must preserve:

- construct identity and validity;
- subject and level selection;
- baseline and comparator comparability;
- causal assumptions and alternative explanations;
- relevant variation, novelty, transfer, and failure;
- measurement uncertainty, missingness, disagreement, and replication;
- dimensional gains, losses, interactions, and omitted variables;
- longitudinal persistence, decay, and revalidation;
- component/whole and multilevel analysis; and
- limitations of every aggregate, score, or classification.

No universal scientific instrument is selected by Stream B.

### 23.3 Engineering View

Engineering may represent:

- candidate Bearer identifiers and versioned boundaries;
- component and dependency graphs;
- Domain, Scope, Context, time, baseline, comparator, and target profile;
- capability claims, profiles, dimensions, Evidence, failures, and uncertainty;
- gain/loss/unchanged/unknown states;
- integration and removal-test evidence;
- source, assessor, authority, intended use, status, challenge, and lineage; and
- separate participant, beneficiary, affected-subject, Authority, and Responsibility relations.

Every representation remains a Projection. A `bearer_id`, capability vector, benchmark, system boundary diagram, dependency graph, `amplified=true` field, or certificate does not create the actual subject, integration, capability, or relation.

### 23.4 Alignment rule

Material disagreement among views is Evidence. Engineering must not force disputed boundaries into a boolean; Scientific uncertainty must not erase lived dependency or affected consequence; Human language must not universalize an intuitive Bearer. The claim remains qualified, restricted, or disputed until reconciled or explicitly preserved.
