## 1. Question and analytical result

> What minimum answer-quality boundary must SymK establish so that an answer can be assessed across epistemic, practical, and responsible-use dimensions without compressing them into one universal score, importing Domain thresholds, or confusing quality with fluency, confidence, consensus, Authority, adoption, benchmark performance, value, or favorable outcome?

Stream D supports the following working answer:

> **Answer quality is a scoped, multidimensional, evidence-bearing assessment profile relating an identified answer version and governed question version to declared quality dimensions, standards, methods, assessors, uncertainty, affected distribution, intended use, time, and challenge conditions. A material quality claim states which dimensions apply, what each means in the Domain and Context, what Evidence supports the assessment, what remains unknown or contested, who may assess and who may set thresholds or authorize reliance, and which transformations, trade-offs, losses, and consequences are material. No single dimension, label, score, average, benchmark, or outcome constitutes answer quality universally.**

> **Epistemic adequacy concerns whether the answer's content and support are fit for the governed epistemic obligation, including question correspondence, correctness or truth conditions where applicable, relevance, coverage, precision, Evidence, reasoning linkage, uncertainty calibration, robustness, currency, and defeaters. Practical usefulness concerns whether identified users can understand and use the answer for a declared purpose under actual constraints. Neither epistemic adequacy nor usefulness alone establishes responsible use, value, Authorization, decision quality, action quality, or favorable consequence.**

> **A responsible-answer profile combines the material epistemic, practical, affected-subject, safety, fairness, legality, privacy, security, Authority, challenge, correction, and lineage conditions relevant to a declared use. It is not a universal moral score or certification. An answer may be epistemically strong but unusable, useful but weakly supported, safe but incomplete, lawful but unfair, fair but unlawful, timely but fragile, or high-scoring yet irresponsible for the proposed reliance. Such tensions remain visible rather than being silently averaged away.**

This working result keeps at least eight states separate:

1. **answer and question relation:** the Stream C content-to-question relation being assessed;
2. **quality dimension:** a declared aspect under which an answer or its use may be evaluated;
3. **quality observation or Evidence:** material used in evaluating a dimension;
4. **quality assessment:** an evidence-bearing judgment by an identified assessor under a declared method;
5. **quality profile:** a structured plurality of dimension-specific assessments, unknowns, conflicts, and applicability states;
6. **threshold, weight, or decision rule:** an authorized policy for a declared purpose, not intrinsic quality;
7. **status, adoption, Reliance Authorization, use, and consequence:** governed relations downstream of assessment; and
8. **record and lineage:** durable Representation of objects, methods, assessors, results, disagreements, changes, uses, corrections, and supersession.

No definition or disposition is accepted here. Stream E must analyze speed, time, total cost, value, benefit, outcome, consequence, and trade-offs without treating the Stream D profile as a universal value function.

## 3. Why SymK needs this boundary

Without this boundary, materially different claims are compressed into “good answer”:

- a fluent statement is treated as accurate because it is easy to read;
- a confident answer is treated as calibrated because it contains a probability;
- a true fragment is treated as complete because no listed fact is false;
- a correct benchmark answer is treated as robust outside the benchmark distribution;
- a citation is treated as support although it does not entail or apply to the claim;
- a valid proof is treated as practically relevant to a different question;
- an expert opinion is treated as true because the source has standing;
- an authorized institutional answer is treated as epistemically superior because it is operative;
- a popular or consensual answer is treated as fair to affected minorities;
- a fast response is treated as timely although its data, law, or Context is stale;
- an actionable answer is treated as safe because a user can execute it;
- a useful workaround is treated as lawful, maintainable, or responsible;
- an average benchmark score hides a catastrophic failure mode;
- a favorable outcome retroactively validates a weakly supported answer; or
- one quality score is optimized while uncertainty, omitted groups, correction cost, and challenge failure disappear.

The opposite error is defining quality so strongly that only perfect answers have quality. Quality is assessable under partial Evidence, multiple dimensions, disagreement, and uncertainty. A profile can contain high, low, mixed, unknown, not-applicable, contested, or method-limited assessments without becoming meaningless.

SymK therefore needs a universal conceptual grammar for quality claims while leaving substantive dimension definitions, methods, thresholds, weights, and trade-offs to competent Domain and external authorities.

| assessment occurrence | Bounded evaluation by an identified assessor | Answer quality as timeless fact |

| quality profile | Structured set of results, unknowns, conflicts, dependencies, and losses | Scalar utility or permission to use |
| threshold, weight, aggregation, or decision rule | Purpose- and authority-bound policy over profile elements | Natural law, intrinsic quality, or universal SymK value function |

Every material transformation among these layers must state source, loss, uncertainty, and authority.

## 5. Working anchors

### 5.1 Answer-quality profile

An **answer-quality profile** is a scoped and challengeable collection of dimension-specific assessments concerning an identified answer version in relation to an identified question version, Domain, Context, time, intended use, affected horizon, standards, methods, and assessors.

It should identify:

- applicable, conditionally applicable, disputed, unknown, and not-applicable dimensions;
- dimension meanings and objects;
- Evidence, counterevidence, methods, and limitations;
- results and uncertainty without false commensurability;
- assessors and material disagreements;
- threshold, weighting, aggregation, and Authorization sources if used;
- affected distributions and non-compensable conditions;
- dependencies, interactions, and failure modes;
- current version, time, expiry, revision, and supersession; and
- challenge, correction, appeal, repair, and monitoring paths.

The profile can be incomplete. Its incompleteness must itself be visible when material.

### 5.2 Epistemic adequacy

**Epistemic adequacy** is the degree and manner in which an answer satisfies the material epistemic obligations of its governed question and declared use under applicable standards and available Evidence. It may involve correspondence, truth or correctness, relevance, coverage, precision, support, reasoning adequacy, calibration, robustness, currency, and defeater treatment.

Epistemic adequacy is not identical to truth. A true unsupported guess may be accurate but epistemically weak for reliance; a well-supported answer may later prove false; a proof can be valid but inapplicable; an honest qualified no-answer can be epistemically preferable to a fabricated substantive answer.

### 5.3 Practical usefulness

**Practical usefulness** is a scoped relation between identified answer content and an identified user's or organized subject's declared purpose, capability, constraints, time, resources, environment, and intended action or decision.

Usefulness is not intrinsic, universal, or automatically beneficial. An answer may be useful to one subject and unusable, harmful, inaccessible, unlawful, or strategically adverse to another. Stream E owns the fuller value, benefit, cost, and distribution treatment.

### 5.4 Responsible-answer profile

A **responsible-answer profile** extends the quality profile to material conditions of reliance and consequence, including affected subjects, safety, fairness, legality, privacy, security, Authority, Responsibility, transparency, challenge, correction, and lifecycle governance.

It does not certify moral goodness, legal compliance, or harmlessness universally. It shows which claims were assessed, by whom, under which authority and method, what remains unresolved, and what conditions or restrictions govern reliance.

## 7. Profile rather than universal scalar

### 7.1 Why one scalar fails

A universal scalar would require hidden answers to questions that SymK cannot answer uniformly:

- which dimensions matter for every answer;
- how constructs are operationalized across Domains;
- whether a defect is compensable;
- whose preferences and harms count;
- which time horizon applies;
- how uncertainty and missing data are scored;
- how rights, duties, legal prohibitions, or safety constraints enter aggregation;
- how catastrophic tails compare with average gains;
- how affected distributions are weighted; and
- who has authority to set thresholds and trade-offs.

These are substantive Domain, governance, ethical, legal, professional, and project decisions. Hiding them in a score does not remove them.

### 7.2 Legitimate local scalarization

A Domain or project may use a score when it declares:

- the construct and intended use;
- applicable population, Context, and time;
- dimensions, methods, scales, weights, and missing-data rules;
- non-compensable constraints or vetoes;
- uncertainty, sensitivity, and distribution;
- authority for thresholds and decision rules;
- validation and monitoring Evidence;
- known exclusions, gaming risks, and proxy limits; and
- challenge, correction, and retirement conditions.

Such a score is a Projection of selected profile relations. It cannot replace the profile or receive universal SymK authority.

### 7.3 Non-scalar result states

Dimension results may be:

- supported or unsupported;
- high, moderate, low, mixed, or indeterminate under a declared ordinal rule;
- interval- or distribution-valued;
- conditional on assumptions or intended use;
- disputed among assessors;
- unknown due to missing or protected Evidence;
- not assessed;
- not applicable under a reasoned rule;
- failed due to a non-compensable condition; or
- expired, defeated, or superseded.

“Unknown,” “not assessed,” and “not applicable” are different states. None equals zero or pass by default.

## 8. Dimension applicability and materiality

No fixed inventory is exhaustive or universally mandatory. A dimension becomes material through combinations of:

- the governed question's answer obligation;
- answer kind and form;
- intended reliance, decision, action, or communication;
- consequence severity, reversibility, and distribution;
- Domain standards and external authority;
- uncertainty, novelty, and change;
- population and affected-subject diversity;
- dependency, opacity, and failure mode;
- power asymmetry and challenge constraints;
- use across Contexts, jurisdictions, or time; and
- disagreement among Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views.

Applicability must be reasoned. Declaring a difficult dimension “not applicable” cannot be used to evade a material obligation. Conversely, requiring every dimension for every trivial answer creates noise, cost, and false assurance.

Materiality concerns whether omission or misassessment could change the answer's interpretation, reliance, decision, action, affected distribution, or responsible-use status. Materiality is itself scoped and challengeable; it is not established by provider preference.

## 9. Correctness, accuracy, and truth

### 9.1 Correctness

Correctness is conformity of identified answer content with an applicable rule, reference, constraint, solution condition, or accepted evaluation procedure. It is especially natural for calculations, formal systems, classifications, specifications, and tasks with governed answer conditions.

Correctness is always relative to what is being checked. A syntactically correct program may implement the wrong requirement; a rubric-correct response may answer a misframed prompt; a legally correct statement in one jurisdiction may be wrong in another.

### 9.2 Accuracy

Accuracy concerns closeness, fidelity, or error relative to an identified referent or target under a declared method. It may apply to measurements, estimates, labels, forecasts, summaries, translations, or factual representations.

An average accuracy value can hide subgroup, tail, or class-specific failures. Accuracy does not establish calibration, relevance, support, fairness, usefulness, or safety.

### 9.3 Truth

Truth concerns whether propositional content is the case under the relevant semantics, world, Context, and time. SymK does not select one complete theory of truth here.

Not all answer content is truth-apt in the same way. Recommendations, designs, plans, procedures, value judgments, interpretations, and demonstrations may require adequacy conditions other than simple factual truth. Even where truth applies, an answer can contain true claims while remaining irrelevant, incomplete, misleading, unsupported, unsafe, or unusable.

### 9.4 Error and defect localization

An incorrect answer should preserve which part failed, under which standard, with what consequence. Error in one claim does not automatically falsify every component; a correct fragment does not redeem a materially defective whole. Corrections should preserve lineage rather than overwrite the earlier state.

## 10. Correspondence, relevance, and responsiveness

Stream C's thin answer relation records that content is presented as satisfying all or part of an answer obligation. Stream D assesses whether it actually does so adequately.

**Relevance** concerns the material bearing of answer content on the identified question, issue, decision, or use. Topic overlap is insufficient. Relevance can vary across subquestions, audiences, times, and intended uses.

**Responsiveness** concerns whether the answer addresses the requested form, level, timing, and communicative obligation. A substantively relevant answer may be nonresponsive if it refuses the requested comparison, omits the requested basis, or answers at an unusable level.

Correspondence, relevance, and responsiveness must remain distinct:

- claimed correspondence can exist while assessed relevance is weak;
- relevant background may not answer the question;
- a direct response can be false;
- a true answer can be poorly adapted to the requested form; and
- a qualified no-answer may be highly responsive and epistemically responsible without substantive content satisfying the original obligation.

## 11. Completeness, coverage, and residual obligation

Completeness is not universal maximal detail. It concerns whether the answer covers the material components of the governed answer obligation at an appropriate level.

Coverage assessment should identify:

- addressed and unaddressed subquestions;
- included and excluded populations, cases, periods, jurisdictions, and scenarios;
- assumptions, exceptions, boundary cases, and interactions;
- uncertainty and conflicting Evidence;
- affected subjects and consequence classes;
- residual obligations after partial or staged answers; and
- whether omitted material is unavailable, protected, deferred, or overlooked.

An answer can be complete for a narrow question and dangerously incomplete for the underlying Domain problem. A long answer can be incomplete; a one-word answer can be complete. Completeness does not imply correctness, support, relevance, usefulness, or responsible use.

## 12. Precision, specificity, granularity, and resolution

Precision concerns the fineness or repeatability of expression, estimate, or distinction under a declared method. Specificity concerns how narrowly content identifies a subject, condition, class, or claim. Granularity and resolution concern the level at which distinctions are represented.

More precision is not automatically better:

- unsupported decimal places create false precision;
- narrow specificity may omit uncertainty or transfer limits;
- high technical resolution may reduce Human usability;
- aggregation may be appropriate for privacy but hide distribution;
- coarse ranges may be better calibrated than point estimates; and
- a precise answer to the wrong question remains poor.

The appropriate level is relative to question, Evidence, intended use, consequence, and audience.

## 13. Support, warrant, Evidence, and provenance

### 13.1 Support relation

Support concerns whether identified Grounds and Evidence bear appropriately on identified answer claims under declared methods and assumptions. Citation presence is not support by itself.

A support assessment should distinguish:

- source existence from source integrity;
- source relevance from entailment;
- entailment from real-world applicability;
- direct from indirect Evidence;
- independent corroboration from copied dependence;
- current from stale Evidence;
- available Evidence from all possible Evidence;
- public absence from protected or inaccessible Evidence; and
- evidentiary strength from authority or popularity.

### 13.2 Warrant and method

Warrant concerns why the move from Grounds and Evidence to answer claim is acceptable under a declared epistemic or Domain method. A large evidence volume cannot replace an invalid method. A valid method cannot repair fabricated data.

### 13.3 Provenance

Provenance records origin, custody, transformation, contribution, and version relations. It supports assessment but does not establish truth, quality, ownership, Authorization, or Responsibility automatically.

### 13.4 Counterevidence and defeaters

Rebutting Evidence challenges content; undercutting Evidence challenges support; applicability defeaters challenge transfer; governance constraints may restrict reliance; consequence Evidence may reopen safety or responsible-use assessment. Defeat must identify which relation changes.

## 14. Reasoning adequacy, explanation, and comprehensibility

### 14.1 Reasoning adequacy

Reasoning adequacy evaluates a claimed reasoning episode or product under relevant inference, deliberation, or judgment standards. It may bear on answer support, but it is not the answer's whole quality.

A correct answer from luck can have weak process support. A sound process can produce a wrong answer when material Evidence is unavailable. A proof can establish a formal result while leaving real-world premises or applicability unresolved.

### 14.2 Explanation quality

Explanation quality is relative to an explanation question, audience, purpose, causal or justificatory standard, and level. An explanation may be clear yet false, faithful yet inaccessible, causally accurate yet irrelevant to justification, or useful for learning while not tracing the actual process.

Generated rationales and traces remain fallible Representations under Stream B. Fluency and detail do not prove fidelity.

### 14.3 Comprehensibility

Comprehensibility concerns whether intended users can form an adequate understanding of the answer, conditions, limitations, and implications. It is affected by language, accessibility, expertise, cognitive load, presentation, and context.

Comprehensibility is not simplification at any cost. A simplified Representation must declare material loss. Technical precision and Human intelligibility may require aligned but different Views.

## 15. Calibration, uncertainty, confidence, and humility

Calibration concerns the relation between expressed confidence or uncertainty and observed or justified frequency, error, or credibility under a declared class and method. A single probability cannot represent every uncertainty type.

Material uncertainty may concern:

- question Identity, framing, assumptions, and answerability;
- facts, measurements, labels, and missing data;
- source integrity and provenance;
- model, method, inference, and process attribution;
- interpretation and semantic ambiguity;
- population, transfer, Context, time, and jurisdiction;
- alternatives, causal relations, outcomes, and side effects;
- values, weights, thresholds, Authority, and Responsibility; or
- assessment disagreement and unknown unknowns.

Confidence expression should match the claim and evidence basis. Assertiveness, hedging, numerical probability, consensus, or model confidence is not calibration automatically.

Epistemic humility requires visible limits and revision readiness; it does not require indiscriminate doubt, equal weight to unsupported alternatives, or refusal to act. Overconfidence and performative uncertainty are both failure modes.

## 16. Robustness, sensitivity, transfer, and generalization

**Robustness** concerns whether a material assessment or answer relation remains adequate under declared relevant variation. Variation may concern data, assumptions, model, method, parameter, source, population, environment, adversary, component, Representation, or time.

**Sensitivity** identifies which variations materially change a result. High sensitivity is not always defect; it may reveal a genuinely conditional Domain relation. Concealing sensitivity is a defect when it changes responsible reliance.

**Transfer** and **generalization** concern applicability beyond the Evidence-producing or assessment Context. They require an identified source population or environment, target population or environment, preserved and changed conditions, mechanism or similarity basis, uncertainty, and counterevidence.

Robustness cannot mean invariant under every possible change. A robust answer for one Scope may fail under Domain shift. Average stability may hide subgroup or tail failure. Repeated benchmark success may reflect leakage or benchmark-specific optimization rather than transfer.

Where robustness is not tested, the profile should say “not assessed” or “unknown,” not silently infer stability.

## 17. Timeliness, currency, and temporal fitness

Timeliness concerns whether answer delivery and availability fit the governed use and consequence horizon. Currency concerns whether content, Evidence, models, law, dependencies, and Context remain applicable at the relevant time.

An immediate response can be stale. A slow answer can remain current. A formerly correct answer can expire without becoming historically false. A preliminary answer may be timely for triage but inadequate for final reliance.

Temporal fitness may require distinguishing:

- request time;
- source and Evidence time;
- model, data, law, and configuration versions;
- answer occurrence and assessment time;
- review, verification, challenge, and correction time;
- intended decision or action time;
- consequence horizon;
- expiry and revalidation conditions; and
- latency or deadline constraints.

Stream E owns the full response-latency and responsible-answer-time treatment. Stream D records timeliness and currency as quality dimensions without making speed intrinsically good.

## 18. Actionability, usability, accessibility, and practical fitness

### 18.1 Actionability

Actionability concerns whether identified users can translate answer content into a declared decision, action, inquiry, or next step under actual constraints. It may require specificity, sequence, prerequisites, resources, permissions, timing, alternatives, failure handling, and stop conditions.

An answer can be actionable but wrong, unauthorized, unsafe, unlawful, or manipulative. An epistemically strong answer can be non-actionable because the question seeks understanding rather than conduct or because no legitimate action is available.

### 18.2 Usability

Usability concerns effective, efficient, and comprehensible use by identified users in an identified setting. It can involve discoverability, form, navigation, language, cognitive load, interoperability, and workflow fit.

Ease of use does not establish answer truth or value. Friction may be justified where review, consent, deliberation, security, or affected-party challenge is material.

### 18.3 Accessibility

Accessibility concerns whether subjects with relevant sensory, cognitive, linguistic, technical, economic, geographic, or institutional conditions can meaningfully access and use the answer. It is not reducible to interface compliance or availability of a file.

Accessibility can be a Human, practical, fairness, legal, and responsible-use condition simultaneously. These relations should be linked without collapsing their distinct grounds and authorities.

### 18.4 Feasibility and practicality

Feasibility concerns whether proposed conduct can occur under material resource, capability, dependency, time, Authority, and environmental constraints. Practicality concerns fit with the real operating setting. Neither establishes desirability, legitimacy, benefit, or value.

## 19. Safety, harm, security, and privacy

### 19.1 Safety

Safety assessment concerns hazards, exposures, protective conditions, failures, and consequences associated with answer content, communication, reliance, or use. It is claim-, use-, subject-, and time-specific.

A safe answer is not simply one that caused no observed harm. Absence of harm may reflect non-use, luck, underreporting, delay, or shifted burden. Safety may require precaution, uncertainty treatment, monitoring, fallback, correction, repair, and escalation.

### 19.2 Harm and affected subjects

Harm claims should identify subject, interest or capability affected, mechanism, severity, likelihood or uncertainty, duration, reversibility, distribution, causal Contribution, alternatives, and remedy path. SymK does not define one complete harm theory here.

Provider, user, beneficiary, and affected subject can differ. A useful answer for the user can expose another subject to material harm.

### 19.3 Security

Security concerns confidentiality, integrity, availability, authenticity, misuse, abuse, adversarial conditions, and resilience under a declared threat model. More transparency is not always more secure; secrecy is not proof of security.

A correct answer can be unsafe to disclose. A refusal can be responsible where disclosure risk is material, provided the refusal and any safe alternative are governed rather than falsely presented as the substantive answer.

### 19.4 Privacy

Privacy concerns governed collection, inference, disclosure, linkage, use, retention, control, and consequence relations involving persons or protected subjects. De-identification, consent, legality, security, and public availability do not establish privacy adequacy automatically.

Privacy-preserving transformation may reduce completeness, precision, auditability, or usefulness. The loss and authority for the trade-off must remain visible.

## 20. Fairness, distribution, inclusion, and non-erasure

Fairness is not one universal statistic. It may concern treatment, opportunity, process, representation, burden, benefit, error distribution, standing, remedy, or another Domain- and authority-specific relation.

A fairness assessment should identify:

- subjects, groups, and comparison classes;
- protected, relevant, contested, and proxy attributes;
- the object of allocation, treatment, prediction, error, or burden;
- the fairness criterion and its source;
- data and measurement limitations;
- distribution across ordinary, minority, edge, and high-consequence cases;
- incompatible criteria and non-compensable constraints;
- affected-subject participation and challenge;
- temporal and cumulative effects; and
- response, correction, repair, or remedy paths.

Equal average performance does not establish fair process or consequence. Different treatment does not establish unfairness without the governing criterion. Consensus among designers does not erase excluded subjects.

The DR-014 minimum ethical boundary requires affected-subject non-erasure and visible Power, dependence, vulnerability, uncertainty, distribution, challenge, and repair. Stream D applies those accepted boundaries without selecting substantive fairness law or policy.

## 21. Legality, compliance, standards, and Authority

### 21.1 Legality

Legality concerns conformity with applicable law under an identified jurisdiction, time, authority, subject, act, and interpretation. Legal assessment may itself be uncertain, contested, or outside the assessor's Authority.

Lawfulness is not truth, fairness, safety, morality, usefulness, or value. An answer may accurately describe unlawful conduct; a lawful recommendation may remain harmful; a legally prohibited disclosure may contain true and relevant content.

### 21.2 Compliance

Compliance concerns conformity with an identified external, professional, organizational, contractual, procedural, or technical requirement. Passing a checklist or audit does not establish the underlying construct beyond the requirement's Scope and method.

### 21.3 Standards

Standards can define dimensions, methods, evidence requirements, thresholds, or reporting forms within their jurisdiction. Their existence, adoption, or popularity does not establish universal SymK authority or current applicability.

### 21.4 Authority separation

At least the following may differ:

- authority to define a construct;
- authority to issue a standard;
- competence to perform an assessment;
- authority to set a threshold or weight;
- authority to certify or approve;
- authority to authorize reliance;
- authority to decide or act;
- responsibility for monitoring, correction, or remedy; and
- standing to challenge.

An assessor's expertise does not create decision Authority. A decision Authority's approval does not create epistemic quality. A provider's metric choice does not bind affected subjects or external authorities automatically.

## 22. Value, benefit, outcome, and favorable-consequence boundaries

Quality concerns assessed properties and relations of an answer and its responsible use. Value concerns what matters to identified claimants, beneficiaries, affected subjects, institutions, or Domains under declared purposes and commitments. They overlap but do not substitute.

An answer may be:

- high quality yet low value because the question is trivial or obsolete;
- valuable despite limited quality when no better information exists and urgency is material;
- epistemically adequate but practically useless for the intended user;
- useful but unsafe or unfair;
- lawful but low value;
- valuable to one subject and costly or harmful to another;
- followed by a favorable outcome through luck; or
- followed by harm because application, timing, or Context changed.

Outcome Evidence can reopen a quality assessment but does not retroactively prove every dimension. Stream E owns value, benefit, outcome, consequence, cost, and trade-off analysis.

## 23. Responsible-answer profile

### 23.1 Purpose

The responsible-answer profile prevents “high quality” from laundering an answer into permission or desirability. It asks whether the material conditions for a declared reliance have been identified and assessed proportionately.

### 23.2 Candidate profile families

The profile may include, where material:

1. question correspondence and framing adequacy;
2. correctness, accuracy, and truth conditions;
3. relevance, responsiveness, completeness, and precision;
4. Evidence, warrant, provenance, and reasoning linkage;
5. explanation, comprehensibility, and accessibility;
6. uncertainty, calibration, robustness, transfer, and currency;
7. actionability, usability, feasibility, and operational fit;
8. safety, security, privacy, and misuse resistance;
9. fairness, inclusion, distribution, and affected-subject non-erasure;
10. legality, compliance, professional standards, and jurisdiction;
11. Authority, Authorization, Responsibility, challenge, and remedy;
12. dependency, fallback, correction, supersession, and lifecycle; and
13. declared value, cost, time, outcome, and consequence interfaces without deciding them.

This is a candidate family inventory, not a universal checklist or metric.

### 23.3 Minimum responsible-reliance condition

For a material intended use, a claim of responsible answer reliance requires at least:

- an identified answer and question version;
- a declared use, users, affected horizon, Context, and time;
- materially applicable dimensions and standards;
- proportionate Evidence and uncertainty;
- visible conflicts, unknowns, exclusions, and non-compensable failures;
- competent assessment and sourced threshold or reliance Authority where required;
- protected challenge and accountable response;
- correction, restriction, withdrawal, repair, and lineage paths; and
- no substitution of score, status, adoption, outcome, or nominal oversight for these conditions.

This does not create an actual Authorization or certify responsible use. It is a conceptual claim envelope.

### 23.4 Non-compensable and veto conditions

Some applicable regimes may designate conditions that cannot be offset by strengths elsewhere, such as a legal prohibition, unacceptable safety hazard, invalid consent, missing Authority, or severe non-remediable harm. SymK does not select those conditions universally.

When such a condition applies, aggregation must preserve it. A high average score cannot silently compensate for it.

## 24. Assessment, assessor, and assessment Evidence

### 24.1 Assessment occurrence

An assessment is a bounded process or judgment relating an identified object to a declared dimension, standard, method, Evidence set, assessor, Context, and time. It may be performed by Human, artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, or hybrid arrangements.

The lowest adequate organized level should be identified. “The system assessed quality” is insufficient when the result depends on a benchmark author, model, reviewer, workflow, threshold owner, and approval body.

### 24.2 Assessor competence

Competence is dimension-, Domain-, method-, population-, and use-specific. Expertise in one area does not transfer automatically to another. Credentials, employment, authority, confidence, or model performance are Evidence candidates, not competence by definition.

### 24.3 Independence and conflict

Assessment should disclose material incentives, dependencies, self-review, provider control, data control, affected interests, and institutional pressure. Independence is not binary and does not guarantee correctness.

### 24.4 Human and automated assessment

Human review is not sufficient merely because a Human is present. Automated evaluation is not invalid merely because it is automated. Each requires Evidence of construct validity, competence, effective attention, failure detection, challenge, and correction proportionate to use.

### 24.5 Inter-assessor disagreement

Disagreement should identify whether assessors differ about object, Evidence, construct, standard, method, threshold, values, jurisdiction, or result. Aggregating votes can erase the reason for disagreement. Consensus can be wrong; dissent can be unsupported.

## 25. Thresholds, weights, priorities, and trade-offs

### 25.1 Thresholds

A threshold is a decision or classification boundary applied to a declared measure or assessment for a declared purpose. It requires a source, Authority, Scope, Context, time, population, uncertainty treatment, and review condition.

Threshold crossing does not change the underlying answer magically. Close cases, measurement error, and distribution around the threshold remain material.

### 25.2 Weights and priorities

Weights encode relative importance under an objective or decision rule. Priorities may be ordinal, conditional, lexicographic, constrained, or non-compensable rather than numeric.

Weights do not emerge neutrally from data. They express or operationalize a purpose, policy, preference, duty, or authority choice. Different stakeholders may have legitimate incompatible weights.

### 25.3 Trade-offs

A trade-off claim should identify:

- dimensions and objects being traded;
- subjects who gain and bear loss;
- baseline and counterfactual;
- amount, uncertainty, and time horizon;
- reversibility and remedy;
- authority and consent where relevant;
- non-compensable conditions;
- distribution and externalization; and
- alternative designs that avoid or alter the trade-off.

Observed negative correlation is not proof of an unavoidable trade-off. Provider inconvenience is not a universal constraint. Stream E develops the fuller quality-speed-cost-value trade-off boundary.

### 25.4 Missing dimensions

A scoring or decision rule must not treat omitted, unmeasured, protected, or unknown dimensions as neutral. Missingness requires its own state and, where material, sensitivity or restriction.

## 26. Aggregation, comparison, ranking, and optimization

### 26.1 Comparison prerequisites

Claims that one answer is better than another require materially stable:

- question and answer obligations;
- answer kind, Scope, Context, population, and time;
- intended use and affected horizon;
- dimension meanings and applicability;
- Evidence and assessment methods;
- baseline, comparator, and version;
- uncertainty and missingness treatment;
- threshold, weight, and authority assumptions; and
- transformation and loss records.

If these change, the claim may compare different objects rather than improvement.

### 26.2 Pareto and dominance claims

One answer may dominate another across declared dimensions and subjects, but dominance depends on dimension selection, direction, uncertainty, and non-compensable conditions. Apparent dominance can disappear when omitted affected groups, tail risks, lifecycle costs, or time horizons enter.

### 26.3 Ranking

Ranking orders answers under a declared rule. It does not show absolute adequacy, meaningful distance, or responsible reliance. Several answers may be incomparable, tied within uncertainty, or differently preferable for different uses.

### 26.4 Optimization

Optimization requires an objective, constraints, feasible set, model, data, horizon, and authority. Optimizing a proxy can degrade the underlying construct, shift costs, or induce gaming. No selected metric becomes the goal by SymK definition.

### 26.5 Benchmark boundary

A benchmark is an assessment instrument over a defined item set, population, method, and scoring rule. It may provide Evidence for limited dimensions. It does not by itself establish question framing, real-world truth, transfer, robustness, safety, fairness, responsible use, value, or Domain outcome.

Benchmark contamination, leakage, evaluator dependence, selective reporting, distribution shift, and repeated tuning must remain visible.

## 27. Quality lifecycle, monitoring, correction, and supersession

Quality is not timeless. Material events may include:

- profile creation and initial assessment;
- independent review or replication;
- challenge and dissent;
- qualification, restriction, or Reliance Authorization;
- answer revision or question reframing;
- new Evidence or counterevidence;
- model, data, law, population, dependency, or Context change;
- observed use, misuse, outcome, harm, or distribution;
- correction, withdrawal, repair, remedy, or recurrence prevention;
- expiry, revalidation, reaffirmation, or supersession; and
- historical retention.

A changed score need not indicate changed answer quality; it may reflect a new instrument or threshold. An unchanged score can hide changed Context. Reassessment should preserve object, method, and version lineage.

Correction of content, assessment, status, use, and consequence are separate events. Overwriting a wrong answer erases evidence needed for accountability, repair, and Learning.

## 28. Candidate approaches considered

| Approach | Strength | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| Quality equals truth | Protects factual orientation | Excludes non-truth-apt answers and ignores relevance, support, uncertainty, use, and responsibility |
| Quality equals correctness against expected answer | Easy to test | Reifies rubric, benchmark, or misframed prompt and hides transfer |
| Quality equals user satisfaction | Captures experience | Popularity and preference can reward false, harmful, manipulative, or flattering answers |
| Quality equals expert approval | Uses competence | Confuses expertise, Authority, consensus, and assessment; experts disagree and err |
| Quality equals successful outcome | Consequence-sensitive | Outcome may follow luck, intervention, delay, or shifted harm and cannot validate process or content |
| Quality equals benchmark score | Repeatable and scalable | Instrument-specific proxy hides construct validity, distribution, gaming, and real use |
| Quality equals weighted universal scalar | Supports ranking and optimization | Conceals weights, authority, non-commensurability, missingness, vetoes, and affected distribution |
| Fixed universal checklist | Promotes consistency | Over- and under-includes dimensions across answer kinds and consequences |
| Domains alone define quality | Respects specialization | Leaves cross-Domain substitutions, provenance, authority, and lifecycle uncontrolled |
| Epistemic quality alone | Protects truth-seeking | Omits usability, safety, fairness, legality, affected subjects, and responsible reliance |
| Responsible use equals compliance | Operationally auditable | Law and checklist conformity do not exhaust epistemic, ethical, practical, or consequence conditions |
| Scoped multidimensional evidence-bearing profile | Preserves plurality, authority, unknowns, distribution, and challenge | Requires more explicit governance and leaves metrics and thresholds downstream |

The last approach is provisionally preferred because it supplies a universal conceptual grammar without imposing a universal quality function.

## 32. Human, Scientific, and Engineering View obligations

### 32.1 Human View

The Human View should expose, where material:

- the question and answer being assessed in comprehensible form;
- intended use, users, beneficiaries, and affected subjects;
- material dimensions and why they matter;
- strengths, defects, unknowns, disagreements, and limitations;
- uncertainty without false precision or performative hedging;
- source, assessor, competence, conflicts, Authority, and status;
- practical usability, accessibility, safety, fairness, and legal limits;
- non-compensable conditions and affected distribution;
- what changed through summary, scoring, translation, or interface presentation; and
- effective challenge, clarification, correction, appeal, and withdrawal paths.

It must not let tone, fluency, confidence, interface polish, expert title, badge, ranking, “approved” label, or outcome anecdote manufacture quality.

### 32.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View should expose, where material:

- constructs, operationalizations, populations, Contexts, and time;
- truth, correctness, relevance, coverage, precision, support, and applicability conditions;
- Evidence, counterevidence, methods, models, assumptions, uncertainty, and defeaters;
- measurement validity, reliability, calibration, sensitivity, robustness, and transfer;
- comparator, baseline, missingness, distributions, tails, and subgroup results;
- assessor and instrument dependence, replication, disagreement, and conflicts;
- causal limits among answer, use, decision, action, outcome, and consequence; and
- conditions of restriction, defeat, revalidation, and reopening.

It must not convert statistical significance, model fit, publication, citation count, benchmark score, average accuracy, consensus, or replication into complete quality or responsible use.

### 32.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View should expose, where material:

- identifiers and versions for question, answer, profile, Evidence, method, model, data, configuration, and assessor;
- dimension, applicability, result-form, unknown, disputed, and not-applicable states;
- measurement, benchmark, score, threshold, weight, aggregation, and decision-rule metadata;
- lineage, transformations, summaries, translations, redactions, and declared loss;
- distribution, failure modes, alerts, restrictions, expiry, and revalidation triggers;
- access, privacy, security, retention, correction, rollback, withdrawal, and supersession behavior;
- use, reliance, decision, action, outcome, and consequence links without causal overclaim; and
- mappings to Human and Scientific meanings and unresolved mismatches.

It must not treat schema validity, endpoint success, test completion, dashboard status, threshold crossing, deployment, logging, or automation as construct validity, quality, responsible use, or outcome.

### 32.4 Cross-view alignment

The Views may use different forms and granularity but must preserve the same material answer and question versions, dimension meanings, Evidence, standards, methods, uncertainty, assessors, Authority, distribution, profile status, use conditions, and lineage. Transformations and omissions must be declared.

View disagreement is governable Evidence. It may reveal unclear meaning, invalid construct, inaccessible communication, proxy drift, implementation defect, missing affected subjects, or legitimate Domain pluralism. No View prevails automatically because it is more intuitive, formal, measurable, authoritative, or operational.

### 35.1 DQ-014

DQ-014 asks how answer quality, speed, and total cost should be measured and traded off.

## 38. Confidence and reopening conditions

**Confidence: High** that answer quality cannot be reduced universally to truth, correctness, fluency, confidence, consensus, Authority, adoption, benchmark score, scalar value, or favorable outcome.

**Confidence: High** that construct, dimension, Evidence, assessment, profile, threshold, status, use, and record must remain distinct; and that unknown, not assessed, not applicable, disputed, and failed states must not collapse.

**Confidence: Medium–High** in the scoped multidimensional profile anchor, 32-part claim envelope, epistemic-adequacy and practical-usefulness distinctions, responsible-answer profile, authority separation, and non-compensable-condition preservation.

**Confidence: Medium** that Answer will retain its six-test admission after Streams E–F; that Answer Quality is best routed as a governed compound profile rather than an independently Foundational scalar property; and that the candidate dimension families cover the universal minimum without becoming a checklist.

**Confidence: Low to Medium** in universal materiality criteria, the exact boundary between answer quality and responsible use, the role of timeliness within quality, the minimum profile for protected Evidence, and the universality of any non-compensable condition.

The model must be reopened if later analysis shows that:

- a single universal scalar preserves every material construct, authority, distribution, and non-compensable condition without loss;
- quality can be intrinsic to answer content without question, use, Context, time, or assessor relation;
- the thin Answer anchor cannot survive actual relevance assessment;
- epistemic adequacy cannot be distinguished from truth or correctness;
- practical usefulness is constitutive of every answer-quality claim;
- responsible-answer profile necessarily becomes a complete ethical or legal theory;
- dimension applicability cannot be governed without one fixed checklist;
- unknown, not assessed, not applicable, disputed, and failed states cannot be represented distinctly;
- local scalarization can replace the profile without material loss;
- assessor competence, independence, and authority cannot be separated;
- affected distributions and veto conditions cannot coexist with legitimate aggregation;
- safety, fairness, legality, privacy, or security proves wholly outside answer-quality governance;
- Stream E shows speed, cost, value, or outcome is constitutive of quality rather than an interface;
- Stream F shows feedback or Learning requires a different quality lifecycle;
- Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views cannot preserve one profile across forms;
- derived-project grounding yields systematic false quality or responsible-use classifications;
- formalization cannot preserve plural dimensions, applicability, uncertainty, distribution, authority, challenge, and loss; or
- the model conflicts with Product Vision or Stage-Accepted DR-009 through DR-015.
