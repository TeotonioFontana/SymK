## Question

> What is amplified, to whom or what may that amplification be attributed, and what minimum conceptual conditions distinguish a Domain Intelligence Amplifier from an ordinary tool, information system, automation, or coordinated group?

## Required distinctions

- amplification, augmentation, automation, substitution, and replacement;
- participant capability, cooperative capability, and system-level capability;
- bearer, manifestation, attribution, and evidence;
- domain boundary, domain Knowledge, and domain purpose;
- DIA system class versus one implementation architecture;
- artificial Intelligence as possible participant versus necessary defining component.

## Consequence boundary

The package will clarify professional amplification, intermediate-work automation, displacement, dependency, deskilling, concentration, and changed expert-formation pathways as foreseeable conceptual consequences. It will not prescribe universal labor or selection policy.

## Deliverables

- mature working definitions of Amplified Intelligence and DIA;
- bearer and attribution account;
- minimum DIA conditions and explicit non-conditions;
- conceptual treatment of DQ-011, DQ-012, and DQ-016;
- bounded conceptual contribution to DQ-013 and DQ-019;
- evidence categories to be operationally tested in 2.7;
- cross-paper revision-ledger entries.

## Out of scope

Operational certification, product architecture, AI mandate, domain success thresholds, or complete policy for professional displacement and formation.
