## 1. Question and analytical result

> How must SymK distinguish amplification from assistance, enablement, augmentation, acceleration, scaling, optimization, automation, substitution, and replacement before identifying an amplified Intelligence Bearer or defining the Domain Intelligence Amplifier system class?

Stream A supports the following working answer:

> **Amplification is a comparative, scoped, temporal, and evidenced relationship in which an identified intervention, resource, relation, or organized arrangement contributes to an increase in an identified subject's relevant effective capability relative to a declared and materially comparable baseline, across relevant variation, with the subject boundary, capability dimensions, integration, gains, losses, dependencies, alternatives, uncertainty, and conditions of persistence made explicit.**

> **Assistance and enablement concern support or possibility; augmentation concerns added resources, functions, reach, or available capacity; acceleration concerns time; scaling concerns volume or reach; optimization concerns performance against a selected criterion; automation concerns transferred or mechanized execution; substitution concerns replacement of one contribution, activity, function, role occupancy, or pathway by another; and replacement concerns temporal change in components, participants, roles, or systems. Each may contribute to amplification, coexist with it, or occur without it. None establishes amplification by itself.**

This candidate is deliberately relational. It does not make amplification a substance stored in a tool, a label attached to a product, a scalar percentage, or a synonym for improvement. It also does not require every gain to be internalized by an unaided Human. A capability increase may be:

- **concurrent or relation-enabled**, existing while a declared support relation remains available;
- **retained**, continuing after the support or intervention is removed;
- **configured-system-level**, attributable to a stable organized whole rather than any component alone; or
- **developmental**, emerging over time through Formation, Learning, practice, feedback, or institutional change.

These are candidate temporal and level qualifications, not accepted dispositions. Stream B must determine the amplified Bearer and the relation between actual configuration, attributed profile, integration, and trade-offs. Stream C must determine the DIA system-class boundary.

The working result separates eleven questions that an amplification claim must not collapse:

1. **Subject:** whose or what capability is claimed to change?
2. **Capability object:** which capacity, dimension, specialization, configuration, or task-family competence is claimed to increase?
3. **Baseline:** relative to which prior, unaided, alternative, counterfactual, or matched state?
4. **Intervention:** through which resource, relation, participant, process, environment, or organized arrangement?
5. **Change:** what became possible, more reliable, more integrated, more transferable, more robust, or otherwise increased?
6. **Variation:** across which materially different cases, conditions, novelty, perturbations, and failures?
7. **Boundary and level:** which components, participants, resources, and dependencies are inside or outside the proposed subject?
8. **Time:** when does the gain exist, persist, decay, transfer, or disappear?
9. **Trade-offs and dependence:** which dimensions, practical alternatives, retained capabilities, or affected conditions worsened or became dependent?
10. **Attribution and status:** who claims and assesses the change, with which Evidence, authority, uncertainty, challenge, and intended use?
11. **Value and Responsibility:** is the change epistemically adequate, authorized, responsible, ethically compatible, beneficial, or worth its total cost?

Stream A addresses the first ten only far enough to establish the boundary. The eleventh remains governed by DR-014 and later treatment in 2.1.8, 2.2–2.3, and derived projects. A valid capability increase does not automatically establish a better answer, beneficial outcome, responsible operation, acceptable distribution, or lower total cost.

## 3. Why SymK needs this boundary

The Product Vision calls Amplified Intelligence the target capability and Domain Intelligence Amplifier the system class. Without a controlled boundary, those terms can become circular marketing labels: a DIA is a system said to amplify Intelligence, and Amplified Intelligence is whatever a DIA produces.

The boundary must prevent at least these substitutions:

- a larger repository becomes “more Intelligence”;
- retrieval becomes reasoning;
- assistance becomes a capability of the assisted subject without identifying dependence;
- an added component becomes an integrated whole-system capability;
- faster execution becomes broader or better Intelligence;
- higher throughput becomes improved reasoning capacity;
- a benchmark gain becomes multidimensional amplification;
- automation becomes Human augmentation regardless of what capability is lost;
- labor substitution becomes professional amplification because output is maintained;
- one correct answer becomes stable capacity across relevant variation;
- a product label becomes a system-class classification;
- use of artificial Intelligence becomes sufficient or necessary for a DIA;
- organizational productivity becomes every member's amplified Intelligence;
- a system-level gain becomes a user's retained capability;
- an unaided-after-use gain becomes the only admissible amplification, erasing genuine relation-enabled capacity;
- an external dependency is either silently absorbed into the Bearer or ignored;
- the baseline is weakened or changed after the result;
- the comparison uses unequal time, information, tools, selection, or assistance;
- benefits to immediate users erase costs and losses imposed on affected subjects;
- capability gain becomes proof of responsible or ethical operation; or
- implementation success becomes semantic authority.

SymK does not need a universal psychometric theory, one capability taxonomy, one production benchmark, one economics of labor, or one architecture. It needs a minimum comparative relation that preserves what changed, for whom or what, against what, through what, across which variation, at which level, with which dependence and losses, and under whose assessment.

## 4. Minimum amplification-claim anatomy

A material amplification claim must proportionately preserve the following fields. The list is a semantic claim envelope, not a required database schema.

| Dimension | Required question | Invalid shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Predicate identity | Is the claim about Intelligence, one capability dimension, a specialization, competence, skill, reasoning, throughput, access, or another object? | Using “intelligence” for every useful performance |
| Proposed subject | Which Human, artificial system, team, institution, configured system, distributed arrangement, hybrid, profession, or other organized subject is claimed to gain? | “The users,” “the AI,” or “the system” without a boundary |
| Subject level | Is the claim component-, participant-, relation-, team-, institution-, configured-system-, network-, or hybrid-level? | Copying a gain across levels |
| Identity and continuity | What makes the baseline and later subject the same comparison subject, or what controlled succession relation connects them? | Comparing different subjects as if one improved |
| Capability object | Which dimension, specialization, configuration, task family, or integrated capacity changed? | A generic “became smarter” claim |
| Domain | Within which field of inquiry or practice? | Universalizing a Domain-local success |
| Scope | Across which tasks, questions, populations, environments, stakes, and conditions? | One benchmark or use case as universal Scope |
| Context | Which institutional, cultural, technical, temporal, resource, and consequence conditions affect meaning? | Context-free gain |
| Baseline | Which version, period, resources, assistance, participants, selection conditions, and prior capabilities define the comparison? | Convenient before-state or weak control group |
| Comparator | Is the alternative prior state, unaided state, matched system, best available alternative, counterfactual removal, or another governed comparator? | Switching comparator after seeing results |
| Intervention or relation | Which tool, participant, Knowledge resource, process, environment, training, governance relation, or system arrangement contributed? | Treating co-occurrence as amplification cause |
| Mechanism | How could the intervention produce the claimed capability change? | Correlation, adoption, or attribution status as mechanism |
| Change type | Is the gain concurrent, retained, configured-system, developmental, or another declared type? | Treating every gain as internalized and durable |
| Dimensions and integration | Which dimensions increased, declined, remained stable, or became newly coordinated? | One aggregate score hiding profile changes |
| Relevant variation | Which materially different and not individually specified cases test capacity rather than memorized or accidental success? | Repetition of near-identical items |
| Time | When does the gain begin, persist, decay, require refresh, or end? | Timeless attribution from one observation |
| Resources and assistance | Which information, models, experts, interfaces, infrastructure, supervision, and environmental supports were available? | Unequal resource comparisons |
| Dependencies | Which providers, data, models, participants, skills, institutions, permissions, maintenance, and infrastructure are necessary or material? | Calling dependence independence |
| Counterfactuals and alternatives | What happens under removal, degradation, substitution, novel cases, failure, or an alternative intervention? | Success-only evidence |
| Gains, losses, and distribution | Who or what gains or loses which capabilities, alternatives, time, cost, Power, resilience, or exposure? | Netting unlike gains and losses silently |
| Evidence and uncertainty | Which methods, observations, failures, counterevidence, assumptions, and uncertainty support or defeat the claim? | Score, testimonial, explanation, or audit as proof |
| Claimant, assessor, and authority | Who makes and evaluates the claim, for which use, under which authority and challenge path? | Vendor, user, expert, regulator, or market status as truth |
| Status and record | Is the claim proposed, assessed, accepted, challenged, restricted, superseded, or rejected, and how is it represented? | Record or approval creating the capability |
| Consequence and Responsibility interface | Which affected subjects, risks, retained relations, response paths, and unresolved value questions remain? | Capability gain as ethical adequacy |

Low-consequence claims may use proportionately lighter evidence, but they may not omit a dimension whose absence would reverse the claim, hide a material loss, or change the proposed subject.

## 5. Neighboring change and intervention families

### 5.1 Assistance

**Assistance** is a contribution that supports another subject's activity, performance, inquiry, or access. Assistance may be episodic, external, replaceable, or tightly integrated. It establishes neither that the assisted subject gained a capacity nor that the combined arrangement is a new Bearer.

A calculator can assist arithmetic performance without changing unaided arithmetic capability. A colleague can assist a diagnosis without producing a new collective Intelligence. Repeated assistance may contribute to retained Learning, continuing dependence, or configured-system capability, but these are additional claims.

### 5.2 Enablement

**Enablement** makes an activity, option, access path, or performance possible under declared conditions. It concerns possibility, not necessarily increase. An accessibility device can enable a task previously impossible in a Context; this may support an amplification claim, but the subject, capability object, baseline, dependence, and variation still require evidence.

Enablement is not weaker in value than amplification. It is a different relation. SymK must not force every significant benefit into the amplification vocabulary.

### 5.3 Augmentation

**Augmentation** adds a resource, function, interface, participant, representation, modality, pathway, or available capacity to an identified subject or arrangement. Addition may increase reach without producing integrated capability, may duplicate an existing function, may introduce conflict or fragility, or may support genuine amplification.

Augmentation therefore answers “what was added?” Amplification answers “whose relevant effective capability increased, relative to what baseline, across which variation, with which integration and dependence?”

### 5.4 Acceleration

**Acceleration** reduces time to perform an activity or reach a state. Speed can be one capability dimension or a value dimension, but it is neither general capability nor answer quality. Faster failure, faster copying, faster unsupported inference, or faster escalation is not amplification by speed alone.

### 5.5 Scaling

**Scaling** increases volume, concurrency, coverage, frequency, reach, or number of served cases or participants. Scaling may amplify a service system's capacity while leaving reasoning quality unchanged; it may also increase error, concentration, dependency, or harm. Throughput is not Intelligence unless the admitted capability object and its relevant variation justify that classification.

### 5.6 Optimization

**Optimization** improves performance against an explicit objective or criterion under stated constraints. It may narrow a system around the chosen metric, trade away omitted dimensions, exploit measurement artifacts, or change the distribution of costs. Optimization supplies evidence about the selected criterion, not automatic amplification of Intelligence.

### 5.7 Automation

**Automation** transfers or mechanizes some execution, control, sequencing, monitoring, transformation, or decision-support activity so that it occurs with reduced contemporaneous intervention by a previously involved subject.

Automation can:

- assist or enable a Human or institution;
- augment a configured system;
- amplify a capability at one level;
- substitute for a participant's activity;
- reduce or eliminate practice that sustained a capability;
- create new supervision, verification, maintenance, or recovery work;
- shift Power, dependence, risk, and Responsibility; or
- leave the relevant Intelligence unchanged.

Automation is therefore neither necessary nor sufficient for amplification.

### 5.8 Substitution

**Substitution** occurs when one contribution, activity, function, pathway, resource, or role occupancy performs, replaces, or is used instead of another for a declared object and period.

Substitution must identify:

- what is substituted for what;
- whether the object is a task, function, contribution, activity, role occupant, capability, service, or outcome pathway;
- who selected or authorized the substitution;
- whether the previous capability remains available;
- which new dependencies and failure modes arise;
- whether output equivalence hides process, quality, challenge, or consequence differences; and
- what happens to displaced subjects and future capability formation.

Equivalent outputs do not establish equivalent capability, reasoning, Authority, Responsibility, or value.

### 5.9 Replacement

**Replacement** is a temporal relation in which a component, participant, role occupant, resource, process, model, organization, or configured system succeeds or displaces another. It raises Identity and succession questions that substitution during one activity may not.

Replacement may preserve, increase, reduce, or transform capability. A model upgrade, personnel transition, organizational merger, or complete technical rebuild cannot be called amplification until the comparison subject and continuity relation are justified. If the original subject no longer exists, the claim may concern successor capability rather than amplification of one persistent Bearer.

### 5.10 Complementation, composition, and integration

**Complementation** supplies capabilities or perspectives absent or weaker elsewhere. **Composition** arranges several contributions or capacities in one process or system. **Integration** is the stronger claim that relations among parts support a capacity attributable at the declared whole or relational level and not exhausted by independent component performances.

Complementarity and composition may support integration. Neither proves it. Stream B must test integration through organization, coordination, cross-component dependence, relevant variation, boundary sensitivity, counterfactual degradation, and competing lower-level explanations.

## 6. Baseline and comparison discipline

### 6.1 Amplification is baseline-relative

No amplification claim is intelligible without a baseline. The baseline must state:

- subject identity and version;
- capability object and profile;
- Domain, Scope, Context, and period;
- resources, assistance, Knowledge, tools, participants, and infrastructure;
- prior Formation, Learning, experience, selection, and adaptation;
- relevant variation and evaluation conditions;
- known failures, uncertainty, and environmental change; and
- why it is materially comparable to the later state.

“Without AI,” “before deployment,” “manual process,” or “typical professional” is insufficient unless those terms are operationally and semantically qualified.

### 6.2 Baseline kinds

Several baselines may answer different questions:

| Baseline | Question answered | Principal hazard |
|---|---|---|
| Prior-state | Did this subject change over time? | Maturation, selection, environment, or practice confounds |
| Unaided-state | What can the subject do without the identified support? | May erase relation-enabled capability as a legitimate object |
| Supported-state | What can the subject do with the declared arrangement active? | May hide dependence, external control, or resource inequality |
| Matched-comparator | How does a comparable subject or system perform without the intervention? | Matching and selection bias |
| Best-available alternative | Does the intervention improve on a realistic alternative? | Domain and resource assumptions |
| Counterfactual-removal | What degrades when the intervention or relation is removed? | Dependence does not prove integration or Intelligence |
| Successor-state | How did capability change across replacement or succession? | Subject Identity may not persist |
| Target-profile | Which governed capability configuration is sought? | Target values may be contested or omit losses |

No one baseline owns every claim. A project may need several comparisons, and disagreements among them must remain visible.

### 6.3 Comparison invariance

Unless the difference is itself the object of analysis, the comparison should hold materially relevant conditions stable or explicitly model their change. These include:

- subject boundary and level;
- task and question distribution;
- information and Knowledge access;
- time and attention;
- training and practice;
- interface and usability;
- supervision and verification;
- economic and institutional incentives;
- environmental conditions and stakes;
- participant selection;
- external providers and infrastructure; and
- evaluation method and threshold.

A claim may still be valid when several conditions change, but it must then be a multi-factor change claim rather than a simple intervention effect.

### 6.4 No baseline shopping

A claim fails the baseline boundary when its proponent:

- chooses the weakest observed Human or system performance after seeing results;
- compares assisted performance with an unaided baseline while calling the gain retained;
- excludes verification time or failure recovery from one state;
- changes the subject boundary only in the successful state;
- compares a selected expert/tool team with an unselected general population;
- counts training, data, infrastructure, or external labor on only one side;
- moves from individual to organization level across the comparison; or
- uses a benchmark version, threshold, or metric unavailable at baseline without declaring the transformation.

## 7. Capability change, manifestation, and performance

### 7.1 Capability is not one performance event

An actual capability is a disposition or organized capacity manifested across relevant variation, not merely one successful output. Performance evidence may support or defeat a capability attribution, but a material claim should address:

- repeated and varied cases;
- cases not individually specified in advance;
- novel combinations or perturbations;
- transfer beyond rehearsed examples where the Scope requires it;
- failure recognition and recovery;
- robustness under materially changed conditions;
- sensitivity to relevant Context;
- counterfactual response to altered inputs or resources;
- alternative explanations such as leakage, memorization, selection, or external completion; and
- temporal stability appropriate to the claimed change type.

This does not require every Intelligence to learn online, generalize universally, or succeed without error. It requires evidence proportionate to the claimed capability and Scope.

### 7.2 Effective capability

“Effective capability” in the Stream A candidate means capability available to the declared subject under the declared conditions, resources, relations, and dependencies. It is not limited to what is internally stored or unaided.

This permits a professional-plus-tool arrangement to support a genuine concurrent capability claim while preventing the gain from being copied automatically to:

- the unaided professional;
- the tool alone;
- the organization;
- every participant;
- the configured system as a new Intelligence Bearer; or
- the future professional after the relation ends.

Each attribution requires its own subject, boundary, evidence, and time.

### 7.3 Manifestation versus change

An intervention may reveal a pre-existing capability rather than increase it. Better access, reduced physical barriers, a clearer interface, or a less biased assessment can change observed performance without changing the underlying subject capability. Such enablement can be materially beneficial and should not be mislabeled to make it count.

Conversely, a subject may gain a capacity before available evaluations detect it. Absence of a score change is not proof of no change when the metric omits the relevant dimension. Scientific and Human Views must preserve competing explanations.

### 7.4 Claim states

| State | Meaning | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Observed performance difference | A measured or described output difference under stated conditions | Capability change, cause, amplification, or value |
| Candidate capability change | A hypothesis that the subject's capacity changed | Actual change or accepted attribution |
| Amplification attribution | A claim connecting subject, capability, baseline, intervention, variation, and time | Truth, governance acceptance, Authorization, or value |
| Amplification assessment | Evidence-based evaluation of that claim and alternatives | Actual amplification by assessment alone |
| Governance status | Proposed, accepted, restricted, challenged, rejected, or superseded use status | Underlying capability, ethical adequacy, or universal meaning |
| Amplification record | Representation of the claim, assessment, status, and lineage | Amplification itself |

## 8. Subject, level, boundary, and continuity

### 8.1 Stable-subject rule

An amplification claim ordinarily compares capability states of the same identified subject. If the subject boundary changes, the claim must instead state one of the following explicitly:

- the same subject persisted while its available resources or relations changed;
- a configured system was created and a new whole-level capability is proposed;
- a predecessor–successor relation permits qualified continuity without identity;
- a role, service, institution, or profession changed while its participants changed;
- or the evidence compares different subjects and supports only a cross-sectional difference.

Adding a tool to a Human does not automatically create a Human-tool Bearer. Removing the tool after failure does not automatically shrink the Bearer back to the Human. The boundary must be selected before outcome-based interpretation and held stable across favorable and unfavorable cases.

### 8.2 Candidate levels

| Level | Possible claim | Required caution |
|---|---|---|
| Component | A model, memory, interface, or mechanism gains a scoped capacity | Component gain does not transfer to system or user |
| Individual | A Human or other individual gains concurrent or retained capability | Assistance and manifestation must remain alternatives |
| Participant relation | A relation among participants enables a joint capacity | Relation is not automatically a separate Bearer |
| Team or collective | Organized interaction supports a whole-level capacity | Member gain and whole gain require separate Evidence |
| Institution | Procedures, roles, memory, and continuity support institutional capacity | Legal identity or records do not create Intelligence |
| Configured system | Stable organization of components and relations supports a system capacity | Product or architecture naming does not establish integration |
| Distributed arrangement | Capacity depends on organized relations across sites or actors | Boundary, control, dependency, and persistence may be difficult |
| Hybrid | Human and non-Human components contribute to one proposed organized capacity | Origin does not decide level, Authority, or Responsibility |
| Profession or service system | Aggregate practice capacity changes across institutions and generations | Population change, standards, access, inequality, and future formation matter |

### 8.3 No gain transfer

The following transfers are prohibited without separate evidence:

```text
component capability gain      -/-> configured-system amplification
system capability gain         -/-> participant capability gain
participant gain               -/-> team or institutional gain
organization productivity      -/-> every member's Intelligence
tool performance               -/-> user's retained capability
user performance               -/-> tool Intelligence
service output gain            -/-> professional amplification
successor capability           -/-> predecessor amplification
affected benefit               -/-> beneficiary Intelligence
market adoption                -/-> any capability predicate
```

### 8.4 Boundary-sensitivity analysis

A material claim should be re-tested under plausible alternative boundaries. If amplification appears only when successful resources are included and failures, external labor, verification, infrastructure, or maintenance are excluded, the attribution is unstable. Boundary sensitivity is evidence about uncertainty; it does not mean that the widest possible boundary is correct.

## 9. Integration, aggregation, coordination, and composition

### 9.1 Access and aggregation

Putting more content, models, experts, or tools in one environment creates access or aggregation. It may increase option value and performance, but it does not establish an integrated capacity. An unsearched archive, an unused expert, a disconnected model, or a duplicated function can enlarge inventory without changing capability.

### 9.2 Coordination

Coordination aligns timing, sequence, interface, allocation, or exchange. It may reduce friction and support capability. It can also impose rigid routing, erase dissent, or create bottlenecks. Coordination does not establish Cooperation, integration, Intelligence, or amplification automatically.

### 9.3 Candidate integration evidence

Stream B must test integration more fully. Stream A identifies at least these evidence families:

- organized relations that connect differentiated capabilities;
- information or state exchange that materially changes downstream selection or response;
- coordination across relevant variation rather than a fixed script alone;
- capability that is not exhausted by independent component outputs placed side by side;
- counterfactual degradation when a material relation is removed or disrupted;
- recovery, adaptation, or reconfiguration at the proposed subject level where claimed;
- stable boundary and identity across the attribution period;
- failure modes traceable to relations as well as components;
- comparative alternatives such as one stronger component, independent parallel work, or a simpler tool; and
- no hidden external subject performing the purported integrated work.

No single item is sufficient universally. Counterfactual degradation shows dependence and possible integration, but a passive power supply is indispensable without thereby becoming an Intelligence dimension.

### 9.4 New capability versus better composition

A composed arrangement may perform work no participant can perform alone. This can support a whole-level capability claim, but “new” must mean more than a novel output assembled from independent contributions. The analysis should determine whether the organization supports repeatable capacity across relevant variation and whether the proposed whole is the adequate explanatory level.

## 10. Dependency, assistance, and removal

### 10.1 Dependency is not disqualifying

All actual capabilities depend on substrates, environments, resources, histories, and relations. Dependence therefore does not defeat amplification. It qualifies the claim.

A material dependency record should state:

- dependency object and provider or source;
- whether it is internal, external, shared, replaceable, scarce, revocable, or controlled by another subject;
- availability, latency, reliability, maintenance, and update conditions;
- information, permission, contract, and Authority constraints;
- effects of degradation or removal;
- alternatives and switching costs;
- who can inspect, challenge, repair, or terminate it;
- whether retained capability survives its loss; and
- which affected subjects bear failure or transition costs.

### 10.2 Concurrent and retained gain

| Change type | Working boundary | Required disclosure |
|---|---|---|
| Concurrent gain | Increased effective capability while the support relation or configured arrangement is active | Dependency, access, control, failure, and boundary conditions |
| Retained gain | Capability remains materially increased after the identified support is removed | Duration, decay, transfer, practice, and alternative support |
| Configured-system gain | Increased capability is attributed to the organized whole | Whole boundary, integration, identity, components, and relations |
| Developmental gain | Capability changes through Formation, Learning, practice, feedback, or institutional evolution | Learner level, mechanism, history, persistence, and non-substitution |

These types may coexist. A professional may have concurrent gain while using a system and retained gain through Learning. The configured system may also have a separate whole-level capability. None should absorb the others.

### 10.3 Removal and degradation tests

Removal may show:

- that a resource was necessary under the tested conditions;
- that the claimed capability was relation-enabled;
- that a configured-system boundary was incomplete;
- that the subject retained or failed to retain capability;
- that a backup or alternative path exists; or
- that an external provider held material control.

Removal does not by itself show Intelligence, integration, amplification, beneficial value, or proper Responsibility allocation. It is one counterfactual instrument.

### 10.4 Dependency inversion

An intervention can initially increase capability and later reduce practical alternatives, retained skill, or institutional resilience. If the subject becomes unable to perform, evaluate, challenge, recover, or replace the support, the later state may include both amplification and dependency-related loss. The result cannot be reduced to one positive or negative scalar.

## 11. Time, persistence, update, substitution, and replacement

### 11.1 Temporal qualification

An amplification claim must state whether it concerns:

- an encounter or episode;
- repeated supported performance;
- a continuing relation;
- a persistent configured system;
- a retained individual, collective, or institutional capacity;
- a developmental trajectory;
- a version interval;
- or predecessor–successor capability.

The time needed to establish capability depends on the claim. One episode may evidence a narrow concurrent capacity, but it cannot silently support long-term retention, transfer, robustness, or institutional continuity.

### 11.2 Updates

An update may change:

- capability dimensions and their integration;
- the system boundary or version;
- resources, data, model, interface, policy, or dependency;
- failure and uncertainty patterns;
- provider/deployer/user control;
- affected population and consequences;
- baseline comparability; and
- Responsibility relations.

Therefore, an amplification attribution does not persist automatically across updates. Reassessment should be proportionate to material change.

### 11.3 Substitution and retained capability

Substitution analysis must preserve at least four capability objects:

1. capability to produce the immediate service or output;
2. capability of the displaced or assisted subject;
3. capability of the configured system or institution;
4. capability to inspect, challenge, recover, replace, and form successors.

Output continuity can coexist with loss in the other three. Calling this “amplification” without level qualification hides the actual transformation.

### 11.4 Replacement and succession

When components, participants, institutions, or systems are replaced, the analysis must distinguish:

- persistence of the same subject;
- succession by a different subject;
- continuity of a service or role;
- continuity of Knowledge assets and records;
- continuity of practical capability;
- continuity of dependency and control;
- continuity of Responsibility; and
- continuity of affected consequences.

No one continuity creates the others.

## 12. Multidimensional gain, loss, trade-off, and distribution

### 12.1 No conserved scalar

Intelligence and capability are not a single conserved quantity. Stream A therefore rejects the claim that amplification is necessarily a positive scalar delta.

A capability change may contain:

- gains in speed, breadth, recall, comparison, generation, coordination, or access;
- gains in robustness, error recognition, explanation, contextual sensitivity, challenge, or recovery;
- new integration among previously separate capacities;
- unchanged dimensions;
- losses in judgment, tacit skill, social interpretation, independence, practical refusal, or transfer;
- increased dependence, opacity, concentration, fragility, verification burden, or uncertainty; and
- different distributions across participants, institutions, providers, users, and affected subjects.

Whether the combined change qualifies as Amplified Intelligence belongs mainly to Stream B. Stream A requires that no material loss be hidden by one gain or aggregate score.

### 12.2 Target profile and values

A target capability profile identifies which dimensions and relations matter for a declared purpose. It cannot be value-neutral when it selects some gains and tolerates some losses. The target must therefore disclose:

- source and authority;
- Domain purpose and affected subjects;
- selected and omitted dimensions;
- thresholds and trade-off rules, if any;
- uncertainty and challenge;
- consequences of false positive and false negative classification; and
- relationship to later answer/value analysis.

Stream A does not select the target profile or weighting. It prevents those choices from being concealed inside the amplification predicate.

### 12.3 Distribution

“Who is amplified?” may have several different answers:

- the immediate user gains concurrent capacity;
- the team gains coordination capacity;
- the institution gains service volume;
- the configured system gains a new integrated capacity;
- the provider gains control and learning from aggregated use;
- future professionals lose formation opportunities;
- affected subjects experience changed risk or access; or
- no stable Intelligence Bearer gains despite output improvement.

Unlike gains cannot be netted silently. Distribution is part of the claim and its Responsibility interface.

### 12.4 Outcome symmetry

The proposed subject and boundary must remain stable across success and failure. A successful result cannot be attributed to the integrated system while a failure is attributed only to one component, user, or external factor without independent evidence. Conversely, a harmful outcome does not erase a genuine capability gain; it changes the value, Responsibility, and ethical assessment.

## 13. Causal and evidentiary attribution

### 13.1 Capability difference is not intervention effect

Observed change may arise from:

- the declared intervention;
- selection of stronger participants or cases;
- prior training or experience;
- additional time, information, or supervision;
- environmental change;
- measurement change or leakage;
- incentive and behavior adaptation;
- regression, chance, or publication bias;
- hidden external labor or services;
- organizational restructuring; or
- combinations and interactions among these.

An amplification attribution therefore requires a stated causal model proportionate to consequence. It need not establish one philosophically final cause, but it must preserve alternatives and uncertainty.

### 13.2 Evidence families

Potential evidence includes:

- matched before/after or controlled comparisons;
- longitudinal observation;
- varied and novel cases;
- mechanism and process evidence;
- component, relation, or resource removal and degradation;
- boundary-sensitivity analysis;
- counterfactual alternatives;
- failure-mode and recovery analysis;
- transfer and retention tests;
- independent replication or assessment;
- qualitative professional judgment with declared criteria and dissent;
- affected-subject and operational consequence evidence; and
- cross-view reconciliation.

No universal hierarchy is accepted here. Domain science and project grounding determine appropriate methods under DR-009/DR-012 constraints.

### 13.3 Necessary, sufficient, and contributing conditions

The analysis must distinguish:

- **necessary for the tested configuration:** removal defeats the capability under stated conditions;
- **sufficient under the tested conditions:** the intervention reliably produces the claim within Scope;
- **contributing:** the intervention makes a relevant difference but is neither necessary nor sufficient alone;
- **enabling:** it makes the activity possible without proving increase;
- **constitutive:** it belongs inside the accepted subject or capability organization;
- **evidentiary:** it helps assess the claim without producing it; and
- **representational:** it records or communicates the claim.

A benchmark, profile, audit, explanation, or accepted system card is evidentiary or representational; it is not constitutive by being recorded.

## 15. Candidate approaches considered

### A — Amplification as any improved output or performance

**Rejected as the governing model.** It turns episodic luck, leakage, external completion, or metric gaming into capability and collapses 2.1.7 into 2.1.8.

### B — Amplification as more Knowledge, content, access, or retrieval

**Rejected.** These may support inquiry or capability, but DR-011/DR-012 prohibit content, Representation, and epistemic-status substitution.

### C — Amplification as augmentation or tool use

**Rejected.** Addition identifies a changed resource arrangement, not necessarily a changed effective capability or amplified subject.

### D — Amplification as automation, productivity, or labor reduction

**Rejected.** Automation and output efficiency may coexist with substitution, deskilling, fragility, externalized cost, or unchanged reasoning capacity.

### E — Amplification only when the unaided original subject retains the gain

**Rejected as too narrow.** It erases legitimate concurrent, relational, and configured-system capabilities. Retention remains an important qualification, not the universal definition.

### F — Amplification belongs automatically to the widest successful Human–tool–institution system

**Rejected.** It invites boundary shopping, property transfer, and Responsibility laundering. Whole-level attribution requires integration and stable-boundary evidence.

### G — Amplification as a weighted scalar profile increase

**Rejected as the universal model.** Weighting imports purpose and value; aggregation can hide losses, dependence, and distribution. Scores may remain Domain Projections.

### H — Comparative capability relation with declared subject, baseline, variation, integration, trade-offs, and dependency

**Retained as the Stream A working direction.** It preserves relation-enabled capability without confusing assistance, output, score, automation, value, or Representation with the predicate. Stream B must test its Bearer and multidimensional implications before any disposition.

## 17. Human, Scientific, and Engineering View obligations

### 17.1 Human View

The Human View must make understandable:

- who or what is claimed to gain which capability;
- the baseline and why the comparison is fair;
- how assistance, augmentation, automation, substitution, and replacement differ;
- whether the gain is concurrent, retained, system-level, or developmental;
- which gains, losses, dependencies, and practical alternatives matter;
- who benefits, who loses capability or opportunity, and who is affected;
- what remains uncertain or disputed;
- why a score or product label is insufficient; and
- how the claim can be challenged, restricted, corrected, or withdrawn.

### 17.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View must preserve:

- construct identity and competing definitions;
- subject and comparison design;
- Domain, Scope, Context, and sampling;
- baseline and comparator validity;
- capability dimensions and measurement limits;
- relevant variation, transfer, robustness, failure, and novelty;
- causal model and alternative explanations;
- resources, dependencies, interventions, and confounds;
- gains, losses, uncertainty, distribution, and effect heterogeneity;
- replication, counterevidence, conditions of defeat, and model revision; and
- disagreement about whether observed performance supports capability or amplification.

### 17.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View must preserve:

- subject, component, relation, and system boundaries;
- versions, configurations, dependencies, providers, data, models, interfaces, and infrastructure;
- capability claim, profile, baseline, comparator, test conditions, and status;
- assistance, automation, substitution, replacement, update, and failure pathways;
- integration and counterfactual degradation evidence;
- operational availability, latency, maintenance, rollback, recovery, and switching conditions;
- retained capability, supervision, verification, challenge, and escalation interfaces;
- traceability from evidence to assessment and use status;
- affected-subject and Responsibility interfaces; and
- transformation, omission, approximation, uncertainty, and Projection loss.

### 17.4 Alignment rule

The three views must refer to the same predicate version, subject boundary, capability object, Domain, Scope, Context, baseline, intervention, period, dependencies, gains, losses, and status where material. A mismatch may be preserved as dissent, but it may not be hidden by a shared identifier.
