# SymK 2.1.3 — Knowledge and Intelligence as a Reciprocal Core

**Status:** Planned — explicitly not started at the 2.1.2 pause checkpoint
**Decision identifier reserved for next proposal:** `SYMK-2X-DR-011`
**Depends on:** Stage-Accepted 2.1.1–2.1.2
**Opening condition:** Review the M0 pause checkpoint and any Observed maturation notes before substantive analysis begins

## Central question

> What minimum concepts of Knowledge and Intelligence does SymK require, and how can their reciprocal relationship be expressed without making either concept a hidden definition of the other?

## 2.1.3.1 — Knowledge

Examine Knowledge, knowledge claim, knowledge asset, Representation, grounds, epistemic status, truth, belief, reliance, information, data, understanding, familiarity, and practical Knowledge.

Required tests include:

- whether SymK requires factivity or a more differentiated operational vocabulary;
- who or what may bear Knowledge;
- how responsible reliance differs from truth and authorization;
- how scope and Context qualify application without making truth arbitrary;
- what a durable asset preserves and what it cannot preserve.

## 2.1.3.2 — Intelligence

Examine Intelligence, substrate, organized system, bearer, capability, competence, skill, performance, attribution, adaptation, agency, autonomy, rationality, understanding, consciousness, and moral status.

Required tests include:

- minimum conditions for Intelligence attribution;
- capacity versus manifested performance;
- bearer boundaries for individual, collective, institutional, artificial, distributed, and hybrid systems;
- substrate independence without substrate indifference;
- attribution versus authority and moral standing.

## 2.1.3.3 — Reciprocal consistency

Determine how Intelligence may pursue, develop, challenge, apply, and revise Knowledge, and how Knowledge participates in the formation and manifestation of Intelligence. Declare legitimate reciprocal dependence and reject merely circular formulations.

## Deliverables

- minimal working definitions and governed distinctions;
- bearer, scope, and attribution model;
- reciprocal-dependency account;
- examples, counterexamples, and attribution failures;
- initial treatment of DQ-001, DQ-002, and DQ-012;
- Foundation Paper revision-ledger entries for Knowledge and Intelligence;
- consequences for the candidate axioms and DIA concept.

## Out of scope

Complete Epistemology, philosophy of mind, consciousness theory, moral-status theory, psychometrics, or final formal representation.
