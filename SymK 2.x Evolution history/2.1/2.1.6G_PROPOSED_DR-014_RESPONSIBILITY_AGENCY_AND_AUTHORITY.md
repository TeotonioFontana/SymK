## 1. Decision question

> What minimum concept of Responsibility must SymK govern; how must responsibility remain distinct from agency, action, causation, control, role, authority, authorization, decision, duty, answerability, accountability, blame, liability, remedy, allocation, consent, standing, and record; and how can Human, artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, and hybrid relations be represented without predicate copying, responsibility laundering, an automatic residual bearer, or a complete imported moral or legal theory?

## 2. Proposed decision

> **SymK shall govern Responsibility as a Foundational concept. Responsibility is a sourced, scoped, phased, and challengeable relationship family in which an identified subject at a declared level stands, under a stated governing source, frame, jurisdiction, and standard, in a prospective or retrospective relation to an identified object. A material Responsibility claim preserves the relevant factual and governance links, expected or available response, Evidence, uncertainty, status, lifecycle, affected subjects, and challenge, discharge, breach, reassessment, correction, repair, remedy, and unresolved-gap interfaces.**
>
> **Responsibility is not a source-free property, personality trait, conserved substance, percentage, role title, approval state, legal status, moral judgment, workflow field, or record. It does not arise from causal Contribution, agency, capability, Intelligence, control, Power, role, authority, authorization, legal personality, compliance, contract, consent record, Human presence, artificial operation, output, audit, explanation, payment, or outcome alone. An asserted assignment or allocation must therefore remain distinguishable from a relation that actually holds under an applicable source and from later attribution, assessment, acceptance, performance, discharge, breach, enforcement, response, and record states.**
>
> **Prospective Responsibility concerns what a subject is entrusted, expected, or required to attend to before or while conduct and consequences remain governable. Retrospective Responsibility concerns an assessed relation to past conditions, conduct, decisions, omissions, risks, artifacts, processes, or consequences under stated criteria. Duty and obligation are sourced normative relationship families; answerability is a relation to provide an account to an identified claimant or forum; accountability is an arrangement through which conduct or conditions can be called, examined, challenged, assessed, and connected to response. Responsibility, duty, obligation, answerability, accountability, blame, culpability, sanction, punishment, liability, correction, repair, remedy, and record remain distinct even when they overlap.**
>
> **Agency shall be governed as a supporting capability concept and Agent as a scoped attribution role. Agency is a scoped capability of an organized subject to govern conduct through integrated selection and control processes that respond systematically to relevant variation, such that materially different conduct remains attributable to the subject under materially different conditions. An action is an event or process attributable, within a declared target and Scope, to an exercise of agency. Causal Contribution is a model-relative difference made to an event, condition, action, omission, or consequence. Control is a scoped relation involving effective pathways to select, initiate, continue, modify, inhibit, terminate, or materially constrain a target. None of these facts establishes moral agency, legal personality, authority, or Responsibility by itself.**
>
> **Role, Authority, Authorization, Decision, Delegation, and Supervision shall be governed as supporting governance relationship, status, occurrence, and lifecycle families. Authority is sourced, jurisdiction-bound standing to perform specified governance acts within declared Domain, Context, Scope, time, and conditions. Authorization is scoped permission or mandate under an applicable authority. Decision occurrence, decision authority, validity, adoption, approval, authorization, execution, effect, and record remain separate. Delegation states what is conveyed and retained; it does not automatically transfer every duty, accountability, liability, or Responsibility. Supervision is effective only to the extent that monitoring, understanding, review, intervention, escalation, and response capacity are evidenced rather than inferred from title, policy, interface, or Human presence.**
>
> **Responsibility allocation shall be governed as an object-first, sourced, scoped, typed, multi-subject, multi-level, temporal, challengeable, and non-additive mapping distinct from the underlying relations and its Representation. Distinct, sequential, parallel, nested, shared, joint, collective, institutional, distributed, hybrid, retained, transferred, inherited, disputed, and unresolved relations may coexist. Whole-level relations do not copy to members or components; member or component relations do not aggregate automatically to a whole. One subject's Responsibility does not automatically reduce another's. No Human, artificial component, provider, deployer, user, institution, legal entity, or final approver is the universal residual bearer or responsibility sink.**
>
> **A responsibility gap is object-specific and is not established by complexity, distribution, opacity, automation, missing blame, or absence of one all-controlling subject. A material gap exists only relative to a stated governing need when no valid, capable, reachable, and sufficiently empowered subject or forum can perform, answer, assess, correct, remedy, or route the required relation. Gaps may be semantic, evidentiary, attributional, allocative, authoritative, capability-based, enforcement-related, remedial, temporal, or conflict-based, and may be genuine, manufactured, representational, or governance failures.**
>
> **SymK-compatible Responsibility governance shall preserve a bounded seven-commitment ethical minimum: affected-subject non-erasure; non-privilege and non-substitution; consequence-sensitive due regard; no fabricated consent; effective protected challenge and accountable response; prevention-to-repair continuity; and visible jurisdiction and unresolved conflict. This minimum qualifies SymK compatibility; it does not determine whether every source-relative Responsibility relation exists and does not select a complete theory of good, justice, autonomy, dignity, equality, welfare, rights, personhood, blame, liability, punishment, risk, legitimate authority, or remedy.**
>
> **Affected-party standing is a supporting eligibility relation arising from material effect or exposure rather than operational role alone. It concerns representation, Evidence, challenge, capable assessment, reasoned response, and access to applicable correction, review, appeal, repair, or remedy routes. It does not automatically grant Participation, decision authority, veto, legal standing, moral status, Responsibility, or a particular remedy. Consent is a scoped and temporal relationship distinct from notice, receipt, understanding, voluntariness, practical refusal, withdrawal, authorization, compliance, contract, acceptance event, and record. This decision does not require consent for every action or establish legal consent validity.**
>
> **Every material Responsibility claim shall preserve, proportionately to consequence, its object; relation kind and phase; subject and explanatory level; source, frame, jurisdiction, standard, Domain, Context, Scope, time, and lifecycle; relevant factual, causal, agency, control, role, authority, authorization, decision, delegation, and supervision links; allocation form and overlapping relations; affected subjects, Power, vulnerability, consent claims, standing, challenge, accountability, response, prevention, correction, repair, remedy, residual harm, gaps, external authority, and unresolved conflict; actual, attributed, assessed, accepted, performed, discharged, breached, enforced, responded, and recorded states; Evidence, counterevidence, uncertainty, dissent, confidence, defeat, reopening, Provenance, history, succession, and Projection loss; and Human, Scientific, and Engineering View alignment with declared material loss.**
>
> **SymK owns this universal semantic and compatibility minimum. Constitutional work owns its normative force. Domains, projects, organizations, professions, legal systems, moral and political authorities, claimants, and forums own applicable allocations, duties, liability, blame, standing, consent validity, thresholds, procedures, sanctions, and remedies within jurisdiction. Engineering artifacts may represent or project the system but do not own its meaning.**

No clause has authority until the project steward explicitly Stage-Accepts this proposal.

## 5. Six-plane Responsibility architecture

| Plane | Governed content | Prohibited substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Factual | Conditions, events, actions, omissions, behavior, execution, causal models, agency, control, information, capability, consequence | Facts do not create normative force by themselves |
| Governance | Roles, Authority, Authorization, decisions, delegation, supervision, source, jurisdiction, procedure, validity, status | Governance standing does not create warrant, legitimacy, competence, effect, or complete Responsibility |
| Responsibility | Subject, object, source/frame, standard, prospective/retrospective phase, duty, answerability, accountability, assessment, response | Responsibility does not collapse into one adjacent relation or scalar status |
| Allocation and level | Subjects, levels, allocation forms, transfer/retention, whole/member, component/system, predecessor/successor, gaps | No property copying, universal residual bearer, fixed pie, or boundary shopping |
| Ethical and affected-subject | Affected subjects, Power, vulnerability, consent claims, standing, challenge, response, prevention, repair, remedy, residual harm | Compliance, consent record, explanation, audit, payment, or favorable outcome does not establish adequacy |
| Representation and status | Claims, Evidence, attribution, assessment, acceptance, performance, discharge, breach, enforcement, records, views, Projections | A field, matrix, workflow, contract, log, or judgment does not become the complete relation |

The planes are analytically non-substitutable but interdependent. A claim may require links across all six without implying that one subject occupies every link.

## 6. Material Responsibility claim envelope

A material claim shall preserve, proportionately to consequence:

1. claim identifier, asserting subject, authority, purpose, and version;
2. exact Responsibility object or objects;
3. relation kind and phase;
4. proposed subject or bearer and exact explanatory level;
5. source, governing frame, jurisdiction, standard, and authority;
6. Domain, Context, Scope, population, stakes, time, and lifecycle phase;
7. relevant condition, event, action, omission, decision, process, or consequence;
8. causal model, causal Contributions, uncertainty, and alternative explanations;
9. agency, action, control, information, Knowledge, intention, foreseeability, and alternatives where material;
10. role, Authority, Authorization, decision, delegation, supervision, and retained relations;
11. allocation form and every overlapping or disputed relation;
12. whole/member, component/system, legal/operational, representative/represented, predecessor/successor, and Human/artificial boundaries;
13. capability, resources, access, control, Authority, independence, time, and practical alternatives for performance;
14. affected subjects, interests, beneficiaries, burdened parties, claimants, and representation;
15. Power, dependence, vulnerability, distribution, severity, reversibility, and residual effect;
16. consent requirement or claim and its notice, understanding, voluntariness, practical-refusal, Scope, duration, and withdrawal conditions;
17. answerability, forum, accountability, assessment, challenge, protection, response, review, and appeal;
18. prevention, monitoring, containment, cessation, correction, repair, remedy, recurrence, and Learning;
19. external authorities, duty conflicts, emergency conditions, and unresolved incompatibilities;
20. gap type, cause, affected object, missing capability or Authority, and genuine/manufactured/representational/governance assessment;
21. current actual-relation, attribution, assessment, acceptance, performance, discharge, breach, enforcement, and response states;

23. Evidence, counterevidence, uncertainty, dissent, confidence, defeat, and reopening conditions; and
24. Provenance, record, history, succession, supersession, correction, and Projection loss.

This is a semantic preservation envelope, not a mandatory universal schema. Consequence-proportional profiles may compact low-consequence claims but may not hide material dimensions.

## 7. Core responsibility and divergence rules

1. A Responsibility claim must identify the object; “responsible” without “for what” is materially incomplete.
2. A claimed actual relation must identify its source and applicability; a source assertion alone may remain an assignment or attribution rather than an established relation.
3. Source-relative existence, external validity, epistemic warrant, practical effectiveness, legitimacy, ethical compatibility, and legal force remain separate assessments.
4. Prospective and retrospective Responsibility may coexist but neither entails the other universally.
5. Agency, causal Contribution, control, role, Authority, Authorization, answerability, liability, remedy, and record may belong to different subjects and levels.
6. Divergence is represented through typed links, not repaired by inventing one subject possessing every predicate.
7. No subject type receives privilege, immunity, or residual assignment solely from being Human, artificial, collective, institutional, configured, hybrid, or legally recognized.
8. An incapable bearer may still be subject to a sourced duty while the allocation or governance arrangement is defective; capability and validity remain separate.
9. Responsibility may exist where blame does not; blame or liability may exist under one framework without identical Responsibility under another.
10. Answerability and accountability must remain connected to capable assessment and response without becoming necessary existence conditions for every source-relative Responsibility relation.
11. Historical and current relations remain separately representable after outcome, reassessment, update, delegation, replacement, merger, dissolution, or succession.
12. A favorable outcome does not validate an irresponsible process; a harmful outcome does not establish universal fault.

## 8. Agency, action, causation, and control rules

1. Agency attribution requires a declared subject, conduct target, Scope, relevant variation, integrated selection/control basis, Evidence, uncertainty, and competing explanations.
2. Output and behavior may evidence agency but are insufficient alone.
3. Action requires attribution to an exercise of agency; execution may occur without such attribution.
4. Omission requires a declared non-occurrence, relevant alternative, opportunity, capacity, expectation or source, time, and causal/evaluative model.
5. Causal Contribution remains model-relative and may be necessary, sufficient, enabling, preventing, probabilistic, structural, or otherwise qualified without becoming Responsibility.
6. Control claims identify target, mechanism, information, alternatives, access, capacity, exercise, timing, constraints, dependency, and effect.
7. Nominal control, intervention capacity, actual exercise, and achieved effect remain distinct.
8. Component agency does not copy to the whole; whole-level agency does not copy to components.
9. Operational agency does not establish moral agency, consciousness, sentience, personhood, rights, or legal personality.
10. Human approval does not prove meaningful review or absorb other subjects' factual and governance relations.

## 9. Role, Authority, Authorization, decision, delegation, and supervision rules

1. Role definition, assignment, occupancy, capability, performance, Authority, Authorization, Responsibility, assessment, and record remain separate.
2. Authority claims identify source, authority kind, jurisdiction, subject, object, Domain, Context, Scope, time, conditions, procedure, challenge, and lifecycle.
3. Authority validity does not prove epistemic warrant, practical effectiveness, legitimacy beyond the source, responsible exercise, or favorable consequence.
4. Expertise and capability do not grant Authority; Authority does not create expertise or capability.
5. Authorization identifies applicable Authority, subject, act/resource/role/reliance object, Scope, time, conditions, revocation, review, and record.
6. Authorization does not constitute consent, occurrence, ethical justification, or Responsibility discharge.
7. Decision occurrence, valid decision status, adoption, approval, Authorization, execution, effect, and record remain separate.
8. Delegation cannot convey more than the delegator may convey and states every transferred and retained relation explicitly.
9. Delegation, outsourcing, procurement, contract, automation, or workflow reassignment does not transfer every duty, accountability, liability, remedy, or Responsibility automatically.
10. Supervision and oversight claims distinguish nominal role from information, competence, independence, time, intervention, escalation, protection, and response capacity.
11. Ex post ratification may change later governance status without rewriting the earlier act or its historical consequences.
12. Conflicting Authorities and duties remain visible with source, jurisdiction, priority claim, routing, decision, dissent, residual conflict, review, and response.

## 10. Allocation, boundary, transfer, succession, and gap rules

1. Allocation begins with a Responsibility object and relation kind, not a preselected role or nearest subject.
2. Every allocated relation identifies subject, level, source, Scope, phase, time, basis, capability, Authority, control, Evidence, challenge, response, and status.
3. Several subjects may hold full, partial, overlapping, distinct, shared, joint, sequential, parallel, or nested relations concerning one object without an assumed conserved total.
4. A percentage is meaningful only under a declared additive model and is never a universal Responsibility quantity.
5. Whole-level and member/component-level relations require their own sources and Evidence in both directions.
6. System boundaries must remain outcome-symmetric: they may not expand to claim benefit and contract to deny harm, or the reverse.
7. Provider, deployer, user, approver, institution, component, or legal-entity status is never a universal allocation rule.
8. Delegation, outsourcing, procurement, contract, update, maintenance, replacement, merger, dissolution, and succession preserve historical and retained relations unless a valid source changes them explicitly.
9. Successor relations do not copy predecessor blame automatically; continuity, received assets, control, notice, capability, Authority, duties, and response must be assessed separately.
10. Complexity, opacity, distribution, automation, missing whole understanding, or absence of one blameworthy person does not establish a gap.
11. A gap diagnosis states object, required relation/function, source, missing subject/forum/capability/Authority, cause, affected subjects, interim protection, response route, and status.
12. Manufactured and representational gaps remain distinguishable from genuine absence and may themselves create governance or ethical concerns.

## 11. Minimum ethical compatibility rules

The seven proposed commitments are:

1. **Affected-subject non-erasure:** material effect remains visible despite absent contract, account, role, Participation, direct cause, legal identity, or recognized voice.
2. **Non-privilege and non-substitution:** subject type, capability, Authority, compliance, contract, consent record, approval, automation, audit, outcome, or status is insufficient by itself for ethical adequacy or absolution.
3. **Consequence-sensitive due regard:** Power, dependence, vulnerability, alternatives, foreseeability, uncertainty, severity, reversibility, distribution, and residual effects receive rigor proportionate to possible consequence.
4. **No fabricated consent:** notice, access, continued use, silence, compliance, signature, click, contract, or recorded acceptance is insufficient by itself for understanding, voluntariness, practical refusal, Scope, continuing agreement, or valid representation.
5. **Effective challenge and accountable response:** material claims, decisions, allocations, and consequences require a practical protected path able to receive Evidence, reach a capable forum, produce a reasoned response, and make response possible.
6. **Prevention-to-repair continuity:** Responsibility does not end at explanation, blame, punishment, payment, audit, or record; prevention, containment, cessation, correction, repair, remedy, residual harm, recurrence, review, and revision remain connected where applicable.
7. **Visible jurisdiction and unresolved conflict:** external Authority remains explicit; competing duties, absent Authority, incapable forums, unremediable effects, and unresolved incompatibilities are not converted into fictional discharge.

## 12. Claim, status, assessment, record, and lifecycle rules

1. Actual relation, asserted claim, attribution, assessment, governance status, and record are distinct.

8. Confidence, uncertainty, counterevidence, dissent, conditions of defeat, and reopening remain attached to material claims.

## 15. Alternatives considered

### A — Responsibility as causal Contribution

**Rejected:** it converts model-relative factual connection into normative relation, loses prospective duties and institutional or remedial relations, and makes causal complexity look like ethical allocation.

### B — Responsibility as agency or control

**Rejected:** agents and controllers may lack the applicable relation, while institutions, legal subjects, role bearers, successors, or remedial subjects may bear sourced Responsibility without identical agency or control.

### C — Responsibility as answerability

**Rejected as exhaustive:** account-giving is indispensable in many cases but does not exhaust prospective attention, performance, correction, repair, remedy, or source-relative relations.

### D — Responsibility as accountability

**Rejected as exhaustive:** accountability is an examination-and-response arrangement that can fail while Responsibility remains or can exist procedurally without creating valid Responsibility.

### E — Responsibility as legal liability

**Rejected:** legal liability is jurisdiction-specific and may exist without fault or operational control; Responsibility also includes non-legal prospective, institutional, epistemic, professional, organizational, and remedial relations.

### F — Responsibility belongs to the final approver

**Rejected:** approval may be ceremonial, uninformed, incapable, unauthorized, too late, or narrow in Scope and cannot absorb provider, designer, deployer, operator, institutional, causal, supervisory, or affected-party relations.

### G — Responsibility belongs to the most capable or controlling subject

**Rejected:** capability and control are object- and time-specific factual relations, not complete sources of duty, Authority, liability, answerability, or remedy.

### H — Responsibility is a conserved allocation pie

**Rejected:** typed relations can overlap, remain full, be noncomparable, and arise under different sources. Percentages require a declared model rather than a universal total.

### I — Distribution creates shared or equal Responsibility

**Rejected:** distribution states spread across subjects or levels; shared, joint, equal, several, or additive force requires separate source and Evidence.

### J — Complexity creates a responsibility gap

**Rejected:** complexity may increase evidentiary difficulty but does not establish absence of valid, capable, reachable, or empowered subjects or forums.

### K — One universal Human or institution is the residual bearer

**Rejected:** it creates type privilege, ceremonial Human sinks, provider or institution absolution, and false certainty. Residual or retained relations must be sourced and object-specific.

### L — Ethical adequacy is legal compliance

**Rejected:** law is jurisdictional and authoritative within Scope but may not exhaust affected-subject, consent, challenge, uncertainty, repair, or unresolved-conflict concerns.

### M — Consent is the complete ethical criterion

**Rejected:** consent may be inapplicable, absent, invalid, dependent, incomplete, or insufficient and cannot waive every duty, affected interest, correction, or remedy.

### N — Transparency and explanation are accountability

**Rejected:** disclosed information or explanation may not reach a capable forum, permit challenge, change a decision, correct a condition, repair harm, or provide remedy.

### O — Ethical compatibility determines whether Responsibility exists

**Rejected:** an unethical or ineffective arrangement may still contain source-relative Responsibility relations. Compatibility is separately assessed so defects remain nameable rather than erasing Responsibility.

### P — Pure Domain pluralism

**Rejected:** total delegation would let projects, contracts, legal statuses, implementations, or local vocabularies erase affected subjects, fabricate consent, suppress challenge, launder gaps, or redefine Responsibility silently.

### Q — Sourced relational Responsibility plus bounded ethical qualification

**Retained:** this proposal preserves divergence, plural Authority, distribution, response, affected subjects, and non-empty SymK compatibility without selecting a complete moral or legal theory.
