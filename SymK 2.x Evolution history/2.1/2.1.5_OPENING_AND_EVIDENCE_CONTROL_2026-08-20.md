## 5. Inherited constraints — not questions reopened by default

2.1.5 inherits these Stage-Accepted constraints:

1. Cooperation is a process in the SymK value chain, not the final objective or an unconstrained good.
2. The principal value remains better answers to complex questions, produced faster and at lower total cost only under epistemic accountability, responsibility, learning, and consequence constraints.
3. Knowledge is a plural epistemic achievement and governed medium; it is not a transferable substance, shared artifact, consensus status, or common belief.
4. Intelligence is a multidimensional capacity of an organized Bearer; Intelligence does not entail wisdom, cooperative disposition, authority, responsibility, moral standing, or correct action.
5. Actual bearing, attribution, assessment, governance status, Reliance Authorization, role, authority, and record remain distinct.
6. A participant, collective, institution, artificial system, or hybrid arrangement cannot be declared cooperative, educated, or trained solely through naming, membership, output, compliance, score, credential, or organizational adoption.
7. The epistemic lifecycle is recursive; application, consequence, challenge, correction, revision, preservation, and decay can reopen earlier assessments.
8. Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views must remain aligned where material. Human readability, formal expression, and machine inspectability are capabilities, not participant classes or exhaustive views.
9. Derived projects own authorized Domain concepts, practices, professional standards, formative methods, thresholds, and operational Projections within the universal SymK minimum.
10. Detailed responsibility, agency, authority, accountability, and liability remain assigned to 2.1.6; 2.1.5 may expose their interfaces without pre-deciding them.

## 6. Required distinctions

The analysis must keep at least these distinctions explicit:

- Cooperation from coordination, communication, aggregation, compliance, consensus, competition, coercion, collusion, alliance, exchange, and coexistence;
- shared undertaking from shared objective, common belief, equal benefit, identical role, agreement, harmony, or fusion;
- participant from actor, Bearer, represented subject, source, authority, beneficiary, affected party, and responsible party;
- cooperation attribution from cooperation claim, assessment, status, authorization, record, and successful outcome;
- individual contribution from collective pattern and system-level relation;
- productive difference from assimilation, fragmentation, obstruction, and domination;
- conflict from harm, coercion, competition, disagreement, and non-cooperation;
- power asymmetry from justified role differentiation and from domination;
- learning from instruction, Training, Education, socialization, adaptation, conditioning, and mere change;
- Education from information transfer, Knowledge acquisition, schooling, certification, alignment, compliance, and indoctrination;
- Training from repetition alone, model training, optimization, fine-tuning, reinforcement, rehearsal, and Education;
- habit from rule, disposition, performance, routine, policy, and attributed status;
- formation from formative intervention, formative environment, intended curriculum, hidden curriculum, and observed developmental effect;
- integration from assimilation;
- correction from punishment, suppression, erasure, and repair;
- development from adaptation, improvement, drift, decay, and irreversible conditioning;
- cooperative-system learning from individual learning and from a stored institutional record;
- persistence from one-time interaction, repeated contact, institutional continuity, and Bearer continuity;
- challengeability from permission to speak, practical ability to challenge, institutional response, and actual revision;
- and SymK's universal minimum from Domain pedagogy, culture, policy, professional formation, and operational governance.

## 7. Principal analytical hazards

- **Objective substitution:** allowing cooperation to replace better answers and responsible amplification as the governing value.
- **Coordination substitution:** treating synchronized action or compatible interfaces as cooperation.
- **Consensus substitution:** treating agreement, majority, confidence, or common representation as cooperation or truth.
- **Compliance substitution:** treating obedience or policy conformance as cooperative formation.
- **Collusion sanitization:** calling coordinated exploitation or exclusion cooperation because participants benefit.
- **Coercion concealment:** ignoring compulsion, inability to refuse, dependency, sanctions, or power asymmetry.
- **Outcome substitution:** inferring cooperation from one successful product, answer, or performance.
- **Intelligence entailment:** assuming capable participants are cooperative or educable in the same way.
- **Bearer instability:** attributing learning or formation to a collective or hybrid while changing the system boundary opportunistically.
- **Artifact reification:** treating a curriculum, policy, memory store, model weights, or training record as Education or formation itself.
- **Training–Education collapse:** treating repetition, optimization, fine-tuning, instruction, or successful performance as Education.
- **Formation–indoctrination collapse:** suppressing dissent or difference in the name of integration.
- **Hidden curriculum neglect:** treating interfaces, incentives, defaults, metrics, examples, sanctions, and tolerated violations as neutral.
- **Anthropomorphic transfer:** applying human developmental vocabulary to artificial or collective Bearers without evidence and qualification.
- **Responsibility preemption:** deciding agency, authority, duty, accountability, or liability before 2.1.6.
- **Axiom pre-acceptance:** treating A0 or A3 as governing because they are already drafted.
- **Domain leakage:** universalizing a cultural, pedagogical, organizational, legal, clinical, or technical practice.
- **Permanent-status error:** treating cooperation, Education, competence, or habit as irreversible once assessed.

## 9. Governed analysis sequence

### A. Cooperation and adjacent-relation boundary

- establish why SymK needs a Cooperation concept and what work it performs in the value chain;
- compare cooperation with coordination, communication, aggregation, compliance, consensus, competition, coercion, collusion, and coexistence;
- test whether shared objective, mutual benefit, consent, contribution, interdependence, challenge, and consequence are necessary, contingent, or disqualifying conditions;
- propose an initial minimum without assigning final disposition.

### B. Participant, contribution, and relational-bearer model

- distinguish participant, contributor, beneficiary, affected party, authority, actor, and Bearer;
- test individual, artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, and hybrid arrangements;
- determine how cooperation is attributed without reifying a group or inferring it from membership, output, or status;
- preserve multidimensional capability, dependency, asymmetry, and changing boundaries.

### C. Formation concept system

- compare learning, instruction, Training, Education, socialization, adaptation, conditioning, habit, reflection, correction, repair, and decay;
- test the reciprocal but non-identical relation between Education and Training;
- distinguish formative activity, environment, process, effect, achievement, attribution, and record;
- test propositional, practical, familiarity, relational, and institutional formation without artifact reification.

### D. Power, conflict, dissent, coercion, and challenge

- determine when conflict, competition, asymmetry, or disagreement remain compatible with cooperation;
- expose domination, collusion, coercive integration, suppressed dissent, hidden curricula, and inability to refuse;
- distinguish challenge permission, challenge capacity, response, correction, and repair;
- identify but do not pre-decide 2.1.6 responsibility and authority questions.

### E. Persistence, reciprocal formation, and system learning

- test brief interaction against persistent relation and institution;
- examine how interfaces, incentives, defaults, metrics, memory, examples, sanctions, and operational feedback form participants;
- test artificial, collective, institutional, and hybrid learner attributions under stable Bearer criteria;
- analyze developmental gain, drift, decay, repair, supersession, and loss without permanent status.

### F. Consolidated stress test and consequences

- apply all mandatory counterexamples and cross-view tests;
- test Product Vision, DR-009 through DR-012, candidate A0–A4 and J1, DQ-003, DQ-004, DQ-019, Foundation Paper impacts, derived-project ownership, and 2.1.6–2.1.9 dependencies;
- record alternatives, rejected interpretations, non-effects, dissent, confidence, and reopening conditions;
- determine whether the result is sufficient to prepare Proposed DR-013.

### G. DR-013 proposal checkpoint

Only after Streams A–F pass may a complete Proposed `SYMK-2X-DR-013` be entered in the Decision Register and presented to the project steward. No intermediate analytical note has decision authority.

## 10. Mandatory counterexample set

At minimum, the analysis must explain:

1. efficient coordination used to dominate or exclude a third party;
2. colluding participants who share an objective and benefit mutually;
3. policy compliance produced by credible threat and no practical right to refuse;
4. productive disagreement that improves a shared undertaking;
5. material conflict that remains compatible with continued cooperation;
6. consensus produced by suppressed dissent or asymmetric agenda control;
7. a one-time exchange that succeeds without a persistent relationship;
8. repeated interaction that creates dependency but no cooperation;
9. competition deliberately structured to contribute to a wider cooperative process;
10. participants who contribute unequally or receive unequal benefits;
11. a successful answer produced through a non-cooperative process;
12. a cooperative process that produces a false, harmful, or valueless answer;
13. a highly Intelligent participant that is deceptive, uncorrectable, or unsafe for cooperation;
14. a training program that produces reliable compliance but destroys challenge capacity;
15. reflective Education whose principles disappear under pressure because no habits were formed;
16. repetition that stabilizes a harmful habit;
17. machine training or fine-tuning that changes outputs without supporting an Education attribution;
18. a fixed model inside a Human–AI arrangement whose cooperative system improves through changed roles and practices;
19. an institution whose members change while memory, incentives, and routines preserve or degrade a collective pattern;
20. a curriculum that states one norm while interfaces and sanctions train the opposite behavior;
21. integration that requires erasure of language, identity, perspective, or legitimate dissent;
22. a participant formally permitted to challenge but practically unable to do so;
23. a once-effective cooperative habit that decays or becomes harmful after Context changes;
24. one Human, Scientific, or Engineering View reporting success while another exposes coercion, invalid evidence, or implementation-induced behavior.
