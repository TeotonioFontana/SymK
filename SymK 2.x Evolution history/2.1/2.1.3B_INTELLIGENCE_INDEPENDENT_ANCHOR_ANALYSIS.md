# SymK 2.1.3B — Intelligence Independent-Anchor Analysis

**Status:** Analytical working proposal — no DR-011 authority
**Date:** 20 August 2026
**Working baseline:** `2.1-dev.10`
**Evidence record:** `SYMK-2X-EV-089`
**Governing method:** Stage-Accepted DR-009
**Structural constraints:** Stage-Accepted DR-010

## 1. Analytical question

> What minimum meaning of Intelligence does SymK require that remains applicable to individual, artificial, collective, distributed, and hybrid candidates without defining Intelligence through Knowledge, human resemblance, one performance, agency, autonomy, rationality, consciousness, or moral standing?

This document completes the second independent-anchor stream defined by the 2.1.3 opening control. It proposes an Intelligence anchor for later Bearer and reciprocal testing. It does not decide which candidate systems actually qualify, complete Amplified Intelligence, or create a DR-011 proposal.

## 2. Why an independent anchor is necessary

The Product Vision names Intelligences as participants and Amplified Intelligence as the target capability. If Intelligence means merely Human-like behavior, accumulated Knowledge, successful output, a score, goal pursuit, learning, agency, or consciousness, SymK cannot distinguish:

- capacity from one manifestation;
- a System from its substrate or components;
- competence and skill from general or transferable capacity;
- adaptation from learning and developmental change;
- Intelligence attribution from authority or responsibility;
- a coordinated collection from a system-level Bearer;
- and Intelligence from Knowledge itself.

The v0.1 Intelligence paper already establishes many of these negative boundaries. Its definition, however, includes Knowledge among the resources integrated by an Intelligent system. That is plausible as a developmental and operational relationship but unsafe inside the independent definition while Knowledge and Intelligence are being tested as a reciprocal pair.

## 4. Term normalization

For this analysis:

- **Intelligence** names the candidate universal SymK concept.
- **capability** is a scoped capacity of a Bearer to produce a class of effects or performances.
- **competence** is capability meeting a declared standard with sufficient reliability in a practice or task class.
- **skill** is a developed, organized ability to perform a class of tasks.
- **performance** is an event, behavior, output, or result produced under particular conditions.
- **Intelligence profile** is a governed characterization of several capability dimensions and their limits.
- **Intelligence attribution** is a revisable claim that an identified Bearer possesses Intelligence or an Intelligence profile within declared Scope.
- **adaptation** is a change in response, organization, model, strategy, or state that maintains or improves fit under relevant variation.
- **learning** is a sufficiently persistent change in capability or disposition arising from experience, evidence, instruction, or feedback.

These terms remain ordinary or working distinctions until DR-011 assigns dispositions.

## 5. Candidate approaches

### Candidate A — Human resemblance or psychometric projection

> Intelligence is the general capacity measured by human cognitive tests or demonstrated through Human-like reasoning and behavior.

**Strength:** operational traditions, comparative data, and a richly studied reference case.

**Failure:** makes one manifestation and its measurement institutions the universal template; underdescribes artificial, collective, embodied, distributed, and domain-specialized capacities; and may confuse proxy, score, or imitation with the concept.

**Disposition:** retain human and psychometric profiles as scoped projections, not the universal anchor.

### Candidate B — Rational goal achievement

> Intelligence is the capacity to achieve objectives across environments through rational action.

**Strength:** supports formal evaluation, decision theory, planning, and cross-task breadth.

**Failure:** may collapse Intelligence into agency, objective possession, or rationality. Interpretive, advisory, exploratory, and distributed systems may contribute Intelligence without owning objectives or actions. A system can also be Intelligent while irrational, deceived, biased, or directed toward a harmful end.

**Disposition:** retain goal achievement as one capability/evaluation family, not a universal constitutive condition.

### Candidate C — Observed successful performance

> A system is Intelligent when it produces sufficiently successful outputs on a relevant test.

**Strength:** observable and measurable.

**Failure:** one success may result from memorization, leakage, luck, external control, or a narrow lookup. One failure may reflect adverse resources, instructions, or environment rather than absent capacity. The test is a projection whose validity depends on Scope and alternatives.

**Disposition:** reject as identity; retain performance as evidence.

### Candidate D — Adopt the v0.1 definition unchanged

> Intelligence is the substrate-realized capacity of an organized System to produce context-sensitive interpretation or effective action across a relevant range by integrating structure, information, experience, and Knowledge, with some adaptation beyond prior cases.

**Strength:** non-anthropocentric, substrate-aware, scoped, capacity-centered, integrative, and sensitive to variation.

**Failure:** embeds Knowledge in the definition; does not state whether adaptation means online learning, flexible response, or evolutionary/designed fit; and may classify or exclude systems based on an unclear novelty threshold.

**Disposition:** revise rather than adopt unchanged.

### Candidate E — Integrated flexible capacity of an organized Bearer

> **Intelligence is a scoped, substrate-realized capacity attributable to an organized Bearer to integrate available structure, signals, experience, and feedback in interpreting situations or producing effective responses across relevant variation, including cases not exhaustively fixed in advance.**

This is the current analytical recommendation.

It changes the v0.1 formulation in five material ways:

1. **Knowledge is removed from the independent anchor.** Knowledge may later enable, develop, or evidence Intelligence without constituting its definition by stipulation.
2. **Bearer makes attribution explicit.** Organized System remains necessary support, but Bearer identifies the subject of the capability claim.
3. **Flexible response replaces mandatory adaptation language.** A system need not learn online if it can integrate and respond across relevant non-preenumerated variation; persistent learning remains a profile dimension and evidence source.
4. **Effectiveness is scoped to an evaluative standard.** It means success relative to a task, environment, question, or practice—not moral goodness, authority, or legitimacy.
5. **Capacity remains distinct from performance.** Intelligence is attributed from a pattern of manifestations, interventions, transfer, failures, and counterfactual evidence.

“Not exhaustively fixed in advance” excludes mere replay or a finite lookup as sufficient evidence while avoiding a demand for human-like open-ended generality. The relevant novelty and variation remain part of Scope.

## 6. Candidate constitutive nucleus

| Dimension | Candidate minimum | What does not suffice |
|---|---|---|
| Realization | A material, computational, biological, social, institutional, or hybrid substrate supports the capacity | Naming a substrate or component |
| Organization | Relations, architecture, coordination, memory, feedback, or control integrate the relevant resources at the Bearer level | Mere collection, co-location, connectivity, or population |
| Capacity | A disposition extends beyond one event and can be probed through variation and counterfactual conditions | One output, score, anecdote, or role title |
| Integration | The Bearer combines multiple relevant signals, structures, experiences, constraints, or feedback relations | Independent fixed reactions with no system-level integration |
| Interpretation or response | The capacity supports situation-sensitive discrimination, inference, generation, selection, coordination, or action | Passive storage or unselected transmission alone |
| Relevant variation | The capacity persists, transfers, reorganizes, or adjusts when material features vary inside its Scope | Exact repetition of an exhaustively predetermined case |
| Evaluability | The claimed effectiveness and limits can be tested against a declared task, environment, purpose, or standard | The unqualified label `intelligent` |

No single dimension independently proves Intelligence. The candidate describes their organized conjunction.

## 8. Learning, adaptation, novelty, and goals

### Learning is not universally constitutive

A fixed but richly organized system may reason, interpret, plan, or generalize across cases without changing its capabilities online. Excluding it solely because it does not learn during operation would confuse developmental history with present capacity. Learning remains important evidence about transfer, revision, and persistence.

### Minimal flexibility is constitutive under Candidate E

A claimed Intelligence must handle relevant variation not exhausted by one predetermined input-output case. This flexibility may arise from designed structure, evolution, training, search, inference, feedback, collaboration, or later learning. The source does not settle the attribution; the realized capacity does.

### Adaptation has multiple timescales

- **situational adjustment:** response changes within an episode;
- **stateful adaptation:** internal state or strategy changes across episodes;
- **learning:** persistent capability/disposition changes from experience or feedback;
- **development:** longer reorganization through formation or growth;
- **evolution or design:** capacity changes across generations or versions.

These must not be represented by one boolean.

### Goals are not universally constitutive

Evaluation requires a task, question, environment, practice, or standard, but the Bearer need not originate or own one stable objective. An advisory, interpretive, scientific, or distributed capability can be Intelligent while its goals are externally assigned, plural, contested, or enacted by another participant. Agency and teleology remain separate questions.

## 9. Scope, Context, Domain, and evaluative standards

Every Intelligence attribution must declare:

- the capability dimensions claimed;
- the Domain, tasks, environments, and relevant variation;
- the time/version and expected persistence;
- priors, training, inherited structures, and available representations;
- tools, collaborators, data, computation, permissions, and other resources;
- success, quality, reliability, efficiency, or transfer standards;
- observed failures, exclusions, and alternative explanations;
- and whether the attribution concerns an individual, component, collective, distributed, institutional, or hybrid Bearer.

Context affects manifestation and assessment. Scope bounds the attribution. Domain specializes what counts as relevant variation, competence, consequence, and acceptable evidence. Applicability determines whether an established profile supports the present role or task. None grants authority or moral approval.

## 10. Bearer and System boundary

The Intelligence Bearer is the subject at whose organized level the claimed integrated capacity exists. It is not selected opportunistically after observing success.

A candidate Bearer must satisfy at least:

1. **Identity:** a justified continuity condition across the evidence period;
2. **Boundary:** explicit components, environment, tools, and external participants;
3. **Organization:** integration relations producing the claimed capacity;
4. **Causal contribution:** the candidate level explains relevant performance and failure better than mere aggregation;
5. **Counterfactual dependence:** material changes to organization or components affect the claimed profile in expected ways;
6. **Resource accounting:** external assistance and priors remain visible;
7. **Temporal validity:** updates, member changes, retraining, and environmental shifts can restrict or supersede the attribution;
8. **Stable accountability boundary:** the success boundary cannot expand while the responsibility/failure boundary contracts without explicit analysis.

Static artifacts such as weights, files, prompts, datasets, documents, or code are not automatically Intelligence Bearers. An executing organized system may use them as substrate or resources. Whether a particular model, agent, institution, team, or human-machine ensemble is the appropriate Bearer depends on the actual integration and claimed capability.

## 11. Dependency analysis

The current candidate dependencies are:

- Intelligence attribution **presupposes** Bearer, Identity, Context, Scope, System boundary, and typed Relationship from DR-010.
- Intelligence is **constituted partly by** organized, integrated capacity across relevant variation.
- Substrate and organization **enable and constrain** Intelligence without being identical to it.
- Performance **evidences** Intelligence but does not constitute it alone.
- Competence, skill, learning, adaptation, rationality, agency, autonomy, and understanding may **contribute to or characterize** an Intelligence profile without being universally equivalent to Intelligence.
- Intelligence **contrasts with** passive storage, fixed replay, unintegrated aggregation, authority, consciousness, and moral standing.
- An Intelligence attribution or profile **represents** selected evidence and may be wrong.
- Domain Intelligence **specializes** the universal concept through domain tasks, standards, methods, constraints, and consequences.
- Intelligence does **not definitionally presuppose** Knowledge under Candidate E. Knowledge may later be an enabling resource, developmental scaffold, product, object of competence, or evidence of some Intelligence manifestations.

## 12. Counterexample tests

| Case | Result under Candidate E |
|---|---|
| Correct answer copied or leaked | Performance is successful, but no integrated capacity across variation is established |
| Capable system fails under missing power, access, or time | One failure does not erase capacity; Context and resource conditions qualify the evidence |
| Fixed chess engine that cannot learn online but searches and evaluates novel positions | Intelligence remains possible because organized capacity handles relevant non-preenumerated variation; online learning is not required |
| Thermostat maintaining one set point | Demonstrates control and limited adaptation, but normally lacks the integration and relevant variation required for the claimed Intelligence Scope; it remains a boundary case rather than a definition by intuition |
| Language model evaluated with hidden retrieval and human correction | The evidence supports the full configured system unless component-level causal tests justify a narrower Bearer |
| Team averaging independent answers | Aggregation may improve performance, but system-level Intelligence is not established without integration, feedback, organization, or emergent capacity |
| Institution with distributed roles, memory, review, and revision | Candidate institutional Intelligence remains possible if the organized level explains capacity and persists across members; it does not imply unitary agency or moral personhood |
| Highly autonomous simple controller | Autonomy is present; Intelligence does not follow without the candidate capacity nucleus |
| Highly Intelligent advisory system with no action permission | Intelligence may be present while agency, authorization, and authority remain elsewhere |
| Conscious but severely impaired subject | Consciousness does not guarantee the claimed Intelligence profile; moral standing is not reduced to capability |

Candidate E survives these tests better than Candidates A–D while preserving their useful evaluation dimensions.

## 14. Human-semantic nucleus

For an informed reader, the minimum explanation is:

> Intelligence is not a brain, processor, model file, institution, score, or successful answer. It is a capacity of an organized Bearer, realized through some substrate, to integrate relevant resources and respond effectively across meaningful variation. We infer that capacity from patterns of performance and failure under known conditions. Learning, agency, autonomy, rationality, consciousness, authority, and moral standing may matter, but none follows automatically from calling something Intelligent.

## 15. Machine-semantic preservation obligations

Without selecting a formal language, later projections must be able to distinguish at least:

- Bearer identity and System boundary;
- substrate, organization, architecture, and external resources;
- capability dimensions from competence standards and skills;
- Intelligence profile from Intelligence attribution;
- performance events, outputs, tests, assessors, and conditions;
- Domain, Scope, Context, task, environment, variation, and novelty;
- priors, training, experience, tools, collaborators, and permissions;
- learning, adaptation, development, and version change;
- successes, failures, uncertainty, alternative explanations, and counterfactual evidence;
- agency, autonomy, rationality, understanding, consciousness, moral standing, authority, and responsibility as separately grounded predicates;
- challenge, restriction, expiry, supersession, and attribution lineage;
- and component, individual, collective, distributed, institutional, and hybrid levels.

No boolean `is_intelligent`, participant-type label, benchmark score, or model category can by itself preserve the concept.

## 16. Consequences if retained

### Product Vision

“Intelligences” can include plural organized Bearers without defining them through Human likeness or requiring artificial participation. “Amplified Intelligence” must later identify whose integrated capacity is increased and how that profile differs from better performance alone.

### Candidate Constitution

- A2 gains a sharper Capability/Intelligence/role/authority separation and must treat origin as neither proof nor disproof.
- A1's performance/Representation humility is reinforced: an Intelligence attribution remains a fallible governed claim.
- A4 may engineer conditions that enable or constrain Intelligence without claiming that architecture or stored Knowledge is the capacity itself.

These are impact findings, not Ratification or axiom wording decisions.

### Foundation Paper revision ledger

The Intelligence v0.2 revision should:

- remove Knowledge from the independent minimum definition and reintroduce it only through typed reciprocal relations;
- distinguish Bearer from organized System and substrate explicitly;
- separate minimal flexible response from learning, persistent adaptation, and development;
- clarify that goals and agency are not universally constitutive;
- replace any one scalar tendency with a scoped profile and declared projection;
- strengthen resource, assistance, novelty, failure, and counterfactual evidence requirements;
- and keep authority, responsibility, consciousness, and moral status independently grounded.

The Knowledge, Education, and Knowledge Engineering papers must later align with the same distinctions.

### Derived projects

Projects own domain tasks, capability dimensions, competence thresholds, tests, resources, novelty regimes, system boundaries, and lawful role assignments. Product labels such as “AI,” professional credentials, benchmark scores, or autonomous operation cannot substitute for a governed Intelligence attribution.

## 17. Preserved dissent and open questions

1. “Intelligence” may be a family-resemblance cluster rather than one concept with necessary and sufficient conditions.
2. Candidate E may still be too permissive for simple adaptive controllers or too restrictive for fixed but powerful formal systems.
3. “Integration” requires stronger criteria to distinguish a genuine organized capacity from convenient system description.
4. “Not exhaustively fixed in advance” requires a principled characterization of novelty and preenumeration.
5. Some theories treat goal-directedness, intentionality, rational normativity, representation, or learning as constitutive; Candidate E keeps them non-universal.
6. The relation between Intelligence and understanding remains unresolved.
7. It remains open whether institutions, collectives, and hybrid systems literally bear Intelligence or whether system-level attribution is explanatory shorthand.
8. A profile may be unavoidable for assessment, but the minimum dimensions and comparability rules remain undecided.
9. Substrate-independent definition may still conceal substrate-specific mechanisms that matter to capability identity and risk.
10. The threshold separating broad competence from Intelligence remains contestable, especially for narrow domain systems.

## 18. Confidence and reopening conditions

**Confidence:** Medium-High that Intelligence requires an independent organized-capacity anchor and foundational disposition; Medium in Candidate E's exact flexibility and integration language; lower for the threshold and collective-bearer criteria.

Reopen or revise this analysis if:

- the Bearer analysis cannot distinguish system-level capacity from aggregation;
- fixed specialized systems expose a contradiction in the variation requirement;
- simple adaptive controllers satisfy the wording without the intended distinction;
- the Knowledge reciprocity test shows that Knowledge is constitutive rather than merely enabling for all Intelligence;
- cross-domain tests cannot define relevant variation without project-local circularity;
- 2.1.6 reveals that agency or normative responsiveness is unavoidable for SymK participants;
- or the candidate cannot support an inspectable, non-anthropocentric attribution practice.

## 19. Working conclusion and next dependency

Candidate E is the strongest current independent anchor and Intelligence passes the DR-009 eligibility gate as a **candidate foundational concept**. Neither conclusion has Stage-Accepted authority.

With both independent anchors now available, the next required action is the joint Bearer and attribution model. That analysis must determine which attribution conditions are shared, which are concept-specific, and when individual, artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, or hybrid systems qualify as bearers. Only then may reciprocal Knowledge–Intelligence relationships be classified.
