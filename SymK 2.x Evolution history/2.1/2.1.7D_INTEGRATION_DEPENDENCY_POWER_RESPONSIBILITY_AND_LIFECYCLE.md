## 1. Question and analytical result

> How can a Domain Intelligence Amplifier integrate plural Intelligence participants and governed Domain Knowledge through actual mechanisms, dependencies, Power, Responsibility, and change without turning one mechanism into a universal architecture, hiding material external contributors, or laundering responsibility through the system boundary?

Stream D supports the following working answer:

> **A DIA does not require one universal integration mechanism or architecture. It requires evidence that, for the declared Domain purpose and complex-question class, one or more identified and governed mechanisms actually organize the seven Stream C conditions across relevant variation and time. Coordination, mediation, Representation, retrieval, reasoning support, orchestration, validation, feedback, Learning, supervision, memory, automation, and other mechanisms may contribute singly or in changing bundles. None is universally necessary or sufficient by name.**

> **Every material DIA and Amplification claim must expose the dependencies, providers, controllers, authorities, responsibility relations, affected subjects, failure modes, lifecycle transitions, and boundary choices on which the claimed capability depends. Externality, contract, automation, opacity, distribution, update, replacement, or a continuous service name cannot erase causal Contribution, Power, retained duties, challenge, response, history, or a genuine gap.**

> **Amplification remains a descriptive capability-change predicate. A capability gain can be real while its production, deployment, distribution, or use is irresponsible. “Responsible amplification” is therefore a qualified claim: an actual Amplification relation plus an independently evidenced, scoped DR-014 Responsibility-compatibility assessment. “SymK-compatible DIA” is a possible governance qualification, not a synonym for DIA membership and not a new status accepted by this stream.**

The resulting model has nine central commitments:

1. **Mechanism non-universality:** no named mechanism, technology, role, architecture, or sequence is constitutive merely by label.
2. **Functional integration evidence:** the system must show materially adequate organization of its constitutive conditions, not merely connection or co-location.
3. **Dependency disclosure:** capability claims remain scoped to material enabling, operational, epistemic, governance, economic, formative, and infrastructural dependencies.
4. **Boundary stability:** the system boundary cannot expand to claim success and contract to disclaim failure, cost, dependency, or responsibility.
5. **Power visibility:** access, agenda, update, classification, evidence, authorization, challenge, exit, repair, and succession controls remain visible and level-qualified.
6. **Role non-substitution:** provider, developer, integrator, deployer, operator, user, participant, beneficiary, affected subject, authority, and responsible subject are distinct roles.
7. **Responsibility anti-laundering:** no component, configured system, provider, deployer, user, organization, Human approver, or “the ecosystem” is a universal residual bearer.
8. **Lifecycle revalidation:** material change can alter system Identity, DIA membership, Amplification evidence, authorization, responsibility, and qualification independently.
9. **Descriptive/qualified separation:** DIA membership and Amplification do not entail benefit, legitimacy, Authorization, ethical compatibility, or SymK compatibility.

These are analytical directions only. Stream E must test professional transformation, retained capability, formation, and future experts; Stream F must consolidate the full evidence before Proposed DR-015 can exist.

## 2. Authority, status, and force

This artifact is evidence for later 2.1.7 work. It does not have Stage-Accepted semantic, constitutional, professional, legal, or operational force.

It distinguishes:

1. a **descriptive system claim** that an identified arrangement satisfies the DIA conditions;
2. a **descriptive relation claim** that an identified Bearer was amplified under a declared comparison;
3. a **mechanism claim** about how an effect occurred;
4. a **dependency claim** about conditions on which capability or operation relies;
5. a **responsibility relation or allocation claim** under a declared source and frame;
6. a **governance qualification claim** such as responsible or SymK-compatible operation;
7. an **Authorization claim** concerning permitted use or reliance; and
8. a **record or status** representing any of the preceding claims.

No record creates its represented claim. No use of “must” below creates accepted project authority; it states what the working model requires to remain coherent and testable.

## 3. Inherited constraints

Stream D preserves the following boundaries:

1. DIA is the Stream C candidate specific System class, not a synonym for product, platform, project, workflow, tool, organization, or marketing label.
2. The seven candidate conditions remain: organized System; Domain purpose and complex-question class; plural qualified Intelligence participants; actual Cooperation; governed Domain Knowledge; identified amplified-Bearer/profile target; and evidenced repeatable Amplification capability.
3. DIA system, Amplifier, amplified Bearer, Intelligence-bearing system, component, Participant, user, beneficiary, and affected subject remain non-substitutable roles.
4. A DIA need not itself be an Intelligence Bearer or the amplified Bearer.
5. Artificial Intelligence is neither sufficient nor universally necessary at the candidate class level.
6. Amplification is a comparative, multidimensional, Bearer-attributed capability relation; access, output, speed, automation, substitution, and value do not establish it.
7. A gain in one dimension does not erase a material loss, changed resources, dependency, or comparator inequality.
8. Cooperation is a thin but real process among plural Intelligences; connection, coordination, workflow, aggregation, obedience, or output combination is insufficient.
9. Cooperation can coexist with asymmetry, conflict, coercion, harm, or irresponsible qualification, but suppression can defeat the mutual-orientation evidence required for Cooperation.
10. Domain Knowledge is governed through claims, grounds, methods, assets, Representation, challenge, application, consequence, and revision; a repository is not Knowledge substance.
11. Power capacity, exercise, mechanism, effect, qualification, authority, response, and record remain distinct.
12. Authority, Authorization, control, agency, causation, Contribution, decision, Responsibility, duty, liability, blame, remedy, and standing do not entail one another.
13. Responsibility is a sourced, scoped, phased, challengeable relationship family, not a trait, conserved substance, or undifferentiated status.
14. Responsibility allocation is object-first, multi-subject, multi-level, temporal, and non-additive.
15. No universal residual responsibility bearer exists.
16. Affected nonparticipants remain within the consequence horizon without becoming Participants or responsible subjects by that fact.
17. Formal challenge permission is not an effective challenge path.
18. DR-014 preserves affected-subject non-erasure, non-privilege, consequence-sensitive due regard, consent-claim integrity, effective challenge and accountable response, prevention-to-repair continuity, visible jurisdiction, and unresolved conflict.
19. External authority may govern within its jurisdiction but cannot silently become universal SymK semantics.
20. Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views remain distinct and aligned through declared Representation and Projection loss.
21. A0–A4, A6, J1, the conflict/challenge protocol, and the Foundation Papers remain non-ratified sources or candidates.
22. Package anatomy remains 2.5; Domain realization 2.6; empirical validation 2.7; formalization and lowering 2.9.
23. No actual DIA, Bearer, provider, system, profession, project, or deployment is classified by this analysis.
24. DR-015 remains reserved without content or status.

## 4. Mechanism, condition, effect, and evidence

The analysis requires five separate layers:

| Layer | Question | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutive condition | What must be true for the candidate class? | A condition is not a named implementation |
| Mechanism | Through what process or arrangement may the condition be realized? | Mechanism presence does not establish effectiveness |
| Operation | What occurred in this episode and configuration? | One operation does not establish repeatable capability |
| Effect | What capability, behavior, dependency, burden, or consequence changed? | Output and value are not automatic capability effects |
| Evidence | What supports the claim, with what uncertainty and defeats? | A record, metric, or certificate is not the represented fact |

“Integration mechanism” therefore means an identified process, relation, interface, role arrangement, or technical/organizational facility through which material DIA conditions are coordinated or connected in operation. It does not mean that every integration is technical, centralized, automated, persistent, or whole-level Intelligence.

A mechanism can:

- be present but ineffective;
- support one condition while undermining another;
- create performance without Bearer capability change;
- work only under hidden external support;
- improve one profile dimension while reducing another;
- transfer Power without transferring Responsibility;
- preserve operation while degrading retained participant capability;
- disappear or change at update time; or
- be represented inaccurately by the system description.

## 5. Candidate mechanism families

The following families must be considered when material. None is universally necessary or sufficient by name.

| Mechanism family | Possible function | Typical evidence | Frequent prohibited substitution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coordination | Align timing, roles, dependencies, tasks, and shared undertaking | plans, interaction records, handoffs, conflict handling | workflow equals Cooperation |
| Mediation | Connect subjects, vocabularies, claims, interests, or interfaces | translation history, mediator actions, negotiated changes | intermediary presence equals understanding |
| Representation and Projection | Express Domain claims or one view in another form | provenance, semantics, mapping, declared loss | representation equals represented Knowledge |
| Access and retrieval | Make sources, claims, examples, or records available | recall/precision, provenance, access conditions, failure cases | more content equals Intelligence gain |
| Interpretation and translation | Relate meanings, contexts, languages, models, or professional vocabularies | cross-view tests, ambiguity handling, loss declarations | formatted output equals semantic alignment |
| Reasoning support | Generate, compare, test, critique, explain, calculate, or simulate | varied-case capability evidence, error detection, counterargument | one correct answer equals Amplification |
| Orchestration and allocation | Select or route tasks, resources, agents, tools, and review | allocation rationale, alternatives, intervention points | routing equals whole-level capability |
| Validation and challenge | Test claims, outputs, assumptions, boundaries, and evidence | dissent, review, adversarial cases, correction | approval equals warrant |
| Decision and Authorization | Select a course or permit action/reliance | declared authority, Scope, reasons, limits, review | authority equals truth or responsibility |
| Execution and automation | Perform actions, transformations, or recurring work | action trace, controls, reversibility, failure recovery | labor reduction equals Amplification |
| Feedback and correction | Return outcome and error information to claims, behavior, or configuration | feedback provenance, uptake, correction, recurrence | feedback existence equals Learning |
| Memory and persistence | Preserve relevant state, history, or institutional continuity | versioned records, retrieval, succession tests | stored history equals system Intelligence |
| Learning and adaptation | Change a Bearer or organized system capability through experience | before/after profiles, transfer, retention, attribution | changed output equals Learning |
| Supervision and oversight | Observe, constrain, intervene, escalate, or review | competence, information, authority, response time | Human presence equals effective supervision |
| Infrastructure and access control | Supply compute, communications, identity, security, availability, and permissions | dependency map, access logs, failure/fallback tests | infrastructure owner equals sole responsible party |
| Social and institutional organization | Sustain norms, roles, trust, professional judgment, incentives, and continuity | governance evidence, role practice, turnover tests | institutional name equals integrated capability |

Other mechanisms remain possible. The taxonomy is an analytical search aid, not an accepted closed vocabulary.

## 6. No universal mechanism or sequence

The candidate class must remain architecture-independent. Therefore:

- coordination may be distributed or centralized;
- mediation may be Human, artificial, institutional, procedural, or hybrid;
- retrieval may be internal, external, transient, or unnecessary for a particular configuration;
- reasoning support may be performed by participants, tools, procedures, or configured combinations;
- orchestration may be explicit, emergent, rotating, negotiated, or externally constrained;
- feedback may be immediate, delayed, indirect, sampled, or absent in a bounded episode;
- Learning may occur in one participant, several participants, the organized whole, or nowhere;
- supervision may be Human, institutional, automated, collective, layered, or legitimately absent for some functions;
- memory may reside in people, practices, artifacts, systems, or their organization; and
- execution may remain manual, be automated, or combine both.

No fixed pipeline is implied. A DIA may use different bundles by question, episode, participant, lifecycle phase, jurisdiction, or failure state.

The universal requirement is narrower:

> There must be sufficient evidence that the actual mechanism bundle organizes the seven conditions for the claimed Domain, Scope, Context, time, and relevant variation, with material dependencies and losses exposed.

“Sufficient” remains Domain-owned and later empirically grounded. Stream D does not set a metric or threshold.

## 7. Integration as an evidenced relation

Integration is not a substance and not an automatic property of connection. Stream D uses the following working candidate:

> **Integration is the context-, purpose-, and level-qualified organization of distinguishable subjects, components, resources, relations, and mechanisms such that their materially related Contributions can participate in the operation or capability of an identified system without erasing their boundaries, dependencies, conflicts, or independent predicates.**

An integration claim should identify:

1. the integrated system, version, boundary, Domain, purpose, and question class;
2. the subjects, components, resources, environments, and external dependencies;
3. the mechanisms and interfaces relating them;
4. the Contributions and conditions actually organized;
5. the relevant operational episodes and variation;
6. what coordination or coherence is observed at the claimed level;
7. what remains attributable only to members, components, providers, or environment;
8. incompatibilities, losses, conflicts, gaps, uncertainty, and failure modes;
9. persistence, replacement, update, and counterfactual-removal behavior; and
10. the Evidence, assessor, authority, status, dissent, and reopening conditions.

Integration can support a system-level capability claim but does not create one. It can also support a configured-system Bearer claim, but only independent Bearer evidence can establish that predicate.

## 8. Material integration dimensions

The following dimensions are diagnostic, not universal conjuncts:

| Dimension | Central question | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose and task | Are Contributions organized toward the declared Domain purpose and question class? | unrelated outputs merely aggregated |
| Epistemic | Are claims, grounds, status, uncertainty, challenge, and revision connected? | source access without warrant or consequence return |
| Semantic and representational | Are meanings and Projection losses governed across views and interfaces? | vocabulary mapping hides incompatible meanings |
| Capability | Do Contributions support the target Bearer/profile change across variation? | output coordination without capability change |
| Cooperative and relational | Do qualified Intelligences actually cooperate under DR-013? | command chain, connection, or parallel work only |
| Operational | Do roles, timing, resources, decisions, execution, and failure handling cohere? | nominal process fails in actual use |
| Temporal and lifecycle | Does relevant organization persist or get revalidated through change? | continuous label hides discontinuous configuration |
| Governance and responsibility | Are authority, control, challenge, response, and responsibility relations connected without laundering? | final approver or “system” absorbs all relations |
| Infrastructure and dependency | Are material external services, access, maintenance, and alternatives exposed? | omitted provider makes claim appear self-contained |

Not every dimension is equally material in every case. The claim must justify exclusions rather than silently treating missing evidence as absence of dependency.

## 9. Mechanism adequacy and defeat

A mechanism-bundle assessment should test:

- **relevance:** relation to a constitutive condition and target capability;
- **actuality:** operation in the claimed configuration, not intended design;
- **coverage:** material questions, participants, Knowledge states, and variation;
- **quality:** error, uncertainty, conflict, contextual sensitivity, and robustness;
- **interaction:** complementarities, interference, bottlenecks, and emergent effects;
- **attribution:** member, component, system, provider, and environment separation;
- **counterfactuals:** removal, degradation, substitution, and fallback;
- **persistence:** retention or repeatability across time and turnover where claimed;
- **governance:** status, challenge, correction, response, and consequence return; and
- **boundary sensitivity:** whether the result changes under a more complete dependency boundary.

Defeat conditions include hidden leakage, cherry-picked episodes, unequal comparator resources, unrecorded expert rescue, nonrepresentative cases, suppressed dissent, inaccessible failure evidence, boundary shopping, and a status that outlives its evidentiary basis.

## 10. Dependency model

Dependency is relational and scoped:

> **A dependency exists when an identified capability, operation, claim, status, response path, or lifecycle function materially relies on another subject, component, resource, condition, authority, environment, or relation under a declared frame.**

Material dependency families include:

| Family | Examples | Claim consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutive | participant Cooperation, governed Knowledge, target Bearer | absence may defeat DIA membership |
| Enabling | tools, interfaces, facilities, credentials, compute | capability is qualified by supplied condition |
| Operational | hosting, communications, staffing, workflow, support | episode or service may fail when removed |
| Epistemic | data, experts, methods, models, validation, provenance | warrant and capability boundary may change |
| Governance | authority, supervision, audit, challenge, remedy | responsible qualification or Authorization may fail |
| Economic | pricing, licensing, procurement, labor, funding | sustainability, access, alternatives, and cost change |
| Formative | Training, Education, mentoring, practice, tacit Knowledge | retained capability and succession may change |
| Infrastructural/environmental | energy, network, hardware, legal access, social trust | apparent system capability depends on context |

Each may be internal or external, visible or hidden, replaceable or single-source, voluntary or constrained, reversible or irreversible, transient or persistent, stable or volatile, authorized or unauthorized, governed or unmanaged.

Dependency does not by itself decide system membership. A service can remain external yet material; a component can be internal yet replaceable. System boundary and dependency relation are separate claims.

## 11. External providers and the extended capability boundary

A provider, model service, expert, platform, data source, host, maintainer, standards body, professional institution, or public infrastructure may be indispensable without becoming:

- a member of the claimed DIA;
- a DIA Participant;
- the amplified Bearer;
- part of a configured-system Bearer;
- the sole Amplifier;
- the sole cause of the gain; or
- the sole responsible subject.

A material external-dependency record should identify:

1. provider or source Identity and relevant version;
2. supplied capability, resource, Knowledge, infrastructure, authority, or service;
3. access, pricing, licensing, data, jurisdiction, and continuity conditions;
4. control over update, withdrawal, filtering, monitoring, and disclosure;
5. substitution and migration feasibility;
6. outage and degradation behavior;
7. provider/deployer/operator/user retained roles;
8. evidence and audit access;
9. affected subjects and information flows;
10. challenge, incident, correction, and remedy interfaces; and
11. how removal changes DIA membership, system capability, and Bearer amplification separately.

The system may still satisfy the DIA candidate when materially dependent. The claim cannot represent support-conditioned capability as self-sufficient or retained capability.

## 12. Fragility, concentration, and lock-in

Fragility is not mere dependency. It concerns susceptibility to material loss or uncontrolled change under plausible disruption.

Lock-in can arise through:

- proprietary formats or semantics;
- nonportable data, history, configuration, or learned practice;
- exclusive access to models, experts, credentials, or infrastructure;
- contractual, financial, or jurisdictional barriers;
- network effects and participant concentration;
- loss of alternative professional capability;
- switching cost, retraining, validation, or downtime;
- opaque updates or unavailable prior versions;
- remedy, audit, or challenge tied to one provider; or
- dependence that becomes visible only after withdrawal.

Lock-in may coexist with genuine Amplification. It can increase Power, total cost, outage consequence, and succession risk and can defeat responsible or SymK-compatible qualification when materially concealed or left without proportionate response. It is not by itself a universal class disqualifier.

## 13. Power and control in a DIA

Power remains a relational capacity to shape another subject's actions, alternatives, conditions, access, exposure, standing, or consequences. A DIA analysis should test control over:

- purpose and agenda;
- participant admission, exclusion, and role;
- Knowledge sources, status, visibility, and deletion;
- Representation, ranking, filtering, and framing;
- infrastructure, credentials, access, and price;
- model, configuration, defaults, prompts, and updates;
- baseline, metric, benchmark, evidence, and publication;
- task allocation, decision, execution, and intervention;
- Authorization, deployment, reliance, suspension, and retirement;
- monitoring, privacy, disclosure, and surveillance;
- voice, refusal, exit, representation, and appeal;
- incident recognition, correction, repair, remedy, and recurrence;
- system boundary, membership claim, and status record; and
- archive, replacement, succession, and historical narrative.

The holder of one control does not hold all Power. Technical execution does not establish recognized Authority. Authority does not prove legitimacy, warrant, capability, or Responsibility. Lack of a single controller does not imply absence of Power.

## 14. Cooperation under asymmetry, coercion, and suppression

Power asymmetry does not automatically defeat Cooperation or DIA membership. Legitimate expertise, coordination, emergency response, professional duty, or public authority may produce asymmetry.

However, a cooperation claim is defeated or restricted when the supposed plural Intelligences do not actually contribute toward a shared undertaking because their contributions are:

- merely commanded or copied without relevant reciprocal orientation;
- systematically suppressed or excluded from uptake;
- manipulated so that the shared undertaking is fictional;
- treated as replaceable resources with no relevant contribution relation;
- prevented from challenging material claims required by the process; or
- represented as cooperative solely because the output combines multiple sources.

Coercively constrained Cooperation can still be descriptively classifiable in some cases while failing responsible qualification. The assessment must preserve both the thin occurrence and the qualification rather than defining harm away.

## 15. Role and subject separation

The following roles can overlap but never collapse automatically:

| Role | Question |
|---|---|

| Knowledge/source governor | Who governs collection, provenance, status, access, and limits? |
| Developer/designer | Who shapes capabilities, defaults, interfaces, and failure modes? |
| Provider/supplier | Who supplies a service, component, infrastructure, or warranty? |
| Procurer | Who selects suppliers, terms, evidence, exit, and acceptance conditions? |
| Integrator/configurator | Who composes components, semantics, permissions, and controls? |
| Assessor/auditor | Who evaluates what, under which criteria and limitations? |
| Authority/authorizer | Who has recognized power to decide or permit a scoped act? |
| Deployer | Who selects the actual Context, population, safeguards, and reliance policy? |
| Operator/user | Who acts through, configures, relies on, or intervenes in an episode? |
| Intelligence Participant | Which qualified Bearer actually participates in Cooperation? |
| Amplifier | What causally contributes to the evidenced amplification relation? |
| Amplified Bearer | Whose capability configuration changes? |
| DIA system | Which organized system satisfies the candidate class conditions? |
| Beneficiary | Who receives a claimed benefit? |
| Affected subject | Who bears material consequence or risk? |
| Challenger/representative | Who raises Evidence or represents absent or constrained interests? |
| Supervisor/monitor | Who can observe, understand, intervene, escalate, and correct? |

A “user is responsible” clause cannot absorb provider design, deployment, institution, authority, or remedy relations. “The provider is responsible” cannot absorb local purpose, integration, deployment, reliance, or use. “Human in the loop” cannot absorb system Power or become a universal responsibility sink.

## 16. Responsibility application

Stream D applies DR-014 without reallocating any actual responsibility.

A material DIA responsibility analysis should identify:

1. the responsibility object, source, type, phase, standard, and jurisdiction;
2. the subject and level to which the relation is attributed;
3. capability, information, resources, authority, control, and practical alternatives;
4. purpose, design, supply, procurement, integration, assessment, Authorization, deployment, operation, supervision, maintenance, update, and retirement functions;
5. transferred, retained, parallel, nested, shared, joint, collective, institutional, distributed, hybrid, predecessor, successor, and unresolved relations;
6. affected subjects, representatives, challengers, forums, and response paths;
7. prevention, monitoring, containment, correction, repair, remedy, recurrence, and revision;
8. uncertainty, conflicts, gaps, dissent, assessment, and status; and
9. Provenance, version, history, and reopening conditions.

Responsibility for producing a defect, current correction, reliance authorization, supervision, harm response, and historical answerability can belong to different subjects. None cancels the others automatically.

## 17. Responsible amplification

Stream D retains the following analytical construction:

> **Responsible amplification is an actual Amplification relation whose production, provision, integration, deployment, use, maintenance, consequence, and lifecycle are assessed under an explicit Responsibility frame and satisfy the applicable candidate DR-014 compatibility conditions for the claimed Scope.**

This is not a new capability dimension. It does not mean:

- the gain is larger;
- the answer is correct or valuable;
- every affected party consented;
- no harm occurred;
- one subject bears all Responsibility;
- external law or professional standards are satisfied;
- the DIA is universally ethical; or
- the qualification persists after material change.

A system can support genuine Amplification and still conceal affected costs, suppress challenge, use fabricated consent, create unmanaged lock-in, or lack capable response. The correct result is “Amplification supported; responsible-amplification qualification unsupported or disputed,” not denial of the capability evidence merely to preserve a favorable label.

Conversely, a carefully governed tool that does not establish capability change may be responsible assistance without being Amplification.

## 18. Possible SymK-compatible DIA qualification

A future governance status may distinguish:

1. **DIA membership:** descriptive satisfaction of the seven class conditions;
2. **responsible-operation assessment:** scoped Responsibility compatibility for an episode or lifecycle frame;
3. **SymK-compatible DIA qualification:** a possible broader claim that the DIA's representations, evidence, governance, challenge, response, lifecycle, and jurisdiction satisfy accepted SymK obligations; and
4. **Authorization:** permission for a specific deployment, use, or reliance.

Stream D does not accept the third status or define a certification. It identifies minimum candidate content:

- accurate system and dependency boundaries;
- no role, substrate, origin, authority, compliance, contract, consent record, approval, automation, outcome, or status privilege;
- affected-subject non-erasure;
- proportionate consequence, Power, vulnerability, alternatives, uncertainty, severity, reversibility, distribution, and residual-effect analysis;
- valid claims about consent, notice, comprehension, refusal, scope, duration, and revocation;
- effective, protected, receivable, assessable, and consequential challenge;
- reasoned and capable response;
- prevention-to-repair continuity;
- visible sources, jurisdictions, conflicts, and genuine gaps;
- versioned evidence, responsibility, Authorization, and qualification;
- revalidation at material change; and
- no laundering through component, contract, system, ecosystem, or successor boundaries.

Exact obligations remain for 2.2–2.3, Domain projects, and applicable external authority.

## 19. Lifecycle model

A DIA lifecycle analysis should test these function phases when material:

| Phase | Material questions |
|---|---|
| Purpose and conception | Domain, question class, population, alternatives, affected horizon, sponsor, legitimacy |
| Design and sourcing | participant roles, Knowledge sources, models, interfaces, defaults, dependency choices |
| Procurement and contracting | provider selection, evidence access, allocation, update, exit, audit, remedy |
| Configuration and integration | boundary, semantics, permissions, mechanism bundle, control and failure paths |
| Validation and assessment | class conditions, Bearer profiles, comparators, variation, uncertainty, dissent |
| Authorization and deployment | authority, Context, population, safeguards, reliance, challenge and suspension |
| Operation and use | actual Cooperation, capability, intervention, affected consequence, records |
| Monitoring and challenge | drift, failures, exclusions, dependency, incidents, voice, response |
| Containment, correction, repair, remedy | stop, rollback, correct, restore, compensate, disclose, learn |
| Update and maintenance | component/version change, retraining, Knowledge revision, revalidation |
| Suspension and retirement | continuing effects, alternatives, records, affected notification, responsibility |
| Replacement and succession | Identity, capability, duties, open incidents, archives, remedy and history |

The same subject may occupy several functions; several subjects may occupy one. A title is not a responsibility relation. Lifecycle completeness does not require one centralized workflow.

## 20. Change-impact and revalidation rule

A material change may concern:

- purpose, Domain, question class, Context, population, or Scope;
- participant, amplified Bearer, role, Contribution, or Cooperation relation;
- Knowledge source, status, Representation, Ontology, Logic, or Projection;
- model, component, provider, infrastructure, data, configuration, interface, or default;
- capability profile, comparator, variation, dependency, alternative, or failure behavior;
- authority, Authorization, control, responsibility, challenge, response, or remedy;
- cost, access, licensing, jurisdiction, affected subjects, or distribution; or
- system Identity, boundary, name, owner, successor, or record.

Material change requires impact assessment and selective revalidation. It does not automatically erase the prior DIA or Amplification result, and it does not automatically preserve it.

At minimum, revalidation asks independently:

1. Is this still the same relevant system or a successor?
2. Do the seven DIA conditions still hold?
3. Is the amplified Bearer still identified and stable?
4. Is the baseline/comparator still legitimate?
5. Does repeatable capability evidence still cover relevant variation?
6. Which gains, losses, dependencies, and affected consequences changed?
7. Which authority, Authorization, responsibility, challenge, and response relations changed?
8. Does any responsible or SymK-compatible qualification remain supported?

A continuous product or service name answers none of these by itself.

## 21. Outage, degradation, and fallback

An outage can affect different claims differently:

- the DIA may be temporarily unable to operate;
- the configured system may lose membership because a constitutive condition is unavailable;
- participants may retain some or all amplified capability;
- users may retain output access through a fallback without retained Intelligence;
- responsibility and remedy relations may persist;
- a prior membership record may remain historically accurate;
- current Authorization may be suspended; and
- affected consequences may continue after the service stops.

Assessments should distinguish planned/unplanned, partial/complete, transient/persistent, detectable/hidden, recoverable/irreversible, internal/external, and graceful/catastrophic degradation.

A fallback should identify capability limits, Knowledge freshness, authority, supervision, affected risk, challenge, response, and restoration criteria. “Human fallback” is unsupported when Humans no longer have the information, time, practice, access, authority, or retained capability to perform it.

## 22. Replacement, retirement, and succession

Replacement can concern a component, provider, Participant, amplified Bearer, mechanism, configured system, institution, or service. The analysis must preserve:

- predecessor and successor Identity;
- continuity source and boundary;
- transferred/retained capabilities, Knowledge, records, roles, authority, and responsibilities;
- changed dependencies, alternatives, failure modes, and affected subjects;
- open incidents, dissent, challenges, remedies, and residual effects;
- revalidation, migration, rollback, and retirement evidence; and
- archive and historical answerability custody.

A successor does not inherit DIA membership, Intelligence, Amplification, Authorization, responsibility, or responsible qualification merely by taking a name, contract, data store, model, staff, role, or service.

Participant turnover can preserve whole-level capability through organization, practice, memory, and recruitment without copying Intelligence to every new member. It can also hollow out capability while the label persists. Both require evidence.

## 23. Responsibility gaps

A gap is typed and object-specific. Stream D distinguishes:

- **genuine gap:** no subject has the required relation or capable response under the frame;
- **manufactured gap:** choices about design, contract, automation, opacity, resources, or boundary create or preserve avoidable absence;
- **representational gap:** the relation may exist but the model or record omits or misstates it;
- **governance gap:** a relation is nominally allocated but authority, capability, information, resource, challenge, enforcement, or remedy is ineffective;
- **jurisdictional conflict:** sources disagree or no forum can resolve the collision; and
- **coverage gap:** answerability exists but prevention, correction, repair, remedy, recurrence, or revision does not.

Complexity, distribution, artificial operation, provider separation, user acceptance, contractual disclaimer, audit, or “shared responsibility” does not prove a genuine gap.

A gap record does not cure the gap. A responsible-qualification claim must expose the object, attempted allocation, source, missing capability or authority, affected subjects, interim containment, escalation, response limits, and reopening conditions.

## 24. Whole, member, component, and ecosystem boundaries

Capability, Power, Responsibility, and affected consequence must be attributed at the level supported by Evidence.

- A member's Intelligence does not make the whole intelligent.
- A whole's integrated capability does not copy to every member.
- A component's control effect does not make it the sole power holder.
- A configured system's operation does not erase provider or deployer relations.
- An ecosystem label does not establish a subject capable of bearing Responsibility.
- An institutional Responsibility relation does not absolve members, officers, providers, or external authorities.
- A Human decision does not absorb upstream framing, defaults, evidence, or institutional constraint.
- A model output does not become a decision, Authorization, or responsibility allocation by being acted upon.
- An affected subject need not be inside the DIA boundary to remain materially relevant.

The chosen system boundary must remain stable across favorable and unfavorable evidence. Broader causal, dependency, responsibility, and consequence graphs may extend beyond the DIA membership boundary.

## 25. Lifecycle states and independent statuses

Possible analytical distinctions include:

| State or status | Meaning | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate | Under assessment for class conditions | not membership |
| Active member | Conditions evidenced for declared frame | not permanent or responsible |
| Degraded | Material capability or condition reduced but claim not yet fully defeated | requires Scope and response |
| Suspended | Use, reliance, or assessment paused | not historical erasure |
| Expired | Evidence or Authorization period ended | not proof of current failure |
| Superseded | New version or assessment replaces current status | predecessor remains historical |
| Retired | Operation intentionally ended | responsibility and effects may persist |
| Historical member | Prior membership remains supportable for its period | not current membership |
| Responsible-operation supported/disputed/failed | qualification assessment | not class membership |
| Authorized/restricted/revoked | governance permission | not capability or warrant |

These are working analytical labels only. Final status anatomy remains 2.5.

## 26. Alternatives tested

### A — Require a fixed mechanism stack

**Rejected.** It would confuse one implementation with the universal class and exclude valid Human, institutional, distributed, or future architectures.

### B — Treat any connected workflow as integration

**Rejected.** Connection can exist without Cooperation, governed Knowledge, capability integration, or repeatable Amplification.

### C — Make Learning, feedback, memory, or supervision universally necessary

**Rejected.** Each may be common or important, but none is required by every bounded DIA configuration. Domain and lifecycle qualifications remain possible.

### D — Include every dependency inside the DIA boundary

**Rejected.** Dependency and membership are separate. Boundary inflation destroys useful attribution and still may omit environmental or institutional conditions.

### E — Ignore external dependencies because they are outside

**Rejected.** It converts support-conditioned capability into apparent self-sufficiency and conceals fragility, Power, and cost.

### F — Any lock-in or Power asymmetry defeats DIA membership

**Rejected.** Descriptive class capability can coexist with harmful or irresponsible conditions. These conditions affect qualification and may defeat Cooperation only where its actual process disappears.

### G — A final Human approver makes operation responsible

**Rejected.** Presence, click, or formal approval does not establish competence, information, time, independence, authority, control, alternatives, or effective response.

### H — The configured system is always the responsible subject

**Rejected.** Responsibility requires a source, object, level, capability, authority, and response relation. A system can occupy some roles without becoming a universal sink.

### I — Provider or user terms fully transfer responsibility

**Rejected.** Contracts may allocate scoped tasks or liability but cannot erase actual Contribution, retained control, external duties, affected nonparties, or incapable allocation.

### J — Only responsible capability gain counts as Amplification

**Rejected.** It would corrupt descriptive evidence to secure a favorable ethical label and make irresponsible gains conceptually invisible.

### K — Responsible operation follows automatically from valid Amplification

**Rejected.** Capability, warrant, benefit, Authorization, Responsibility, and ethical compatibility remain separate predicates.

### L — Continuous product identity preserves every status

**Rejected.** Material update, replacement, or boundary change can alter each claim independently.

### M — Bounded mechanism plurality with explicit dependencies and separate qualification

**Retained as the working direction.** It preserves architecture independence, causal and responsibility visibility, lifecycle change, and honest capability evidence.

## 28. Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views

### 28.1 Human View

The Human View must make intelligible:

- who participates, benefits, depends, controls, decides, challenges, responds, and bears consequence;
- whether assistance becomes dependency or loss of practical alternative;
- how professional judgment, tacit Knowledge, voice, refusal, exit, and retained capability change;
- whether formal oversight is practical and effective;
- how outage, update, replacement, and succession affect people and institutions; and
- why a capability claim does not settle value, responsibility, or legitimacy.

### 28.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View must permit testable hypotheses about:

- mechanism contribution and interaction;
- system/member/component capability attribution;
- dependency and counterfactual removal;
- robustness across variation;
- fragility, degradation, recovery, persistence, and turnover;
- causal alternatives and boundary sensitivity;
- Power and challenge effects on available Evidence; and
- uncertainty in class, amplification, and qualification assessments.

It must not treat one benchmark, architecture, metric, or aggregate as universal.

### 28.3 Engineering View

Engineering representations may include:

- versioned system, boundary, component, provider, environment, and dependency graphs;
- mechanism and interface maps;
- participant, role, authority, control, responsibility, affected-subject, and response relations;
- capability profiles, baselines, comparators, variation, uncertainty, and failures;
- lifecycle states, change events, revalidation, rollback, retirement, and succession;
- challenge, incident, correction, repair, remedy, and recurrence paths; and
- independent class, capability, Authorization, responsibility, and qualification statuses.

Every graph, card, schema, field, checklist, score, or certificate remains a Representation. A complete record cannot create the represented capability or responsibility.

### 28.4 Alignment rule

Material disagreement among Views is Evidence. Engineering convenience cannot choose the ontology of the system boundary; scientific aggregation cannot erase lived dependency or affected consequence; Human familiarity or trust cannot substitute for capability evidence. Projection loss, authority, dissent, and unresolved incompatibility remain explicit.
