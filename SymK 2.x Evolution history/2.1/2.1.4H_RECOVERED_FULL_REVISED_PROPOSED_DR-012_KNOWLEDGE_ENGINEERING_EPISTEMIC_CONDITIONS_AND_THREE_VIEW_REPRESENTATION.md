## 1. Decision question

> What does SymK engineer when it cannot engineer truth or Knowledge directly; which epistemic conditions, artifacts, relationships, lifecycle distinctions, and representation obligations must it govern; and how must Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views remain aligned without allowing formalism, evidence models, storage, authority, or implementation to redefine Knowledge?

## 2. Revised proposed decision

> **SymK shall govern Knowledge Engineering as a foundational concept. Knowledge Engineering is the disciplined design, construction, governance, evaluation, and evolution of epistemic conditions: arrangements of participants, processes, roles, resources, representations, controls, environments, and feedback that materially affect how Knowledge is pursued, achieved, attributed, expressed, preserved, communicated, assessed, challenged, applied, and revised within declared Contexts and Scopes.**
>
> **Knowledge Engineering may enable, constrain, preserve, expose, or impair epistemic achievement, but it does not manufacture truth, create actual bearing by declaration, or transform a claim, Document, model, graph, database, semantic package, assessment, approval, or operational record into Knowledge merely through storage, structure, inference, authority, adoption, or naming. Epistemic Condition shall be governed as a supporting distinction naming the discipline’s direct engineered object, not as a container, universal Entity parent, or guarantee of epistemic success.**
>
> **SymK shall govern the epistemic lifecycle as a recursive model rather than a linear promotion pipeline. Purpose and question formation, participant and boundary identification, inquiry, encounter, acquisition, formation, expression, preservation, transformation, assessment, challenge, reliance authorization, application, consequence observation, revision, supersession, withdrawal, restriction, retirement, Education, and institutional memory may interact through typed, scoped, temporal, and challengeable relationships. A lifecycle transition changes an activity, Representation, assessment, authorization, or operational condition; it does not by itself create truth or actual Knowledge.**
>
> **The lifecycle shall preserve propositional, practical, and familiarity Knowledge without forcing one mode’s representation or validation process onto the others. Propositional Knowledge requires truth and an appropriate non-accidental epistemic connection; practical Knowledge requires competence across a relevant task class; familiarity requires direct or accumulated epistemically significant encounter. Claims, procedure Documents, demonstrations, performance records, and encounter histories may support attribution without containing the achievement attributed to the Bearer.**
>
> **SymK shall distinguish Knowledge, Knowledge semantics, Knowledge attribution, Knowledge claim, Ground, Evidence, Provenance, Epistemic Assessment, uncertainty, disagreement, governance status, Reliance Authorization, Knowledge asset, Epistemic record, Document, semantic package, runtime instance, and platform Projection. `KnowledgeObject` shall not be admitted as a foundational concept or subtype of Knowledge. It may be reconsidered only as a qualified engineering-level umbrella whose use creates no epistemic entailment and whose constituent roles remain explicit.**
>
> **Ground shall identify a reason, process, competence, experience, encounter, or relation materially relevant to a claim or epistemic achievement. Evidence shall identify the contextual role something plays in supporting, challenging, or assessing a specified object under declared criteria. Provenance shall preserve origin, participation, custody, derivation, transformation, and responsibility. Ground, Evidence, and Provenance may contribute to epistemic support, but none alone entails truth, Knowledge, warrant, applicability, or authorization for reliance.**
>
> **Epistemic Assessment, governance status, Reliance Authorization, applicability, lifecycle status, challenge state, representation integrity, and normative or legal authority shall remain separate. Truth shall not be treated as an authority-assigned workflow status. Reliance may be authorized proportionately under uncertainty without claiming Knowledge, and a well-supported claim may remain unauthorized for a particular use because of Scope, consequence, privacy, professional responsibility, or external authority.**
>
> **Representation shall retain the DR-010 foundational meaning: a situated form through which selected aspects of a subject are made available for interpretation, communication, reasoning, storage, or action. Projection shall retain its governed supporting meaning: the relationship through which selected dimensions of a source are expressed in another Representation with transformations, additions, omissions, strengthening, weakening, and known semantic loss declared. A reduced operational Projection may refer to a fuller governed record; it may not present itself as complete when material meaning has been lost.**
>
> **SymK shall govern three coordinated semantic views: Human, Scientific, and Engineering. These views communicate, investigate, and realize selected aspects of the same governed subject. They do not constitute three independent bodies of Knowledge and do not acquire semantic authority merely through their form, institutional location, technical precision, empirical status, or operational use.**
>
> **The Human View shall preserve purpose, meaning, definitions, distinctions, explanation, Context, Scope, examples, counterexamples, interpretive alternatives, uncertainty, dissent, affected parties, consequences, and known limits. The Scientific View shall preserve questions, hypotheses, observations, measurements, methods, evidence, counterevidence, explanatory or predictive models, uncertainty, competing accounts, robustness, reproducibility, empirical adequacy, limitations, and conditions of defeat. The Engineering View shall preserve specifications, constraints, interfaces, contracts, formal profiles, algorithms, architectures, validation rules, test fixtures, runtime records, deployment conditions, observability, failure handling, migration, and operational Projections.**
>
> **Human readability, formal expression, and machine inspectability are cross-view representational capabilities. A Human, Scientific, or Engineering Representation may use natural language, diagrams, mathematics, logic, structured data, schemas, code, or executable artifacts. Format and machine processability do not determine the view. Every material formal or machine-inspectable Representation shall identify which view’s claims it expresses and the Scope and authority of that expression.**
>
> **Material alignment shall examine Human–Scientific, Scientific–Engineering, and Human–Engineering correspondence. Pairwise agreement does not establish complete alignment when the third view materially diverges. Neither a clear human explanation, a scientifically adequate model, nor a correct implementation can compensate automatically for material failure in another view.**
>
> **Semantic authority belongs to the governed subject record and its decision lineage. Evidence discovered through any view may challenge another view or the governed subject but does not amend it automatically. When views materially disagree, no view silently rewrites another; the mismatch, authority boundary, affected use, and correction lineage remain explicit.**
>
> **SymK shall require only the minimum Ontology and Logic capabilities necessary to preserve concept and term, subject and Representation, Identity and identifier, actual bearing and attribution, process and artifact, Context, Scope, Domain, Applicability, typed relationships, provenance, assessment, authority, time, contradiction, absence, uncertainty, revision, and non-entailments. SymK 2.1 selects no universal upper ontology, formal Logic, serialization, schema, inference regime, or lowering architecture.**
>
> **The three semantic views remain distinct from the three operating lanes established by the migration program: the semantic and constitutional lane, corpus-integrity lane, and grounding and verification lane. Views organize meaning, inquiry, and realization; program lanes organize work, evidence flow, migration, and verification. Neither structure substitutes for specialization layers, maturity states, stages, concept roles, or execution environments.**
>
> **Derived projects and relevant external authorities shall own Domain-specific concepts, epistemic criteria, evidence standards, professional roles, status vocabularies, uncertainty measures, reliance thresholds, Ontologies, Logics, and operational Projections while preserving the universal SymK minimum. Runtime and repository structures instantiate or project governed semantics; their operational centrality does not grant them semantic authority.**

## 4. Relationship to DR-009

DR-009 remains authoritative for:

- jurisdiction-first, purpose-led concept governance;
- separation of foundationality, maturity, and artifact form;
- the human-semantic interpretive nucleus;
- machine-inspectable expression of declared formal claims;
- projection limits;
- mismatch visibility;
- and semantic authority residing in the governed subject and lineage.

This decision proposes a material clarification:

> DR-009 established the minimum distinction between human-semantic interpretation and machine-inspectable expression. DR-012 distinguishes Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views and treats human readability, formal expression, and machine inspectability as cross-view capabilities rather than as an exhaustive two-view architecture.

Acceptance of DR-012 would not reopen DR-009’s concept-governance method. It would supersede only the interpretation that exactly two semantic lanes exhaust the available views.

## 6. Epistemic-condition admission rules

A candidate Epistemic Condition must identify:

1. the epistemic subject, activity, or relationship affected;
2. the relevant Bearer or proposed Bearer;
3. the typed enabling, constraining, evidentiary, preservational, impairing, or exposing effect;
4. participants, processes, roles, resources, environments, or controls involved;
5. Domain, Context, Scope, Applicability, and time;
6. evidence and uncertainty supporting the attribution;
7. failure, challenge, and revision conditions;
8. relevant alternatives and dependencies;
9. material consequences and affected parties where applicable;
10. and the authority governing any required response.

Not every cause of belief, system feature, or environmental fact is thereby a governed Epistemic Condition.

## 7. Lifecycle rules

1. **Recursive rather than linear:** challenge, consequence, and new inquiry may enter at any phase.
2. **Multiple entry points:** Knowledge may precede expression; practical formation may precede explicit claims; familiarity may arise without a discrete proposition.
3. **No automatic promotion:** no sequence of workflow states entails truth or Knowledge.
4. **Mode plurality:** propositional, practical, and familiarity paths retain distinct evidence and validation requirements.
5. **Transformation lineage:** translation, summarization, formalization, aggregation, inference, and generation create traceable derivations.
6. **Qualified assessment:** every assessment identifies its object, criteria, assessor, method, Evidence, Scope, Context, time, uncertainty, and challenge path.
7. **Scoped reliance:** every material reliance decision identifies authorized use, authority, safeguards, consequence, review, and expiry conditions.
8. **Application inside the lifecycle:** use, non-use, bypass, misuse, and operational deviation are epistemically relevant.
9. **Consequential feedback:** observed effects become new contributions requiring interpretation and may challenge any earlier layer or view.
10. **Historical preservation:** revision, supersession, withdrawal, restriction, and retirement do not rewrite earlier states or uses.
11. **Formation:** interfaces, incentives, defaults, practices, and institutions may educate or train participants whether or not they declare that purpose.
12. **Institutional memory:** artifact retention does not guarantee continued capacity to interpret, challenge, or apply what was preserved.

## 8. Grounds, evidence, assessment, and status rules

1. Something is Evidence only relative to an inquiry, claim, attribution, assessment, or challenge.
2. The same item may support one claim, challenge another, and remain irrelevant to a third.
3. Grounds may include non-artifact relations such as competence, practice, experience, or encounter.
4. Provenance establishes lineage, not truth.
5. Formal validity establishes consequence from premises under a formalism, not premise truth or responsible applicability.
6. Scientific adequacy remains method-, model-, evidence-, and Scope-dependent.
7. Consensus and confidence are Evidence requiring interpretation, not epistemic conclusions by themselves.
8. Disagreement is preserved and typed before resolution.
9. Absence does not universally entail false, unknown, uncollected, unavailable, inapplicable, rejected, or prohibited.
10. A material Epistemic Assessment remains qualified by criteria, assessor, Scope, Context, authority, and time.
11. Truth shall not share one field or identifier with governance status.
12. Reliance under uncertainty must preserve risk, consequence, reversibility, safeguards, alternatives, and review conditions.
13. Earlier authority, reliance, and consequence remain historical facts after an assertion is defeated or retired.

## 9. Three-view rules

### 9.1 Human View

The Human View shall preserve, where material:

- purpose and audience;
- ordinary and specialized meaning;
- definitions and distinctions;
- rationale and interpretation;
- examples and counterexamples;
- Context and Scope;
- uncertainty, disagreement, and dissent;
- affected parties;
- consequences;
- and known limits.

Human intelligibility is necessary but does not confer truth or authority by itself.

### 9.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View shall preserve, where material:

- inquiry question or hypothesis;
- studied subject and population;
- method and instrument;
- observation and measurement;
- dataset, sampling, and exclusion conditions;
- Evidence and counterevidence;
- model assumptions;
- competing explanations;
- uncertainty;
- robustness and reproducibility;
- empirical or evidential adequacy;
- limitations;
- and conditions under which the result may be defeated.

“Scientific” does not mean that every question is reducible to experimental science. Philosophical, legal, historical, qualitative, professional, and other Domain-appropriate inquiry methods retain their own validity conditions and authorities.

### 9.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View shall preserve, where material:

- governing semantic source;
- requirements and constraints;
- architecture and interfaces;
- reference and computational models;
- formal profiles and schemas;
- algorithms and inference regimes;
- test and conformance rules;
- implementation and deployment assumptions;
- runtime assertions and operational records;
- observability and failure handling;
- migration and compatibility;
- security and authorization;
- and operational limitations and consequences.

Technical correctness does not establish semantic fidelity, scientific adequacy, or authorization for use.

## 10. Cross-view alignment rules

Every material cross-view Projection shall declare:

1. source and target views;
2. source and target artifact identities and versions;
3. governing concept or package version;
4. purpose, audience, and owning layer;
5. source claims or dimensions it intends to express;
6. preserved identities, distinctions, relationships, and constraints;
7. translation, operationalization, abstraction, aggregation, decomposition, and other transformations;
8. additions, enrichment, or inferred content;
9. strengthened or weakened constraints;
10. omissions and inexpressible dimensions;
11. known semantic or evidential loss;
12. uncertainty about the mapping;
13. affected reasoning, applicability, or reliance;
14. alignment or verification evidence;
15. route to the fuller source;
16. and authority status.

A reduced Projection need not duplicate the complete governed record. It must not conceal that it is reduced.

## 11. Triangular coherence rules

Material alignment shall test:

| Alignment | Required question |
|---|---|
| Human ↔ Scientific | Does the inquiry or model investigate the intended meaning and problem? |
| Scientific ↔ Engineering | Does the realization preserve the tested model, evidence conditions, and limitations? |
| Human ↔ Engineering | Does the operational system preserve intended meaning, usability, and responsibility boundaries? |
| Human ↔ Scientific ↔ Engineering | Are meaning, evidence, and realization jointly coherent within the declared Scope? |

Specific prohibitions follow:

- human clarity does not repair invalid evidence;
- scientific adequacy does not repair implementation distortion;
- engineering correctness does not repair semantic misunderstanding;
- agreement between two views does not erase material disagreement with the third;
- and no view acquires amendment authority merely by exposing a defect.

## 12. Machine-inspectability rule

Machine inspectability is a cross-view capability.

A machine-inspectable artifact shall identify whether it projects:

- Human meaning or interpretation;
- Scientific evidence, method, model, or result;
- Engineering specification, constraint, implementation, or operational state;
- or a declared combination.

The generic phrase `machine-semantic lane` shall not be used as though all machine-processable artifacts share one epistemic role.

## 13. Minimum Ontology and Logic rules

Future formal profiles must be capable of preserving or explicitly addressing:

- stable Identity and version;
- concept, term, instance, subject, and Representation;
- view identity;
- typed relations rather than generic connectivity;
- Context, Scope, Domain, Applicability, authority, and time;
- actual bearing, attribution, assessment, authorization, and record;
- claim, Ground, Evidence, Provenance, uncertainty, disagreement, and defeaters;
- explicit non-entailments;
- open-world, closed-world, explicit-negation, default-negation, or local-closure assumptions;
- contradiction without forced consensus or uncontrolled inference;
- temporal validity, supersession, and retirement;
- defeasible and non-monotonic revision where applicable;
- asserted, assessed, permitted, required, authoritative, and relied-upon modalities;
- inferential premises, rules, methods, and lineage;
- and declared expressive limits.

No single profile must express every SymK dimension. Its omissions and authority boundary must be explicit.

## 14. Program-lane separation

The migration program’s operating lanes remain:

1. **Semantic and constitutional lane** — governs meaning, disposition, decision, and authority.
2. **Corpus-integrity lane** — preserves sources, versions, Provenance, lineage, recovery, migration, and publication.
3. **Grounding and verification lane** — develops counterexamples, project trials, operational evidence, cross-project validation, and conformance.

The views and lanes do not map one-to-one.

A corpus artifact may express any view. Grounding may produce Human, Scientific, or Engineering evidence. The semantic lane governs meaning but cannot fabricate scientific support. Engineering success supplies evidence but not automatic universal authority.

## 15. Alternatives considered

### A — Treat Knowledge itself as the engineered product

**Rejected:** governance and implementation would appear capable of manufacturing truth and actual bearing.

### B — Use only the conventional artifact-centered Knowledge Engineering definition

**Rejected as insufficient:** acquisition, representation, reasoning, and validation do not adequately govern authority, consequence, formation, disagreement, practical Knowledge, or familiarity.

### C — Make `KnowledgeObject` the universal epistemic parent

**Rejected:** it conflates achievement, claim, asset, record, Document, semantic declaration, and operational resource.

### D — Retain Human and machine-semantic lanes as the exhaustive architecture

**Rejected:** this collapses scientific/evidential inquiry and engineering realization merely because both may be machine-processable.

### E — Treat Human, Scientific, and Engineering as three independent bodies of Knowledge

**Rejected:** each is a view or family of Representations of governed subjects, not an independent epistemic universe.

### F — Make the Scientific View universally controlling

**Rejected:** empirical and formal inquiry cannot acquire semantic, normative, legal, or constitutional authority merely by being called scientific.

### G — Treat a repository or knowledge graph as semantic authority

**Rejected:** operational centrality and structural richness do not confer truth, warrant, completeness, or conceptual ownership.

### H — Use one status, confidence, or validity field

**Rejected:** assessment, governance, applicability, reliance, lifecycle, authority, integrity, and uncertainty answer different questions.

### I — Select a formal Ontology or Logic first

**Rejected:** the formalism would preselect expressible meaning and risk defining Knowledge through representational convenience.

### J — Govern epistemic conditions, three views, cross-view alignment, lifecycle, typed artifacts, status separation, and declared loss

**Revised Proposed:** preserves the accepted Knowledge boundary, restores the missing Scientific View, supports engineering, and retains proper Domain and implementation ownership.

## 17. Consequences if accepted

### Product Vision

The governed Knowledge medium becomes operationally intelligible without becoming a stored substance. Human meaning, scientific inquiry, and engineering realization remain connected while Knowledge, cooperation, capacity, reasoning, answer, evaluation, action, consequence, and learning remain distinct.

### DR-009 lineage

DR-009’s human/machine minimum is retained. Its interpretation as an exhaustive two-view architecture is superseded by the Human–Scientific–Engineering clarification.

### Candidate Constitution

- A1 should preserve truth, Knowledge, Ground, Evidence, assessment, authority, status, and reliance as distinct.
- A4 should use the complete Epistemic Condition boundary and avoid implying guaranteed achievement.
- Detailed authority and responsibility remain coordinated with 2.1.6.

No axiom is Ratified or revised by DR-012.

### Foundation Papers

Future v0.2 papers must incorporate:

- the artifact and status boundaries;
- recursive lifecycle;
- plural Knowledge modes;
- Ground/Evidence/Provenance distinctions;
- typed uncertainty and disagreement;
- Reliance Authorization separation;
- Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views;
- cross-view alignment;
- triangular coherence;
- and declared loss.

### Later 2.1 work

- 2.1.5 owns Cooperation, Education, Training, and formation.
- 2.1.6 owns agency, authority, authorization, responsibility, and accountability.
- 2.1.7 owns Amplified Intelligence and DIA integration.
- 2.1.8 owns reasoning, answer quality, value, cost, and feedback.

Neither may silently reverse the semantics accepted here.

### Derived projects

Projects inherit the universal distinctions and own Domain-specific evidence rules, methods, standards, Ontologies, Logics, professional authorities, thresholds, vocabularies, and operational Projections.

No current project or implementation is accepted or modified through DR-012.

## 18. Non-effects of acceptance

- Ratify a constitutional norm;
- complete SymK 2.1;
- establish a complete theory of Knowledge, truth, warrant, Evidence, or belief;
- decide every possible Knowledge Bearer;
- settle practical Knowledge/Intelligence overlap;
- make Knowledge storable or transferable as an artifact;
- accept `KnowledgeObject`;
- accept a final status or uncertainty vocabulary;
- establish universal evidence thresholds;
- require laboratory-scientific methods for every Domain;
- make the Scientific View the owner of semantic, legal, normative, or constitutional authority;
- require every low-consequence artifact to materialize all three views fully;
- authorize reliance in a particular Domain or deployment;
- establish detailed agency, authority, or responsibility rules;
- create or migrate a Knowledge semantic package;
- decide whether Knowledge and epistemic-artifact semantics use one package or several;
- accept current property or Document scaffolds;
- revise a Foundation Paper or candidate axiom;
- select an Ontology, Logic, schema, serialization, database, API, programming language, or platform;
- modify any derived system;
- grant production records semantic authority;
- merge the semantic views with the program lanes;
- or reopen the substantive dispositions of DR-009 through DR-011.

## 19. Preserved dissent and open questions

1. `EpistemicCondition` may be too broad to deserve an independently named distinction.
2. Knowledge Engineering may be a foundational discipline or method rather than a foundational concept.
3. The term “Knowledge Engineering” may remain misleading.
4. Ground and Evidence may ultimately be roles within one support family.
5. Warrant may remain too theory-dependent for universal formalization.
6. Knowledge claim may require separate content, assertion-act, commitment, and attributed-proposition treatment.
7. Knowledge asset may be better modeled as a role, graph, collection, or lifecycle boundary.
8. A qualified engineering-level `KnowledgeObject` may retain interoperability value.
9. Status and uncertainty vocabularies may require stronger Domain ownership.
10. Practical and familiarity Knowledge may require additional primitives.
11. Some artificial, institutional, collective, or hybrid systems may literally bear Knowledge.
12. Semantic packages may act as Knowledge assets without containing Knowledge.
13. Assessment/Reliance Authorization boundaries partly depend on 2.1.6.
14. Minimum machine-inspectable obligations may need consequence-proportional profiles.
15. “Scientific View” may be too narrow for philosophical, historical, legal, or qualitative epistemic inquiry.
16. The eventual name may be `Scientific/Evidential View` or `Epistemic Inquiry View`.
17. Some formal semantic models may bridge Scientific and Engineering Views.
18. Some packages may legitimately mark a view as not materially applicable.
19. Triangular alignment may need more than three views in future Domains.
20. No formalism has demonstrated complete preservation of every required distinction.
