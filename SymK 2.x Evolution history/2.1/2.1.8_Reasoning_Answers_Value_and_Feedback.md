## Question

> What must the elements of the accepted SymK value chain mean so that an output, a fluent statement, or a fast and inexpensive process cannot be mistaken automatically for a better answer?

## Scope

Examine complex question, inquiry, reasoning, inference, judgment, answer, recommendation, decision, action, domain outcome, consequence, feedback, learning, quality, speed, and total cost.

## Required distinctions

- question versus prompt or task instruction;
- reasoning versus computation, retrieval, explanation, or presentation;
- answer versus output, recommendation, decision, or action;
- quality versus fluency, confidence, acceptance, or authority;
- response latency versus time to responsible answer;
- direct price versus total cost, transferred cost, risk, and later correction;
- domain outcome versus epistemic quality;
- feedback versus evidence and learning versus mere parameter or behavior change.

## Constrained-value rule to test

“Better,” “faster,” and “lower total cost” are joint, bounded dimensions. None independently overrides responsibility, uncertainty, prevention of harm, affected parties, or domain authority.

- conceptual meanings and prohibited optimizations for quality, speed, and total cost;
- consequence and learning loop;
- conceptual portion of DQ-014;
- metrics and thresholds explicitly routed to 2.6–2.7;
- Foundation Paper revision-ledger entries;

## Out of scope
