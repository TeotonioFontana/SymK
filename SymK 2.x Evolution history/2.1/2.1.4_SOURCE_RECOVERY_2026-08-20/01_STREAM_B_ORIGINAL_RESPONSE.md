## 2.1.4B — Epistemic-condition and lifecycle model

### Central result

> The SymK epistemic lifecycle is a governed, recursive arrangement of epistemic activities, artifacts, assessments, decisions, applications, consequences, and formative changes. It can improve or impair the conditions for Knowledge, but its transitions do not manufacture truth or actual Knowledge.

The inherited eleven-step lifecycle is directionally sound, but it should not become a single linear workflow. SymK needs a lifecycle graph with multiple entry points, feedback loops, parallel claims, and mode-specific paths.

### The lifecycle layers

SymK should keep six layers distinct:

1. **Epistemic reality**

   Actual Knowledge, truth-connected relations, competence, familiarity, and actual bearing. These are not created by database transitions.

2. **Epistemic activity**

   Inquiry, encounter, observation, practice, reasoning, interpretation, learning, testing, and challenge.

3. **Epistemic contribution**

   Claims, demonstrations, testimony, measurements, models, procedures, examples, questions, counterexamples, and generated proposals.

4. **Representation and record**

   Documents, graphs, structured assertions, assessments, provenance records, status records, and platform projections.

5. **Governance and reliance**

   Review, acceptance, authority, authorization, restriction, application decisions, and retirement.

6. **Action, consequence, and formation**

   Use changes the environment; consequences generate new evidence; repeated participation changes the capabilities and habits of participants and cooperative systems.

No transition between these layers implies automatic equivalence.

## Lifecycle phases

### 1. Purpose and question formation

An epistemic episode begins with a purpose, problem, uncertainty, or encounter—not necessarily with an existing claim.

It must identify:

- what is being pursued;
- for whom and with what consequences;
- the relevant Domain, Context, and Scope;
- what would count as success or failure;
- and which questions remain outside the episode.

A malformed question can corrupt everything downstream even when later processing is technically correct.

### 2. Boundary and participant identification

Identify:

- proposed Bearers;
- sources and subjects;
- contributors and affected parties;
- assessors and authorities;
- relevant systems and environments;
- dependencies, exclusions, and stable boundaries.

A source, contributor, Bearer, assessor, authority, and affected party may be the same participant in a particular case, but these are different roles.

### 3. Inquiry, encounter, acquisition, and formation

This phase gathers or develops epistemically relevant material through:

- observation;
- testimony;
- document examination;
- measurement;
- data collection;
- elicitation;
- reasoning;
- practice and Training;
- direct or accumulated encounter;
- Education;
- or cooperative investigation.

“Acquisition” must not imply extraction of a transferable substance. It may construct a representation, develop a competence, establish familiarity, or generate a candidate claim.

### 4. Contribution and expression

A participant may express:

- a question;
- claim;
- hypothesis;
- observation;
- procedure;
- demonstration;
- model;
- interpretation;
- objection;
- counterexample;
- or recommendation.

Expression creates a contribution or Representation. It does not prove that the contributor knows, that the contribution is true, or that another participant acquires Knowledge by receiving it.

### 5. Preservation and transformation

Contributions may be recorded, translated, summarized, classified, formalized, aggregated, or projected.

Every material transformation must preserve:

- source identity;
- derivation;
- responsible participant or process;
- applicable Context and Scope;
- relevant uncertainty and disagreement;
- version;
- declared additions, omissions, and transformations;
- and a route to the fuller source where loss occurred.

Preservation does not mean semantic identity. A stored artifact may survive while its meaning, applicability, or supporting conditions decay.

### 6. Assessment, validation, and challenge

Assessment applies declared criteria to a specified object:

- structural validation evaluates form;
- logical validation evaluates inference under stated premises;
- empirical validation evaluates correspondence with observations;
- competence evaluation tests performance across a task class;
- familiarity assessment examines the relevant history of encounter;
- domain review evaluates specialized meaning;
- consequential evaluation examines actual effects;
- governance review evaluates compliance and authority.

Challenge may occur before, during, or after any other phase. It is not a terminal exception.

An assessment produces an assessment—not truth or actual bearing.

### 7. Reliance decision and authorization

A claim or asset may be:

- permitted for exploratory use;
- accepted provisionally;
- restricted to a Context or population;
- authorized for operational reliance;
- prohibited;
- or required to receive further review.

Reliance must be proportionate to consequence, uncertainty, reversibility, novelty, affected parties, and available alternatives.

Authorization to rely on uncertain content may be rational and necessary. It must not relabel that content as Knowledge.

### 8. Application and action

An authorized contribution may inform:

- interpretation;
- a decision;
- a professional judgment;
- system behavior;
- instruction;
- classification;
- intervention;
- or further inquiry.

The application record must preserve what was used, its version, applicable restrictions, responsible roles, and deviations from the authorized conditions.

### 9. Consequence observation

Application changes the environment from which later evidence arises.

Relevant consequences include:

- intended and unintended effects;
- success and failure;
- harm and benefit;
- participant adaptation;
- changes in incentives or behavior;
- non-use, bypass, or misuse;
- newly exposed uncertainty;
- and effects on affected parties.

Observed consequences are new contributions requiring interpretation. They do not automatically validate or refute the original claim.

### 10. Revision, supersession, withdrawal, and retirement

These transitions must remain distinct:

- **Revision:** a governed successor changes a contribution or asset.
- **Supersession:** a successor becomes preferred or controlling for a declared use.
- **Withdrawal:** an authority or contributor removes current endorsement.
- **Restriction:** applicability or permitted reliance narrows.
- **Retirement:** an artifact or model leaves active use.
- **Invalidation:** an assessment concludes that specified criteria are no longer met.
- **Deletion:** physical removal, governed separately from epistemic and historical status.

None should rewrite history. Earlier assertions, assessments, uses, and consequences remain historically true events even after the asset is defeated or retired.

### 11. Education and institutional memory

Education is not merely the lifecycle’s last step. It is a cross-cutting feedback process.

The lifecycle changes:

- what participants know;
- what they can do;
- what they attend to;
- how they disclose uncertainty;
- whether they challenge authority;
- how institutions remember failure;
- and which habits interfaces and incentives reinforce.

A repository can retain records while the institution loses the capacity to interpret them. Conversely, a community may preserve practical Knowledge despite incomplete records. Artifact preservation and institutional memory are therefore related but non-identical.

## Knowledge-mode paths

### Propositional Knowledge

A typical path is:

`question → claim → grounds → assessment/challenge → possible Knowledge attribution → reliance decision → application → consequence`

Truth and an appropriate non-accidental epistemic connection remain necessary. Acceptance, authority, provenance, or successful application alone do not supply them.

### Practical Knowledge

A typical path is:

`task exposure → instruction/demonstration → practice → feedback → performance across variation → competence assessment → continued application and adaptation`

The Bearer’s competence is central. A procedure, demonstration, or training record may support and evidence practical Knowledge but does not contain it.

### Familiarity Knowledge

A typical path is:

`encounter → repeated or significant engagement → discrimination and orientation → contextual responsiveness → attribution`

A profile, description, simulation, or copied record does not reproduce the direct or accumulated encounter.

These paths may interact, but SymK must not force all three into a claim-centered lifecycle.

## Mandatory lifecycle invariants

1. **No status manufactures truth.**
2. **No record manufactures actual bearing.**
3. **No Representation is identical to its subject.**
4. **Every material attribution identifies its proposed Bearer, mode, Scope, Context, time, and criteria.**
5. **Assessment, governance status, reliance authorization, availability, and truth remain distinct.**
6. **Transformation creates lineage; it does not silently replace its source.**
7. **Contradictory contributions may coexist without forced consensus.**
8. **Challenge can enter at every phase.**
9. **Application and consequence remain inside the epistemic lifecycle.**
10. **Supersession and retirement preserve historical state.**
11. **Practical and familiarity Knowledge cannot be reduced to stored claims.**
12. **Human, artificial, collective, institutional, and hybrid participants receive roles from evidence—not origin alone.**
13. **A lifecycle may support Knowledge without proving that Knowledge resulted.**
14. **Every lifecycle transition remains scoped, typed, attributable, and temporally qualified.**

## Status families to preserve

Stream B confirms that a single `status` field would be conceptually destructive. At minimum, later work must separate:

- epistemic assessment;
- governance/workflow status;
- reliance authorization;
- applicability;
- lifecycle/availability status;
- representation-integrity status;
- and challenge/disagreement state.

Truth is not just another authority-assigned lifecycle status.

The exact vocabularies and transition rules belong partly to Stream C and partly to Domain authorities.

## Counterexample result

The model handles the required cases:

- A true claim accepted for a bad reason may be operationally accepted but lacks an adequate Knowledge attribution.
- A false claim can retain impeccable provenance and formal validity without becoming true.
- Practical competence may develop without a complete procedure document.
- Familiarity cannot be transferred by copying a profile.
- Conflicting claims may remain together in a governed asset.
- Emergency reliance can be authorized without epistemic promotion.
- An authoritative assertion can later be defeated while its earlier authority and use remain historical facts.
- A generated answer enters as a contribution, not as Knowledge.
- Valid inference from false premises remains logically valid but epistemically insufficient.
- Retirement changes active-use status without erasing lineage.
- One Document can change among evidentiary, instructional, historical, and operational roles.
- Different platform representations remain projections of governed records, not independent semantic owners.

## Stream B gate

The result passes for continuation because it:

- preserves DR-009’s concept-governance method;
- uses DR-010’s structural kernel;
- preserves DR-011’s Knowledge modes and bearing/attribution boundary;
- accommodates propositional, practical, and familiarity Knowledge;
- keeps package and formal-language decisions deferred;
- treats application and consequence as epistemically relevant;
- and does not grant semantic authority to Documents, repositories, schemas, or platforms.

It does not authorize DR-012.

The next step is **Stream C: grounds, evidence, provenance, uncertainty, disagreement, epistemic assessment, governance status, and reliance authorization**.
