## 2.1.4E — Consolidated stress test and impact analysis

### Consolidated working position

> **Knowledge Engineering is the disciplined design, construction, governance, evaluation, and evolution of epistemic conditions: arrangements of participants, processes, roles, resources, representations, controls, environments, and feedback that materially affect how Knowledge is pursued, achieved, attributed, expressed, preserved, communicated, assessed, challenged, applied, and revised within declared Contexts and Scopes.**

Knowledge Engineering can materially enable, constrain, preserve, or impair Knowledge. It does not manufacture truth, create actual bearing by declaration, or convert an artifact into Knowledge through storage, formalization, approval, or naming.

## Consolidated conceptual structure

```text
Reality, practice, and encountered subjects
        ↓ inquiry, experience, observation, competent action
Actual epistemic achievements and proposed attributions
        ↓ expression, testimony, demonstration, abstraction
Claims, grounds, evidence roles, assessments, and challenges
        ↓ modeling, preservation, translation, projection
Documents, assets, semantic declarations, and records
        ↓ governance, authorization, interpretation, application
Decisions and actions
        ↓
Consequences, feedback, formation, revision, and retirement
```

Every arrow is typed, scoped, challengeable, and potentially lossy. No arrow is an automatic epistemic promotion.

## Mandatory counterexample results

| Case | Result | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| 1. True claim accepted for the wrong reason | Truth and acceptance can coexist while the defective ground prevents a responsible Knowledge attribution | Pass |
| 2. False claim with impeccable provenance and schema validity | Provenance and conformance remain useful; neither entails truth or warrant | Pass |
| 3. Rich graph or Document labeled `KnowledgeObject` | Classified as Representation, asset, record, or resource according to role; the label creates no Knowledge | Pass |
| 4. Practical competence not recoverable from its procedure | Competence belongs to the Bearer; procedures and performance records support but do not contain it | Pass |
| 5. Incompatible claims retained in one asset | Both remain recoverable with distinct grounds, scopes, assessments, and challenges | Pass |
| 6. Authoritative assertion later defeated | Earlier authority, reliance, and consequences remain historical; current assessment and authorization may change | Pass |
| 7. Correct generated answer without adequate grounding | Correct output is retained as a contribution but does not establish epistemic sufficiency | Pass |
| 8. Valid inference from false or inapplicable premises | Logical validity is preserved separately from premise truth and applicability | Pass |
| 9. Emergency reliance on an uncertain claim | Reliance may be authorized proportionately without upgrading uncertainty into Knowledge | Pass |
| 10. Same claim in Markdown, JSON, database, and SharePoint | Representations share governed identity and projection lineage without becoming four independent claims | Pass |

| 12. Legal and medical document types share a relation | Universal classification/property relations may be shared; value schemes and criteria remain Domain-owned | Pass |
| 13. One Document changes epistemic roles | Evidentiary, instructional, historical, and operational roles remain contextual and temporal—not intrinsic permanent types | Pass |
| 14. Asset is superseded or retired | Current availability changes while prior identity, assessments, uses, and consequences remain preserved | Pass |

No mandatory case requires a foundational `KnowledgeObject`, a single status field, a universal ontology, or one formal representation.

- epistemic grounds sufficient for attribution;

- and authorization for practical reliance.

### Candidate A4 — Engineering Epistemic Conditions

**Pass, with clarification.**

A4 should also avoid implying that the engineered process guarantees the “achievement” named in its lifecycle verbs.

Neither observation grants A4 constitutional authority or Ratification.

## Downstream consequences

### Foundation Papers v0.2

The Knowledge and Knowledge Engineering papers must eventually:

- distinguish Knowledge from attribution, claim, asset, record, Representation, and reliance;
- replace any remaining container language;
- treat acquisition as situated construction;
- make the lifecycle recursive rather than simply sequential;
- distinguish Ground, Evidence, Provenance, assessment, and authorization;
- separate all material status planes;
- accommodate practical and familiarity Knowledge;
- add explicit Projection alignment and semantic-loss obligations;
- and preserve unresolved disagreement rather than presenting the working model as philosophical closure.

The Education and Intelligence papers require related terminology alignment. Revision remains assigned to 2.1.10.

### 2.1.5

Education and Training must be treated as formative epistemic conditions and lifecycle feedback without being absorbed into Knowledge Engineering.

### 2.1.6

Detailed treatment of agency, authority, responsibility, authorization, accountability, and distributed consequences must preserve the authority separations established here.

### 2.1.8

Reasoning and answer quality must distinguish:

- logical validity;
- epistemic adequacy;
- applicability;
- authorization;
- usefulness;
- consequence;
- and value.

### 2.5

Semantic packaging must provide governed expression for Knowledge and the epistemic-artifact relationships established here. It must decide package splits, identifiers, files, dependency structure, and alignment contracts only after the integrated 2.1 baseline is accepted.

### 2.9

Engineering readiness must select formal profiles, validation mechanisms, runtime record structures, projection rules, and lowering mechanisms without allowing them to redefine the accepted semantics.

## Preserved dissent

1. `EpistemicCondition` may remain too broad for a separately named governed distinction.
2. Knowledge Engineering may be better classified as a discipline or method than as a foundational concept.
3. “Knowledge Engineering” may continue to invite objectification despite its disciplined interpretation.
4. `Ground` and `Evidence` may be better represented as roles in one broader support family.
5. “Warrant” may be irreducibly theory-dependent.
6. `KnowledgeClaim` may require separate content, assertion act, commitment, and attributed proposition.
7. `KnowledgeAsset` may be a role, governed boundary, or graph rather than an object class.
8. A generic `KnowledgeObject` may retain interoperability value at an engineering level.
9. Status and uncertainty vocabularies may require greater Domain specialization than currently expected.
10. Practical and familiarity Knowledge may require additional primitives.
11. Some artificial, institutional, collective, or hybrid systems may literally bear Knowledge.
12. Semantic packages may themselves act as Knowledge assets without containing Knowledge.
13. The boundary between epistemic assessment and reliance authority remains partly dependent on 2.1.6.
14. The minimum machine-semantic obligations may prove too expensive for low-consequence systems.
15. No formalism has yet demonstrated adequate treatment of every required distinction.

## Reopening conditions

The consolidated result must be reopened if:

- practical or familiarity Knowledge cannot be modeled without artifact reification;
- a valid use case demonstrates that actual Knowledge must literally be stored or transferred;
- the claim/asset/record separation produces irreducible identity contradictions;
- derived-project validation shows that the universal minimum forces Domain leakage;
- the status-plane model cannot support authoritative action under real uncertainty;
- a required formal profile cannot preserve the accepted non-entailments;
- projection-loss obligations prove incapable of protecting materially affected use;
- responsibility analysis in 2.1.6 contradicts the authority distinctions;
- or observed consequences show that the model creates false confidence, destructive bureaucracy, or concealed epistemic failure.
