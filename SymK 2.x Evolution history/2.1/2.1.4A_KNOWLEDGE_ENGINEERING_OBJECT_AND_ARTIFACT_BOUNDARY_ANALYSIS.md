# SymK 2.1.4A — Knowledge Engineering Object and Epistemic-Artifact Boundary Analysis

**Status:** Analytical working proposal — no decision authority
**Date:** 20 August 2026
**Working baseline:** `2.1-dev.19`
**Authority:** 2.1.4 opening instruction `SYMK-2X-EV-098` and control `SYMK-2X-EV-099`
**Decision target:** Future Proposed `SYMK-2X-DR-012`; not yet drafted
**Depends on:** Stage-Accepted DR-009 through DR-011

## 1. Question and analytical result

> If Knowledge is an actual epistemic achievement of a Bearer rather than a stored object, what does Knowledge Engineering engineer, and what are the semantic roles of claims, assets, records, Documents, and packages?

The first 2.1.4 stream supports the following working answer:

> **Knowledge Engineering directly engineers epistemic conditions: designed and governed arrangements of participants, processes, roles, resources, representations, controls, environments, and feedback that materially affect the pursuit, achievement, expression, preservation, assessment, application, challenge, or revision of Knowledge within declared Contexts and Scopes.**

It may causally contribute to Knowledge, but it does not manufacture truth, create actual bearing by declaration, or turn a Representation into Knowledge by storage or status.

The package question has a split answer:

- SymK needs a governed semantic expression of the Stage-Accepted Knowledge concept.
- SymK also needs semantics for epistemic artifacts and relationships used in Knowledge Engineering.
- Individual records are runtime instances governed by those semantics, not semantic packages.
- `KnowledgeObject` is too ambiguous to serve as the universal parent for these levels. It may survive only as a tightly qualified engineering convenience, if later analysis proves that useful.

These are analytical candidates for continuation of 2.1.4. They are not Stage-Accepted dispositions and do not create a package.

## 2. Accepted predecessor constraints

The analysis begins from, and does not redefine, these accepted commitments:

1. Knowledge has an actual Bearer.
2. The universal Knowledge concept is not an actual Knowledge instance.
3. Actual Knowledge, Knowledge attribution, assessment, authority for reliance, and record are distinct.
4. A claim may be false, defeated, unsupported, or not known by any Bearer.
5. A rich Representation, formal model, document, graph, repository, or approval record does not establish Knowledge by itself.
6. A Representation is not identical to its subject.
7. A Projection is a governed mapping whose additions, omissions, transformations, and loss must be visible.
8. Universal SymK meaning and Domain specialization have different owners.
9. Artifact form does not determine foundationality.

## 3. Why the direct object cannot be Knowledge

Calling Knowledge the engineered product produces four contradictions.

### 3.1 Truth is not an engineering status

A workflow may approve a false claim, and an unknown true claim may remain outside the workflow. Governance can assess, authorize, challenge, or preserve; it cannot make a proposition true by declaration.

### 3.2 Bearing is not created by a record

Writing `bearer_knows(subject, content)` records an attribution. It does not create the actual epistemic relation or achievement it reports.

### 3.3 Practical Knowledge is not reducible to an artifact

A procedure, video, model, or demonstration may support competent practice without containing the competence. Some knowing-how is realized only through a Bearer's organized capability in Context.

### 3.4 Familiarity is not transferable by copying a profile

A detailed Representation of a place, person, system, or practice does not reproduce the direct or accumulated relation that may constitute familiarity.

The engineered target must therefore be something that can be deliberately altered without pretending that epistemic success follows automatically.

## 4. Candidate concept: Epistemic Condition

### 4.1 Working definition

> An **Epistemic Condition** is a condition that materially enables, constrains, shapes, evidences, preserves, impairs, or exposes the pursuit, achievement, attribution, communication, assessment, application, challenge, or revision of Knowledge for specified subjects within a Context and Scope.

An epistemic condition may be:

- organizational — roles, separation of powers, review rights, incentives;
- procedural — inquiry, elicitation, validation, challenge, revision;
- representational — claims, models, vocabularies, provenance, uncertainty;
- technical — interfaces, retrieval, lineage, versioning, constraint checking;
- social or institutional — trust practices, authority, access, testimony;
- formative — Education, Training, practice, feedback;
- environmental — access to subjects, instruments, cases, or consequences.

### 4.2 Admission constraint

Not every cause of belief or every system feature is thereby an Epistemic Condition worth governing. A qualifying attribution must identify:

- the epistemic subject or activity affected;
- the relevant Bearer or proposed Bearer;
- the typed effect or mechanism;
- Context, Scope, Domain, and Applicability;
- supporting evidence and uncertainty;
- failure, challenge, and revision conditions; and
- material consequences where relevant.

### 4.3 Candidate disposition

`EpistemicCondition` is best treated provisionally as a **governed supporting distinction**, not as a container, universal Entity parent, or automatic standalone package. It explains the direct object of Knowledge Engineering while its individual kinds retain more specific meanings and owners.

## 5. The necessary levels

| Level | What it is | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge | Actual scoped epistemic achievement of a Bearer | File, graph, package, status, assertion, or authorization |
| Knowledge concept | Universal governed meaning and its modes, boundaries, bearer rules, and relations | An actual knower or stored achievement |
| Knowledge attribution | Fallible claim that a proposed Bearer knows within a mode and Scope | The actual bearing relation |
| Knowledge claim | Representable content presented with an epistemic stance | Necessarily true, warranted, accepted, or known |
| Ground | A reason, experience, competence, process, relation, or basis relevant to a claim or achievement | Automatically sufficient evidence |
| Evidence | A role played by something in a particular assessment, inquiry, or challenge | An intrinsic label guaranteeing support independently of Context |
| Provenance | Traceable history of origin, custody, derivation, transformation, or responsibility | Truth, warrant, or authority by itself |
| Epistemic assessment/status | Governed conclusion or state under a declared scheme and authority | Truth or Knowledge itself |
| Reliance authorization | Permission or decision to use a claim or asset for a purpose within Scope | Proof that the relied-upon content is known or true |
| Knowledge asset | Durable governed aggregate supporting preservation, evaluation, communication, and use of epistemic contributions | A metaphysical container of Knowledge |
| Epistemic record | Computational record of a claim, attribution, assessment, status, evidence relation, or lifecycle event | The subject or actual relation recorded |
| Document | Representation or resource that may express claims and play evidentiary, instructional, historical, or operational roles | A subtype of Knowledge merely because of content |
| Semantic package | Governed declaration of meanings, dependencies, constraints, examples, and alignment obligations | A collection of production instances or an operational metadata store |
| Runtime instance | Identified operational assertion or record conforming to declared semantics | The semantic authority that defines its meaning |
| Platform projection | Reduced representation for SharePoint, AWS, MySQL, a search index, an API, or another carrier | The complete semantic record unless equivalence is demonstrated |

## 6. The `KnowledgeObject` challenge

### 6.1 Why the term is attractive

Engineering systems need handles for things that can be identified, versioned, stored, linked, validated, challenged, and exchanged. `KnowledgeObject` appears to offer one convenient parent for Documents, claims, graphs, models, cases, rules, and packages.

### 6.2 Why the term is dangerous

The name suggests that Knowledge has been objectified and contained. It can collapse at least five distinct questions:

1. Is this an actual epistemic achievement?
2. Is it a Representation expressing a claim?
3. Is it a governed aggregate supporting epistemic work?
4. Is it a record of an attribution or assessment?
5. Is it merely a resource used by a knowledge system?

If one generic type answers all five, storage, inheritance, and status can silently redefine Knowledge.

### 6.3 Working disposition

`KnowledgeObject` is **not admitted as a foundational concept or as a subtype of Knowledge**. Later work may retain it only as an explicitly engineering-level umbrella with typed roles and a prohibition against epistemic entailment. The preferred vocabulary for current analysis is more precise:

- `KnowledgeClaim` for presented epistemic content;
- `KnowledgeAsset` for a durable governed aggregate;
- `EpistemicRecord` for a computational record;
- `Document` for a representational resource; and
- `KnowledgeAttribution` for the claim that a Bearer knows.

No positive case for the generic umbrella has yet survived the precision test.

## 7. Semantic-package consequence

### 7.1 A Knowledge semantic package is required in principle

Because DR-011 classifies Knowledge as foundational, its meaning cannot remain only in prose scattered across a decision and Foundation Paper. The future governed corpus must provide a stable semantic package or equivalent governed unit capable of expressing:

- Knowledge identity and modes;
- Bearer and actual-bearing requirements;
- Knowledge attribution;
- Scope, Context, Domain, and Applicability;
- distinctions from claim, asset, Representation, status, and reliance;
- typed relationships with Intelligence and epistemic processes;
- prohibited inferences, examples, counterexamples, dissent, and lineage; and
- human/machine alignment obligations.

This is a **semantic requirement**, not approval of a folder name, manifest, schema, identifier, or final module boundary.

### 7.2 Epistemic-artifact semantics are also required

Knowledge Engineering needs governed meanings for claims, grounds/evidence roles, provenance, assessments, assets, lifecycle events, and reliance decisions. Whether these become modules inside `knowledge`, a separate `knowledge_engineering` or `epistemic_record` package, or another composition is deferred to 2.5.

### 7.3 Documents do not specialize Knowledge

A Document may:

- express a Knowledge claim;
- preserve grounds or testimony;
- serve as evidence in a qualified inquiry;
- participate in a Knowledge asset;
- record an attribution, assessment, decision, or consequence; and
- receive runtime property assertions.

Those roles do not make Document a subtype of Knowledge. The strongest current candidate is that Document specializes Representation or a governed resource kind and participates through typed epistemic roles. The final Document disposition remains for integrated analysis and 2.5 package construction.

## 9. Human- and machine-semantic obligations

### 9.1 Human-semantic lane

A mature account must explain:

- why a document or database is not Knowledge;
- what is claimed, by whom, about which subject, and within what Scope;
- what supports or challenges the claim;
- which status is epistemic, governance, or authorization status;
- what changed across representations and lifecycle events;
- who may challenge, decide, rely, revise, or retire; and
- which uncertainty, disagreement, and dissent remain.

### 9.2 Machine-semantic lane

Without selecting a schema, a future expression must be capable of preserving:

- stable identity for subjects, claims, assets, records, and representations;
- typed relationships among Bearer, content, source, assessor, authority, evidence, and affected use;
- Context, Scope, Domain, Applicability, and time;
- claim content separately from attribution and status;
- provenance and derivation across transformations;
- uncertainty, disagreement, challenge, supersession, and retirement;
- semantic-package version or digest;
- Representation and Projection lineage; and
- declared additions, omissions, transformations, and loss.

A runtime store may hold these records centrally and project reductions to repositories. The neutral store does not acquire semantic authority from centrality, and a SharePoint/AWS/MySQL projection does not become complete merely because it is operationally useful.

## 10. Counterexample results

| Case | Result under the candidate boundary |
|---|---|
| True claim accepted for the wrong reason | Accepted status and truth coexist, but no qualifying Knowledge attribution follows from the defective grounds |
| False claim with provenance and schema validity | Provenance and conformance remain valuable conditions; neither supplies truth |
| Rich graph labeled `knowledge object` | Treat as Representation or asset candidate; label creates no epistemic achievement |
| Surgeon or craftsperson with unarticulated competence | Practical Knowledge may be borne despite incomplete representation; artifacts may support but do not contain the competence |
| Two incompatible claims in one asset | Asset governance preserves both, their grounds, status, and conflict; container membership does not resolve truth |
| Authoritative assertion later defeated | Authority and prior reliance remain historical facts while epistemic status and authorization change through traceable events |
| Valid inference from false premises | Logical validity is preserved as one condition; the conclusion is not thereby known |
| Emergency reliance on uncertain content | Reliance may be authorized under risk without upgrading uncertainty to Knowledge |
| Same claim in Markdown, JSON, database, and SharePoint | One claim may have several Representations; identity and projection lineage prevent duplicate semantics |
| SharePoint column drops provenance and disagreement | It is a declared reduced Projection and must retain a route to the neutral/full record |
| Legal versus medical document types | Both may specialize a universal classification relation while the value schemes remain Domain-owned |
| Retired asset | Retirement changes availability or governance status, not historical truth by deletion or rewriting |

No tested case requires a generic foundational `KnowledgeObject`.

## 12. Consequences

### Foundation Papers

The Knowledge and Knowledge Engineering v0.2 papers must distinguish actual Knowledge, Knowledge semantics, epistemic artifacts, and runtime records; replace container metaphors where they imply stored Knowledge; and explain `KnowledgeAsset` without making it a metaphysical container. The property/projection paper's generic `knowledge object` usage requires review before any future publication status is considered.

### Candidate Constitution

A1 must preserve the separation of truth, Knowledge, claim, assessment, governance status, and reliance. A4 may govern engineering of epistemic conditions but must not imply that constitutional or technical procedure manufactures Knowledge.

### 2.5 package construction

2.5 must provide a governed expression of Knowledge semantics and the required epistemic-artifact relationships. It must decide whether these form one package or a dependency-aligned set only after the complete 2.1.4 lifecycle and representation obligations are known.

### Derived systems and runtime storage

Derived systems specialize Domain meanings and instantiate records. Neutral operational storage may preserve full assertions; SharePoint, AWS, MySQL JSON, search indexes, APIs, and code structures remain declared projections. No storage carrier becomes the semantic owner.

## 13. Preserved dissent and open questions

1. `EpistemicCondition` may be too broad to deserve an independent governed distinction.
2. Knowledge Engineering may be a foundational discipline/method rather than a foundational concept package.
3. `KnowledgeClaim` may need separate treatment of content, assertion act, speaker commitment, and attributed proposition.
4. Ground and evidence may be roles in one relation family or materially distinct concepts.
5. A Knowledge asset may be better modeled as a governed collection, a graph of records, a lifecycle boundary, or a role rather than an object class.
6. Some institutions or artificial systems may literally bear Knowledge; the artifact distinction does not settle that DR-011 dissent.
7. A Document may have identity as work, expression, manifestation, file, version, or record; the present boundary does not decide those levels.
8. A generic `KnowledgeObject` may remain useful for interoperability despite its semantic danger.
9. The minimum machine-semantic fields may differ by Knowledge mode and Domain.
10. Practical and familiarity Knowledge may require lifecycle models that are not claim-centered.
11. Semantic packages themselves may be Knowledge assets in some governed role without thereby containing actual Knowledge.
12. The boundary between epistemic assessment and authority for reliance requires 2.1.6 coordination.

## 14. Confidence and reopening conditions

**Confidence:** High that Knowledge, semantic declaration, epistemic artifact, runtime record, and platform Projection must remain distinct; High that Document is not a specialization of Knowledge; High that a Knowledge semantic expression is required in principle; Medium-High in the direct-object formulation; Medium in `EpistemicCondition` as a named governed distinction; Low-Medium in any surviving use of `KnowledgeObject`.

Reopen this working result if:

- Stream B cannot model practical or familiarity Knowledge without artifact reification;
- a mandatory use case requires Knowledge itself to be stored or transferred and survives the DR-011 tests;
- the claim/asset/record separation creates irreducible identity contradictions;
- the Knowledge semantic requirement cannot be expressed without prematurely selecting package anatomy; or
- cross-domain or projection tests require a generic `KnowledgeObject` that is both useful and non-misleading.
