## 1. Question and analytical result

> What minimum boundary must SymK establish among a Domain problem, complex question, framing, formulation, prompt, request, task, search query, test item, decision problem, and inquiry before it analyzes reasoning or judges an answer?

Stream A supports the following working answer:

> **A question is a scoped and challengeable inquiry object that relates an identified subject matter to what is being sought about it, for a declared purpose and audience, under a materially explicit Domain, Scope, Context, time, assumptions, constraints, uncertainty, affected horizon, and answer obligation. Its wording, prompt, query, task, ticket, or formal expression is a Representation of that question and may preserve, narrow, expand, distort, or replace it.**

> **A complex question is a question whose responsible treatment materially depends on plural, interacting, uncertain, contested, distributed, dynamic, cross-level, cross-jurisdictional, or consequence-bearing elements such that no isolated source, participant, operation, fixed decomposition, or unqualified answer may be presumed adequate. Complexity is a scoped relational claim requiring evidence; it is not established by length, obscurity, difficulty, computational expense, number of steps, professional prestige, or the use of a Domain Intelligence Amplifier.**

> **Problem framing is the selective and revisable activity—and its resulting frame—through which a Domain situation, condition, discrepancy, opportunity, tension, or decision need is represented as one or more questions. Inquiry is the organized, revisable pursuit of an answer, qualified no-answer, or warranted reframing through admissible operations, participants, sources, Evidence, challenge, and review. Neither framing nor inquiry guarantees reasoning, truth, Knowledge, answer quality, decision, action, value, or successful outcome.**

This working result keeps four states separate:

1. **Domain situation or problem:** what is occurring, absent, conflicting, uncertain, desired, or at stake in the Domain;
2. **question object:** what is being asked about that matter and what would count as addressing it;
3. **question Representation:** the words, prompt, task, query, issue, schema, form, test item, or other expression used to present it; and
4. **inquiry:** the organized pursuit through which the question may be clarified, challenged, decomposed, investigated, answered, suspended, reframed, or left unresolved.

The model rejects two opposite errors. A question is not merely a sentence or prompt, because semantically equivalent formulations can preserve one question and one formulation can encode several questions. But a question is not an unbounded “real problem” detached from every expression, because its identity must be inspectable, contestable, versioned, and related to evidence-bearing Representations.

The minimum does not require every ordinary lookup to carry a maximal governance dossier. Materiality and consequence govern the required detail. A low-consequence, well-bounded lookup may be represented compactly. A high-consequence or materially complex question requires proportionately explicit framing, assumptions, stakeholders, uncertainty, alternatives, challenge, and lineage.

No concept disposition is accepted here. Stream B must analyze reasoning, inference, computation, retrieval, judgment, and explanation without silently inheriting a theory of reasoning from this question model.

## 3. Why SymK needs this boundary

The Product Vision makes better, faster, lower-total-cost answers to complex Domain questions the principal constrained value. Without a governed question boundary, every later term becomes unstable:

- an answer can appear correct only because the question changed;
- speed can improve only because verification, clarification, or affected-party review was removed;
- cost can fall only because part of the problem or consequence horizon was excluded;
- retrieval can appear to reason because the prompt hid the interpretive work;
- a benchmark can reward conformity to a test item rather than response to the Domain question;
- a recommendation can answer the wrong decision problem while remaining fluent and well supported;
- decomposition can make each subquestion tractable while omitting the interaction that made the original question complex;
- an institution can frame affected subjects, uncertainty, alternatives, or transferred cost out of view;
- an implementation can turn fields, tickets, prompts, search queries, or workflow stages into semantic authority; and
- a favorable outcome can retrospectively redefine the question so that the system appears successful.

This boundary is therefore prior to reasoning and answer assessment. No answer-quality, speed, cost, value, outcome, or feedback claim is comparable unless the governed question Identity and material framing conditions are sufficiently stable or their changes are declared.

## 4. Conceptual layers

| Layer | Working role | What it must not be mistaken for |
|---|---|---|
| Domain situation | Conditions, events, absences, opportunities, tensions, needs, or consequences in a Domain | A problem merely because someone labels it one |
| Domain problem | A situated condition or discrepancy represented as requiring understanding, resolution, choice, prevention, change, or justified non-action | Its formulation, requested task, chosen solution, or authorized decision |
| problem frame | Selected boundary, perspective, purpose, concepts, assumptions, stakeholders, constraints, causal horizon, and salience structure | The whole Domain situation or a neutral description |
| question object | The governed relation between subject matter and what is sought about it | Its wording, prompt, task, query, expected answer, or answer record |
| question formulation | A Human-, Scientific-, or Engineering-View expression of a question | The question itself or proof that its Identity was preserved |
| complex-question qualification | A scoped claim that material interacting or uncertain elements prevent presumptive isolated treatment | Mere length, difficulty, obscurity, computational expense, or status |
| inquiry | Organized and revisable pursuit of an answer, qualified no-answer, or warranted reframing | Search, retrieval, reasoning, experiment, dialogue, workflow, or answer by definition |
| inquiry plan | Proposed operations, roles, sources, checkpoints, Evidence needs, and stopping or escalation conditions | Actual inquiry performance or adequate completion |
| inquiry episode | A temporally and contextually bounded occurrence of inquiry activity | The persistent question, complete inquiry lineage, or final answer |
| question/inquiry claim | An assertion about Identity, complexity, framing, status, adequacy, or relation | The actual predicate or an accepted assessment |
| assessment and status | Evidence-bearing judgment and governed status concerning a question or inquiry | Truth, answer quality, Authorization, or completion by record alone |
| record or Projection | Prompt, ticket, form, query, issue, map, model, log, identifier, dashboard, or schema entry | Semantic authority or the actual question/inquiry |

These layers may be connected but may not be collapsed. One prompt may represent several questions. One question may have several formulations. One Domain problem may generate competing questions. One question may be pursued through several inquiries. One inquiry may reveal that the original question was malformed, incomplete, or outside the presumed jurisdiction.

## 5. Domain situation, problem, and decision need

### 5.1 A situation is not automatically a problem

A Domain situation becomes a problem claim only relative to a declared purpose, expectation, norm, need, risk, opportunity, uncertainty, or affected condition. The same condition may be:

- an error for one role and an intended state for another;
- a local optimization and a system-level failure;
- a technical malfunction and a governance success;
- a short-term cost and a long-term capability investment;
- a legal issue, an engineering constraint, a professional judgment, or no problem within another jurisdiction; or
- a problem for affected nonparticipants even when users and operators report success.

The problem claim therefore requires a claimant or source, object, Domain, Scope, Context, time, affected horizon, standard or purpose, Evidence, uncertainty, and challenge path where material. It does not require agreement, but disagreement must remain visible.

### 5.2 A problem is not its proposed solution

“Which model should we deploy?”, “How do we automate this task?”, and “How can we reduce response time?” may encode a selected solution class before the underlying problem is established. A governed inquiry must be able to ask whether:

- deployment is needed;
- automation is the relevant intervention;
- the selected task boundary is appropriate;
- faster response is the material objective;
- the affected parties and external costs are represented;
- a no-change, prevention, repair, redesign, policy, educational, or nontechnical path is relevant; or
- competent Domain authority has already constrained the legitimate options.

Challenging a solution-presupposing frame is not refusal to answer. It may be necessary to identify the actual question.

### 5.3 Problem, question, and decision problem

A problem may generate descriptive, explanatory, predictive, interpretive, evaluative, normative, design, planning, counterfactual, diagnostic, or decision questions. A **decision problem** is a governed choice situation involving possible options, constraints, uncertainty, consequences, Authority, and a decision requirement. It may include epistemic questions, but it is not identical to them. A well-supported answer may inform a decision without making it; a legitimate decision may incorporate non-epistemic constraints without making the supporting answer false.

## 6. Working question model

### 6.1 Minimum working anchor

A material question claim should identify, as applicable:

1. **question identifier and version** — the lineage anchor, not a claim of semantic sameness by identifier alone;
2. **Domain purpose or inquiry purpose** — why an answer is sought and what broader undertaking it serves;
3. **subject matter or object** — what entities, relations, events, conditions, practices, claims, systems, decisions, or consequences are at issue;
4. **ask or question operator** — what is sought: description, explanation, prediction, interpretation, evaluation, comparison, design, recommendation, decision support, justification, or another answer kind;
5. **Domain and jurisdiction** — which bodies of practice, inquiry, professional authority, law, science, governance, or project semantics apply;
6. **Scope and exclusions** — what is included, excluded, bounded, or deferred;
7. **Context and time** — material circumstances, location, configuration, version, period, deadline, and temporal horizon;
8. **terms and distinctions** — meanings whose variation could change the question or the admissible answer;
9. **assumptions and presuppositions** — what is taken as given, including what remains challengeable;
10. **stakeholders and roles** — question source, framer, requester, inquirer, answerer, assessor, decision authority, responsible subject, beneficiary, and affected subject where material;
11. **knowns, unknowns, uncertainty, and disagreement** — the present epistemic condition and material alternatives;
12. **constraints and dependencies** — resource, evidential, temporal, legal, professional, ethical, technical, organizational, and external conditions;
13. **decomposition and interaction structure** — subquestions, dependencies, feedback, emergent relations, and residual whole-question obligations;
14. **answer obligation and admissible form** — what kind of response could address the question, including partial, plural, conditional, probabilistic, contested, abstaining, or no-answer forms;
15. **adequacy and defeat conditions** — what would count against the framing, inquiry, or proposed correspondence between question and answer;
16. **challenge, revision, and lineage** — who may contest or revise the frame and how identity-affecting change is preserved.

These are material dimensions, not a universal requirement for sixteen populated database fields. A compact question may omit dimensions shown to be immaterial. A material omission cannot be converted into absence by leaving a field blank or by choosing a format that cannot express it.

### 6.2 Question occurrence, content, claim, assessment, status, and record

The following must remain separate:

- a question is formulated or asked;
- the semantic content attributed to it;
- a claim that two formulations express the same question;
- an assessment of the question's clarity, answerability, relevance, validity, complexity, or jurisdiction;
- an inquiry's status concerning it;
- a governance status authorizing work or reliance;
- an answer's claimed correspondence to it; and
- records of any of these states.

A row marked “answered” does not establish that the question was addressed. A prompt stored under one identifier does not establish stable Identity across revisions. A governance status can authorize inquiry without making the question well formed or its presuppositions true.

## 7. Complex-question qualification

### 7.1 Complexity is relational and scoped

Complexity is not a substance inside a sentence. It is a qualification of a question relative to its object, Domain, Scope, Context, time, available Knowledge, participants, methods, dependencies, consequences, and responsible-answer conditions. A question may be simple for one Scope and complex for another. A familiar lookup may become complex when jurisdiction, conflicting Evidence, safety, affected distribution, or temporal change is material.

### 7.2 Candidate complexity sources

| Source | Working test | Typical false shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| structural or relational | Material elements interact so that isolated treatment changes the whole-question answer | Counting elements without testing interaction |
| epistemic | Evidence is incomplete, distributed, conflicting, uncertain, defeasible, or dependent on interpretation | Equating more documents or data with resolution |
| semantic | Key terms, categories, boundaries, or question readings are ambiguous or contested | Treating one wording or schema as authoritative |
| dynamic or temporal | The object, Evidence, participants, constraints, or consequences change materially over time | Freezing one snapshot or extrapolating one episode |
| cross-level | Individual, component, team, institutional, configured-system, ecosystem, or successor levels diverge | Transferring a result between levels |
| cross-jurisdictional | Several Domain, professional, legal, scientific, organizational, or constitutional authorities apply or conflict | Letting one authority settle all dimensions |
| multi-stakeholder and Power-sensitive | Participants, beneficiaries, decision authorities, responsible subjects, and affected parties have materially different positions or visibility | Treating requester preference as the whole problem |
| normative or value-plural | Legitimate values, duties, rights, benefits, harms, or non-commensurable considerations differ | Imposing one utility function or net score |
| operational or infrastructural | Dependencies, interfaces, providers, failures, versions, or lifecycle conditions materially affect resolution | Treating the visible product or current configuration as self-contained |
| consequence-bearing | Error, delay, action, omission, externalization, repair, or irreversibility makes framing material | Evaluating only immediate answer content |
| reflexive or adaptive | Inquiry, measurement, publication, or intervention changes the object or incentives | Treating observation and response as independent |
| decompositional | Subquestions are tractable but interactions, circularities, emergence, or residual uncertainty resist independent recomposition | Assuming solved parts entail a solved whole |

No one source is universally necessary or sufficient. A material complex-question claim should state which sources apply, their Evidence and uncertainty, and how they affect the inquiry. SymK does not select one universal complexity score or threshold.

### 7.3 Complex is not merely difficult or complicated

- **difficult** concerns the capability, resources, or conditions required by a subject;
- **complicated** may concern many parts or steps that remain decomposable under stable relations;
- **complex** here concerns material interaction, uncertainty, distribution, change, plural authority, consequence, or non-decomposability relative to the responsible inquiry;
- **ambiguous** concerns multiple plausible meanings;
- **underdetermined** concerns insufficient grounds to select one conclusion;
- **contested** concerns disagreement that may be epistemic, semantic, normative, political, professional, or jurisdictional;
- **high-consequence** concerns the stakes of error or action;
- **wicked**, **ill-structured**, or similar Domain terms may be useful specializations but are not imported as universal synonyms.

A question may be difficult but not complex, complex but easy to state, complicated without material emergence, or high-consequence despite a narrow factual form.

### 7.4 Complexity does not establish DIA relevance or success

A complex-question class is one constitutive DIA condition under DR-015, but:

- a complex question does not establish that a candidate system is a DIA;
- a DIA classification does not establish that every submitted question is complex;
- use of a DIA does not make a question complex;
- successful treatment does not retrospectively prove the initial complexity claim;
- failure does not prove complexity; and
- a question's complexity does not allocate Authority, Responsibility, resources, or value.

## 8. Question Identity, version, and lineage

### 8.1 Identity is not wording equality

Two differently worded, translated, reordered, or formally represented formulations may preserve one question when they preserve the material answering obligation. Identical wording may express different questions when Domain, Scope, Context, time, assumptions, stakeholders, or intended use differ.

A question-Identity claim should test whether material changes occurred in:

- subject matter or object;
- ask or answer kind;
- Domain or jurisdiction;
- Scope or exclusions;
- Context, configuration, version, or time;
- terms, categories, or distinctions;
- assumptions or presuppositions;
- stakeholders, affected horizon, or decision setting;
- uncertainty, Evidence availability, or applicable constraints;
- answer obligation, adequacy, or defeat conditions; and
- purpose or downstream use where it changes what must be answered.

No single field is always identity-defining. Materiality is governed by the question and Domain. The basis of sameness or difference must be explicit enough for challenge.

### 8.2 Question-change relations

| Relation | Working meaning | Identity consequence |
|---|---|---|
| re-expression | Different wording or Representation with no material change in answering obligation | May preserve Identity with declared translation or loss |
| clarification | Makes latent meaning explicit without changing material obligation | Usually preserves Identity; challenge remains possible |
| correction | Repairs an error in wording, reference, or metadata | May preserve or change Identity depending on materiality |
| refinement | Adds precision or resolves ambiguity | May preserve a narrower reading or create a successor question |
| restriction | Narrows Scope, cases, time, assumptions, or answer obligation | Usually a related narrower question, not silently identical |
| expansion | Broadens Scope, horizon, stakeholders, alternatives, or answer obligation | Usually a related broader question |
| decomposition | Creates subquestions intended to contribute to the original | Does not replace the whole-question obligation automatically |
| reformulation | Changes expression or structure to make inquiry tractable | Identity must be tested rather than presumed |
| reframing | Changes perspective, purpose, salience, boundary, assumptions, stakeholders, or causal/value horizon | Often creates a new or successor question |
| replacement | Substitutes a materially different question | Original remains preserved in lineage |
| withdrawal or invalidation | Removes the current obligation because the question is no longer pursued, admissible, meaningful, or based on tenable premises | Does not erase the historical question or inquiry effects |

### 8.3 Stable-identity rule

Answer, speed, cost, quality, and outcome comparisons require either:

1. the same governed question version under materially comparable conditions; or
2. an explicit relation among question versions that identifies what changed and why the comparison remains meaningful.

Question-version shopping is prohibited. A system may not claim improvement by narrowing Scope, removing difficult cases, changing assumptions, weakening answer obligations, excluding affected parties, or changing the time horizon without declaring the successor relation.

## 9. Problem framing

### 9.1 Framing is selective and consequential

A frame selects what counts as the object, purpose, boundary, relevant evidence, cause, stakeholder, constraint, alternative, timescale, consequence, and admissible response. Selection is unavoidable; neutrality is not presumed. A frame may be useful, incomplete, biased, strategic, imposed, inherited, provisional, or contested.

The fact that every frame is selective does not make every frame equally defensible. Material framing claims remain assessable through Evidence, alternatives, omissions, counterexamples, affected consequences, competent Authority, and challenge.

### 9.2 Minimum frame record

Where material, a problem frame should preserve:

- source and purpose;
- Domain, jurisdiction, and applicable external authority;
- selected problem object and boundary;
- included and excluded subjects, relations, events, times, and consequences;
- terms, categories, models, and causal assumptions;
- affected parties and distributions made visible or omitted;
- options, interventions, and no-action alternatives considered or excluded;
- Evidence, counterevidence, uncertainty, disagreement, and missing information;
- Power, incentives, conflicts of interest, and agenda control affecting salience;
- answer form, decision relation, and intended use;
- challenge, review, revision, and escalation path; and
- links to alternative, predecessor, and successor frames.

### 9.3 Misframed and loaded questions

A question may be syntactically clear yet materially defective because it:

- presupposes a false or unsupported condition;
- embeds an unauthorized objective or solution;
- excludes a material stakeholder, alternative, dependency, or consequence;
- imports a contested category as settled;
- assigns causation, fault, agency, Authority, or Responsibility in the question itself;
- narrows the time horizon to hide later cost or harm;
- treats one jurisdiction as exhaustive;
- asks for a scalar ranking where dimensions are non-commensurable; or
- demands certainty or a binary answer unsupported by the evidence state.

An adequate response may correct, qualify, split, reframe, or decline the question. Doing so must remain distinguishable from evasion or unauthorized replacement.

## 10. Scope, Context, assumptions, and presuppositions

### 10.1 Scope and Context are not interchangeable

Scope bounds what the question applies to or includes. Context supplies circumstances relevant to meaning, applicability, inquiry, or answer. A question may retain Scope while Context changes its answer, or retain Context while a Scope change creates a new question.

Material Scope and Context should include negative boundaries: what the question does not cover, which interpretations are excluded, and which later or adjacent questions remain open.

### 10.2 Assumptions and presuppositions

An **assumption** is a proposition provisionally adopted for inquiry, modeling, or decision. A **presupposition** is treated as already holding or required for the question to operate as formulated. Either may be explicit or hidden, justified or unjustified, fixed by competent authority or open to challenge.

The question record should distinguish:

- accepted Domain facts from inquiry assumptions;
- working simplifications from claims about reality;
- legal or governance stipulations from epistemic truth;
- counterfactual conditions from actual conditions;
- model boundaries from Domain boundaries;
- answer-format constraints from substantive assumptions; and
- assumptions that may be challenged from those fixed for a particular inquiry while remaining contestable elsewhere.

If a material presupposition fails, the appropriate result may be correction, conditional answer, no-answer, or reframing—not forced selection from the original answer space.

## 11. Stakeholders, roles, Authority, and affected horizon

### 11.1 Roles do not transfer

A material question may involve:

- a **problem claimant** who identifies a situation as problematic;
- a **framer** who selects the problem boundary and salience structure;
- a **question source or requester** who asks for treatment;
- an **inquirer** who organizes inquiry;
- participants, contributors, sources, experts, models, tools, and environments;
- an **answerer** or answer-producing arrangement;
- an **assessor** who evaluates framing, inquiry, or answer correspondence;
- a **decision authority** who may adopt, reject, or use an answer;
- a **responsible subject** for identified objects and phases;
- beneficiaries, users, clients, payers, providers, and operators; and
- affected subjects whose interests or consequences may be material without participation or control.

One subject may occupy several roles, but the roles do not become identical. The requester does not automatically own the Domain problem, determine truth, define every affected interest, authorize every method, or assess the answer conclusively. Expertise does not create decision Authority; Authority does not create epistemic adequacy; affected status does not automatically create every decision right; and a system that records or routes the question does not become the inquirer or responsible subject.

### 11.2 Framing Authority and challenge

Some frames are constrained legitimately by courts, regulators, scientific protocols, professional standards, organizational mandates, contracts, or project governance. Their force is typed and jurisdiction-bound. A validly imposed frame may still be epistemically incomplete, and a well-supported alternative frame may remain unauthorized for the current decision.

Material framing should therefore record:

- who may set, modify, or approve the operative frame;
- the source, kind, jurisdiction, Scope, time, and limits of that Authority;
- which epistemic, professional, legal, constitutional, or organizational challenges remain available;
- whether affected subjects have notice, representation, voice, review, appeal, or another response path where applicable; and
- whether an alternative frame is rejected as false, irrelevant, outside Scope, unauthorized, infeasible, or merely deferred.

Stream A does not grant such Authority or standing. It preserves the distinctions required for later allocation.

### 11.3 Affected-horizon rule

The question's affected horizon must not be limited automatically to requester, user, payer, operator, or immediate beneficiary. When consequences, transferred cost, risk, displacement, capability loss, or remediation burden may be material, the frame should identify affected subjects and uncertainty far enough to prevent their erasure. This is an inquiry obligation, not a conclusion that every affected subject has the same interest, right, veto, or decision Authority.

## 12. Decomposition, dependency, and recomposition

### 12.1 Decomposition is a relation, not equivalence by default

Complex questions are often divided into subquestions. Decomposition can expose structure, distribute work, select methods, and make inquiry tractable. It can also erase the very interactions that create complexity.

A decomposition claim should preserve:

- the parent question and version;
- each subquestion and its Scope;
- the rationale for the partition;
- coverage of the parent question's material obligations;
- dependency direction among subquestions;
- shared assumptions, terms, Evidence, and boundary conditions;
- interactions, feedback, circularity, emergence, or conflicts across parts;
- order, concurrency, and iteration requirements;
- residual whole-question uncertainty;
- rules or methods for recomposition; and
- Evidence that answers to subquestions jointly address the parent.

Solving every listed subquestion is neither necessary nor sufficient by itself for answering the parent. Some subquestions may be redundant, misframed, conditional, or rendered unnecessary; material interactions or residuals may remain.

### 12.2 Decomposition failure modes

- **coverage loss:** a material part of the parent question is absent;
- **interaction loss:** cross-part relations are ignored;
- **assumption drift:** subquestions use incompatible premises or contexts;
- **boundary drift:** different levels, subjects, times, or system configurations are combined;
- **double counting:** the same Evidence, benefit, cost, or consequence is treated as independent;
- **recomposition failure:** locally valid results do not support the whole conclusion;
- **authority fragmentation:** each subquestion is answered within one jurisdiction while cross-jurisdictional conflict is hidden;
- **uncertainty compression:** local confidence is aggregated as if independent;
- **affected-party fragmentation:** no subquestion owns a distributed consequence; and
- **optimization leakage:** each part improves its local metric while the whole becomes worse.

### 12.3 Emergent and residual obligations

If an interaction cannot be assigned adequately to one subquestion, it remains a parent-level or cross-cutting inquiry obligation. A decomposition record must be able to say “unresolved” or “not recomposable” without manufacturing completeness.

## 13. Reformulation, reframing, translation, and representation loss

### 13.1 Reformulation for inquiry

An inquirer may reformulate a question to remove ambiguity, state assumptions, make dependencies explicit, or select a tractable representation. Reformulation is legitimate only when its relation to the source question remains inspectable.

The reformulation record should state:

- which material meanings are preserved;
- which are made explicit;
- which are narrowed, generalized, idealized, abstracted, formalized, operationalized, or omitted;
- why the transformation is necessary;
- what loss or uncertainty it introduces;
- whether the result is claimed to be the same, a specialization, a model, a subquestion, or a successor question; and
- who may challenge that relation.

### 13.2 Translation across views and languages

Natural-language, mathematical, logical, diagrammatic, schema-based, query, and code representations may express aspects of the same question. No format owns the question by itself.

- A Human View may preserve purpose, ordinary meaning, alternatives, lived consequence, dissent, and explanation while remaining ambiguous or incomplete.
- A Scientific View may operationalize constructs, variables, hypotheses, methods, models, uncertainty, and defeat conditions while idealizing the Domain.
- An Engineering View may assign identifiers, fields, types, constraints, dependencies, interfaces, states, and validation rules while omitting material semantics not representable in the chosen profile.

Cross-view or cross-language translation requires declared correspondence and loss. Formal precision about the wrong or narrowed question is not semantic adequacy.

### 13.3 Query and prompt lowering

A search query, database query, API request, model prompt, workflow input, or test case is a lowering of some inquiry need into a technical interface. The lowering may:

- encode only one subquestion;
- omit Context or assumptions held elsewhere;
- introduce system-specific constraints;
- select an answer format;
- add instructions unrelated to the Domain question;
- expose or conceal retrieval and ranking assumptions; or
- transform an open question into classification, extraction, generation, or execution.

Success at the lowered task does not establish success on the source question. The relation, omissions, and intended use must remain available where material.

## 14. Answer form, answerability, and qualified no-answer

### 14.1 Answer form is part of the obligation when material

A question may call for:

- a fact, claim, definition, explanation, interpretation, prediction, estimate, comparison, evaluation, classification, proof, counterexample, diagnosis, recommendation, design, plan, decision support, or another response kind;
- one answer, several alternatives, a range, distribution, scenario set, conditional structure, uncertainty statement, dissent, or exception;
- a summary, full argument, formal derivation, source-supported analysis, executable artifact, professional opinion, or mixed form; and
- immediate provisional response, verified result, periodic update, or lifecycle-maintained answer.

Format is not always identity-defining, but it becomes material when changing it changes what must be supported, who can use it, which uncertainty or consequence is visible, or whether the question is answerable responsibly.

### 14.2 Answerability is qualified

“Answerable” may mean:

- conceptually meaningful;
- within a competent Domain or method;
- resolvable in principle;
- resolvable with presently available Evidence;
- resolvable by the current inquirer or system;
- resolvable within the authorized Scope, time, cost, or procedure;
- capable of one determinate answer; or
- capable only of conditional, plural, probabilistic, contested, or provisional treatment.

These meanings must not be collapsed. A question may be meaningful but presently underdetermined; answerable by an external authority but not by the system; or answerable factually while not legitimately decidable by the answerer.

### 14.3 Qualified no-answer and reframing

An adequate inquiry result may be:

- **abstention:** the answerer declines because capability, Evidence, Authority, safety, or conditions are inadequate;
- **underdetermination:** available Grounds do not select one answer;
- **unanswerability under the frame:** the formulation or assumptions prevent a responsible answer;
- **outside jurisdiction or Scope:** another authority or inquiry is required;
- **clarification request:** material ambiguity prevents stable correspondence;
- **conditional answer:** results depend on explicit assumptions or scenarios;
- **plural answer set:** incompatible answers remain reasonable under unresolved Evidence or values;
- **reframed answer:** the original question is challenged and a related question is treated, with the change declared; or
- **suspension:** inquiry remains active pending evidence, event, review, or authority.

These are not automatically failures. They must not, however, be used to hide avoidable incompleteness, evasion, lack of effort, or unauthorized replacement.

## 15. Inquiry and neighboring operations

### 15.1 Working inquiry anchor

Inquiry is an organized, scoped, revisable pursuit of an answer, qualified no-answer, or warranted reframing of a question. A material inquiry claim should identify:

- question version and operative frame;
- inquiry purpose, Domain, Scope, Context, time, constraints, and intended use;
- inquirer, participants, roles, Authority, Responsibility, and affected horizon where relevant;
- plan, methods, operations, sources, tools, models, and dependencies;
- Knowledge, Evidence, Provenance, assumptions, uncertainty, disagreement, alternatives, and gaps;
- challenge, review, validation, escalation, stopping, reopening, and revision conditions;
- outputs, candidate answers, unresolved items, and relation to later decision or action; and
- lifecycle, lineage, assessment, status, and record.

No single operation is universally necessary. The requirement is that the claimed inquiry be adequate to its question and that its actual operations not be inferred from labels or outputs.

### 15.2 Neighboring operations

| Operation | Working distinction | Why it is not inquiry by itself |
|---|---|---|
| search | Locates candidate items or paths under a query and search space | May not assess relevance, truth, completeness, or the source question |
| retrieval | Returns selected stored or accessible material | Access does not establish interpretation, Evidence, reasoning, or answer correspondence |
| observation | Registers a condition or event through a method or subject | May be theory-, instrument-, sampling-, or frame-dependent and may not address the question alone |
| measurement | Assigns values or categories under a construct and procedure | A metric is a Projection and may not validly represent the intended construct |
| experiment | Intervenes or compares under a design to produce Evidence | Design, inference, generalization, ethics, and applicability remain separate |
| investigation | Organized examination of facts, causes, events, or conduct | May be one inquiry kind with Domain-specific authority and procedure |
| computation | Produces a result under specified inputs, operations, and representations | Does not establish premise adequacy, model validity, interpretation, or relevance |
| modeling or simulation | Represents selected structures or dynamics and explores consequences | Model results remain conditional on abstraction, parameters, and applicability |
| dialogue or consultation | Exchanges questions, interpretations, testimony, expertise, or challenge | Participation, consensus, or expert status does not establish truth or completeness |
| reasoning or inference | Relates premises, Evidence, models, rules, alternatives, and conclusions | Full boundary belongs to Stream B; it is neither identical with nor necessary to every trivial inquiry operation |
| deliberation or judgment | Weighs reasons, uncertainty, constraints, values, or choices | May depend on Authority and value not supplied by epistemic support alone |
| workflow | Orders tasks, roles, inputs, outputs, gates, and transitions | Execution and completion status do not establish inquiry adequacy |
| explanation or presentation | Expresses an account, rationale, derivation, or result | May be retrospective, incomplete, pedagogical, or causally unrelated to the actual process |

An inquiry may combine these operations. A simple lookup may require only a bounded search/retrieval and source check. A complex inquiry may require several methods, participants, iterations, and challenge paths. Complexity does not imply that every operation must occur.

### 15.3 Inquiry does not entail reasoning, and reasoning does not entail inquiry

A lookup can be an inquiry episode without a material reasoning claim. A theorem derivation or calculation can be reasoning relative to a given problem without constituting an adequate inquiry into whether the premises, model, Scope, or application are appropriate. Stream B must preserve this reciprocal non-identity.

### 15.4 Inquiry plan, execution, and completion

The existence of a plan does not establish execution. Execution of every planned task does not establish question coverage. Completion status does not establish an answer, and an answer does not establish complete inquiry. Deviations, failed methods, missing Evidence, unresolved dissent, and frame changes must remain visible where material.

## 16. Question and inquiry lifecycle

### 16.1 Candidate lifecycle

A material lineage may include:

1. Domain situation observation or problem claim;
2. problem framing and alternative frames;
3. question formulation;
4. clarification, Scope, Context, assumptions, stakeholder, and answer-form declaration;
5. complexity and jurisdiction assessment;
6. decomposition, dependency mapping, and inquiry planning;
7. inquiry execution, challenge, and revision;
8. candidate answer, qualified no-answer, or reframing;
9. assessment, adoption, reliance, decision, action, and consequence as separate later states;
10. feedback, correction, reopening, or question succession; and
11. preservation, supersession, withdrawal, or closure with lineage.

This is not a mandatory linear workflow. Stages may iterate, branch, merge, suspend, fail, or occur concurrently. A later answer or consequence may reopen the frame without erasing the prior question.

### 16.2 Candidate statuses

Question and inquiry statuses may include proposed, active, clarified, scoped, decomposed, under inquiry, contested, reframed, suspended, unresolved, provisionally answered, superseded, withdrawn, or closed. These are candidates for later package design, not accepted universal vocabulary.

Status must identify its object. “Closed” may mean the requester ended work, an authorized decision occurred, the available Evidence was exhausted, a response was delivered, or the question was resolved under a declared standard. Those states do not entail one another.

### 16.3 Feedback and question revision

A later signal, consequence, complaint, contradiction, model failure, changed Context, new Evidence, or affected-party challenge may support review of the question or frame. It does not automatically prove that the original question was wrong, create a correction, or establish Learning. Stream F will govern that return path.

## 17. Proportionality, evidence, and burden

### 17.1 Proportional governance

The minimum record should be proportional to:

- uncertainty and disagreement;
- consequence severity, distribution, reversibility, and timing;
- complexity sources and interactions;
- novelty, instability, and lifecycle change;
- number and diversity of jurisdictions, roles, and affected subjects;
- dependence on hidden providers, models, data, expertise, or infrastructure;
- challenge, audit, reuse, and future-reliance needs; and
- cost of misframing, delayed correction, or loss of lineage.

Proportionality must not become an excuse to omit the very information needed to assess risk. Nor should the universal minimum turn every ordinary question into a bureaucratic project.

### 17.2 Evidence for a complex-question claim

Possible evidence includes:

- demonstrated interacting dependencies;
- conflicting or materially incomplete sources;
- sensitivity to assumptions, boundary, context, time, or model choice;
- plural competent interpretations or jurisdictions;
- decomposition and recomposition failures;
- distributed Knowledge or expertise requirements;
- affected-party or Power asymmetries;
- consequence or irreversibility analysis;
- instability across versions or scenarios;
- historical inquiry failures, corrections, or surprises; and
- explicit unknowns that defeat presumptive isolated treatment.

No evidence family is universally necessary. “Complex” remains revisable and challengeable. A question may be reclassified as simpler after clarification, or a seemingly simple question may reveal material complexity.

### 17.3 Burden and stopping

Inquiry adequacy is not infinite investigation. Stopping may be justified by sufficient support under a declared standard, diminishing relevance, deadline, resource constraint, external decision, safety boundary, inability to obtain material Evidence, or lack of Authority. The stopping claim must remain distinct from truth, completeness, value, or permanent closure.

## 19. Candidate approaches considered

### A — Question as an interrogative sentence

Rejected as the governing minimum. It cannot preserve Identity across language, paraphrase, formalization, dialogue, implicit questions, or version change, and it gives wording semantic authority.

### B — Question as prompt, request, task, query, or ticket

Rejected as a conflation. These are social, procedural, or technical Representations and operations that may encode, transform, omit, combine, or presuppose questions.

### C — Question as an information gap

Rejected as sufficient. It underrepresents interpretation, explanation, evaluation, design, normative questions, decision support, affected consequences, disagreement, and challenge. Information-gap language may describe one inquiry condition.

### D — Question as a formal answer set or input–output function

Rejected as the universal meaning. It is useful for bounded formal or Engineering Views but may silently fix semantics, admissible answers, assumptions, and loss.

### E — Question as a decision problem or objective

Rejected as universal. Many questions are not decisions; decision problems also require Authority, options, constraints, values, consequences, and procedures not supplied by question semantics alone.

### F — Complex question as long, hard, novel, expensive, multidisciplinary, or multi-step

Rejected as sufficient. These properties are subject-, resource-, or representation-relative and do not establish material interaction, uncertainty, distribution, or non-decomposability.

### G — Complex question as a fixed checklist score or universal threshold

Rejected as universal. A checklist may support assessment, but weights and thresholds remain Domain-, scientific-, professional-, project-, or external-authority-owned and may erase a decisive low-frequency condition.

### H — Scoped governed inquiry object plus evidence-bearing relational complexity qualification

Retained as the strongest working direction. It preserves Representation independence while keeping Identity inspectable; supports multilingual and three-view expression; separates problem, frame, question, formulation, inquiry, answer, decision, and record; enables proportional governance; and leaves Domain methods and thresholds with competent owners.

## 21. Human, Scientific, and Engineering View obligations

### 21.1 Human View

The Human View must make understandable, proportionately to materiality:

- what situation or problem is at issue and why;
- what is actually being asked and what is not;
- whose purpose, perspective, terms, assumptions, and constraints shape the frame;
- which alternative frames and interpretations were considered;
- which participants, decision authorities, responsible subjects, beneficiaries, and affected subjects are visible or missing;
- what is known, unknown, uncertain, disputed, or unavailable;
- how the question was decomposed or reformulated and what may have been lost;
- what response kinds, including abstention or plural answers, are admissible;
- what challenge, revision, and appeal paths remain; and
- which consequences may reopen the question.

Human readability is not mere fluency. A polished formulation that hides presuppositions, jurisdiction, affected parties, or loss fails the Human View.

### 21.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View must preserve, where applicable:

- the question construct, object, variables, relations, population, environment, and level;
- hypotheses, alternatives, models, assumptions, operationalizations, and causal or explanatory claims;
- evidence needs, source and method limits, sampling, measurement, uncertainty, sensitivity, robustness, and counterevidence;
- decomposition and recomposition validity;
- identifiability, testability, underdetermination, generalization, and applicability;
- conditions of defeat, replication, revision, or suspension; and
- the distinction between a Domain question and the experimentally or formally tractable question used as its model.

Scientific precision does not settle normative, legal, professional, affected-party, or decision-authority questions outside the relevant jurisdiction.

### 21.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View must preserve, where applicable:

- stable identifiers, versions, lineage, statuses, relations, and timestamps;
- Domain, Scope, Context, assumptions, constraints, stakeholders, roles, Authority, and intended use;
- parent/subquestion, predecessor/successor, re-expression, restriction, expansion, decomposition, reformulation, reframing, and replacement relations;
- required and optional answer forms, uncertainty, challenge, review, and reopening conditions;
- mappings from question to prompt, task, search query, workflow, model, schema, test, answer, decision record, and feedback channel;
- declared transformation, omission, approximation, and loss;
- source, Evidence, Provenance, dependency, configuration, and external-provider references; and
- validation rules that check a declared representation contract without defining the Domain question by field availability.

A technically valid prompt, schema, workflow, or record may still represent the wrong question or omit material content.

### 21.4 Alignment rule

The three Views must refer to the same declared question version, problem frame, Domain, Scope, Context, assumptions, stakeholder and affected horizon, decomposition, answer obligation, uncertainty, and status where material. Their representations may differ, but correspondence, transformation, omission, and disagreement must be explicit.

If one View cannot represent a material element, the loss must be declared or the representation is inadequate for the claimed use. No shared identifier, interface, formalism, or document title establishes alignment by itself.

## 28. Confidence and reopening conditions

**Confidence: High** that question Identity must be separated from wording, prompt, request, task, query, ticket, test item, decision problem, answer, and record; that Domain problem, frame, question, formulation, and inquiry cannot substitute for one another; that material assumptions, Scope, Context, stakeholders, uncertainty, answer obligations, and lineage must remain challengeable; and that decomposition does not entail whole-question coverage.

**Confidence: Medium–High** in the scoped governed inquiry-object anchor, the relational complexity qualification, question-change relations, proportionality rule, inquiry-operation distinctions, and three-view obligations. These are sufficiently coherent for Stream B but may need consolidation after reasoning and answer analysis.

**Confidence: Medium** in the exact distinction between Domain situation and Domain problem, the role of purpose and audience in question Identity, the completeness of the twelve complexity-source families, and the preliminary Foundational direction for Complex Question.

**Confidence: Low to Medium** in any universal complexity threshold, fixed question taxonomy, complete status vocabulary, exact formal identity criteria, or universal stopping rule. Those remain intentionally unselected.

The working model must be reopened if later analysis shows that:

- a question cannot be represented coherently apart from its linguistic or formal expression;
- the inquiry-object anchor excludes clear questions lacking an explicit asker, audience, or answer form;
- purpose or stakeholder fields make question Identity unstable under ordinary use;
- material identity cannot be tested without one Domain-specific theory;
- the complexity qualification admits every uncertain or consequential question or excludes clear complex cases;
- complex-question status cannot remain architecture- and Intelligence-substrate-independent;
- decomposition and reformulation relations cannot preserve usable lineage;
- qualified no-answer cannot be distinguished from evasion without importing full answer quality prematurely;
- Stream B cannot connect reasoning to the question model without circularity;
- Stream C shows that answer correspondence requires a materially different question anchor;
- Stream D shows that framing adequacy cannot be part of quality without collapsing question and answer;
- Stream E shows that speed, cost, or value comparison cannot use question-version controls coherently;
- Stream F shows that feedback and Learning require a different lifecycle boundary;
- Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views cannot preserve one material question without contradiction;
- project grounding reveals systematic question-version shopping, over-governance, false complexity, or hidden affected-party loss;
- formalization forces sentence-, prompt-, field-, or workflow-based semantic authority; or
- the model contradicts the Product Vision or Stage-Accepted DR-009 through DR-015.
