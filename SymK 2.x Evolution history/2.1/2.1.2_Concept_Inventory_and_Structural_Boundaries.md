# SymK 2.1.2 — Concept Inventory and Structural Boundaries

**Status:** Stage-Accepted for continuation of SymK 2.1
**Decision:** `SYMK-2X-DR-010`
**Depends on:** Stage-Accepted `SYMK-2X-DR-009`
**Authority/date:** Project steward, 18 August 2026
**Normative effect:** Provisional program authority for later 2.x analysis; no historical `FC-*` status or canonical artifact is changed

## 1. Decision question

> Which concepts and distinctions are required by the accepted SymK identity, and which apparent primitives belong instead to project Ontology, formal representation, or implementation?

## 2. Why structural classification must precede the substantive concepts

Knowledge, Intelligence, cooperation, Education, responsibility, Amplified Intelligence, and answers cannot be analyzed coherently while the following questions remain hidden:

- What remains the same when a concept, participant, system, or asset changes representation or version?
- Which conditions are relevant to interpretation and application?
- Where does an attribution, claim, authority, or validation apply?
- What makes a field of inquiry or practice a domain rather than merely a label?
- What is represented, what represents it, and what does a projection omit or add?
- To what subject is Intelligence, Knowledge, responsibility, or another status attributed?
- Must every governed subject be modeled as an Entity?
- Does every semantic association require a universal Relationship object?

If 2.1 answers these questions by copying the historical engineering model, the implementation vocabulary will define the concepts. If it ignores them, later definitions will use Identity, Context, Scope, Domain, Representation, and Bearer inconsistently.

The objective is therefore a **minimum structural semantic kernel**, not a universal ontology.

## 4. The inherited structural model

The current `FC-*` sequence proposes:

1. Entity;
2. Relationship;
3. Identity;
4. Representation;
5. Context;
6. Intelligence;
7. Knowledge.

Its working approach assumes that the concept reducing the most ontological uncertainty should be evaluated first. This produced useful attacks against “Entity means anything that exists” and against attempts to reduce semantic Relationship to pointers, keys, or containment.

The model nevertheless contains three assumptions that 2.1 must not inherit without review:

1. **First-class engineering object as foundation:** What deserves independent metadata, lifecycle, JSON, or graph treatment is treated as evidence of conceptual fundamentality.
2. **Universal Entity parent:** Concepts and semantic relations appear to require classification as Entity to receive identity or participate in other relations.
3. **Representation-driven completion:** A concept specification is expected to contain JSON, YAML, graph, and reference engineering models before SymK has decided which projections are necessary.

These assumptions may be useful in a later meta-model. They are not necessary to state SymK’s identity and therefore cannot control foundational admission.

## 6. Stage-Accepted foundational structural concepts

### 6.1 Identity

#### Minimum working definition

> **Identity is the continuity condition under which a governed subject is recognized as the same subject across relevant differences in time, state, Context, or Representation within a declared Scope.**

#### Required distinctions

| Distinction | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Identity | What makes continued reference to the same governed subject legitimate |
| Identifier | A symbol or value used to refer to that subject within an identification system |
| Identification | The process and evidence used to recognize the subject |
| Representation | A form expressing selected aspects of the subject |
| State | The subject’s condition at a particular point or interval |
| Version | A governed state in an explicit change lineage |

The same identifier may be reused incorrectly for a replacement, and several identifiers may refer to the same subject in different systems. Identifier equality is therefore evidence about Identity, not Identity itself.

#### Why foundational

SymK requires identity to preserve concept continuity, version lineage, participant attribution, knowledge-asset history, project inheritance, challenge, migration, and the claim that a DIA or cooperative system persists through change. Project-local definitions cannot be allowed to make the same SymK concept become unrelated merely because storage, language, or implementation changes.

#### Boundary

SymK governs identity only as needed for semantic and engineering continuity. It does not claim a complete metaphysics of personal identity, object persistence, consciousness, or legal identity.

### 6.2 Context

#### Minimum working definition

> **Context is the set of conditions relevant to interpreting, evaluating, attributing, or applying a governed subject in a particular inquiry or action.**

Context may include time, place, jurisdiction, population, task, purpose, assumptions, stakes, participants, resources, history, environment, and relationships. Which dimensions are material depends on the subject and consequence.

#### Required distinctions

- Context is not every fact in the universe.
- A context record is a Representation of selected contextual conditions, not the Context in full.
- Context does not automatically change truth; it may change meaning, evidence, standards, applicability, or responsible reliance.
- Hidden or omitted Context may invalidate application even when the represented statement remains unchanged.
- “Context” must not become one untyped field concealing time, jurisdiction, population, assumptions, and stakes.

#### Why foundational

Knowledge attribution, Intelligence performance, Education, role allocation, authority, validation, answer quality, and consequence are all Context-sensitive. If every project defines Context incompatibly, SymK cannot preserve the meaning of its own claims across domains.

#### Boundary

SymK governs the minimum role and disclosure of Context. Derived projects define which contextual dimensions and evidence matter in law, infrastructure, medicine, or another domain.

### 6.3 Scope

#### Minimum working definition

> **Scope is the declared boundary of applicability, attribution, validity, authority, or responsibility for a governed concept, claim, capability, decision, process, or artifact.**

Scope answers “how far does this statement or authority claim to reach?” It may be expressed across domain, time, jurisdiction, population, task, purpose, assumptions, stakes, version, organization, or deployment.

#### Context versus Scope

Context describes relevant conditions. Scope declares the boundary within those or other dimensions for which an attribution or governing claim is made.

For example:

- the complete procedural posture is part of a legal Context;
- a legal conclusion’s Scope may be one jurisdiction, court, issue, and time;
- changed Context may move a case outside that Scope;
- Applicability asks whether the conclusion may responsibly be used in the changed Context under its declared Scope.

#### Why foundational

Bounded jurisdiction is part of the Stage-Accepted SymK identity. Knowledge, Intelligence, authority, responsibility, validation, compatibility, and project specialization all require declared scope. Scope cannot be supplied only by implementation defaults or local intuition.

#### Boundary

Scope does not guarantee that a claim is true, an authority legitimate, or a model adequate. It states the reach being claimed so that those questions can be tested.

### 6.4 Domain

#### Minimum working definition

> **A Domain is a bounded field of inquiry or practice within which questions, concepts, Knowledge, methods, standards, roles, and consequences acquire specialized meaning.**

A Domain may be professional, scientific, technical, institutional, organizational, civic, or hybrid. It need not correspond to one academic discipline, industry, database, organization, or ontology.

#### Domain versus Scope and Context

- Domain identifies an organized field of specialized meaning and practice.
- Scope states the reach of one attribution, claim, authority, or artifact.
- Context supplies the relevant conditions of one inquiry or action.

A DIA has a declared Domain, but each question, answer, participant, and authority within it may have a narrower Scope and a changing Context.

#### Why foundational

Domain is part of the name and identity of the system class SymK exists to create. Without a minimum Domain concept, “Domain Intelligence Amplifier,” “domain Knowledge,” “domain reasoning,” and “domain purpose” can be interpreted inconsistently or reduced to a product category label.

#### Ownership boundary

SymK owns only this minimum cross-project meaning. A derived project owns:

- the identity and purpose of its particular Domain;
- its Domain Ontology and Logic;
- its substantive concepts and methods;
- applicable professional and external authorities;
- its reasoning standards and answer thresholds.

LexBrain may define the legal Domain; sshConnectivity a secure-connectivity Domain; IaaC an infrastructure design and operation Domain. None may redefine the minimum SymK meaning of Domain for every other project.

### 6.5 Representation

#### Minimum working definition

> **A Representation is a situated form through which selected aspects of a subject are made available for interpretation, communication, reasoning, storage, or action.**

Representations include natural language, diagrams, demonstrations, rules, logical formulas, graphs, records, schemas, code, embeddings, model parameters, procedures, measurements, and performances when they stand for or expose selected aspects of another subject.

#### Constitutive boundaries

- A Representation is not identical to the represented subject.
- A Representation always selects and may omit, transform, simplify, distort, or add structure.
- Multiple Representations may express overlapping aspects without being interchangeable.
- A precise Representation is not necessarily epistemically adequate.
- A Representation may be false, misleading, obsolete, or misapplied while remaining a Representation.
- Meaning does not migrate automatically to the easiest format to compute.

#### Why foundational

SymK’s central epistemic correction distinguishes Knowledge from claims, assets, models, and stored forms. The two-lane concept, project specialization, formal semantics, reasoning, provenance, and implementation all depend on a stable Representation concept. Delegating the distinction entirely to projects would allow storage or formalization to redefine Knowledge and other SymK concepts.

#### Boundary

SymK does not require one universal Representation, one complete encoding, or the claim that every dimension of Knowledge, Intelligence, skill, experience, or Context is representable without remainder.

## 7. Stage-Accepted governed supporting distinctions

### 7.1 Relationship

> **Relationship is the general semantic category through which two or more governed subjects are associated in a stated way and Scope.**

The generic category is necessary, but SymK gains meaning from typed relationships—such as specializes, represents, projects, bears, applies within, challenges, causes, authorizes, or cooperates—not from treating every association as one universal object type.

Accepted disposition:

- govern Relationship as a supporting semantic category;
- govern material relationship types independently with source, target, direction, cardinality where relevant, Context, Scope, status, and authority;
- do not require every Relationship to be an Entity;
- do not prohibit a later S2 or P2 meta-model from representing a material relationship as a first-class object when its identity, provenance, lifecycle, or participation requires it.

This preserves the valid result of PRR-0002—semantic relation is not reducible to pointer or foreign key—without accepting its unresolved claim that Relationship is universally an Entity.

### 7.2 Applicability

> **Applicability is the relationship through which a concept, claim, capability, rule, model, decision, or artifact is judged relevant and usable in a Context under its declared Scope and governing criteria.**

Applicability is not truth, validity, authorization, or success. A proposition may be true but irrelevant; a model valid within one Scope but inapplicable to a changed Context; an applicable rule may still require authorized use.

Accepted disposition: governed supporting relationship spanning Context and Scope, rather than an independent foundational property. The existing `FP-002 Applicability` placeholder provides no contrary evidence.

### 7.3 Bearer

> **A Bearer is the subject to which a capability, status, role, responsibility, identity, or other governed predicate is attributed within a Scope.**

Bearer is an attribution role, not a claim that the subject is an Entity, legal person, autonomous agent, or morally responsible actor. A person, organization, artificial system, collective, distributed arrangement, or hybrid system may be a candidate Bearer depending on the predicate and evidence.

Accepted disposition: governed supporting role used across 2.1.3, 2.1.5, 2.1.6, and 2.1.7. Those packages decide the conditions for bearing Intelligence, Education, responsibility, or Amplified Intelligence.

### 7.4 Projection

> **Projection is the governed relationship through which selected dimensions of a source are expressed in another Representation, with transformations, additions, omissions, and known loss declared.**

Projection is already part of the Stage-Accepted DR-005 interlayer model. It should be governed as a relationship under Representation and the two-lane alignment rules, not presumed to require a separate foundational concept package.

## 8. Imported or engineering-level concepts

### 8.1 System

SymK uses “system” in its ordinary systems-engineering sense: an organized whole considered through interacting parts, boundaries, behavior, and environment. SymK requires specific system concepts—cooperative system and Domain Intelligence Amplifier—but does not need to own a complete universal System concept.

Accepted disposition: imported supporting concept. Later packages must declare the boundary and organization of any system claimed as a Bearer. They may not expand the system opportunistically merely to attribute success or evade responsibility.

### 8.2 Entity

The current definition—“a first-class engineering object within the SymK conceptual model”—states a modeling decision, not a necessary SymK meaning. The ability to possess metadata, lifecycle, relationships, or JSON/YAML fields shows that a subject is useful to model independently; it does not show that Entity is foundational to SymK.

Accepted disposition:

- do not admit Entity as a 2.1 foundational concept;
- preserve the historical `FC-001` work as evidence;
- treat Entity as a candidate engineering meta-model primitive or S2 reference-model abstraction in 2.5;
- allow projects and models to define appropriate entity categories without claiming a universal SymK ontology;
- use “governed subject” in conceptual analysis where no Entity commitment is required.

This disposition does not claim that entities do not exist or that entity modeling is unhelpful. It denies that first-class engineering treatment is sufficient for foundational admission.

## 9. Structural dependency map

| Source | Relationship | Target | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | is evaluated within | Scope | Sameness depends on which changes and boundaries are relevant |
| Context | supplies conditions for | interpretation and application | Meaning, evidence, and responsible use depend on relevant conditions |
| Scope | bounds | applicability and attribution | Declares how far a claim, capability, authority, or responsibility reaches |
| Domain | organizes and specializes | concepts, Knowledge, methods, roles, and standards | Provides a field of specialized meaning without owning universal SymK concepts |
| Domain | contributes to | Context and Scope | Domain is often one relevant condition and boundary, but is not identical to either |
| Applicability | relates | subject, Context, Scope, and criteria | Tests whether use is appropriate under current conditions |
| Projection | maps | source to Representation | Selects and transforms dimensions with declared additions, omissions, and loss |
| Representation | expresses selected aspects of | governed subject | Makes aspects available without becoming identical to the source |
| Bearer | links attribution to | governed subject | Identifies what is claimed to possess a capacity, status, role, or responsibility |
| System | may serve as | Bearer | Requires an independently justified boundary and organization |
| Relationship | types | semantic association | Specific relation meaning cannot be replaced by a generic edge |
| Entity | may model | governed subjects or relationships | Engineering convenience does not establish semantic parenthood |

### 9.1 Four questions that must remain separate

| Question | Governing concept or distinction |
|---|---|
| What subject is this across change? | Identity |
| What relevant conditions surround this interpretation or use? | Context |
| How far is this attribution or authority claimed to apply? | Scope |
| Does this subject properly apply in these conditions under that boundary? | Applicability |

### 9.2 Representation chain

```text
Governed subject
    ↓ selected through Projection
Representation
    ↓ interpreted in Context
Candidate meaning or use
    ↓ tested against Scope and criteria
Applicability judgment
```

The chain is not a claim that every interpretation is correct. Each transition requires evidence and may be challenged.

## 10. Minimum Ontology and Logic boundary

This package supplies the conceptual part of DQ-018 without selecting an ontology or logic.

### 10.1 Minimum Ontology commitments SymK requires

SymK must be able to distinguish:

- a governed concept from its terms, instances, and Representations;
- Identity from identifier, state, and version;
- Context, Scope, Domain, and Applicability;
- source, Projection, and Representation;
- Bearer from the capability, status, role, or responsibility attributed;
- generic Relationship from specific governed relationship types;
- universal SymK meaning from project specialization;
- conceptual subject from modeling Entity.

These are semantic distinctions, not a commitment to one upper ontology, class hierarchy, or metaphysical inventory.

### 10.2 Minimum Logic commitments SymK requires

SymK must be able to state and test that:

- terms, claims, attributions, and constraints have declared Scope and version;
- typed relations preserve source and target meaning;
- specialization cannot contradict or weaken applicable parent meaning silently;
- contradiction is assessed only after Scope, Context, version, and vocabulary are normalized;
- formal inference is conditional on its premises, semantics, rules, and Applicability;
- absence of a represented fact does not acquire one universal meaning across all projections;
- a projection’s additional constraint is not attributed to the source without governed adoption.

This does not select first-order logic, description logic, open-world or closed-world semantics, monotonicity, temporal logic, probabilistic logic, or a reasoning engine. Those choices remain for 2.5, derived projects, and 2.9 within their jurisdictions.

## 11. Cross-domain compatibility probes

### 11.1 LexBrain

- **Domain:** Brazilian legal inquiry and practice as bounded by LexBrain’s project Constitution.
- **Context:** facts, procedural posture, court, date, parties, requested relief, applicable sources, stakes.
- **Scope:** jurisdiction, issue, time, procedural stage, professional use, and confidence required for one answer.
- **Identity:** continuity of a legal source, matter, claim, argument, or governed concept across versions and representations.
- **Representation:** legislation text, judgment, petition, argument graph, citation record, or embedding.
- **Projection:** LibScan extraction, ontology mapping, Composer structure, or formal rule selection.
- **Bearer:** lawyer, court, institution, AI component, team, or hybrid arrangement depending on the attributed capacity or authority.

No universal Entity class is required to preserve these distinctions.

### 11.2 sshConnectivity

- **Domain:** secure remote connectivity diagnosis, configuration, and operation.
- **Context:** hosts, network path, operating system, credentials, policy, time, observed logs, and runtime state.
- **Scope:** environment, host set, protocol version, organization, deployment, and authorized operation.
- **Identity:** continuity of a host, connection profile, tunnel, failure, or concept across configuration changes.
- **Representation:** configuration, topology, log, schema, command output, or diagnostic narrative.
- **Projection:** parsing runtime state into a diagnostic model or generating a validated configuration.
- **Bearer:** engineer, service, automation, organization, or hybrid diagnostic system depending on the claim.

The same structural kernel applies without importing legal Ontology.

### 11.3 IaaC

- **Domain:** infrastructure architecture, provisioning, operation, compliance, and recovery.
- **Context:** provider, organization, region, environment, workload, regulation, failure state, and resource limits.
- **Scope:** account, environment, region, version, policy, and lifecycle phase.
- **Identity:** continuity of a resource, desired state, architecture decision, or governed concept across deployment changes.
- **Representation:** design, code, plan, state file, policy, topology, or operational observation.
- **Projection:** translating architecture into infrastructure code, validation rules, or deployment plans.
- **Bearer:** architect, platform team, toolchain, organization, or hybrid system depending on capability and responsibility.

The translation supports cross-project reach while preserving project-specific concepts and authorities.

## 12. Counterexamples and failure tests

### 12.1 Same identifier, different Identity

A concept is replaced radically but retains the same code to avoid migration work. Identifier continuity cannot conceal semantic replacement.

### 12.2 Different identifiers, same Identity

The same judgment has court, publisher, and internal identifiers. Multiple identifiers do not prove multiple judgments.

### 12.3 Context collapsed into Scope

A model declares use in “Brazilian law” but omits that the proceeding is criminal, interlocutory, confidential, and under a changed procedural rule. A broad Scope label does not preserve material Context.

### 12.4 Scope treated as truth

A false claim is carefully labeled for one jurisdiction and date. Accurate Scope does not make it true or justified.

### 12.5 Domain treated as product taxonomy

A product dropdown labels content “legal.” The label does not establish a coherent Domain, domain Knowledge, standards, roles, or reasoning.

### 12.6 Representation treated as Knowledge

A graph contains a richly linked claim with provenance. Structural richness does not establish Knowledge or responsible reliance.

### 12.7 Projection silently strengthens the source

A schema requires exactly one responsible party where the concept allows distributed responsibility. The cardinality is a projection choice until governed adoption changes the source.

### 12.8 Generic Relationship erases semantics

A graph uses one `relatedTo` edge for supports, contradicts, cites, authorizes, causes, and supersedes. Connectivity is preserved while the meaning required for reasoning is destroyed.

### 12.9 Entity hierarchy becomes universal Ontology

A convenient implementation base class is declared the parent of Knowledge, Intelligence, Context, and Relationship. Code reuse has silently acquired S2 semantic authority.

### 12.10 Opportunistic system boundary

A model’s answer is called system-level Intelligence when successful, but external tools and human corrections are excluded when responsibility is assessed. Bearer and System boundaries must be stable enough for attribution and consequence review.

## 13. Alternatives considered

### Alternative A — Retain Entity and Relationship as the first universal primitives

**Strength:** supplies a simple meta-model and preserves current work.
**Failure:** first-class engineering treatment is mistaken for semantic necessity; generic categories preempt the meaning of specific concepts and relations.

### Alternative B — Admit every cross-cutting structural term as a foundational concept

**Strength:** maximal explicitness and individual versioning.
**Failure:** creates an inflated foundation in which properties, roles, and relations become independent objects merely because they recur.

### Alternative C — Define no structural concepts until Knowledge and Intelligence

**Strength:** avoids abstract modeling.
**Failure:** later definitions would use Identity, Context, Scope, Domain, Representation, and Bearer inconsistently and then retrofit their meanings.

### Alternative D — Select the minimum structural semantic kernel

**Strength:** admits only independently necessary meanings while governing supporting relations and imported concepts explicitly.
**Risk:** the concept-versus-supporting-distinction boundary may need revision when 2.5 tests packaging and machine-semantic alignment.
**Adopted:** Identity, Context, Scope, Domain, and Representation as foundational; Relationship, Applicability, Bearer, and Projection as governed supporting distinctions; System as imported support; Entity as engineering-model candidate.

## 14. Stage-Accepted decision — SYMK-2X-DR-010

> **SymK 2.1 shall use a minimum structural semantic kernel rather than a universal Entity-first ontology. Identity, Context, Scope, Domain, and Representation are Stage-Accepted as foundational SymK concepts because the accepted identity, value chain, constitutional inquiry, two-lane model, cross-project inheritance, and responsible application cannot remain coherent if their meanings are delegated entirely to projects or implementations. Relationship, Applicability, Bearer, and Projection are Stage-Accepted as governed supporting semantic distinctions or roles: they are necessary and may carry binding meaning without requiring independent foundational-concept status. System is Stage-Accepted as an imported supporting concept whose specific SymK classes and attribution boundaries must be governed; SymK does not claim general systems theory. Entity, as currently defined by first-class engineering treatment, is removed from foundational status and preserved as an engineering meta-model or reference-model candidate for 2.5. Specific relationship types must preserve their own semantics and may be represented as first-class objects where justified, but no universal rule makes every Relationship an Entity. Context states relevant conditions; Scope declares claimed reach; Domain identifies a specialized field of inquiry or practice; Applicability relates a subject and current Context under declared Scope and criteria. Identity is distinct from identifier, state, version, and Representation. Projection maps selected source dimensions into a Representation with additions, omissions, transformations, and semantic loss declared. These dispositions establish the minimum Ontology and Logic boundary needed for later concept analysis without selecting a formal language, final `FC-*` package, domain Ontology, or implementation model.**

## 15. Consequences of acceptance

### 15.1 For 2.1.3–2.1.8

- Every definition must declare relevant Context and Scope.
- Every capability or status attribution must identify its Bearer.
- Knowledge, Intelligence, responsibility, Education, and Amplified Intelligence cannot be inferred from a Representation alone.
- Every DIA must declare a Domain without treating a category label as a domain model.
- Projection and Representation consequences must remain distinct.

### 15.2 For the current foundational corpus

- `FC-003 Identity` becomes evidence for a future foundational Identity package, but its present definition and JSON/YAML models are not accepted automatically.
- `FC-004 Context` remains a placeholder whose future concept is supported by this decision.
- Scope, Domain, and Representation require candidate tracking without assigning final `FC-*` numbers in 2.1.
- `FC-001 Entity` and `FC-002 Relationship` retain historical identity and evidence; their current foundational statuses are not carried into the 2.x baseline.
- `FP-002 Applicability` remains a placeholder; its future form should reflect a governed relationship among subject, Context, Scope, and criteria.
- The final split among concepts, properties, relationships, and projection artifacts remains for 2.5.

### 15.3 For Foundation Papers

- All v0.2 papers must use Identity, Context, Scope, Domain, Representation, Projection, Bearer, and System consistently.
- They must distinguish a represented Context or identifier from the governed subject.
- They must not treat a generic `Entity` model as part of the philosophical definition.

### 15.4 For derived projects

- Projects inherit the structural meanings while owning domain-specific Context dimensions, Scope rules, Domain Ontology and Logic, identifiers, representations, projections, and system boundaries.
- A different database, ontology language, or class hierarchy remains compatible when the governing distinctions are preserved.

## 16. Preserved dissent and open questions

1. Scope may ultimately be better represented as a governed property or relationship rather than an independent foundational concept.
2. Domain may be definable inside the DIA concept instead of requiring its own foundational package.
3. Context may be too broad to govern usefully without a taxonomy of contextual dimensions; that taxonomy may be domain-owned.
4. Relationship may deserve independent foundational status because typed semantic association is pervasive and can possess identity, provenance, and lifecycle.
5. Bearer may deserve foundational status if later attribution analysis shows that it has independent conditions beyond a relational role.
6. Entity may prove valuable as a governed S2 reference meta-model even if it remains non-foundational.
7. Representation and Projection may require a joint concept package rather than one concept plus one supporting relationship.
8. The proposed minimum Logic commitments may still conceal assumptions about negation, absence, contradiction, time, and modality that 2.1.4 must expose.

These concerns do not prevent use of the structural kernel. Evidence from 2.1.3–2.1.8 and package reconstruction in 2.5 may reopen individual dispositions through DR-009 governance.

## 17. Scope boundary

DR-010 does not:

- provide mature Knowledge or Intelligence definitions;
- decide what kinds of system can bear Knowledge or Intelligence;
- assign responsibility or legal personality;
- define a complete Context taxonomy;
- construct an upper ontology;
- select FOL, OWL, SHACL, rules, schemas, or database structures;
- assign final `FC-*`, `FP-*`, or relationship identifiers;
- alter historical concept files or statuses;
- or authorize canonical migration.
