## 1. Question and analytical result

> How should SymK represent professional amplification and transformation when capability, tasks, roles, service output, employment, institutional capacity, dependency, deskilling, tacit Knowledge, succession, and future-expert formation can change at different levels and in different directions?

Stream E supports the following working answer:

> **Professional amplification is never established by increased output, adoption, automation, reduced labor, credential, authority, employment, or profession-wide statistics alone. It requires an Amplification claim about an identified organized Bearer—such as a practitioner, team, institution, or configured system—under the Stream A–D subject, baseline, profile, dependency, integration, lifecycle, and Evidence controls. A role, task, service output, occupational label, or abstract profession is not automatically an Intelligence Bearer.**

> **Professional transformation is a multi-level change profile. It may include capability gain, loss, new integration, changed task composition, role and Authority redistribution, dependence, concentration, service expansion, displacement, deskilling, reskilling, changed affected consequences, and altered formation or succession paths. No one dimension represents the whole transformation, and improvement at one level cannot erase loss at another.**

> **Retained capability is a scoped temporal qualification: capability the same identified Bearer can manifest after a declared support withdrawal, degradation, substitution, or delay under stated resources and conditions. It is neither universally required for Amplification nor equivalent to unsupported self-sufficiency. Where responsible or evolvable operation materially relies on capability that can decay, disappear, or cease to be externally available, the arrangement must expose a credible capability-continuity path or an unresolved dependency/gap. That path may involve Education, Training, practice, mentoring, recruitment, external provision, redesigned work, preserved challenge, succession, recovery, or other Domain-governed means. No single means is universal.**

> **DR-013's universal negative answer remains intact: Education is not constitutive of every persistent DIA. The narrower candidate conclusion is conditional—persistent responsible/evolvable operation cannot silently consume professional or expert capability while eliminating every practical path by which the materially required capability is sustained, renewed, challenged, recovered, or succeeded.**

The working model has ten commitments:

1. **Level separation:** practitioner, role, team, institution, configured system, service organization, profession/community, output, beneficiary, and future entrant remain distinct.
2. **Bearer discipline:** professional labels, credentials, tasks, and outputs do not select the amplified Bearer.
3. **Transformation plurality:** capability, task, role, authority, employment, service, dependency, distribution, and formation changes remain separately represented.
4. **No automation shortcut:** task automation, labor reduction, productivity, and service scale do not establish Intelligence Amplification.
5. **No preservation dogma:** eliminating a former task or manual step is not automatically deskilling, displacement, or irresponsible operation.
6. **Retained-capability qualification:** retention is scoped by Bearer, capability, support condition, time, variation, and recovery horizon.
7. **Tacit-Knowledge non-reification:** difficult-to-articulate practice remains evidentially important without becoming an occult substance or universal anti-automation veto.
8. **Formation-path visibility:** removed work must be tested for its actual formative function, not preserved merely because it was traditional.
9. **Capability continuity:** material expert dependencies require a credible continuity, replenishment, substitution, or honest gap account for responsible/evolvable qualification.
10. **Policy jurisdiction:** exact labor, education, credential, staffing, professional, economic, and legal choices remain Domain, constitutional, organizational, and external-authority matters.

These directions are analytical only. Stream F must consolidate the complete 2.1.7 evidence before any Proposed DR-015 can exist.

## 2. Authority, status, and force

This artifact supplies evidence for Stream F. It has no Stage-Accepted semantic, constitutional, professional, labor, educational, economic, legal, or operational authority.

It distinguishes:

1. an actual capability or professional-practice condition;
2. an Amplification claim about an identified Bearer;
3. a transformation claim about one or more levels;
4. an employment, task, service, market, or institutional outcome;
5. a Formation, Learning, Training, or Education claim;
6. a Power, Authority, Authorization, or Responsibility relation;
7. a responsible/evolvable qualification;
8. a value or policy judgment; and
9. an assessment, governance status, credential, or record.

No status, credential, forecast, plan, dashboard, workforce category, curriculum, or “future-ready” label creates its represented condition. Uses of “must” below state the working model's coherence conditions, not accepted obligations.

## 4. Professional-level separation

Statements such as “the profession was amplified,” “the service became intelligent,” or “experts were replaced” can compress non-equivalent subjects and outcomes.

| Level or object | Candidate meaning | Prohibited substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Individual practitioner | Identified organized Human or other subject performing scoped professional practice | credential or tool use proves Amplification |
| Professional role | Governed position, function, expectations, authority, and relations | abstract role is an Intelligence Bearer |
| Task or activity | Bounded work function or episode | task performance is professional capability |
| Team | Identified organized multi-member Bearer candidate | member gain/loss copies to team |
| Institution | Persistent organized subject with roles, memory, governance, and capability candidate | legal identity proves Intelligence |
| Configured system | Stable organized Human/artificial/institutional configuration | successful output proves whole-level Bearer |
| Service organization | Identified organized subject sustaining a service across episodes | service label or demand is a Bearer |
| Profession or professional community | Social/institutional field, population, or organized community under a declared boundary | occupational category is one Intelligence |
| Service output | Answer, report, diagnosis, design, decision support, action, or quantity delivered | output receives Amplified Intelligence |
| Client, user, or beneficiary | Subject receiving access, assistance, service, or claimed benefit | benefit proves capability gain |
| Affected subject | Subject bearing material consequence or risk | affected status makes subject Participant or responsible |
| Future entrant or expert | Prospective trajectory and population under a declared formation frame | forecasted category is a present Bearer |

Every material claim must select the proper level. One transformation can generate several separately true and conflicting claims.

## 5. Professional, role, credential, and Authority

“Professional” may indicate:

- an individual engaged in a recognized practice;
- occupation of a role;
- possession of a credential or license;
- membership in a professional body;
- adherence to standards or ethics;
- recognized expertise;
- employment or service relationship; or
- participation in an organized professional community.

These do not entail one another universally.

For this stream, a **professional-practice subject** is an identified organized subject whose scoped activities are governed by a declared combination of Domain purpose, capability expectations, role relations, standards, Authority, Responsibility, or external recognition. This is an analytical convenience, not a proposed Foundational concept.

Credential, expertise, Intelligence, capability, Authority, Authorization, role occupancy, performance, employment, Responsibility, legal status, and ethical adequacy remain distinct. A credential can be valid while capability has decayed; an uncredentialed subject may have capability without recognized Authority; an artificial component may perform a function without becoming a licensed professional or responsible subject.

## 6. Profession and professional-community claims

An abstract profession is a classification, not automatically an organized Bearer. A profession-level capability claim must identify a subject such as:

- a constituted professional body;
- a community of practice with supportable organization and continuity;
- a distributed field with explicit boundary, processes, memory, and collective capacity;
- an institutionally organized service network; or
- another lowest adequate organized level.

Profession-wide employment, adoption, output, wages, productivity, error rates, or credential counts describe distributions or outcomes. They do not establish one profession-level Intelligence configuration.

A useful profession-level transformation profile may still represent:

- distribution of practitioner capability gains and losses;
- changed entry, progression, discretion, authority, and work;
- institutional and configured-system capacity;
- access, quality, cost, and affected consequences;
- provider and infrastructure concentration;
- formation, recruitment, mentoring, and succession pathways;
- preserved, displaced, or newly required expertise; and
- uncertainty, regional variation, dissent, and countertrends.

This profile is a Representation, not a collective Bearer attribution.

## 7. Service output and service capacity

An output cannot itself be an amplified Intelligence Bearer. Distinguish:

- **output change:** quality, volume, speed, coverage, consistency, form, or cost changes;
- **service performance change:** an organized service produces different outcomes across cases;
- **service capacity change:** an identified service organization can address a broader, harder, or more varied question class;
- **professional capability change:** an identified practitioner/team/institution/configured system changes capability;
- **access change:** more or different beneficiaries can obtain the service; and
- **value change:** the result is judged better, more beneficial, justified, or lower in total cost.

“The service was amplified” is too compressed unless it is explicitly resolved into one or more of these claims.

## 8. Professional transformation

Stream E uses:

> **Professional transformation is a scoped, multi-level change in the organization of professional capability, tasks, roles, Authority, Responsibility, participation, employment, service delivery, dependencies, Power, affected consequences, and formation or succession paths across time.**

Transformation is descriptive and value-neutral. It can include:

- Amplification at one or more levels;
- capability loss or nondevelopment;
- task elimination, creation, recomposition, or redistribution;
- role expansion, narrowing, splitting, fusion, or succession;
- changed judgment, review, challenge, and intervention;
- new dependencies and alternatives;
- service access, scale, quality, cost, or risk changes;
- employment creation, displacement, or changed conditions;
- concentration or redistribution of Power and capability;
- changed tacit/practical Knowledge use;
- changed Training, Education, mentoring, apprenticeship, or practice;
- institutional memory, maintenance, and succession change; and
- beneficiary and affected-subject distribution.

No favorable or unfavorable value judgment follows from “transformation.”

## 9. Task and function decomposition

Professional roles are not indivisible. A material analysis may distinguish:

- inquiry and problem framing;
- source gathering and retrieval;
- interpretation and contextualization;
- calculation, simulation, generation, or drafting;
- hypothesis formation and comparison;
- judgment under uncertainty;
- challenge, verification, and error detection;
- communication, explanation, and relationship work;
- decision, Authorization, and reliance;
- execution and intervention;
- monitoring, feedback, correction, and repair;
- documentation, trace, and record;
- mentoring, demonstration, supervision, and formation;
- coordination, administration, and institutional maintenance; and
- escalation, refusal, advocacy, representation, and remedy.

This is not a universal task taxonomy. Domains own their function models. Decomposition is required only to prevent one automated function from being represented as replacement of the whole role, capability, or profession.

## 10. Assistance, automation, and changed work

Automation means a process or component performs a function with reduced or altered direct Human execution. It may:

- remove repetitive work;
- increase capacity or consistency;
- change timing and scale;
- expose new cases or risks;
- move work to review, exception handling, or data preparation;
- create new configuration, monitoring, maintenance, and challenge work;
- reduce practice in a capability;
- increase cognitive load or vigilance demand;
- shift hidden labor to providers, affected subjects, or lower-status workers; or
- leave relevant reasoning capability unchanged.

Automation is therefore neither professional Amplification nor professional replacement by default. It is a mechanism and work-allocation change whose capability, output, distribution, and lifecycle effects need independent Evidence.

## 11. Substitution

Substitution is object- and function-specific:

> **Substitution occurs when one identified subject, component, process, resource, or arrangement performs or supplies a materially comparable function in place of another under a declared frame.**

Distinguish:

| Form | Replaced object | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Task substitution | execution of a bounded task | does not prove role replacement |
| Method substitution | procedure or technique | capability may persist through a new method |
| Component substitution | tool, model, provider, or participant function | system Identity may or may not persist |
| Judgment substitution | practical selection or interpretation formerly performed by another subject | output equivalence does not establish capability or Authority equivalence |
| Role-function substitution | material set of functions within a role | role may be recomposed rather than removed |
| Service substitution | one service organization or mode replaces another | beneficiary access and responsibility may change |
| Capability substitution | performance is supplied externally instead of borne by the prior subject | retained capability may decline or remain |
| Formation substitution | one developmental path replaces another | exposure equivalence does not establish formative equivalence |

Comparability must include relevant variation, error, context, challenge, recovery, affected consequences, resources, and time—not only average output.

## 12. Replacement and displacement

Replacement and displacement remain distinct.

- **Replacement:** a subject, component, process, role realization, service, or arrangement is succeeded by another for a declared function or system position.
- **Task displacement:** a subject no longer performs a task or performs less of it.
- **Role displacement:** a role loses enough functions, authority, demand, or organizational position to be removed or fundamentally changed.
- **Employment displacement:** an individual's or population's work opportunity ends or contracts through a material causal pathway.
- **Capability displacement:** a capability is no longer practiced, developed, requested, recognized, or locally available because performance is supplied elsewhere.
- **formation displacement:** a developmental opportunity or pathway disappears, narrows, or becomes inaccessible.
- **geographic/institutional displacement:** capability or service moves between regions, organizations, or populations.

Replacement may occur without employment displacement—for example, an unfilled task changes method. Displacement may occur without technical replacement because demand, policy, funding, or organization changes. Causal attribution and alternatives must remain explicit.

## 13. Role recomposition and changed Authority

A transformed role may:

- shift from execution to framing, review, intervention, or relationship work;
- gain or lose discretion;
- receive broader cases with less time;
- supervise systems without adequate information or control;
- become responsible for outputs it cannot inspect;
- lose direct practice while retaining formal Authority;
- gain system-configuration or provider-management functions;
- split across different professional and technical roles;
- become more accessible or more dependent; or
- disappear and be succeeded by a different role.

Role change does not prove capability gain, deskilling, promotion, demotion, or replacement. Formal Authority can rise while effective control falls. Responsibility cannot be assigned from a “human in the loop” label or changed job description alone.

## 14. Professional capability profile

A professional capability profile may need to represent:

- Domain interpretation and contextual sensitivity;
- problem framing and question reformulation;
- practical and tacit judgment;
- reasoning across relevant variation;
- uncertainty recognition and calibration;
- source/evidence assessment;
- error detection and anomaly recognition;
- challenge, dissent, refusal, and escalation;
- explanation and communication;
- action and intervention;
- relationship, care, negotiation, or representation where Domain-relevant;
- tool, model, data, and dependency understanding;
- supervision, monitoring, fallback, and recovery;
- ethical, legal, professional, and institutional judgment;
- learning, adaptation, and capability renewal;
- mentoring and formation contribution; and
- integration with team, institution, configured system, and external authority.

This is an open evidence family, not a universal professional taxonomy. Each Domain must identify material dimensions and declared omissions.

## 15. Retained capability

> **Retained capability is capability that the same identified Bearer can continue to manifest across declared relevant variation after a specified supporting intervention, component, provider, interface, or condition is removed, degraded, delayed, substituted, or made unavailable for a declared period and resource frame.**

A retained-capability claim should identify:

1. Bearer, capability, Domain, Scope, Context, and relevant variation;
2. prior support condition and actual integration;
3. withdrawal/degradation/substitution scenario;
4. time since change and expected decay;
5. remaining tools, people, records, infrastructure, and authority;
6. performance, judgment, challenge, and recovery evidence;
7. uncertainty and alternative explanations;
8. safety, affected consequence, and response boundaries;
9. comparison with concurrent support-conditioned capability; and
10. revalidation and expiry conditions.

Retained capability need not mean completely unaided capability. All organized capabilities depend on environments and resources. The claim identifies which support change is being tested.

## 16. Retention is not universally constitutive

Amplification can be real only while an integrated support arrangement is available. A configured system or professional may gain support-conditioned capability without retaining it after removal. Therefore:

- retained capability is not universally necessary for Amplification;
- complete self-sufficiency is not a coherent universal target;
- reliance on tools, colleagues, institutions, language, infrastructure, or external experts is not automatically deficient;
- removal tests help identify attribution, fragility, fallback, and continuity but do not assign value by themselves; and
- Domain, responsibility, safety, resilience, and professional standards may impose narrower retention or fallback requirements.

Claims must say “support-conditioned,” “retained,” “degraded-condition,” “recovery,” or another supported qualification rather than allowing the audience to infer self-sufficient capability.

## 17. Supervisory, evaluative, fallback, and recovery capability

Operation may rely on different capabilities:

- **execution capability:** perform the task directly;
- **framing capability:** identify the right problem, Scope, constraints, and alternatives;
- **evaluative capability:** assess output, Evidence, limitations, and applicability;
- **challenge capability:** detect and pursue possible error, conflict, exclusion, or harm;
- **intervention capability:** change, stop, override, or redirect operation;
- **fallback capability:** perform or route the function when the normal arrangement fails;
- **recovery capability:** restore capability, reconstruct context, or reconstitute service after disruption;
- **learning capability:** acquire or renew materially required capacity; and
- **formation capability:** support development of later practitioners or successors.

These do not entail one another. A professional may be able to use output without being able to evaluate it; may evaluate without directly executing; may recognize failure without authority to intervene; or may hold formal override without time, information, practice, or alternatives.

“Human supervision” is effective only when the relevant capabilities, information, independence, authority, time, access, and response path exist.

## 18. Deskilling

> **Deskilling is a reduction or nondevelopment of relevant capability, judgment, integration, practice, transfer, recovery, or challenge at an identified Bearer level, connected under a declared causal frame to changed work, substitution, automation, dependency, role, incentives, or formative conditions.**

Distinguish:

- less task execution without capability loss;
- capability decay through reduced practice;
- nondevelopment because formative exposure disappears;
- role narrowing without actual capability change;
- loss of discretion or Authority;
- dependence that raises recovery time;
- deprofessionalization through organizational or institutional change;
- obsolete capability intentionally retired;
- beneficial delegation with preserved evaluation/recovery capability; and
- changed specialization rather than global loss.

Deskilling is not established by fewer manual steps, lower task frequency, changed tools, novice status, or lower head count alone. It can coexist with higher individual support-conditioned capability or higher whole-system performance.

## 19. Reskilling, upskilling, and new specialization

New capabilities may emerge through changed work:

- system framing and configuration;
- cross-source synthesis;
- model/data/provider evaluation;
- exception and failure handling;
- communication and affected-subject representation;
- challenge and audit;
- integration across professions;
- maintenance, correction, and recovery;
- new Domain specializations; or
- mentoring under transformed practice.

“Reskilling” and “upskilling” are governance or program labels unless actual capability change is evidenced. A new gain does not automatically offset a lost material dimension, displaced population, increased dependence, or eliminated formation path. Trade-offs remain level-, distribution-, purpose-, and authority-qualified.

## 20. Tacit and practical Knowledge

Stream E treats tacit Knowledge as a bounded epistemic and capability problem, not a hidden substance.

> **A tacit/practical-Knowledge claim indicates that material competence, discrimination, judgment, timing, sensorimotor coordination, situational interpretation, relational understanding, or practice is not fully preserved by the currently available explicit Representation, instruction, rule, dataset, model, or verbal account under the declared Scope.**

Such a claim should identify:

- the Bearer and practice;
- the relevant cases and variation;
- what competent manifestation looks like;
- which parts are expressible, demonstrable, imitable, situated, embodied, relational, or institutionally distributed;
- what current representations omit or distort;
- evidence of transfer, loss, substitution, or failure;
- how challenge and intersubjective assessment remain possible; and
- whether the claim is current, Domain-specific, and defeasible.

Tacitness does not prove ineffability, Human exclusivity, permanent non-automation, authority, correctness, or value. Conversely, successful average output does not prove that omitted situational or relational capabilities are irrelevant.

## 21. Concentration

Professional transformation may concentrate:

- capability and practical alternatives;
- Knowledge sources, data, models, and infrastructure;
- provider and market Power;
- professional Authority and decision rights;
- evidence, evaluation, and certification;
- maintenance, recovery, and incident response;
- training, mentoring, and credential pathways;
- employment and economic benefit;
- responsibility or apparent responsibility; and
- the capacity to define the Domain problem and acceptable performance.

Concentration is not automatically harmful or disqualifying. It can support rare expertise, quality, coordination, consistency, investment, or safety. It becomes a material qualification when it changes dependency, access, challenge, single-point failure, bargaining power, exit, succession, affected distribution, or response capacity.

A concentration analysis must not infer that wider distribution is always possible or better. It must expose the trade-off and authority for the choice.

## 22. Dependency and professional autonomy

Professional autonomy is not isolation or total self-sufficiency. It can concern:

- ability to exercise judgment within a role;
- practical alternatives and refusal;
- control over methods and tools;
- access to necessary Evidence;
- freedom from improper interference;
- authority to challenge or escalate;
- capacity to act under outage or provider change;
- collective/institutional self-governance; or
- external legal or professional status.

Tool or provider dependency can increase effective capability while narrowing autonomy in one of these senses. It can also reduce arbitrary burden and expand professional discretion. The direction must be evidenced.

Dependency becomes especially material when the professional remains nominally responsible but lacks:

- knowledge of the dependency;
- access to underlying Evidence;
- capacity to evaluate outputs;
- authority to change or refuse;
- time and resources for effective supervision;
- fallback or recovery options;
- protected challenge; or
- a capable provider/institutional response path.

This does not allocate Responsibility; it exposes a possible governance gap under DR-014.

## 23. Future-expert and capability-renewal pipeline

A future expert is not a present Bearer merely because a role is forecast. Stream E analyzes a **capability-renewal pipeline**:

1. attraction, access, and entry;
2. foundational exposure and conceptual orientation;
3. observation, demonstration, and guided participation;
4. practice on varied cases;
5. timely feedback, correction, and challenge;
6. supervised responsibility with real but bounded consequence;
7. increasing independence, judgment, and exception handling;
8. assessment across relevant variation;
9. participation in professional/community standards and dissent;
10. continuing practice, Learning, and adaptation;
11. mentoring, teaching, supervision, or institutional memory Contribution; and
12. succession, renewal, recovery, and transfer across generations or versions.

No universal profession requires this exact sequence. It is a diagnostic map. Stages can overlap, recur, be supplied institutionally or externally, or use Human, artificial, collective, hybrid, simulated, and real-world conditions subject to Domain authority.

## 24. Formative work and automation

Some work performs both productive and formative functions. Entry-level review, drafting, observation, routine cases, manual calculation, data preparation, direct client contact, failure handling, and participation in team discussion may develop capability. They may also be inefficient, exploitative, unsafe, obsolete, or poor educational designs.

When automation removes a task, the analysis should ask:

1. Did the task actually contribute to relevant Formation, Learning, Training, Education, judgment, or professional socialization?
2. For which learner level, capability, and population?
3. Through which exposure, practice, feedback, challenge, or consequence mechanism?
4. Which parts were merely repetitive burden?
5. What new formative opportunities appear?
6. Can simulation, deliberate practice, mentoring, rotation, direct entry at a different level, or another path preserve the material function?
7. Who has access, who bears cost, and who is excluded?
8. Does the replacement path preserve real variation, consequence, feedback, dissent, and responsibility?
9. What Evidence would show equivalent, superior, or inadequate development?
10. What happens if the automated support fails or disappears?

Traditional work is not preserved merely because it once trained people. A replacement path is not declared adequate merely because a curriculum, simulator, or onboarding module exists.

## 25. Capability continuity, replenishment, and succession

Stream E uses the following candidate qualification:

> **A persistent arrangement has a capability-continuity path when the materially required capabilities on which its operation, supervision, challenge, recovery, correction, responsibility, or succession depend can be supportably maintained, renewed, replenished, externally supplied, substituted, recovered, or succeeded across the relevant horizon, with dependencies, access, Power, uncertainty, and gaps explicit.**

Possible paths include:

- retained practice and continuing professional development;
- Education, Training, apprenticeship, mentoring, rotation, or simulation;
- recruitment from an external capability ecosystem;
- provider or specialist support;
- distributed team or institutional memory;
- redesigned roles and task allocation;
- cross-training and redundancy;
- tool-supported learning and challenge;
- documented procedures plus practiced recovery;
- successor organizations or services; and
- deliberate retirement of a no-longer-needed capability.

No path is universally sufficient. A record or course does not establish capability. External replenishment is valid only if its availability, quality, access, continuity, and provider dependencies are real.

## 26. Narrower responsible/evolvable-DIA qualification

DR-013's universal answer remains **No:** Education is not necessary for every persistent DIA.

Stream E adds a narrower candidate:

> **When a claimed responsible or evolvable persistent DIA materially depends on capabilities that are vulnerable to decay, displacement, concentration, or provider loss—and those capabilities are required for operation, evaluation, challenge, fallback, correction, repair, recovery, responsibility performance, or succession—the qualification requires a proportionate, evidenced capability-continuity path or an explicit unresolved dependency/gap.**

This does not require:

- Education in every DIA;
- Training for every Participant;
- retention of every prior manual skill;
- internal production of every expert;
- a Human fallback for every function;
- one curriculum or credential;
- guaranteed employment or entry;
- preservation of one profession's historical boundary;
- complete independence from providers; or
- infinite continuity.

It prevents a system from claiming responsible/evolvable persistence while relying on expert capability that its own operation predictably erodes or makes unavailable and while offering no credible alternative, succession, recovery, or gap response.

Exact materiality, proportionality, horizon, professional duties, procedures, and remedies remain downstream.

## 27. Power, Responsibility, and affected distribution

Professional transformation can redistribute Power over:

- entry, credential, employment, and progression;
- task and role definition;
- access to tools, models, data, cases, and practice;
- target capability profiles and standards;
- evidence, benchmark, assessment, and certification;
- decision, Authorization, override, and reliance;
- provider selection, update, price, and exit;
- attribution of error, success, and Responsibility;
- voice, refusal, appeal, and protected challenge;
- client/beneficiary access and affected-subject representation; and
- correction, repair, remedy, and succession.

Responsible-qualification analysis should identify who gains capability and benefit, who loses work, capability, access, autonomy, or alternatives, who bears transition and residual cost, and who can challenge or respond. A valid Amplification claim does not settle that distribution.

No professional, institution, provider, deployer, user, system, or displaced worker becomes responsible merely from position in the transformation. Apply DR-014 by object, source, phase, level, capability, authority, and response.

## 28. Professional-transformation claim envelope

A material claim should identify:

1. the transformation object, claimant, purpose, authority, version, and time;
2. Domain, Scope, Context, population, geography, institution, and jurisdiction;
3. practitioner, role, task, team, institution, configured system, service, profession/community, provider, beneficiary, affected-subject, and future-entrant levels;
4. relevant capabilities, profiles, baselines, comparators, variation, gains, losses, unchanged dimensions, and unknowns;
5. assistance, automation, substitution, replacement, displacement, and role-change mechanisms;
6. concurrent, retained, developmental, configured-system, fallback, recovery, and formation capabilities;
7. employment, access, service, output, quality, speed, cost, and value claims as separate;

## 29. Alternatives tested

### A — Increased professional output proves Amplification

**Rejected.** Output can rise through automation, selection, hidden labor, easier cases, or external substitution without Bearer capability gain.

### B — Any automated task replaces the professional

**Rejected.** Tasks, roles, subjects, service organizations, and professions are distinct.

### C — Any task removal is deskilling

**Rejected.** Capability may persist, improve, become obsolete, or shift to a new specialization.

### D — Every manual skill must be retained

**Rejected.** It would prohibit legitimate specialization, tools, delegation, and technical progress while ignoring materiality and Domain authority.

### E — Retained unaided capability is necessary for all Amplification

**Rejected.** Support-conditioned capability can be genuine, and no organized subject is absolutely unaided.

### F — Higher system performance offsets member loss automatically

**Rejected.** Level, distribution, Power, affected consequence, recovery, and future formation remain independent.

### G — New “AI skills” automatically offset professional deskilling

**Rejected.** Both gains and losses require capability Evidence and cannot be netted without a governed target and authority.

### H — Tacit Knowledge is uniquely Human and cannot be represented or automated

**Rejected.** Tacitness is scoped to current representational adequacy and evidence, not a substrate or impossibility claim.

### I — Successful output proves tacit Knowledge was unnecessary

**Rejected.** Relevant variation, relationship, anomaly, challenge, and long-horizon effects may remain untested.

### J — Preserve every traditional formative task

**Rejected.** Traditional work can be burdensome, exploitative, obsolete, or pedagogically weak; preserve material formative functions, not rituals.

### K — A course or simulator automatically replaces workplace formation

**Rejected.** Exposure, practice, variation, consequence, feedback, integration, and transfer require Evidence.

### L — Every persistent responsible DIA requires Education

**Rejected.** Capability can be sustained through several paths, and some bounded systems do not materially depend on internally renewed expert capability.

### M — Future experts are entirely an external labor-market concern

**Rejected.** External supply may be legitimate, but when the arrangement depends on it, availability, access, Power, continuity, and gaps remain material.

### N — Professional displacement makes Amplification false

**Rejected.** Descriptive capability and affected consequences must remain separately honest.

### O — Capability continuity with plural paths and explicit gaps

**Retained as the working direction.** It avoids universal Education and skill-preservation requirements while preventing responsibility and capability laundering.

## 32. Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views

### 32.1 Human View

The Human View must make intelligible:

- what work, judgment, relationship, discretion, identity, and practical alternatives change;
- who gains and loses capability, access, employment, authority, burden, and voice;
- whether supervision and fallback are real;
- how novice, expert, mentor, client, affected subject, and displaced subject experiences differ;
- which traditional practices are formative, burdensome, or both;
- how tacit/practical Knowledge and uncertainty are represented without mystification;
- what capability-continuity path exists; and
- which losses, dissent, and unresolved gaps remain.

### 32.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View should support hypotheses and counterevidence about:

- capability change at practitioner, team, institution, configured-system, and organized-community levels;
- support-conditioned versus retained, fallback, recovery, and developmental capability;
- automation, substitution, task/role/employment/formation displacement, and causal alternatives;
- practice frequency, decay, deskilling, transfer, and recovery;
- formative functions and replacement-path equivalence;
- tacit/practical competence across relevant variation;
- concentration, provider dependence, and outage consequences;
- population distribution, selection, access, and longitudinal change; and
- uncertainty, forecast limits, and boundary sensitivity.

No single benchmark, productivity measure, employment statistic, survey, credential, or experimental design is universally sufficient.

### 32.3 Engineering View

Engineering representations may include:

- versioned subject/role/task/team/institution/configured-system/service/profession boundaries;
- capability profiles, baselines, supports, dependencies, retention horizons, and removal scenarios;
- task-function, automation, substitution, replacement, and displacement graphs;
- Authority, Responsibility, provider, affected-subject, challenge, and response relations;
- practice, feedback, mentoring, assessment, formation, recruitment, and succession paths;
- concentration, alternatives, fallback, recovery, outage, and retirement;
- separate capability, employment, output, value, Authorization, and qualification statuses; and
- Provenance, uncertainty, dissent, expiry, and change impact.

Every workforce model, skill taxonomy, task graph, competency matrix, learning record, simulator, credential, dashboard, or continuity plan is a Representation. It cannot manufacture capability or Formation.

### 32.4 Alignment rule

Material disagreement is Evidence. An Engineering task graph cannot decide that lived professional judgment is irrelevant; a Scientific productivity aggregate cannot erase unequal access or future formation; a Human narrative cannot establish capability across variation without Evidence. The claim remains scoped, disputed, or qualified until differences are reconciled or explicitly preserved.

Stream F must consolidate this answer before DR-015.

### DQ-016 — Artificial Intelligence in every DIA

The proposal-ready non-authoritative answer remains **No**. Professional transformation can occur in fully Human, institutional, artificial, or hybrid arrangements. AI presence neither establishes professional amplification nor determines replacement, deskilling, or formation.

### DQ-019 — Education in every persistent DIA

The Stage-Accepted universal answer remains **No**. Stream E supplies a proposal-ready answer to the narrower remainder: a responsible/evolvable persistent DIA materially dependent on vulnerable capability requires a proportionate capability-continuity path or explicit gap, but Education is only one possible path and is not universal.
