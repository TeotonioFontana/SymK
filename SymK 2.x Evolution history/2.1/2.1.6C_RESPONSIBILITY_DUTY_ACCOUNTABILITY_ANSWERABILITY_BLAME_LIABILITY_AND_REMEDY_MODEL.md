## 1. Question and analytical result

> How must SymK distinguish responsibility, duty, obligation, answerability, accountability, blame, liability, sanction, remedy, repair, and correction when causal Contribution, agency, control, role, authority, authorization, legal status, and consequence may belong to different subjects?

Stream C supports the following working answer:

> **Responsibility is a governed relationship family under which an identified subject at an identified level is connected to a responsibility object—such as a role, condition, decision, action, omission, risk, artifact, process, consequence, or response—through a declared source and governing frame, within stated Domain, Context, Scope, time, and criteria. A material responsibility claim must preserve its factual bases, prospective or retrospective phase, expected response or consequence interface, affected subjects or claimants, forum, status, Evidence, challenge, and lifecycle. Responsibility does not arise from causal Contribution, agency, capability, authority, authorization, role occupancy, legal personality, record, blame, liability, or outcome alone.**

> **Prospective responsibility concerns what a subject is entrusted, expected, or required to attend to before or while conduct and consequences remain governable. A duty is an applicable normative requirement to act, refrain, maintain a condition, decide, supervise, disclose, answer, correct, or otherwise respond under an identified source. An obligation is a particularized binding relation under a governing frame, normally identifying an obligor, an object, a source, conditions, and an authority, claimant, beneficiary, or affected horizon. Duty, obligation, responsibility, role, mandate, authorization, capability, performance, discharge, and breach remain distinct.**

> **Retrospective responsibility concerns an evaluated relation to past conduct, conditions, decisions, omissions, risks, or consequences under stated criteria. It is not identical to causal Contribution, role occupancy, answerability, blame, legal liability, sanction, or remedial duty. A subject may be causally connected without being blameworthy; answerable without having caused the event; liable without moral fault; responsible for correction without being punishable; or blameworthy under one framework while not liable under another.**

> **Answerability is a governed relation in which a subject may be called to provide an account—such as information, explanation, Grounds, justification, acknowledgment, or response—to an identified forum or claimant concerning a stated object and period. Accountability is the wider arrangement through which conduct or conditions can be made answerable, examined against applicable criteria, challenged, assessed, and connected to correction, consequence, review, or remedy. Logs, audits, explanations, committees, sanctions, and punishment may participate in accountability but none is sufficient by itself.**

> **Blame is a negative attribution of fault or culpability under a stated normative framework. Liability is exposure under an applicable legal or institutional framework to a prescribed consequence, duty, claim, or remedy. A sanction is an authoritative adverse consequence for a recognized violation; punishment is a narrower intentionally burdensome response whose rationale is itself normatively governed. A remedy is a governed response available to address a recognized wrong, violation, injury, deprivation, defect, or claim; repair is action directed toward restoring, compensating, rebuilding, healing, or otherwise addressing what was impaired; correction changes an erroneous or defective claim, record, decision, process, artifact, or condition. These relations can overlap but are not interchangeable.**

The minimum is not a complete moral theory, legal code, professional standard, or organizational policy. It is a semantic and governance architecture that makes responsibility claims explicit enough to test, challenge, specialize, and connect without laundering responsibility through Human approval, artificial operation, delegation, contract, hierarchy, distribution, or recordkeeping.

The result separates ten questions:

1. **Occurrence:** what condition, event, decision, action, omission, process, or consequence occurred?
2. **Connection:** what causal, agency, control, role, authority, authorization, dependence, reliance, or benefit relations are evidenced?
3. **Source and frame:** which semantic, constitutional, legal, professional, organizational, contractual, project, operational, or other governing source applies?
4. **Prospective relation:** what was a subject entrusted, expected, required, or permitted to govern before or during the relevant period?
5. **Retrospective relation:** what past object is attributed or assessed, under which criteria and possible defenses, excuses, or limitations?
6. **Answerability and accountability:** who may call whom to account, for what, with what information, authority, process, challenge, response, and effect?
7. **Blame and sanction:** what fault or violation judgment is claimed, by which framework and authority, and with what distinct consequence?
8. **Liability:** what legal or institutionally prescribed exposure exists under which external or Domain authority?
9. **Remedy, repair, and correction:** what response is available or required, to whom, for what purpose, and with what authority and status?
10. **Governance state:** what relation actually holds under the declared frame, and what has merely been assigned, attributed, assessed, accepted, performed, discharged, breached, enforced, remedied, or recorded?

Stream C establishes this minimum only far enough for continued analysis. Stream D must test distributed allocation, whole/member relations, retained responsibility, succession, and responsibility gaps. Stream E must determine the minimum ethical boundary, consent, affected-party standing, challenge, and repair. No actual subject receives responsibility, blame, liability, duty, sanction, remedy, or standing here.

## 3. Why SymK needs a responsibility distinction system

Responsibility language is load-bearing across the Product Vision, Foundation Papers, candidate axioms, derived-project boundaries, Knowledge Engineering lifecycle, and the claim that SymK should help produce better answers under consequence constraints. Without a governed distinction system, “responsible,” “accountable,” “liable,” “answerable,” “at fault,” “owner,” “duty,” “remedy,” or “Human oversight” can conceal incompatible claims.

A weak responsibility model permits:

- every causal contributor to be treated as equally responsible;
- the final proximate actor or approver to absorb the entire history;
- organizational or contractual assignment to overwrite actual control, authority, or external duties;
- artificial systems to become blame sinks while provider, deployer, maintainer, and institution relations disappear;
- capability, expertise, agency, autonomy, or legal personality to manufacture responsibility;
- lack of intention or moral agency to erase strict legal, institutional, role, or remedial relations;
- duty to become proof of breach, and breach to become proof of blame;
- answerability to become explanation production without effective examination or response;
- accountability to become a log, audit, committee, policy, metric, apology, sanction, or punishment;
- liability to become moral blame or universal semantic truth;
- compliance to become responsible conduct regardless of epistemic defect or consequence;
- beneficial outcome to excuse an unauthorized or irresponsible process;
- harmful outcome to retroactively manufacture fault;
- consent, acceptance, signature, or continued use to transfer responsibility;
- remedy to become punishment, or punishment to substitute for repair;
- correction of a record to erase the original decision, conduct, consequence, or status;
- collective, institutional, distributed, configured-system, or hybrid naming to create or erase a responsible subject;
- dissolution, update, replacement, merger, or succession to delete historical responsibility; or
- normative pluralism to become an empty refusal to identify any required dimensions.

The minimum system performs eight jobs:

1. **Relation decomposition:** identify the responsibility bearer, object, phase, source, basis, frame, standard, Scope, time, forum, response, Evidence, status, and challenge.
2. **Prospective/retrospective separation:** prevent entrusted responsibility and duty from collapsing into after-the-fact fault or outcome judgment.
3. **Connection preservation:** retain causal, agency, control, role, authority, delegation, supervision, reliance, and consequence evidence without treating any one relation as sufficient.
4. **Governance-state separation:** distinguish a relation that holds under a declared frame from assignment, attribution, assessment, acceptance, performance, discharge, breach, enforcement, remedy, and record.
5. **Accountability reality:** distinguish nominal answerability or audit from a practical arrangement capable of obtaining, examining, challenging, and acting on an account.
6. **Jurisdiction preservation:** represent legal, professional, organizational, contractual, project, operational, and ethical claims without converting one into universal SymK truth.
7. **Anti-laundering:** prevent automation, delegation, procurement, outsourcing, contracting, approval, hierarchy, or distribution from silently erasing retained relations.
8. **Downstream preparation:** give Streams D–F, 2.2–2.3, 2.5–2.6, and 2.9 stable interfaces without preselecting allocation rules or implementation.

SymK does not need a complete theory of free will, moral desert, tort, criminal guilt, fiduciary obligation, corporate law, professional discipline, insurance, compensation, restorative justice, or punishment. It does need enough structure to prevent responsibility from disappearing or being fabricated when these systems are represented and governed.

## 4. Responsibility as a governed relationship family

### 4.1 Minimum dimensions of a material responsibility claim

A material responsibility claim should identify at least:

1. the claim identifier and claimant or asserting source;
2. the proposed responsibility bearer and exact subject level;
3. the responsibility object: role, condition, decision, act, omission, risk, artifact, process, relationship, consequence, response, or another stated object;
4. whether the claim is prospective, concurrent, retrospective, continuing, inherited, or mixed;
5. the authority or normative source from which the relation is claimed to arise;
6. the governing frame and its jurisdiction;
7. Domain, Context, Scope, population, stakes, time, and version;
8. the applicable standard, duty, expectation, criterion, or rule;
9. relevant causal Contribution, agency, control, role, authority, authorization, delegation, supervision, reliance, benefit, dependence, and consequence links;
10. the Evidence and uncertainty supporting each material link;
11. relevant knowledge, intention, foreseeability, capability, practical alternatives, constraints, excuses, defenses, and conflicts where the selected frame makes them material;
12. affected subjects, claimants, beneficiaries, represented subjects, or protected interests where material;
13. the forum or authority competent to receive, assess, challenge, enforce, review, or route the claim;
14. expected answer, performance, correction, consequence, remedy, or other response interface;
15. assignment, allocation, delegation, retention, transfer, succession, and expiry claims;
16. the current state: asserted, applicable, disputed, assessed, accepted, discharged, breached, enforced, remedied, superseded, or otherwise declared;
17. challenge, appeal, reassessment, reopening, and review conditions; and
18. the record and Projection limits.

This is an analytical checklist, not an accepted schema, exhaustive Ontology, or universal legal test. A Domain may require more dimensions. It may use different terms if it preserves the distinctions and declares the mapping.

### 4.2 Source-relative validity without empty relativism

Responsibility is not a source-free physical property. A claim that a responsibility relation actually holds must identify the governing source and show that the source applies to the subject, object, Context, Scope, and period. That does not make every declared source equally legitimate, epistemically sound, externally valid, or SymK-compatible.

At least five questions remain separate:

1. **Existence:** does the asserted source, rule, undertaking, office, relationship, or arrangement exist?
2. **Applicability:** does it apply to this subject, object, place, time, version, and circumstance?
3. **Validity within frame:** was the relation constituted or recognized under the stated frame and authority?
4. **Legitimacy or compatibility:** is the source or exercise justified under the relevant higher, parallel, external, or SymK constraints?
5. **Effectiveness:** can the relation guide conduct, obtain an answer, cause correction, or provide a response in practice?

Separating these questions prevents a contract, job description, permission system, statute, professional code, moral claim, or project declaration from acquiring universal force merely because it is recorded. It also prevents SymK from treating disagreement among frameworks as proof that no responsibility claim can be governed.

### 4.3 Connection is necessary evidence, not automatic sufficiency

Responsibility claims commonly rely on one or more connection families:

- causal Contribution;
- agency or action;
- control or practical alternative;
- role or entrusted function;
- authority or decision power;
- authorization or permission;
- delegation, supervision, or retention;
- creation, design, supply, maintenance, custody, or deployment;
- assumption, undertaking, promise, contract, or representation;
- reliance, dependence, vulnerability, or affected-party relation;
- benefit, risk creation, risk distribution, or consequence exposure;
- ownership, possession, legal personality, office, membership, or succession; and
- knowledge, intention, foreseeability, competence, negligence, recklessness, or another framework-specific condition.

No item on this list is universally necessary or sufficient for every responsibility kind. A legal framework may impose strict liability without blame; a role may create answerability without causal Contribution; moral blame may require conditions that a remedial duty does not; an institution may be responsible at one level while no member bears an identical relation. Material claims must state which connections matter under which source rather than hiding them inside the word “responsible.”

### 4.4 Responsibility object and response interface

Responsibility must be responsibility **for**, **regarding**, or **toward** something. The object may be:

- maintaining a condition or capability;
- performing or refraining from conduct;
- making, reviewing, or implementing a decision;
- supervising or challenging a process;
- governing a risk, artifact, system, relationship, population, or lifecycle;
- preserving Evidence, Provenance, uncertainty, or status;
- answering for past conduct or consequence;
- correcting, repairing, compensating, restoring, stopping, disclosing, or providing another response; or
- ensuring that an adequate owner or external authority receives the question.

The response interface states what kind of consequence follows from the relation if relevant: performance, account, review, correction, containment, remedy, sanction, transfer, escalation, or another governed response. It does not predetermine which response is justified.

## 5. Prospective responsibility, duty, and obligation

### 5.1 Prospective responsibility

Prospective responsibility concerns governance before or during the period in which conduct, conditions, risks, or consequences can still be shaped. It may concern:

- maintaining epistemic or operational conditions;
- deciding, reviewing, authorizing, supervising, or challenging;
- monitoring a known risk or uncertainty;
- preserving practical alternatives or intervention capacity;
- supplying information, Evidence, warnings, competence, resources, or escalation paths;
- protecting an affected population or represented subject;
- maintaining, updating, repairing, retiring, or handing over an artifact or system;
- ensuring that a question reaches an adequate authority; or
- preparing to answer and respond if a material consequence occurs.

Prospective responsibility is not the same as causal responsibility for a later outcome. A subject may hold a prospective responsibility and perform it adequately even when harm occurs. Another subject may cause an outcome without ever having held the relevant duty. Performance, discharge, breach, consequence, and retrospective assessment remain separate.

### 5.2 Duty

A material duty claim should identify:

1. duty bearer and subject level;
2. source and competent authority;
3. required action, restraint, condition, capability, process, or response;
4. beneficiary, claimant, affected horizon, or protected interest where applicable;
5. Domain, Context, Scope, time, triggers, thresholds, exceptions, and priority conditions;
6. required knowledge, capability, control, resources, and practical alternatives, if relevant;
7. relation to role, mandate, authorization, decision, delegation, and supervision;
8. performance and discharge criteria;
9. breach, excuse, impossibility, conflict, waiver, suspension, and reassessment conditions;
10. answerability, enforcement, review, and remedy interfaces; and
11. Evidence, status, record, and challenge.

Duty does not imply that the bearer caused an outcome, acted intentionally, is blameworthy, is legally liable, or deserves sanction. Conversely, legal liability, remedial responsibility, or answerability may exist under some frameworks without a duty of the same type or without individual moral agency.

### 5.3 Obligation

For Stream C, **obligation** is treated as a particularized binding relation under a governing frame. It normally identifies an obligor, an object, a source, conditions, and an authority, obligee, claimant, beneficiary, or affected horizon. It may arise from law, role, undertaking, contract, decision, relationship, prior conduct, remedy, or another recognized source.

Natural language, legal systems, moral theories, and professional codes do not use “duty” and “obligation” uniformly. Some treat them as synonyms; some use duty for status- or role-based requirements and obligation for particular undertakings; some reverse the emphasis. SymK should not universalize one vocabulary. A source that uses either term must declare whether the relation is general or particular, to whom or what it is directed, how it arises, and how it is discharged or changed.

### 5.4 Assignment, assumption, acceptance, performance, discharge, and breach

These states must not collapse:

- **assignment:** an authority or arrangement designates a subject as bearing a responsibility or duty;
- **assumption:** a subject undertakes or begins to occupy the relation;
- **acceptance:** the subject acknowledges or agrees to the assignment or claim;
- **performance:** conduct satisfies some or all applicable requirements;
- **discharge:** the obligation or duty is fulfilled, released, superseded, expired, or otherwise ended under the governing frame;
- **breach:** applicable performance criteria are not met without a recognized defeating condition under the stated frame;
- **waiver or release:** a competent source removes or limits a claim, without necessarily erasing external or retained relations;
- **record:** a Representation states one or more of these events or statuses.

Assignment can be invalid, impossible, unauthorized, or incomplete. Acceptance can be coerced, uninformed, unnecessary to validity, or ineffective against external duties. Performance can occur without acceptance. A breach claim can be contested. A record does not establish the underlying state by itself.

## 6. Retrospective responsibility and evaluative layers

### 6.1 Retrospective responsibility

Retrospective responsibility evaluates a subject's relation to an already existing condition, decision, act, omission, process, risk, or consequence. A material assessment should state:

- what object and period are evaluated;
- which subject and level are assessed;
- which source, frame, criteria, and authority apply;
- which prospective duties or undertakings existed, if any;
- which causal, agency, control, role, authority, authorization, reliance, and consequence links are evidenced;
- which knowledge, intention, foreseeability, competence, alternatives, constraints, excuses, defenses, or conflicts are material;
- whether the assessment concerns answerability, fault, blame, liability, remedial responsibility, sanction, institutional learning, or another relation;
- the Evidence, uncertainty, disagreement, affected subjects, and alternative explanations;
- the response, review, appeal, correction, or remedy interface; and
- the status and record of the assessment.

“Responsible for” therefore cannot be interpreted without its phase and qualification. A sentence may mean “tasked with preventing,” “causally contributed,” “must explain,” “failed a role duty,” “is blameworthy,” “is legally liable,” “must repair,” or merely “is named in a record.” These are materially different claims.

### 6.2 Causal responsibility

When “causal responsibility” means only making a difference to an outcome under a causal model, Stream A's **Causal Contribution** is the clearer term. It preserves model, target, level, mechanism, counterfactual, uncertainty, and multiple sufficient or necessary pathways without importing normative force.

A Domain may retain “causal responsibility” if it explicitly declares that it is a causal attribution rather than duty, blame, liability, or remedy. Causal Contribution is often relevant Evidence for another responsibility claim but neither universal necessity nor universal sufficiency follows.

### 6.3 Role responsibility

“Role responsibility” may refer to:

1. functions expected within a role;
2. duties attached to valid role occupancy;
3. prospective responsibility for maintaining a condition or process;
4. answerability for role performance;
5. retrospective assessment of performance or breach; or
6. an organizational assignment record.

The role does not itself prove authority, capability, actual occupancy, performance, causal Contribution, fault, liability, or remedial duty. Stream B's role source, assignment, occupancy, authority, authorization, performance, and record layers remain explicit.

### 6.4 Excuse, justification, defense, and limitation

A conduct claim may be justified under one framework, excused under another, legally defended, operationally unavoidable, or still remedially significant. Stream C does not define universal excuses or defenses. It requires that a material assessment disclose which conditions are treated as defeating, reducing, redirecting, or leaving unchanged which responsibility relation.

Examples include impossibility, incapacity, lack of authority, lack of control, reasonable reliance, emergency, necessity, coercion, duress, conflicting duties, insufficient information, unforeseeability, mistake, consent, waiver, limitation period, immunity, and compliant process. None has universal force merely by being named.

## 7. Answerability and accountability arrangements

### 7.1 Answerability

Answerability connects an identified subject to a forum or claimant regarding an identified object and period. An answer may include:

- factual information;
- explanation of conduct, process, or outcome;
- Grounds or justification;
- disclosure of uncertainty, limitation, conflict, or dissent;
- acknowledgment of role, decision, contribution, or consequence;
- response to challenge;
- identification of another competent owner or authority; or
- a stated inability to answer and the reasons.

Answerability does not imply causal Contribution, fault, blame, liability, punishment, or a duty to accept the forum's conclusion. A spokesperson may answer for an institution without being the causal actor. A maintainer may answer for the current system without being responsible for its original design. A witness may explain facts without bearing a duty to repair.

An explanation is content supplied within answerability; explanation generation alone is not an accountability arrangement. Fluency, completeness, or technical detail does not establish truth, justification, responsibility, or satisfaction of the relation.

### 7.2 Accountability as an arrangement

A material accountability arrangement should identify:

1. accountable subject and exact level;
2. forum, claimant, reviewer, or authority and its jurisdiction;
3. object, period, population, stakes, and purpose;
4. applicable criteria, duties, commitments, or standards;
5. information, Evidence, Provenance, access, and preservation requirements;
6. notice and practical ability to answer;
7. independence, competence, resources, time, and protection of the forum and participants;
8. challenge, counterevidence, disagreement, representation, and affected-party interfaces;
9. assessment and decision authority;
10. correction, containment, consequence, escalation, remedy, or routing capability;
11. review, appeal, reopening, succession, and continuity; and
12. status, record, uncertainty, and declared loss.

The arrangement is **nominal** for a target when these elements exist only as labels, policies, records, inaccessible procedures, ceremonial review, or powers that cannot be used in time. It is **effective** only to the extent that it can obtain and examine an account, preserve material challenge, reach a competent decision or routing point, and trigger the response it claims to support.

Effectiveness is target-, stakes-, Context-, and time-specific. Ex post audit may be adequate for one reversible low-consequence process and radically inadequate for an irreversible high-consequence action. Stream C sets no universal threshold or metric.

### 7.3 Accountability is not punishment

Accountability can produce:

- acknowledgment or explanation;
- correction or updated status;
- changed authority, authorization, role, or supervision;
- process repair or institutional Learning;
- cessation, containment, rollback, or recall;
- remedy or compensation;
- sanction or discipline;
- referral to an external authority; or
- a justified finding that no breach or response is established.

Punishment is neither necessary nor sufficient. An institution may punish one operator while leaving the responsible design, incentive, authority, and remedy relations untouched. Conversely, an effective process may correct, repair, compensate, and prevent recurrence without punitive response.

### 7.4 Accountability gaps and theater

An accountability gap exists in a declared arrangement when a material object falls outside practical answerability, assessment, or response despite a governing need claimed by that arrangement. The following are not enough to establish such a gap as inevitable:

- many contributors;
- artificial or hybrid operation;
- no single sufficient cause;
- delegation or outsourcing;
- proprietary or opaque components;
- dissolution or replacement;
- a contract disclaimer;
- absence of individual moral blame; or
- absence of one subject with complete control.

These may expose representation, allocation, authority, evidence, or continuity defects. Stream D must decide how distributed and multi-level claims are represented and whether a gap is real, manufactured, or a governance failure. Stream C does not fill a gap by naming the nearest Human, the artificial component, the institution, or the collective as wholly responsible.

## 8. Blame, culpability, sanction, and punishment

### 8.1 Blame and culpability

Blame is a negative attribution of fault or culpability under a stated normative framework. A material blame claim should identify:

- blamed subject and level;
- conduct, omission, decision, disposition, or other object;
- normative source and forum;
- violated or failed standard;
- material agency, control, intention, knowledge, foreseeability, competence, alternatives, and constraints;
- justification, excuse, defense, mitigation, aggravation, and uncertainty;
- relation to causal Contribution, duty, role, authority, and consequence;
- affected subjects and response sought;
- assessment, challenge, review, and status; and
- whether the claim is moral, professional, organizational, legal, political, interpersonal, or another declared kind.

SymK does not adopt one theory of moral blame, culpability, desert, intention, negligence, or free will. Blame remains distinguishable from disapproval, causal explanation, duty breach, answerability, liability, sanction, remedy, and correction.

### 8.2 Sanction

A sanction is an adverse consequence imposed or recognized by an authority for a violation under a governing frame. It requires at least a source, competent authority, subject, violation basis, Scope, procedure, consequence, status, and review interface.

Sanction does not prove moral blame or repair an affected party. A sanction may be invalid, disproportionate, ineffective, discriminatory, purely symbolic, or directed to the wrong level. Absence of sanction does not prove absence of responsibility, and imposition does not establish truth merely through authority.

### 8.3 Punishment

Punishment is treated as a narrower response intentionally imposing a burden, deprivation, or condemnation for an attributed violation. The justification and permissible form of punishment belong to moral, legal, professional, organizational, and political jurisdictions. SymK does not make punishment a universal requirement of responsibility or accountability.

Punishment must not substitute for:

- explanation of the actual causal and decision history;
- correction of Knowledge, status, or record;
- repair of defective processes or artifacts;
- remedy for affected subjects;
- reassignment of authority or supervision;
- prevention of recurrence; or
- examination of whole-level and retained relations.

## 9. Legal liability and legal personality boundary

### 9.1 Liability

Liability is exposure under an applicable legal or institutionally constituted framework to a prescribed claim, duty, loss allocation, adverse consequence, or remedy. A material liability claim should identify:

- governing jurisdiction and authority;
- liable subject and legally recognized level;
- claimant or enforcing authority;
- cause of action, rule, undertaking, status, event, or other legal basis;
- required factual and legal elements;
- applicable defenses, immunities, limits, procedures, and time;
- remedy, sanction, insurance, indemnity, or allocation relation;
- decision, appeal, enforcement, and record status; and
- relation to, but separation from, causal Contribution, agency, control, role, moral blame, and practical repair.

SymK does not determine whether any actual Human, artificial system, organization, institution, collective, configured system, hybrid, or legal entity is liable.

### 9.2 Legal personality

Legal personality is a status conferred or recognized by an applicable legal system. It may allow a subject to hold rights, duties, property, claims, liability, or procedural standing. It does not establish operational agency, Intelligence, consciousness, sentience, moral standing, causal Contribution, control, blameworthiness, or institution-level process integration.

Conversely, operational agency, planning, control, contribution, or answer-generation does not create legal personality. A legal entity may bear liability without any one member satisfying an identical predicate. A Human member may be culpable without the institution bearing the same kind of blame. Stream D must preserve the levels; external authority decides legal effect.

### 9.3 Contract, disclaimer, indemnity, and insurance

Contracts, disclaimers, indemnities, warranties, and insurance may allocate claims, costs, risks, duties, or procedures within their governing frameworks. They do not universally:

- erase causal Contribution or control;
- eliminate duties imposed by other sources;
- create informed consent or legitimate authority;
- transfer moral responsibility;
- convert an artificial system into the responsible party;
- absolve retained design, information, update, deployment, or supervision relations; or
- decide the SymK semantic classification.

Their actual force remains external and Context-specific. SymK must preserve what the instrument claims to allocate, what each source retains, what external rules override or limit it, and what remains disputed.

## 10. Remedy, repair, correction, restitution, and prevention

### 10.1 Remedy

A remedy is a governed response available or required under an applicable frame to address a recognized wrong, violation, injury, deprivation, defect, or claim. A material remedy claim should identify:

1. affected subject, claimant, represented subject, or protected interest;
2. respondent or duty bearer;
3. recognized basis and governing source;
4. competent forum or authority;
5. objective: cessation, prevention, restoration, correction, repair, compensation, restitution, access, disclosure, acknowledgment, review, invalidation, or another declared aim;
6. Scope, time, eligibility, trigger, threshold, and practical accessibility;
7. relation to duty, breach, responsibility, liability, fault, and consequence;
8. implementation, enforcement, review, appeal, monitoring, and completion;
9. residual harm, uncertainty, disagreement, and recurrence risk; and
10. status and record.

The exact right to a remedy, claimant standing, procedure, measure, and priority belong to Stream E, constitutional analysis, Domain specialization, and external authority. Stream C preserves the interface so that responsibility cannot end at attribution or punishment.

### 10.2 Repair

Repair is action or a process directed toward restoring, rebuilding, healing, compensating for, or otherwise addressing what was impaired. It may concern:

- an affected subject or relationship;
- a decision or process;
- a Knowledge claim, Evidence state, or Provenance record;
- an artifact, system, interface, or control pathway;
- authority, authorization, role, or supervision arrangements;
- institutional practice, capacity, memory, or trust; or
- environmental, social, economic, legal, professional, or other Domain conditions.

Repair does not guarantee restoration, erase the original event, discharge every responsibility, establish forgiveness, replace compensation, or make recurrence impossible. An artificial or technical correction can be part of repair without being the complete remedy.

### 10.3 Correction

Correction changes an erroneous, defective, misleading, obsolete, or harmful claim, record, decision, process, artifact, configuration, or condition. It must preserve lineage rather than rewriting history. Correcting a status does not erase the prior reliance, action, consequence, or responsibility evidence.

Correction can occur without blame or liability. It may itself be a prospective duty, a remedial response, an accountability outcome, or routine lifecycle maintenance. The source and reason must be stated.

### 10.4 Restitution, compensation, cessation, and prevention

These are candidate remedy forms rather than universal synonyms:

- **restitution** seeks return or restoration of something wrongfully lost or transferred;
- **compensation** supplies a substitute or payment for recognized loss;
- **cessation** stops continuing conduct or effect;
- **containment** limits propagation or exposure;
- **prevention** changes conditions to reduce recurrence or future harm;
- **acknowledgment** recognizes an event, relation, or affected subject;
- **invalidation or reversal** removes governance effect from a decision or status where authorized; and
- **access or disclosure** enables information, participation, challenge, or another response.

Whether any form is adequate or available depends on the governing frame, affected party, feasibility, proportionality, and residual consequences. Stream C adopts none as universally mandatory.

## 11. Actual relation, claim, governance status, and record

### 11.1 Eleven layers

For every material responsibility object, keep at least these layers separate:

1. **Factual occurrence:** conditions, decisions, actions, omissions, causal Contributions, controls, and consequences.
2. **Source and applicability:** existence, authority, jurisdiction, Scope, time, and relevance of the governing source.
3. **Responsibility relation:** the relation claimed to hold under that specified frame.
4. **Assignment or allocation:** a governance act designating bearers, shares, roles, or response paths.
5. **Attribution:** a claim that a relation, predicate, contribution, fault, or status applies.
6. **Assessment:** a method and conclusion evaluating the claim under declared criteria.
7. **Acceptance or contestation:** acknowledgment, refusal, disagreement, challenge, or unresolved status.
8. **Performance, discharge, or breach:** conduct and status concerning prospective duties or obligations.
9. **Enforcement or consequence:** action taken by an authority, forum, organization, or other governed process.
10. **Remedy, repair, or correction:** response to the recognized object and its effects.
11. **Record:** Representations of any of the preceding layers, with Provenance and Projection limits.

The layers may interact and need not occur in this order. None may silently manufacture another. A court judgment may authoritatively settle legal liability within its jurisdiction while leaving causal uncertainty, moral disagreement, operational defects, or SymK semantic questions open. An internal record may be accurate while the underlying assignment is invalid. An accepted duty may remain unperformed. A remedy may be provided without admitted blame.

### 11.2 Responsibility-claim status

A responsibility claim may be described as alleged, attributed, assigned, applicable, inapplicable, disputed, under assessment, provisionally supported, accepted within a frame, rejected, breached, discharged, enforced, remedied, superseded, reopened, or unresolved. These are working status examples, not an accepted universal vocabulary.

Every status should identify:

- who assigned it and under what authority;
- the exact claim and frame;
- Evidence and assessment method;
- effective time and version;
- whether the status is descriptive, semantic, organizational, professional, legal, moral, constitutional, or operational;
- challenge, appeal, expiry, and reopening; and
- Projection loss.

### 11.3 No retrospective rewriting

Later approval, ratification, correction, payment, apology, sanction, dissolution, succession, or record change must not silently rewrite:

- what authority and authorization existed at the time;
- what subjects acted, decided, controlled, contributed, or were affected;
- what duties or accountability arrangements then applied;
- what information, alternatives, uncertainty, and constraints existed;
- which assessment or response occurred later; or
- which questions remain disputed.

The historical and current states can both be represented. Continuity does not require erasure.

## 12. Subject, bearer, and level tests

### 12.1 Candidate responsibility bearer

A responsibility claim must identify the bearer as a specific subject at a specific level. Candidate levels include:

- individual Human;
- individual artificial component or service;
- Human team or committee;
- artificial multi-component arrangement;
- institution or legal entity;
- distributed process or network;
- configured operational system;
- Human–artificial hybrid;
- role, office, account, or position as distinct from its occupant;
- predecessor, successor, maintainer, or custodian; and
- represented or affected subject.

Listing a candidate does not grant agency, moral standing, legal personality, duty, responsibility, liability, rights, or standing. The governing source must recognize or constitute the relevant relation, and the claim must preserve the subject boundary and level.

### 12.2 Bearer eligibility is relation-specific

Different responsibility relations may require different bearer conditions:

- action responsibility may require agency under the selected frame;
- answerability may be performed through a representative or successor;
- role duty may attach to an office or institution and be performed by occupants;
- legal liability may attach to a legal entity;
- blame may require moral-agency or culpability conditions under a chosen theory;
- remedial responsibility may be allocated by law or governance regardless of blame;
- correction responsibility may attach to a current maintainer who did not create the defect; and
- affected-party claims do not require the affected subject to be a responsible bearer.

There is therefore no single substrate-neutral bearer test that settles every responsibility kind. The universal minimum requires the source, relation kind, bearer conditions, level, and representation to be explicit.

### 12.3 Whole, member, component, and representative

For a collective, institution, distributed arrangement, configured system, or hybrid:

- whole-level responsibility does not copy to every member or component;
- member or component responsibility does not automatically aggregate into a whole-level relation;
- representation of the whole does not make the representative the causal actor or sole responsible subject;
- a legal entity's liability does not settle member agency, blame, or remedial duty;
- a member's fault does not prove organizational fault without the selected framework's criteria;
- institution-level roles, authority, decisions, processes, memory, controls, and continuity require Evidence; and
- whole/member relations may coexist without shares summing to a fixed scalar.

Stream D must analyze allocation, overlap, retention, contribution networks, responsibility gaps, and succession. Stream C prohibits level copying but does not decide the distribution.

### 12.4 Artificial and hybrid subjects

An artificial component may act, control, recommend, decide operationally, execute, explain, monitor, or record under the Stream A–B working models. None of those facts automatically makes it a moral agent, legal person, blameworthy subject, liability bearer, or adequate responsibility sink.

An applicable organizational or technical frame may assign an artificial component a maintenance function, response interface, or operational role. SymK must preserve that assignment while also recording the Human, institutional, provider, deployer, authority, supervision, and retained relations. It neither forbids every responsibility-like relation to artificial systems nor allows their naming to erase externally recognized responsible subjects.

## 13. Responsibility transfer, retention, succession, and gaps

### 13.1 Transfer is typed, not total

A purported responsibility transfer should state:

- source and authority to transfer;
- original bearer, receiving bearer, object, relation type, Scope, time, and conditions;
- acceptance or constitution requirements;
- capability, resources, information, authority, authorization, and control required for performance;
- duties, answerability, accountability, liability, remedy, records, and supervision transferred;
- every retained relation;
- effect on affected parties and external authorities;
- subdelegation, recall, expiry, breach, failure, and succession; and
- Evidence, status, challenge, and record.

Delegating a task, decision, execution, or operational authority does not automatically transfer the delegator's duty to select, resource, supervise, review, answer, correct, or respond. Nor does retained responsibility imply that the delegate bears none. Stream D must test coexisting and distributed relations.

### 13.2 Succession and temporal continuity

Update, replacement, maintenance, merger, acquisition, dissolution, retirement, decommissioning, and succession can change current capability, authority, duty, answerability, and remedy pathways. They do not erase the earlier relation history.

A succession claim should identify:

- predecessor and successor boundaries;
- continuity source and authority;
- assets, roles, records, systems, duties, claims, liabilities, remedies, and capacities transferred or retained;
- time and version boundaries;
- affected-party and claimant continuity;
- gaps created by dissolution, inaccessibility, incapacity, or legal limitation; and
- challenge, external authority, and record.

The same name, repository, model family, organization, account, or service endpoint does not prove continuity. A changed name or component does not prove discontinuity.

### 13.3 Gap classification

A claimed responsibility gap should be classified provisionally as one or more of:

- **semantic gap:** the relation kind or object is undefined;
- **evidence gap:** material facts or links cannot presently be supported;
- **attribution gap:** the appropriate subject or level is unresolved;
- **allocation gap:** applicable relations are not assigned or coordinated;
- **authority gap:** no competent forum can decide or route the question;
- **capability gap:** a bearer or forum lacks practical capacity to perform;
- **enforcement gap:** a recognized relation lacks practical response;
- **remedy gap:** affected subjects lack an adequate response path;
- **continuity gap:** records, duties, authority, or answerability fail across change; or
- **normative conflict:** applicable sources disagree without an accepted priority rule.

This classification does not prove that the gap is acceptable, avoidable, or attributable to any subject. It prevents “the system is complex” from ending the analysis. Stream D owns allocation and distribution; Stream E owns the minimum ethical and affected-party response boundary.

## 14. Alternatives considered

### A — Responsibility as causal production of an outcome

**Rejected.** It over-includes background conditions and innocent contributors, under-represents omissions, roles, duties, answerability, institutions, and remedies, and imports normative conclusions into a model-relative causal relation. Causal Contribution remains separate.

### B — Responsibility as agency plus control

**Rejected.** Agency and control are important for many assessments but do not establish source, duty, claimant, forum, standard, answerability, liability, or remedy. Some strict, institutional, successor, role, and remedial relations do not require the same agency/control predicate.

### C — Responsibility as role or assigned ownership

**Rejected.** A role or “owner” field may identify expected functions but can be invalid, ceremonial, incapable, superseded, or strategically assigned. It cannot erase causal, control, retained, external, or affected-party relations.

### D — Responsibility as legal liability

**Rejected.** Liability is jurisdiction-specific and may be strict, vicarious, contractual, insured, limited, or attached to legal personality. It cannot serve as the universal SymK meaning or decide moral, epistemic, operational, institutional, or remedial responsibility.

### E — Responsibility as blameworthiness

**Rejected.** Blame requires normative and often moral-agency conditions that SymK cannot universalize here. It would erase prospective responsibility, answerability without fault, correction, repair, and institution-level relations.

### F — Accountability as logs, audits, explanation, or punishment

**Rejected.** Records and procedures can exist without practical answerability, competent assessment, correction, consequence, review, or remedy. Punishment can coexist with systemic non-accountability.

### G — Responsibility as answerability only

**Rejected as exhaustive.** Answerability is a strong minimum interface and part of candidate A2, but some responsibility claims concern prospective maintenance, performance, prevention, correction, remedy, or legal exposure beyond providing an account. A subject may answer representationally without bearing the underlying relation.

### H — Leave responsibility wholly to each Domain

**Rejected.** It preserves pluralism but permits incompatible type privilege, causal collapse, responsibility laundering, accountability theater, and gaps across SymK-compatible projects. SymK needs a shared minimum architecture even though substantive allocation and force remain downstream.

### I — Responsibility as a source-declared, scoped, phased, challengeable relationship family with distinct factual, normative, governance, and response layers

**Retained provisionally.** It is necessary to SymK's identity and value chain, travels across Domains, preserves external authority and pluralism, prevents material conflations, and can be bounded and challenged without selecting a complete ethical or legal theory.

## 17. Three-view preservation obligations

### 17.1 Human View

The Human View should preserve:

- how participants, authorities, challengers, claimants, and affected subjects understand the responsibility object and relationship;
- who believed they were entrusted, obligated, answerable, accountable, blamed, liable, protected, or entitled to response;
- actual knowledge, intention, foresight, competence, uncertainty, alternatives, constraints, pressure, dependence, vulnerability, and practical ability to refuse, act, answer, correct, or repair;
- reasons, explanations, justifications, excuses, disagreement, dissent, and unresolved conflict;
- experienced consequences, losses, benefits, needs, repair, residual harm, and recurrence concerns;
- the meaning and practical accessibility of assignment, challenge, review, appeal, remedy, and succession; and
- known gaps between title, policy, record, legal status, institutional narrative, and lived relation.

Human testimony, acknowledgment, apology, signature, acceptance, refusal, or silence is Evidence. None conclusively settles every factual, semantic, legal, moral, or operational relation.

### 17.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View should preserve, where material:

- causal models, comparison classes, target outcomes, mechanisms, counterfactuals, and level choices;
- Evidence concerning action, omission, control, intervention, capability, knowledge, foreseeability, alternatives, supervision, and consequence;
- competing explanations for individual, artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, configured-system, or hybrid behavior;
- methods for assessing accountability effectiveness, warning comprehension, response latency, override, recurrence, correction, repair, and residual effect;
- uncertainty, sensitivity, confounding, selection effects, missing data, contested assumptions, and conditions of defeat; and
- the distinction between empirical findings and normative, legal, professional, or governance conclusions.

No causal estimate, benchmark, explanation score, audit result, compliance rate, incident count, override rate, or outcome metric defines responsibility, blame, liability, or remedy universally.

### 17.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View should eventually preserve:

- subject identities and individual, component, collective, institutional, distributed, configured-system, hybrid, role, predecessor, and successor levels;
- responsibility claim, bearer, object, phase, source, frame, jurisdiction, Scope, time, version, standard, status, and Evidence separately;
- causal, agency, control, role, authority, authorization, decision, delegation, supervision, reliance, consequence, and affected-party links;
- duty, obligation, assignment, assumption, acceptance, performance, discharge, breach, answerability, accountability, assessment, enforcement, sanction, liability, remedy, repair, correction, and record as non-substitutable relations or states;
- claimant, beneficiary, affected subject, forum, assessor, decision authority, responder, representative, and external authority;
- information, Provenance, uncertainty, alternatives, constraints, challenge, counterevidence, dissent, review, appeal, reopening, and Projection loss;
- transfer, retention, succession, expiry, dissolution, and continuity; and
- declared policy, configured system, runtime behavior, observed effect, assessment, and governance record separately.

This stream selects no responsibility matrix, workflow, case-management system, audit platform, policy language, role/permission model, legal ontology, moral formalism, causal model, metric, threshold, scoring rule, allocation algorithm, smart contract, remedy process, or enforcement technology.

### 17.4 Alignment rule

A policy naming an “accountable owner,” a study showing that a contributor affected the outcome, and a system log identifying a final approver remain misaligned if they refer to different subjects, levels, objects, periods, sources, or responsibility kinds. A valid legal judgment does not become a causal model; a causal model does not create a duty; an assigned owner does not prove capability or control; a complete log does not create a forum or remedy. Material mismatch keeps the claim qualified and challenged.

## 19. Consequences for governing artifacts

### Product Vision

The Product Vision's responsibility and epistemic-accountability requirements need a relation system that preserves who contributed, decided, authorized, controlled, relied, was affected, was entrusted, must answer, and can correct or respond. “Better answers” cannot be evaluated only by output quality; the responsible lifecycle includes conditions, reliance, action, consequence, challenge, correction, repair, and Learning. This stream does not change the Product Vision.

### DR-009 through DR-013

- DR-009's semantic/normative distinction is reinforced: Responsibility can have normative dimensions without one definition silently imposing every duty.
- DR-010 supplies the structural Identity, Context, Scope, Domain, Relationship, Applicability, Bearer, Representation, Projection, and System dimensions but does not decide the responsible bearer.
- DR-011's capability, performance, Bearer, Attribution, and status separations prevent Intelligence or Knowledge from manufacturing responsibility.
- DR-012's Epistemic Condition, claim, Grounds, Evidence, Provenance, assessment, status, Reliance Authorization, challenge, and lifecycle layers become responsibility objects and evidence without becoming responsibility themselves.
- DR-013's Cooperation, Participant, Contribution, Power, challenge, affected-party, Persistence, Learning, replacement, and succession boundaries remain intact. Responsible Cooperation remains a later qualification, not an occurrence criterion.

No predecessor is reopened.

### Candidate A0

A0's responsible-Cooperation language must distinguish:

- occurrence of Cooperation from responsible qualification;
- participant and affected-party relations from responsibility bearing;
- shared purpose from duty source;
- Contribution from blame or liability;
- challenge permission from effective accountability;
- responsibility to act from responsibility to answer or repair; and
- project purpose from one universal allocation rule.

A0 may later select ethical or constitutional force, but Stream C does not accept its repair, affected-party, consequence, or responsibility propositions.

### Candidate A1

A1's authority/warrant separation extends to responsibility: an authority can validly assign, assess, or enforce a responsibility relation within its jurisdiction without manufacturing truth, causal fact, moral legitimacy, or external legal effect. Epistemic error may create correction or answerability interfaces without automatically establishing blame.

### Candidate A2

A2 is directionally supported but its compressed definitions require refinement before constitutional selection:

- Responsibility cannot be exhausted by “answerability for obligations and consequences”; it includes prospective governance and must identify source, bearer, object, frame, Scope, phase, response, status, and challenge.
- Accountability is correctly treated as an arrangement, but examination and answer must connect to competent assessment, challenge, correction, consequence, review, or remedy to avoid theater.
- Allocation must distinguish assignment from a relation that validly holds and from practical capability to perform it.
- “Consequence-sensitive” must not turn outcome into retrospective proof of fault.
- Revisability must preserve prior relations, decisions, duties, performance, breaches, consequences, and records.
- Type non-privilege does not imply that every subject type can bear every moral, legal, professional, or operational responsibility relation.
- A Human click cannot absorb responsibility; an artificial component cannot become the sole sink; collective and institutional levels require independent Evidence.

Stream C does not accept, revise, or reject A2.

### Candidate A4

A4's “accountable” definition—attributable, inspectable, challengeable, and answerable to consequences—is useful but incomplete as a responsibility system. Attribution and inspection must connect to source, bearer, object, forum, assessment, response, correction, remedy, and lifecycle. Knowledge Engineering shapes epistemic and operational conditions without making engineers responsible for every downstream consequence or allowing design distance to erase foreseeable, retained, or assigned relations.

### Candidate A6

Constitutional and project inheritance must state which duties, answerability, responsibility claims, accountability arrangements, records, corrective obligations, and remedy interfaces transfer or remain across adoption, specialization, exception, migration, replacement, and succession. Compatibility inheritance does not silently transfer every liability or moral judgment.

### J1 and the conflict protocol

J1's layered jurisdiction is necessary to preserve universal SymK distinctions while routing substantive duties, liability, remedies, and enforcement. “Nearest adequate owner” must not become “nearest available blame target.” A conflict record should preserve each source, bearer, object, jurisdiction, Scope, priority claim, practical alternative, consequence, containment action, unresolved status, and response path.

Legitimate external authority remains a candidate concept not accepted here. External duties may govern action while their semantic, epistemic, ethical, or constitutional compatibility remains challenged.

### Legacy Human Architect / AI Partner role axiom

The legacy axiom's allocation of decisions to the Human and breadth/critique to AI cannot decide responsibility. It collapses type, capability, role, authority, authorization, decision, execution, answerability, and responsibility and risks both the Human-approval sink and artificial-blame sink. Historical functions remain evidence pending 2.4; no canonical revision occurs here.

### Foundation Papers

The four working papers require later revision wherever they:

- use “responsible,” “accountable,” “answerable,” “liable,” “duty,” “obligation,” “repair,” or “Human oversight” without identifying the relation;
- infer responsibility from Intelligence, authorship, Contribution, Cooperation, role, institution, authority, application, or outcome;

- imply that legal, moral, epistemic, professional, and operational responsibility are interchangeable;
- omit affected subjects, practical challenge, correction, remedy, residual consequence, or lifecycle continuity; or
- assume responsibility transfers automatically through delegation, procurement, contract, system update, or organizational succession.

The revision ledger records this impact only. No Foundation Paper source or PDF changes.

## 20. Deferred-question treatment

### DQ-005 — Responsibility when agency, authority, causation, and legal personality diverge

Stream C makes major but non-final progress:

- Responsibility is not one scalar predicate but a sourced, scoped, phased, challengeable relationship family.
- Causal Contribution, agency, control, role, authority, authorization, decision, delegation, supervision, legal personality, duty, answerability, accountability, blame, liability, remedy, and record are non-substitutable.
- A responsibility claim must identify bearer and level, object, source, governing frame, standard, factual links, response, status, Evidence, and challenge.
- Prospective and retrospective relations remain separate.
- Whole/member, legal/operational, artificial/Human/institutional, and predecessor/successor relations require independent Evidence.
- No final approver, artificial component, organization, collective, or “system” becomes the universal sink.
- Lack of blame, legal personality, or one sufficient cause does not prove absence of answerability, correction, remedy, institutional responsibility, or externally governed liability.

DQ-005 remains active. Stream D must test allocation and gaps; Stream E must define the ethical and affected-party minimum; Stream F must consolidate. No universal answer or actual allocation is accepted.

### DQ-017 — Minimum universal ethical commitments

Stream C does not answer DQ-017. It supplies conditions any candidate minimum must confront:

- responsibility cannot disappear through origin, type, automation, delegation, approval, contract, distribution, or record;
- affected subjects and consequences must remain representable even without Participation or contract;
- accountability must be practical rather than ceremonial;
- conflict, uncertainty, dissent, challenge, correction, remedy, and residual harm must remain visible;
- outcome cannot retroactively define responsible process;
- external authority and normative pluralism must remain bounded and challengeable; and
- response cannot end with blame or punishment while correction and remedy are absent.

Whether these become universal ethical commitments, semantic adequacy conditions, constitutional duties, or a combination belongs to Stream E and 2.2. DQ-017 remains active with analytical inputs only.
