# SymK 2.1.4 — Knowledge Engineering, Epistemic Conditions, and Representation

**Status:** Planned  
**Depends on:** 2.1.1–2.1.3

## Question

> What does SymK engineer when it cannot engineer truth or Knowledge directly, and what must a representation preserve to participate responsibly in that work?

## Scope

Examine Knowledge Engineering, epistemic conditions, acquisition, inquiry, claim, grounds, provenance, Context, uncertainty, disagreement, validation, application, consequence, revision, Representation, Projection, formal semantics, and semantic loss.

## Required distinctions

- Knowledge Engineering versus Epistemology, data engineering, information management, software engineering, AI engineering, and Education;
- acquisition versus extraction;
- subject versus representation;
- formal validity versus epistemic sufficiency;
- preservation, strengthening, simplification, and loss in projection;
- governance status versus truth status;
- validation of structure, inference, evidence, practice, and consequence.

## Two-lane result

This package will specify the meaning-preservation obligations shared by human-semantic and machine-semantic lanes. It may identify required semantic capabilities, but it will not select a formal language or lowering architecture.

## Deliverables

- mature minimum Knowledge Engineering concept;
- epistemic-condition and lifecycle map;
- Representation and Projection distinctions;
- minimum Ontology and Logic boundary for SymK;
- dual-lane semantic obligations and declared-loss rule;
- conceptual portion of DQ-018 and DQ-022;
- Foundation Paper revision-ledger entries.

## Out of scope

OWL/FOL/SHACL/profile selection, final concept-package anatomy, project import protocols, schemas, APIs, code generation, storage, or compilation.
