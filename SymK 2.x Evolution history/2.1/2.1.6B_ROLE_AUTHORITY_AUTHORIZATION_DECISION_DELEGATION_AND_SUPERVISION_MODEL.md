## 1. Question and analytical result

> How must SymK distinguish role, authority, authorization, decision, delegation, and supervision when capability, expertise, agency, control, institutional standing, external authority, and responsibility may belong to different subjects?

Stream B supports the following working answer:

> **A role is a scoped governance or process relation that connects an identified occupant or candidate occupant to expected functions, permissions, constraints, interfaces, and review conditions for a declared purpose. Role assignment, occupancy, capability, performance, authority, authorization, control, responsibility, and record remain distinct.**

> **Authority is a sourced, jurisdiction-bound governance relation that gives an identified subject recognized standing to perform specified governance acts—such as deciding, directing, permitting, prohibiting, allocating, adopting, assessing, or representing—within declared Domain, Context, Scope, time, and conditions. Authority does not establish capability, expertise, epistemic warrant, agency, practical control, legitimacy beyond its source, successful effect, or responsibility for every consequence.**

> **Authorization is a scoped governance status or relation by which an applicable authority permits an identified subject to perform a specified act, use a resource, occupy a role, rely on an epistemic object, or operate under stated conditions. Authorization does not create capability, agency, occurrence, control, epistemic warrant, moral justification, or responsible exercise.**

> **A decision occurrence is a selection or commitment by an identified agent or governed process that determines or materially governs the subsequent state of a question, claim, role, authorization, action, resource, or undertaking. A valid governance decision additionally requires the applicable decision authority and procedure. Decision occurrence, decision authority, validity, adoption, approval, authorization, execution, effect, and record remain separate.**

> **Delegation is a governed relation through which a source-bearing subject assigns an identified function, task, decision, authority, authorization, or execution relation to another subject within explicit retained and transferred boundaries. Delegation cannot convey more than the delegator may convey, does not create capability or effective control, and does not automatically transfer duty, accountability, liability, or responsibility. Supervision is a distinct monitoring, review, intervention, escalation, or assurance relation whose practical effectiveness must be evidenced rather than inferred from title, policy, interface, or Human presence.**

These anchors describe a governance architecture without selecting its final constitutional commitments. They permit Human, artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, and hybrid subjects to occupy scoped operational roles when an applicable source permits, while preventing role occupancy from manufacturing personhood, legal standing, moral status, authority, or responsibility.

The result separates eight questions:

1. **Function:** what work, decision, representation, control, or review function is at issue?
2. **Role:** what role relation is assigned or claimed, by which source, for which purpose and period?
3. **Capability and agency:** can the proposed occupant perform the function, and did it act?
4. **Authority:** what governance act may the subject validly perform, under whose jurisdiction and limits?
5. **Authorization:** what act or use is permitted to which subject under what conditions?
6. **Decision and execution:** what selection or commitment occurred, who made it, who adopted or approved it, and what was executed?
7. **Delegation and supervision:** what was assigned, transferred, retained, monitored, challengeable, revocable, or allowed to expire?
8. **Responsibility:** who has prospective duties or must answer, correct, repair, provide remedy, or bear another consequence relation?

Stream B addresses the first seven only far enough to expose the responsibility interfaces. Streams C–E must determine responsibility and ethical force. No role table, authority claim, permission, final approval, delegation instrument, supervision label, or audit record can answer the eighth question by itself.

## 2. Accepted and analytical predecessor constraints

This analysis inherits and does not reopen the following:

1. Intelligence type, origin, capability, performance, Knowledge, Participation, Contribution, Cooperation, Power, legal status, or successful outcome does not automatically confer role, authority, authorization, responsibility, or standing.
2. Actual bearing or occurrence, attribution claim, assessment, governance status, authority for use, Reliance Authorization, and record remain distinct.
3. Identity, Context, Scope, Domain, Relationship, Applicability, Bearer, Representation, Projection, and System retain their Stage-Accepted meanings.
4. Participant, Contributor, actor, source, resource, beneficiary, affected party, assessor, authority, represented subject, and responsible party are non-substitutable roles.
5. Power capacity, exercise, mechanism, effect, qualification, authority, governance response, and record remain distinct.
6. Formal challenge permission is not an effective challenge path. Practical access, capacity, protection, reception, evaluation, response, correction, appeal, and repair remain separate.
7. Epistemic warrant, Epistemic Assessment, governance status, Reliance Authorization, normative authority, legal authority, organizational permission, and practical reliance do not collapse into one status.
8. Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views remain coordinated where material, and no view acquires authority merely from form, institutional location, technical precision, empirical status, or operational use.
9. External authorities may decisively constrain action within their jurisdictions without thereby defining universal SymK meaning or epistemic truth.
10. A cooperative unit, configured system, institution, collective, distributed arrangement, or hybrid does not become an authority, responsible party, legal person, or moral subject through naming, persistence, output, or organization alone.
11. Stream A separates event, output, behavior, execution, action, omission, causal Contribution, agency, control, influence, autonomy, decision, and consequence.
12. Stream A requires target-specific control claims and distinguishes capacity, exercise, effect, nominal control, effective control, joint control, distributed control, and failed control.
13. Agency and control can exist without authority or authorization; authority or authorization can exist without agency, capability, or effective control.
14. Responsibility, duty, obligation, accountability, answerability, blame, liability, remedy, legal personality, moral standing, consent, and affected-party standing remain substantively open for Streams C–E.

## 3. Why SymK needs a role and authority system

The Product Vision requires explicit, justified, and revisable epistemic roles and authority. Without a governed distinction system, “Human oversight,” “AI assistant,” “reviewer,” “owner,” “decision-maker,” “authorized user,” “expert,” “supervisor,” or “system administrator” can conceal materially different relations.

A weak role model permits:

- job title to substitute for actual role occupancy;
- role assignment to substitute for capability;
- expertise to become decision authority;
- authority to manufacture Knowledge or correctness;
- a permission flag to become effective authorization in every Context;
- a recommendation to become a decision after the fact;
- an approval click to absorb the causal and responsibility history;
- automated execution to be called delegation without identifying what was assigned;
- delegation to erase retained authority, control, or responsibility;
- nominal Human supervision to conceal absent information, competence, time, intervention, or protection;
- a contract to overwrite actual control or external authority;
- one authority kind to expand into another jurisdiction;
- institutional authority to hide the individual, process, artificial, or hybrid subjects that acted;
- source code, workflow, access control, or runtime account to become the semantic authority for a role; or
- expired, revoked, superseded, or inherited authority to remain operational because a record was not updated.

The minimum system performs seven jobs:

1. **Role decomposition:** separate expected function, assignment, occupancy, capability, performance, authority, authorization, and record.
2. **Authority provenance:** require every material authority claim to identify its source, kind, jurisdiction, subject, object, limits, and lifecycle.
3. **Decision traceability:** distinguish proposal, recommendation, assessment, selection, decision, adoption, approval, authorization, execution, and effect.
4. **Delegation integrity:** expose transferred and retained relations rather than treating delegation as a total handoff.
5. **Supervision reality:** distinguish nominal presence from effective monitoring, review, intervention, escalation, and challenge.
6. **Jurisdiction preservation:** respect law, professional authority, organizations, deployments, projects, and SymK semantic ownership without turning them into one hierarchy or truth source.
7. **Responsibility preparation:** supply Streams C and D with stable role, authority, delegation, supervision, agency, causal, and control graphs without deciding responsibility by proxy.

SymK does not need to define every public office, fiduciary relation, employment role, professional license, legal competence rule, corporate mandate, access-control policy, or organizational chart. It needs a universal minimum that prevents those Domain-specific systems from silently redefining SymK concepts or making responsibility disappear.

## 4. Role model

### 4.1 Role as a relation, not a trait or title

A role connects a proposed occupant to an expected function within a governed arrangement. A material role claim should identify:

1. role name and function;
2. source or assigning authority;
3. proposed occupant and subject boundary;
4. purpose, Domain, Context, Scope, and period;
5. eligibility and required capability Evidence;
6. expected activities, decisions, outputs, constraints, and interfaces;
7. authority kinds and limits attached to the role, if any;
8. authorizations and resource access attached to the role, if any;
9. control pathways required for effective performance;
10. delegation and subdelegation conditions;
11. supervision, review, challenge, escalation, and independence conditions;
12. activation, suspension, expiry, revocation, replacement, and succession conditions;
13. affected parties and material consequences; and
14. attribution, assessment, status, performance, and record separately.

This is an analytical information envelope, not a final schema or mandatory bureaucracy for every low-consequence interaction.

### 4.2 Role levels

| Level | Question | Prohibited substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Role definition | What function and boundaries does the governed arrangement specify? | A name or title defines every use of the role |
| Role assignment | Who or what is designated as candidate occupant, by which source? | Assignment proves capability, acceptance, activation, or performance |
| Role occupancy | Does the subject currently occupy the role under its activation and eligibility conditions? | Account existence, employment, membership, or interface presence proves occupancy |
| Role capability | Can the occupant perform the expected function under actual conditions? | Status, origin, prior success, or credential proves current capability universally |
| Role performance | What function was actually performed, with what control, limitations, and result? | Assignment or occupancy proves performance |
| Role authority | Which governance acts may the occupant perform because of this role? | Every expected function carries authority, or authority expands beyond Scope |
| Role authorization | Which actions or resources are permitted to the occupant? | Permission establishes capability, action, justification, or responsibility |
| Role assessment/status | What Evidence supports role fitness and current standing? | Evaluation, certification, or accepted status creates actual performance |
| Role record | What Representation preserves the preceding claims and lineage? | Database row, title, account, badge, or organization chart is the role relation |

### 4.3 Function, title, status, and role

- **Function** describes work or contribution and may be performed without a formal role.
- **Title** is a label and may correspond poorly to actual function, authority, or control.
- **Status** is a governance classification and may concern eligibility, activation, suspension, review, or conformance.
- **Role** is the governed relation linking an occupant to expected functions and conditions.
- **Performance** is the actual conduct, not the role or title.

One subject may occupy several roles; several subjects may occupy one distributed role; a role may be vacant; a role may be performed informally by a subject lacking assignment; and an assigned occupant may fail to perform. These differences remain visible.

### 4.4 Capability and expertise

Capability is Evidence-requiring capacity within Scope. Expertise is comparatively developed Domain capability or standing supported by relevant formation, experience, performance, or recognition. Neither is authority.

An expert may:

- supply Grounds or Evidence;
- assess a claim;
- advise or recommend;
- challenge a decision;
- occupy a professional role; or
- receive decision authority from an applicable source.

None of the first five automatically creates the sixth. Conversely, an official may possess decision authority while relying on experts, artificial systems, or committees for analysis. When authority and expertise diverge, the system must expose the gap, reliance, review, and challenge structure rather than copying one relation into the other.

## 5. Authority model

### 5.1 Candidate minimum

An authority claim should identify:

1. **source:** Constitution, law, regulator, profession, institution, organization, project, contract, policy, decision, delegation, or other applicable authority source;
2. **source authority:** why that source may confer or recognize the authority in the relevant jurisdiction;
3. **authority kind:** decide, direct, permit, prohibit, allocate, adopt, assess, review, represent, bind, suspend, revoke, or another specified governance act;
4. **authority holder:** the individual, artificial system, role, team, institution, distributed arrangement, hybrid, or governed process to which standing attaches;
5. **object and target:** which question, claim, role, action, resource, subject, system, or undertaking the authority concerns;
6. **Domain, Context, Scope, and jurisdiction:** where the authority applies and what it does not govern;
7. **conditions:** capability, procedure, participation, evidence, conflict, independence, supervision, external approval, or consequence conditions;
8. **time and lifecycle:** activation, duration, expiry, suspension, revocation, reassessment, replacement, succession, and historical lineage;
9. **challenge and review:** who may contest the authority, decision, exercise, or boundary and through which effective path;
10. **exercise and effect:** whether authority was exercised and whether the exercise affected conduct or status; and
11. **assessment and record:** who claims validity, on what Evidence, under what dissent and unresolved conflict.

Authority is never global merely because its holder is powerful, expert, canonical, legal, technical, Human, artificial, institutional, or successful.

### 5.2 Authority levels

| Level | Question | Prohibited substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Authority source | What applicable rule, institution, process, or external authority can confer standing? | Existence of a source proves its applicability or legitimacy everywhere |
| Authority rule | What standing can be conferred, on whom, through what procedure and limits? | General rule proves a particular assignment |
| Authority assignment or conferment | Was standing conferred on the identified subject under the rule? | Assignment proves current validity, capability, exercise, or effect |
| Authority holding | Does the subject currently hold the authority within Scope and time? | Title, prior authority, or record proves current holding |
| Authority exercise | Did the holder decide, direct, permit, prohibit, allocate, assess, or represent? | Holding proves exercise; exercise proves validity or success |
| Reception or compliance | Did another subject recognize, follow, resist, or ignore the exercise? | Compliance proves authority, legitimacy, consent, or correctness |
| Effect | What changed because of the exercise? | Effect proves lawful, justified, epistemically sound, or responsible authority |
| Authority assessment/status | Is the source, assignment, holding, exercise, or boundary valid, challenged, suspended, or unresolved? | Accepted status establishes truth or moral legitimacy |
| Authority record | What Representation preserves the source, claim, exercise, status, dissent, and history? | Signature, certificate, policy, token, account, or log is the authority relation |

### 5.3 Authority kinds must not transfer silently

| Authority kind | Minimum object | Does not automatically confer |
|---|---|---|
| Semantic authority | Governed meaning, definition, disposition, or lineage within an owned artifact and Scope | Scientific truth, legal permission, project operation, or constitutional Ratification |
| Epistemic assessment authority | Standing to conduct or recognize an assessment under stated criteria | Truth, semantic ownership, decision authority, authorization, or responsibility |
| Decision authority | Standing to settle or commit an identified question within Scope | Expertise, correctness, execution control, authorization over other acts, or liability |
| Directive authority | Standing to issue a binding or governance-relevant direction | Capability of recipient, valid execution, moral legitimacy, or truth |
| Authorization authority | Standing to grant, deny, condition, suspend, or revoke permission | Authority over the underlying meaning, every downstream use, or responsibility transfer |
| Adoption authority | Standing to make a claim, standard, policy, or artifact operative in a governed body | Epistemic truth, universal applicability, or implementation conformance |
| Review or oversight authority | Standing to inspect, challenge, approve, reject, restrict, or escalate under a process | Effective information, competence, practical control, independence, or responsibility absorption |
| Representational authority | Standing to speak, sign, file, negotiate, or act on behalf of another subject within Scope | Ownership of every claim, personal agreement, truth, or unrestricted delegation |
| Operational authority | Standing to initiate, configure, suspend, or terminate specified operation | Semantic, epistemic, legal, professional, or moral authority outside that operation |
| Constitutional or ratification authority | Standing to create or Ratify norms under a constituted process | Truth, implementation fact, Domain expertise, or external legal authority |
| External legal, regulatory, or professional authority | Standing arising outside SymK within applicable jurisdiction | Universal SymK semantics, scientific truth, or unrestricted authority beyond jurisdiction |

One subject may hold several kinds; several subjects may hold different authority over the same undertaking. Conflict is resolved through source, jurisdiction, Scope, type, priority rules, and the conflict protocol—not through a universal assumption that legal, institutional, technical, scientific, or semantic authority always dominates every other kind.

### 5.4 Validity, legitimacy, warrant, and effectiveness

Authority analysis must distinguish:

- **source validity:** whether the authority follows from the applicable source and procedure;
- **jurisdictional applicability:** whether that source governs this subject, object, place, Domain, time, and use;
- **epistemic adequacy:** whether a decision or exercise is supported by appropriate Grounds and Evidence;
- **practical effectiveness:** whether the exercise can actually affect the target;
- **legal or organizational validity:** whether it is recognized under applicable rules;
- **moral or political legitimacy:** whether the authority or exercise is justified under relevant normative standards;
- **responsible exercise:** whether prospective and retrospective responsibilities are satisfied; and
- **consequential acceptability:** whether effects and burdens are acceptable.

These may converge or diverge. Stream B defines no universal legitimacy test and does not use internal validity to sanitize harmful, coercive, epistemically defective, or unchallengeable authority.

### 5.5 De facto power and apparent authority

A subject may be obeyed because it controls infrastructure, information, resources, sanctions, interface defaults, or access while lacking valid authority. This is Power or de facto control, not authority by effect.

A subject may appear authorized because of title, interface, branding, prior practice, delegated access, organizational presentation, or another authority's conduct. Apparent authority may have legal significance in a Domain, but it does not silently become universal SymK authority. The source, reliance, jurisdiction, and consequence remain explicit.

## 6. Authorization, permission, mandate, and reliance

### 6.1 Authorization profile

A material authorization should identify:

- authorizing authority and its authority to authorize;
- authorized subject and stable identity;
- permitted act, role, resource, reliance, operation, or decision;
- purpose, Domain, Context, Scope, target, population, and use;
- conditions, safeguards, capability prerequisites, and supervision;
- exclusions and prohibited interpretations;
- start, duration, expiry, renewal, suspension, revocation, and succession;
- delegation or subauthorization conditions;
- external-authority constraints;
- challenge, review, escalation, and emergency handling;
- actual exercise and effect separately; and
- record, Evidence, status, dissent, and lineage.

Authorization is neither a property of the subject nor an intrinsic property of the action. It is a relation within a governed arrangement.

### 6.2 Permission, authorization, mandate, and prohibition

| Relation | Working boundary |
|---|---|
| Permission | An act or state is allowed under an applicable rule; may be general and need not identify an individual grant |
| Authorization | An applicable authority permits an identified subject or class to perform a specified act, role, use, or operation under conditions |
| Mandate | An authority-backed charge or direction assigning a function, objective, or required course within Scope; exact duty and obligation semantics remain Stream C |
| Directive or order | A specific authority exercise instructing another subject or process to act, refrain, or maintain a condition |
| Prohibition | An applicable governance constraint disallowing an act or use |
| Exception or waiver | A governed narrowing or suspension of an otherwise applicable constraint within authority and conditions |
| Reliance Authorization | DR-012 scoped decision relation permitting or requiring reliance on an epistemic object for an identified use without making it Knowledge or truth |

Permission does not require action. Mandate does not prove capability or compliance. Prohibition does not make action impossible. Authorization does not make action correct, responsible, successful, or epistemically warranted.

### 6.3 Authorization and practical ability

A subject may be authorized but unable to act because it lacks capability, information, time, access, control, resources, protection, or agency. A subject may be capable and act without authorization. A technical permission may be enabled while organizational, legal, professional, or ethical authorization is absent. A legal permission may exist while an access-control system blocks action.

Engineering permission, organizational authorization, legal permission, professional competence, epistemic warrant, moral justification, and effective control must not share one boolean status.

### 6.4 Authorization conflict

When one authority permits and another prohibits or conditions the same action, record:

- each source and authority kind;
- jurisdiction, Scope, subject, object, and time overlap;
- applicable priority or conflict rule;
- unresolved incompatibility and immediate containment;
- affected operations and reliance claims;
- available review, challenge, exception, or escalation route; and
- the separate semantic and epistemic consequences.

External authority may control action within its jurisdiction without becoming universal semantic authority. SymK compatibility cannot authorize prohibited deployment; local authorization cannot amend SymK meaning.

## 7. Decision system

### 7.1 Decision occurrence and governance status

A decision occurrence requires:

1. identified deciding subject or process;
2. question, object, or action state being decided;
3. relevant alternatives or possible dispositions;
4. selection or commitment that closes, narrows, prioritizes, defers, conditions, or redirects the question;
5. mechanism through which the selection governs later conduct or status;
6. Context, Scope, time, inputs, constraints, and uncertainty; and
7. Evidence and competing explanations distinguishing decision from output, recommendation, execution, or post hoc label.

A **governance decision** additionally claims recognized decision status. It must identify the applicable decision authority, procedure, quorum or participation conditions where relevant, challenge path, status, and lineage.

An unauthorized selection can be an actual decision occurrence without becoming a valid governance decision. Conversely, an authorized body may have authority but make no decision, defer, fail quorum, or issue an output that does not commit the system.

### 7.2 Adjacent decision relations

| Relation | Minimum meaning | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Proposal | A presented candidate for adoption, selection, action, or decision | Recommendation, acceptance, authority, or decision |
| Recommendation | Advice favoring a course under stated analysis or criteria | Decision, authorization, adoption, correctness, or responsibility |
| Assessment or judgment | An evaluative conclusion under criteria | Decision authority, commitment, authorization, or action |
| Selection | Choice among alternatives | Governance status, authority, implementation, or responsible exercise |
| Decision | Selection or commitment that governs a subsequent state | Validity, authorization, execution, success, or responsibility |
| Adoption | Governance act making a claim, policy, artifact, standard, or position operative within Scope | Truth, universal force, implementation, or conformance |
| Approval | Authority exercise expressing acceptance for a stated purpose or gate | Epistemic validation, broad authorization, effective review, responsibility transfer, or successful outcome |
| Ratification | Constituted authority act giving a norm the specified higher-order status | Truth, universal legal force, or implementation fact |
| Authorization | Permission relation for a subject, act, use, or role | Selection, decision, occurrence, correctness, or duty |
| Execution | Performance of an instruction, operation, or committed course | Decision authority, approval, responsibility, or intended effect |
| Outcome or consequence | Result or later effect connected to conduct | Decision validity, authorization, agency relative to every effect, or value |
| Decision record | Representation of inputs, selection, authority, status, dissent, and lineage | Decision occurrence, authority, validity, or conformance |

### 7.3 Automated and policy-mediated decisions

A policy can pre-commit a class of cases, while a runtime process applies, interprets, or selects within that policy. The runtime event may be:

- mechanical execution of a prior decision;
- a new decision occurrence under delegated decision authority;
- a recommendation awaiting adoption;
- a control action without decision authority; or
- an output mislabeled as a decision.

The classification depends on actual alternative selection, operative commitment, authority source, conditions, and system boundary—not on the word “automated.” A model is not necessarily the decision-maker merely because its score controls a workflow, and a Human is not necessarily the decision-maker merely because a final click records approval.

### 7.4 Decision authorship, ownership, and responsibility

Who generated a proposal, trained a model, configured a threshold, supplied Evidence, selected a recommendation, adopted a policy, approved use, executed the action, owns the system, and remains responsible may differ. “Decision owner” is ambiguous and must be decomposed before use.

Stream B does not allocate responsibility from decision status or final approval.

## 8. Delegation model

### 8.1 Delegation profile

A delegation claim should identify:

1. delegator and source authority;
2. delegate and stable subject boundary;
3. delegable object: task, function, role, decision, authority kind, authorization, execution, control pathway, or representation;
4. purpose, Domain, Context, Scope, target, population, and time;
5. what is assigned or transferred;
6. what is explicitly retained by the delegator;
7. capability, agency, access, and control prerequisites of the delegate;
8. discretion and permitted alternatives;
9. subdelegation or automation conditions;
10. supervision, reporting, challenge, audit, intervention, escalation, and recall;
11. expiry, suspension, revocation, failure, replacement, and succession;
12. external-authority limits;
13. actual acceptance, exercise, deviation, effect, and return; and
14. responsibility, accountability, liability, remedy, and duty claims preserved separately for Streams C–E.

Delegation must not be represented by a single `delegated=true` field.

### 8.2 What delegation may and may not do

| Relation | Possible delegation effect | Non-effect |
|---|---|---|
| Task or function | Assign performance of identified work | Does not create capability, authority, agency, or completion |
| Role occupancy | Assign or nominate a subject to a role if the source permits | Does not satisfy eligibility, activation, authority, or performance automatically |
| Decision authority | Confer bounded standing to decide if the delegator may confer it | Does not guarantee correct, validly exercised, or responsible decisions |
| Authorization authority | Permit delegate to grant or revoke specified permissions if subauthorization is allowed | Does not expand beyond the delegator's jurisdiction or survive expiry automatically |
| Execution | Assign performance of an instruction or action | Does not transfer the underlying decision, authority, duty, or responsibility automatically |
| Control pathway | Reconfigure access or intervention relations | Words alone do not transfer effective information, timing, ability, or effect |
| Representation | Permit another subject to speak, sign, file, or negotiate within Scope | Does not transfer every belief, intention, ownership interest, or authority kind |
| Supervision | Assign monitoring or review functions | Does not make supervision effective or absorb every retained relation |
| Responsibility, duty, accountability, liability, or remedy | May be affected under applicable later rules | Never inferred automatically from delegation; principal analysis remains Streams C–E and external authority |

### 8.3 Delegation is not disappearance

Delegation can produce several concurrent relations:

- delegate authority over the assigned object;
- delegator retained authority, recall, policy, resource, or supervision control;
- delegate agency and execution;
- provider, organization, or infrastructure causal Contribution;
- another authority's external constraints;
- affected-party challenge or standing interfaces; and
- prospective and retrospective responsibility relations not yet allocated.

A delegator cannot erase these by saying “the delegate decided,” and a delegate cannot erase its exercise by saying “the delegator authorized it.”

### 8.4 Automation and tools

Tool use and automation are not delegation automatically. A calculator may execute without receiving authority. A model may produce recommendations under a role assigned to a configured system. A runtime agent may receive operational authorization and limited decision authority. A Human–artificial hybrid may implement a distributed role.

The system must state whether the arrangement is:

- instrumental execution;
- task assignment;
- decision support;
- delegated decision authority;
- automated application of a prior policy;
- joint or hybrid decision process;
- supervised autonomy; or
- another governed relation.

Product labels do not settle the classification.

## 9. Supervision and oversight

### 9.1 Candidate minimum

Supervision is a governed relation in which an identified supervisor or supervisory arrangement has specified monitoring, review, intervention, escalation, support, assurance, or correction functions over an identified subject, role, process, decision, action, or system within Scope.

A material supervision claim should identify:

- supervisor and authority source;
- supervised subject, process, role, action, or target;
- purpose, Scope, Context, time, consequence class, and expected standard;
- information access, observability, interpretability, competence, time, and attention;
- review timing: ex ante, concurrent, exception-triggered, periodic, or ex post;
- decision, approval, intervention, veto, stop, rollback, correction, and escalation authority;
- effective control pathways and their tested limits;
- independence, conflicts of interest, incentives, sanctions, and protection;
- challenge and affected-party interfaces;
- delegation, staffing, continuity, replacement, and failure conditions;
- actual monitoring, intervention, response, and effect; and
- record, Evidence, uncertainty, status, dissent, and lineage.

### 9.2 Oversight modes are not interchangeable

| Mode | Primary function | Common false inference |
|---|---|---|
| Ex ante review | Evaluate design, plan, model, policy, evidence, or proposed action before operation | Approval controls later execution or every consequence |
| Prior authorization | Permit a subject or act under stated conditions | Authorization guarantees capability, compliance, monitoring, or responsible exercise |
| Concurrent monitoring | Observe operation while it occurs | Observation provides timely comprehension or intervention capacity |
| Human-in-the-loop intervention | Human can participate during a process | Presence, click, or nominal veto is meaningful control |
| Exception review | Investigate selected alerts or deviations | Detection coverage is complete or unalerted cases are safe |
| Periodic supervision | Review patterns and performance at intervals | Periodic access controls each event or prevents harm |
| Ex post audit | Reconstruct conduct and conformance after occurrence | Audit is supervision at action time or provides remedy automatically |
| Independent review | Assess outside the direct operating chain | Formal independence ensures information access, power, competence, or effect |
| External oversight | Review or constrain under external authority | External authority owns SymK semantics or guarantees epistemic correctness |

Several modes may be needed. None automatically establishes effective supervision or accountability.

### 9.3 Nominal versus effective supervision

Supervision is nominal when it exists mainly as title, policy, required click, committee membership, interface placement, contractual statement, or unused access. It becomes more supportable as effective when the supervisory arrangement has:

- timely and material information;
- sufficient intelligibility and capability;
- practical alternatives and protected authority to intervene;
- time, attention, staffing, and resources;
- independence appropriate to the risk and conflict;
- tested stop, veto, correction, rollback, or escalation pathways;
- proportionate coverage and trigger design;
- reasoned response and challenge reception;
- preserved evidence and unresolved dissent; and
- actual capacity to affect the operative system before or after harm as claimed.

Effective supervision need not mean continuous Human observation or complete control. It must match the claim being made. A supervisor can effectively control deployment while lacking control over model-internal reasoning; can review policy without reviewing each event; or can audit consequences without having prevented them.

### 9.4 Supervision does not absorb responsibility

The existence of supervision does not automatically:

- make the supervisor the agent of the supervised action;
- transfer the delegate's role or action to the supervisor;
- erase provider, deployer, operator, user, institutional, or artificial Contributions;
- establish that the supervisor had effective control;
- make the supervised subject irresponsible;
- make the supervisor liable for every consequence; or
- create remedy or answerability.

Streams C and D must test these relations using the actual supervision and control graph.

## 10. Authority lifecycle and change

Authority, authorization, role, delegation, and supervision are temporally governed. Relevant events include:

- proposal or nomination;
- eligibility assessment;
- assignment or conferment;
- acceptance where applicable;
- activation;
- exercise;
- review and reassessment;
- expansion or narrowing;
- temporary suspension;
- revocation;
- expiry and renewal;
- delegation and subdelegation;
- role entry and exit;
- replacement and succession;
- fork, merge, reconstitution, dissolution, or institutional redesign;
- unresolved challenge or external-authority collision; and
- historical record after the relation ends.

An expired authorization remains a historical fact but no longer permits current action. A revoked authority does not retroactively erase prior valid or invalid exercises. A successor does not inherit authority, duty, responsibility, liability, or standing merely by receiving a name, account, asset, role record, model, team, contract, or organizational identity. The applicable source and continuity rules must state what persists.

### 11.1 Role occupancy by artificial or hybrid subjects

SymK does not universally prohibit an artificial or hybrid subject from occupying a role. It also does not infer role occupancy from capability, deployment, interface presence, model invocation, or anthropomorphic language.

The applicable role source must identify whether the occupant is:

- a model;
- a configured runtime or service;
- a toolchain;
- a deployment;
- an institution using an artificial component;
- a Human–artificial hybrid;
- or another organized subject.

Where law or professional rules require a Human or legal person, that constraint governs within its jurisdiction. It remains a scoped external or Domain rule, not a universal truth that artificial systems cannot exercise operational agency or occupy non-legal roles.

### 11.2 No property transfer

- Member authority does not automatically create collective authority.
- Collective authority does not give every member authority to act alone.
- An institution's authority does not make every employee or component an authority.
- A technical permission does not establish organizational or legal authorization.
- A Human approval step does not transfer every prior decision, causal Contribution, control relation, or future responsibility to the Human.
- An artificial recommendation does not acquire decision authority because it is usually followed.
- A delegator's authority does not make the delegate authoritative beyond the delegated object.
- A delegate's action does not erase retained delegator authority or control.
- A role record does not copy role, authority, or authorization to a successor.
- Hybrid decision status does not erase component-level proposals, selections, approvals, executions, or dissent.

## 12. Candidate approaches considered

### A — Permanent Human decision authority and artificial proposal role

**Rejected as a universal SymK rule.** It gives origin categorical role privilege, permits ceremonial Human approval, conflicts with scoped capability and authority evidence, and cannot represent institutional, collective, automated, external-authority, or hybrid arrangements honestly. A Domain may impose such a rule within jurisdiction and must state its source and substance.

### B — The most capable or expert subject should hold authority

**Rejected.** Capability and expertise are relevant evidence but cannot supply jurisdiction, legitimacy, affected-party standing, lawful permission, conflict constraints, or responsibility. The best predictor need not be the authorized decision-maker.

### C — Authority is merely Power or effective control

**Rejected.** Unauthorized subjects can control infrastructure or compel compliance; valid authorities can be ineffective. Authority is sourced governance standing, not success or obedience.

### D — Any authorization or final approval transfers responsibility

**Rejected.** Permission and approval do not erase prior causal, role, decision, control, delegation, or supervision relations. Responsibility remains for Streams C–D.

### E — Delegation transfers the complete role and releases the delegator

**Rejected.** Delegation is typed and partial; it must state what is assigned and retained. Capability, effective control, duty, accountability, liability, and responsibility do not transfer automatically.

### F — A Human-in-the-loop or audit log is sufficient supervision

**Rejected.** Nominal presence, a click, formal access, retrospective record, or policy does not establish timely information, competence, practical intervention, independence, correction, or effect.

### G — Authority as sourced, scoped, typed, temporal, challengeable relation with distinct decision, delegation, and supervision layers

**Retained provisionally.** It preserves non-privilege, external authority, operational reality, multi-level subjects, challenge, and lineage while preventing capability, title, workflow, permission, final approval, or delegation from manufacturing responsibility.

## 14. Three-view preservation obligations

### 14.1 Human View

The Human View should preserve:

- what roles, authority, permission, decision, delegation, and supervision mean to occupants, participants, challengers, and affected parties;
- who understood themselves to propose, assess, decide, approve, authorize, execute, supervise, represent, or bear consequences;
- actual information, expertise, uncertainty, alternatives, time, pressure, dependence, conflicts, and practical ability to refuse or intervene;
- reasons for authority and role allocation, including disputed legitimacy and external constraints;
- dissent over source, jurisdiction, decision, control, approval, or role boundary;
- changed or expired roles and authority; and
- consequences, challenge, explanation, and known limits.

Human testimony, title, organizational narrative, or signature is Evidence, not conclusive authority for the underlying relations.

### 14.2 Scientific View

The Scientific View should preserve, where material:

- questions and methods used to assess capability, expertise, role performance, decision quality, supervision effectiveness, intervention, and outcome;
- competing explanations for apparent Human, artificial, collective, institutional, distributed, or hybrid decision-making;
- selection effects, automation bias, authority bias, rubber-stamping, alert fatigue, workload, latency, and incentive effects;
- evidence for review coverage, comprehension, intervention success, failure detection, false reassurance, and practical alternatives;
- uncertainty, counterevidence, confounding, generalization limits, and conditions of defeat; and
- the distinction between measured performance and normative or legal authority.

No credential, benchmark, calibration score, acceptance rate, override rate, or observed compliance defines authority, legitimacy, or supervision universally.

### 14.3 Engineering View

The Engineering View should eventually preserve:

- subject, role, account, credential, authority source, permission, policy, delegation, decision, approval, execution, and supervision as distinct identities and relations;
- Domain, Context, Scope, target, population, time, version, condition, expiry, revocation, and succession;
- proposal, recommendation, assessment, selection, adoption, approval, authorization, execution, outcome, and record separately;
- technical permission versus organizational, legal, professional, epistemic, and moral statuses;
- model, runtime, configured service, Human, institution, committee, hybrid, and external-authority boundaries;
- information, timing, control, override, veto, stop, rollback, escalation, audit, and failure pathways;
- delegator/delegate, transferred/retained relation, subdelegation, supervision, and recall;
- challenges, unresolved conflicts, external prohibitions, dissent, and declared Projection loss; and
- actual runtime behavior separately from declared policy and role configuration.

This stream selects no role language, authority ontology, policy language, access-control model, identity provider, workflow engine, decision log, delegation protocol, supervision metric, or conformance implementation.

### 14.4 Alignment rule

A policy stating “Human approval required,” a study reporting high reviewer agreement, and an implementation containing an approval button remain misaligned if reviewers lack timely Evidence, competence, independent judgment, practical refusal, or effective intervention. A technical permission does not prove legal authorization; a valid legal role does not prove Engineering access; an implemented decision path does not settle semantic authority. Material mismatch keeps the role, authority, decision, or supervision status challenged.

## 16. Consequences for governing artifacts

### Product Vision

The Product Vision's requirement that epistemic roles and authority be explicit, justified, and revisable needs a relation system rather than a list of Human and AI job titles. “Contributed, decided, authorized, or remains responsible” must remain separate, and role reassignment must not erase causal, decision, authority, consequence, or historical responsibility evidence.

### Candidate A0

Responsible Cooperation cannot infer legitimate authority from actual Cooperation or equalize roles by default. A shared undertaking may include asymmetric expert, decision, authorization, execution, supervision, affected-party, and challenge roles. A0 selection in 2.2 must preserve source, Scope, Power, practical challenge, and consequence without making authority synonymous with domination or consent.

### Candidate A1

A1's authority/warrant separation is reinforced. Authority may adopt, authorize reliance, or direct action under uncertainty, but cannot create truth or Knowledge. Challenge of an authority decision must preserve Grounds, Evidence, Scope, status, and operational consequence separately.

### Candidate A2

A2 is directionally supported but requires significant precision before constitutional selection:

- role is a relation with assignment, occupancy, capability, performance, authority, authorization, and record levels;
- authority is sourced, typed, jurisdiction-bound, scoped, temporal, and challengeable;
- authorization is permission, not capability, action, epistemic warrant, or responsible exercise;
- proposal, review, assessment, decision, adoption, approval, authorization, execution, and accountability must remain distinct;
- Human oversight must be effective for the stated target rather than ceremonial;
- artificial or hybrid role occupancy remains source- and jurisdiction-dependent rather than universally forbidden or granted;
- delegation must state what is transferred and retained; and
- revisability requires expiry, reassessment, suspension, revocation, succession, and historical lineage.

The current A2 definitions are candidate constitutional shorthand, not the accepted semantic baseline. Stream B does not accept, revise, or reject A2.

### Candidate A4

Knowledge Engineering designs role, decision, authorization, supervision, challenge, and control conditions that materially shape epistemic pursuit and application. These conditions must be traceable without claiming that the engineer owns every downstream decision or consequence. A4 should later preserve actual versus declared role systems and prevent logs, workflows, validations, or approvals from manufacturing accountability.

### Candidate A6

Inheritance must state which role, authority, authorization, delegation, decision, supervision, exception, and challenge relations pass to a project or successor. Nothing transfers merely through adoption, compatibility claim, shared repository, account migration, organizational continuity, or technical reuse.

### J1 and the conflict protocol

J1's distinction among SymK, derived-project, organization/deployment, and external-authority jurisdictions is reinforced.
