## 7. Principal analytical hazards

- **Content–capability substitution:** treating stored Knowledge, retrieval volume, context-window size, or access as amplified Intelligence.
- **Performance–capacity substitution:** treating one better answer, benchmark gain, adoption result, or successful episode as enduring capability change.
- **Scalar amplification:** allowing one score, average, speedup, or output count to hide dimensional losses and distributional effects.
- **Baseline manipulation:** selecting a weak, incomparable, transient, or resource-mismatched baseline to manufacture gain.
- **Bearer inflation:** drawing the system boundary around every helpful resource after success and shrinking it after failure.
- **Property transfer:** copying component, participant, user, team, institution, tool, or system capabilities across levels.
- **Aggregation–integration collapse:** treating access, connection, coordination, or parallel work as an integrated capacity.
- **Automation–amplification collapse:** treating fewer Human steps, task completion, labor replacement, or autonomy as amplification.
- **AI essentialism:** defining every DIA through artificial Intelligence despite possible Human, collective, institutional, or other configurations.
- **Architecture essentialism:** equating the DIA system class with one model, agent pattern, knowledge graph, platform, or product stack.
- **Domain leakage:** universalizing one profession's constructs, benchmarks, task priorities, or economic values.
- **Dependency erasure:** ignoring external models, providers, data, infrastructure, expertise, maintenance, supervision, or institutional support.
- **Deskilling concealment:** counting current output gain while hiding lost Human, team, or institutional capability and future-expert formation.
- **Replacement laundering:** calling substitution or displacement amplification without identifying who gains, who loses, and which Bearer persists.
- **Cost externalization:** claiming lower total cost while shifting verification, risk, harm, maintenance, remedy, learning, or future-capability costs to others.
- **Value substitution:** treating amplification as proof of better answers, authorized action, responsible operation, ethical compatibility, or beneficial outcomes.
- **Responsibility laundering:** using the integrated-system label to obscure provider, deployer, user, institution, predecessor, successor, and affected-subject relations.
- **Assessment authority:** allowing a benchmark, expert rating, certification, user preference, market adoption, or governance status to define the predicate.
- **Artifact reification:** treating a profile, score, dashboard, system card, architecture diagram, or audit record as the actual capability.
- **View mismatch:** accepting an Engineering success while Human or Scientific Views expose unclear meaning, invalid evidence, hidden loss, or affected consequences.
- **Axiom or paper pre-acceptance:** importing candidate constitutional or Foundation Paper conclusions as settled semantics.
- **Implementation authority:** allowing deployed behavior or technical convenience to define Amplification or DIA.

## 10. Mandatory counterexample set

At minimum, the analysis must explain:

1. a repository gains far more Domain content but no participant or system gains capability across relevant variation;
2. a tool produces one better answer through luck or hidden leakage without durable capability change;
3. response speed rises while contextual sensitivity, robustness, error detection, or challenge capacity falls;
4. an aggregate score rises while a material capability dimension declines;
5. Human and artificial participants contribute complementary profiles, but Evidence does not support a new system-level Bearer;
6. an automated workflow removes intermediate Human work and reduces labor while the relevant reasoning capacity remains unchanged or narrows;
7. an individual professional gains capability using a system although the organization or configured system does not;
8. a team or institution gains integrated capability while individual members do not and may deskill;
9. a replaceable tool enables performance but counterfactual removal shows no integrated or retained capability change;
10. an external model, data source, platform, or expert is indispensable but omitted from the claimed Bearer and dependency record;
11. a component or model update improves one function but changes the system Identity, baseline, or responsible lifecycle relations;
12. participant turnover preserves institutional capability through processes and memory without copying Intelligence to every member;
13. a system excels on a narrow benchmark but fails materially varied Domain cases;
14. a comparison uses unequal resources, assistance, time, prior Knowledge, or selection conditions and manufactures an apparent gain;
15. a fully Human or institutional arrangement produces genuine integrated capability gain without artificial Intelligence;
16. an AI-rich product automates tasks and scales output but provides no evidence of Amplified Intelligence;
17. the configured system gains capability while its principal users lose retained capability and practical alternatives;
18. current productivity improves while dependency, maintenance burden, lock-in, or outage fragility increases total cost;
19. professional displacement reduces the formation path for future experts whose judgment the system later requires;
20. affected nonparticipants bear increased risk or cost despite a valid capability-gain attribution;
21. a deployed system is authorized, popular, profitable, or legally compliant but its amplification claim remains epistemically unsupported;
22. provider, deployer, user, institution, and configured-system capability or Responsibility claims diverge across levels;
23. feedback and adaptation improve later performance but do not by themselves establish system Learning, Education, persistence, or responsible amplification; and
24. the Human, Scientific, and Engineering Views disagree over the amplified Bearer, baseline, evidence, omitted loss, dependency, or consequence.
