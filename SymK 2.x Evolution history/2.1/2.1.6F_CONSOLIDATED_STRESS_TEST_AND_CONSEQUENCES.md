## 3. Consolidated responsibility spine

The combined system can be stated as a recursive, typed sequence:

```text
identified subjects, systems, levels, contexts, and conditions
    may produce events, outputs, behavior, actions, omissions, and consequences
        through separately evidenced causal Contributions, agency, and control
            while roles, authority, authorization, decisions, delegation, and supervision
                govern only within sourced, scoped, temporal, and jurisdictional relations
                    from which prospective or retrospective Responsibility relations may arise
                        for explicit objects under declared sources, frames, and standards
                            across one or more subjects, levels, phases, and allocation forms
                                with answerability, accountability, correction, repair, and remedy interfaces
                                    under affected-subject, consent, challenge, uncertainty, and ethical-compatibility constraints
                                        whose consequences return Evidence for reassessment, revision, and succession
```

No arrow is automatic. No later status retroactively manufactures an earlier factual or normative relation. No favorable or harmful outcome changes the subject boundary to fit the desired allocation.

### 3.1 Responsibility anchor

> **Responsibility is a sourced, scoped, phased, and challengeable relationship family in which an identified subject at a declared level stands, under a stated governing source, frame, jurisdiction, and standard, in a prospective or retrospective relation to an identified object. A material Responsibility claim preserves the relevant factual and governance links, expected or available response, Evidence, uncertainty, status, lifecycle, affected subjects, and conditions of challenge, discharge, breach, reassessment, correction, repair, remedy, or unresolved gap.**

> **The Responsibility relation remains distinct from its assignment, allocation, attribution, assessment, acceptance, performance, discharge, breach, enforcement, response, and record. Causal Contribution, agency, control, role, authority, authorization, decision, legal personality, outcome, consent, and affected status may support or constrain a Responsibility claim under an applicable frame; none is universally necessary or sufficient by itself.**

The anchor is source-relative without being empty. A source must be identified and its authority, jurisdiction, Scope, standard, and conflicts remain challengeable. A relation may exist under a declared frame even when its bearer cannot perform, no forum can enforce it, or no remedy is available; those are capability, authority, enforcement, accountability, or remedy gaps rather than proof that the relation does not exist.

### 3.2 Agency anchor

> **Agency is a scoped capability of an organized subject to govern conduct through integrated selection and control processes responsive to relevant variation, such that materially different conduct remains attributable to the subject under materially different conditions.**

Agency is factual and explanatory. It does not establish moral agency, free will, intention, authority, authorization, responsibility, legal personality, rights, duties, or standing. Responsibility can arise without agency under role, institutional, strict, remedial, or other sourced frames; agency can exist where no Responsibility relation applies.

### 3.3 Action, omission, causal Contribution, and control

- **Action:** an event or process attributable, within a declared target and Scope, to an exercise of agency.
- **Omission:** absence of an action relative to an evidenced alternative, expectation, capacity, opportunity, or standard; not every non-event is an omission.
- **Causal Contribution:** a model-relative difference made to an event, condition, action, omission, or consequence.
- **Control:** a scoped relation in which a subject or mechanism has and may exercise effective pathways to select, initiate, continue, modify, inhibit, terminate, or materially constrain a target process or condition.

Each claim preserves subject, target, model, Context, Scope, time, alternatives, mechanism, level, uncertainty, and Evidence. Capacity, exercise, effect, attribution, assessment, status, and record remain distinct.

### 3.4 Role, authority, authorization, and decision

- **Role:** a scoped governance or process relation connecting an identified occupant to expected functions, conditions, interfaces, and review under a source.
- **Authority:** a sourced, jurisdiction-bound, typed, scoped, temporal, and challengeable governance relation concerning recognized standing to assess, decide, direct, authorize, adopt, review, represent, or otherwise govern an object.
- **Authorization:** a scoped permission, mandate, or governance status for an identified subject, action, role, use, decision, or resource under an authority.
- **Decision:** an occurrence in which an identified subject or constituted body selects or commits among materially relevant alternatives for a stated object and Scope; governance-valid status requires applicable authority and procedure.
- **Delegation:** a typed assignment or transfer relation that identifies what is conveyed and what is retained.
- **Supervision or oversight:** a monitoring, review, intervention, support, assurance, escalation, or correction relation whose effectiveness depends on practical capability and exercise rather than its label.

Expertise, epistemic warrant, Power, authority, authorization, decision, approval, execution, and outcome remain non-substitutable. A valid authority relation does not establish legitimacy, correctness, ethical adequacy, or responsibility by itself.

### 3.5 Responsibility phases and adjacent normative relations

- **Prospective Responsibility:** a relation concerning entrusted, assigned, or otherwise sourced governance before or during conduct and consequence.
- **Duty:** a sourced normative requirement concerning conduct, condition, role, process, response, or outcome.
- **Obligation:** a particularized binding relation under a source; Domain vocabularies may map duty and obligation differently.
- **Retrospective Responsibility:** a relation concerning evaluated past conditions, actions, omissions, decisions, risks, or consequences.
- **Answerability:** a relation requiring a subject to provide an account to an identified claimant or forum for an object and period.
- **Accountability arrangement:** a governed arrangement through which conduct or consequence can be called to account, examined, answered for, and connected to possible response.
- **Blame or culpability:** a framework-specific negative fault attribution requiring its own criteria.
- **Liability:** a legal or institutionally constituted relation under applicable authority.
- **Remedy:** a governed response available to an affected subject or claimant under an applicable frame.
- **Correction:** response to error, defect, or deviation in a claim, decision, representation, process, configuration, or system.
- **Repair:** response directed toward damaged conditions, capabilities, relationships, practices, records, or systems.

These relations can coexist, diverge, or be absent. Explanation is not answerability; answerability is not blame; blame is not liability; liability is not punishment; punishment is not correction; correction is not repair; repair is not remedy; and payment does not exhaust prevention or recurrence.

### 3.6 Allocation anchor

> **A Responsibility allocation claim is a governed mapping connecting specified Responsibility relations and objects to identified subjects at declared levels, phases, times, and conditions under stated sources and frames. It preserves distinct, shared, joint, overlapping, retained, transferred, inherited, disputed, and unallocated relations; performance capability; affected subjects; response architecture; and gap status. The mapping or record does not create the relations by itself.**

Responsibility is not a conserved substance or one additive pie. Multiple subjects can bear different or overlapping relations concerning the same object. A percentage or share is meaningful only under a declared frame that defines the relation, denominator, method, purpose, and non-transfer implications.

### 3.7 Minimum ethical responsibility boundary

A Responsibility arrangement may claim the candidate SymK ethical minimum only when it preserves:

1. affected-subject non-erasure;
2. non-privilege and non-substitution by type, origin, capability, status, authority, compliance, consent record, or outcome;
3. consequence-sensitive attention to Power, dependence, vulnerability, foreseeability, uncertainty, severity, reversibility, distribution, and residual effect;
4. integrity of consent claims, including understanding, voluntariness, practical refusal, Scope, time, and representation;
5. effective protected challenge and capable accountable response;
6. prevention, containment, correction, repair, remedy, residual harm, recurrence, and revision continuity; and
7. visible jurisdiction, authority conflict, incapacity, gap, and unresolved incompatibility.

This qualification does not decide whether a source-relative Responsibility relation exists. It governs whether the arrangement may claim the candidate SymK ethical compatibility. It does not create a complete ethical theory, universal consent requirement, affected-party veto, rights catalogue, legal status, risk calculus, or remedy.

## 4. Six-plane analytical architecture

Every material Responsibility analysis should preserve six non-substitutable planes.

### 4.1 Factual plane

The factual plane records:

- condition, event, output, behavior, action, omission, and consequence;
- causal model and Contribution;
- agency capacity and exercise;
- control capacity, exercise, mechanism, target, and effect;
- knowledge, information access, intention, foreseeability, and alternatives where material;
- dependence, vulnerability, and Power;
- Evidence, uncertainty, competing explanations, and level.

It does not itself create duty, authority, blame, liability, consent, standing, or remedy.

### 4.2 Governance plane

The governance plane records:

- role source, definition, assignment, occupancy, performance, and review;
- authority source, kind, jurisdiction, conferment, holding, exercise, reception, expiry, revocation, and challenge;
- authorization, permission, mandate, directive, prohibition, exception, waiver, and Reliance Authorization;
- proposal, recommendation, assessment, selection, decision, adoption, approval, execution, and Ratification;
- delegation, retained relations, supervision, and oversight;
- external authority and conflicts.

It does not establish epistemic warrant, factual agency, ethical legitimacy, or responsibility by title or status.

### 4.3 Responsibility plane

The Responsibility plane records:

- object, source, frame, standard, jurisdiction, Scope, phase, and time;
- prospective responsibility, duty, obligation, and retrospective responsibility;
- answerability, accountability arrangement, blame, culpability, sanction, liability, remedy, repair, and correction;
- assignment, attribution, assessment, acceptance, performance, discharge, breach, enforcement, response, and record;
- affected subjects, claimants, forums, and status.

It does not collapse the supporting factual or governance relations.

### 4.4 Allocation and level plane

The allocation plane records:

- individual, component, role, team, committee, collective, institution, organization, legal entity, network, configured system, hybrid, representative, predecessor, successor, claimant, forum, and affected-subject levels;
- provider, data-source, developer, integrator, owner, procurer, deployer, operator, approver, user, maintainer, auditor, insurer, and external-authority functions where material;
- distinct, sequential, parallel, nested, shared, joint, overlapping, collective, institutional, distributed, hybrid, retained, transferred, inherited, vicarious, disputed, and unallocated relations;
- capability, authority, resources, access, control, independence, time, and alternatives for performance;
- stable boundaries, succession, and gaps.

It does not copy predicates among levels or turn a role catalogue into universal allocation.

### 4.5 Ethical and affected-subject plane

The ethical plane records:

- affected subjects, interests, representation, materiality, standing, and distribution;
- consent requirement or claim, notice, understanding, voluntariness, practical refusal, Scope, duration, withdrawal, and third-party effects;
- Power, dependence, vulnerability, foreseeability, uncertainty, severity, reversibility, and residual harm;
- challenge notice, access, capacity, protection, reception, evaluation, response, review, appeal, recurrence, and Learning;
- prevention, mitigation, containment, cessation, correction, repair, remedy, and unresolved response;
- emergency and conflicting duties.

It does not select substantive ethical values, legal rules, or remedies universally.

### 4.6 Representation and status plane

The representation plane records:

- who asserts, assesses, accepts, rejects, challenges, or records each claim;
- Human, Scientific, and Engineering View identity;
- source, authority, purpose, method, transformation, Scope, uncertainty, dissent, and loss;
- actual relation, attribution, assessment, status, acceptance, enforcement, and record separately;
- version, time, lineage, supersession, correction, and current state;
- projection limits of matrices, graphs, dashboards, scores, policies, contracts, logs, model cards, and runtime fields.

It does not manufacture the underlying relation through representation.

**Recommended Stream G disposition:** Foundational concept.

### 8.2 Why Responsibility is Foundational but Agency is not

Responsibility is constitutionally necessary to the Product Vision and cannot be delegated wholly to Domains without making “under responsibility constraints” empty. Agency is indispensable but remains a supporting explanatory concept because:

- Responsibility can exist without agency under sourced role, institutional, legal, strict, remedial, or prospective relations;
- agency can exist without applicable Responsibility;
- DR-011 already supplies the organized-subject/Bearer structure needed for agency attribution;
- agency does not own the normative, allocation, affected-party, or response system;
- and treating Agency as co-foundational would encourage moral/legal inference from operational behavior.

**Recommended Stream G disposition:** Agency as a governed supporting capability concept; Agent as a governed attribution role.

### 8.3 Why Authority is not Foundational

Authority is universal and indispensable, but it is a sourced governance relation supported by the accepted structural kernel, DR-012 governance/status system, DR-013 Power/challenge system, and the Responsibility anchor. It need not be an independent Foundational concept to remain explicit and non-substitutable.

**Recommended Stream G disposition:** Authority as a governed supporting relationship/status family; exact legitimacy and constitutional authority remain 2.2–2.3 and external authority.

### 8.4 Why Accountability is not Foundational

Accountability is an arrangement connecting call, examination, answer, assessment, and possible response. It can fail while a Responsibility relation exists and can be engineered without creating valid Responsibility. Its importance is preserved as a supporting family rather than a separate foundation.

**Recommended Stream G disposition:** Governed supporting relationship/arrangement family.

### 8.5 Why the ethical minimum is a qualification, not a second foundation

If the seven-commitment minimum were built into the existence conditions of Responsibility, an unethical or ineffective arrangement could be misdescribed as containing no Responsibility at all. Keeping actual relation and ethical qualification separate permits SymK to say both:

- a Responsibility relation exists under a source; and
- its allocation or governance violates the candidate SymK minimum.

The minimum nevertheless cannot be delegated entirely because SymK compatibility would otherwise permit affected-party erasure, fabricated consent, suppression, incapable accountability, and gap laundering.

**Recommended Stream G disposition:** Governed constitutive qualification/compatibility system attached to Responsibility; constitutional force remains 2.2.

## 26. Consolidated alternatives and rejected interpretations

### Alternative A — Responsibility is causal contribution

**Rejected.** It over-attributes background conditions, underrepresents omissions and prospective duties, cannot explain strict or institutional relations, and collapses empirical and normative claims.

### Alternative B — Responsibility is agency or control

**Rejected.** Agency and control are important factual links but neither is universally necessary or sufficient. This would erase responsibility under role, institution, law, remedy, or prospective governance and imply moral status from operational behavior.

### Alternative C — Responsibility is answerability

**Rejected as exhaustive.** Answerability captures an important response relation but does not exhaust prevention, duty, control, blame, liability, correction, repair, remedy, or cases where no capable forum exists.

### Alternative D — Responsibility is accountability

**Rejected as exhaustive.** Accountability is an arrangement that can be nominal or absent while a Responsibility relation exists. It is vulnerable to audit and process theater.

### Alternative E — Responsibility is legal liability

**Rejected.** It universalizes one authority kind and cannot represent nonlegal prospective, professional, epistemic, organizational, moral, operational, or remedial relations.

### Alternative F — Responsibility belongs to the final approver

**Rejected.** It creates a Human or authority sink and erases design, information, control, delegation, supervision, deployment, and response relations.

### Alternative G — Responsibility belongs to the most capable or controlling subject

**Rejected.** Capability and control do not create source, authority, duty, legitimacy, consent, or complete allocation.

### Alternative H — Responsibility is a conserved allocation pie

**Rejected.** Different relation kinds are not commensurable; one subject's relation need not reduce another's; percentages require Domain frames.

### Alternative I — Distribution creates shared or equal Responsibility

**Rejected.** Shared, joint, collective, institutional, distributed, and hybrid relations require their own sources, objects, levels, and Evidence.

### Alternative J — Complexity creates a responsibility gap

**Rejected.** A gap is object- and response-specific and may be genuine, manufactured, representational, or governance failure.

### Alternative K — One universal Human or institution is residual bearer

**Rejected.** It assigns relations by type or convenience and may name incapable or unauthorized subjects. Source-specific residual or nondelegable duties remain possible.

### Alternative L — Ethical adequacy is legal compliance

**Rejected.** Legal authority governs within jurisdiction but does not exhaust consent, affected standing, challenge, response, uncertainty, repair, or moral disagreement.

### Alternative M — Consent is the complete ethical criterion

**Rejected.** Consent can be unnecessary, invalid, insufficient, unrepresentative, nonbinding on third parties, or unable to waive duties and remedies.

### Alternative N — Transparency and explanation are accountability

**Rejected.** Information artifacts without capable answerability, challenge, correction, repair, or remedy remain theater.

### Alternative O — The ethical minimum defines whether Responsibility exists

**Rejected.** It would make unethical or incapable arrangements appear responsibility-free. Ethical compatibility remains a separate qualification.

### Alternative P — Pure Domain pluralism

**Rejected.** It leaves SymK unable to prevent responsibility disappearance, affected erasure, consent theater, suppression, or implementation-owned semantics.

### Alternative Q — Sourced relational Responsibility plus bounded ethical qualification

**Retained as the recommended proposal direction.** It preserves plural authority, divergence, allocation, response, and non-empty SymK compatibility without selecting a complete theory.
